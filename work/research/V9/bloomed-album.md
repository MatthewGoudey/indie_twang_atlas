_**Bloomed**_ is the debut album by the American musician [Richard Buckner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_Buckner_(musician) "Richard Buckner (musician)").[\[1\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bloomed#cite_note-1)[\[2\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bloomed#cite_note-2) It was released in 1994 via the German label [Glitterhouse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glitterhouse "Glitterhouse"), and the following year in the United States via [Dejadisc](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dejadisc "Dejadisc").[\[3\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bloomed#cite_note-auto1-3)[\[4\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bloomed#cite_note-MH-4) A 1999 reissue appended five bonus tracks.[\[5\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bloomed#cite_note-5)

## Production

Produced by [Lloyd Maines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lloyd_Maines "Lloyd Maines"), the album was recorded in [Lubbock, Texas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lubbock,_Texas "Lubbock, Texas"), with additional studio work taking place in Austin.[\[6\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bloomed#cite_note-6)[\[7\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bloomed#cite_note-7) [Butch Hancock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Butch_Hancock "Butch Hancock") played harmonica on the album; [Ponty Bone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ponty_Bone "Ponty Bone") played accordion.[\[8\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bloomed#cite_note-8) Buckner decided to make the album without using a rhythm section.[\[9\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bloomed#cite_note-9)

Buckner has originally intended for _Bloomed_ to be a concept album about his lineage and family.[\[3\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bloomed#cite_note-auto1-3)

Professional ratings

_[The Washington Post](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Washington_Post "The Washington Post")_ wrote that "Buckner's compelling, dust-streaked voice and his knack for visual detail and unexpected metaphor make him a natural disciple of the Lubbock church, but he's still a novice when it comes to the rites of irreverent humor."[\[14\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bloomed#cite_note-WP-14) The _[Nashville Scene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nashville_Scene "Nashville Scene")_ thought that "Buckner’s ghostly, sensual voice is the perfect vehicle for his bittersweet songs of parting and regret—he often sounds like Dwight Yoakam, only less self-conscious and more literate."[\[15\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bloomed#cite_note-15) The _[Orlando Sentinel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orlando_Sentinel "Orlando Sentinel")_ praised Buckner's "genuinely lovely, earthy baritone."[\[12\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bloomed#cite_note-OS-12)

The _[Chicago Tribune](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chicago_Tribune "Chicago Tribune")_ determined that _Bloomed_ "starts off strong with several melodically driving tunes and cutting lyrical introspection, but after that engaging intro the proceedings start to drag."[\[16\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bloomed#cite_note-16) The _[Austin American-Statesman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austin_American-Statesman "Austin American-Statesman")_ considered it "a sorrowful, almost frighteningly emotional album loaded with regrets."[\[17\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bloomed#cite_note-17) _[Spin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spin_(magazine) "Spin (magazine)")_ listed it as the 19th best album of 1995.[\[18\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bloomed#cite_note-18)

[AllMusic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AllMusic "AllMusic") thought that "Maines and his small crew of musicians ... give Buckner's songs all the care they deserve, adding to the nuances of the melodies and moods with a master's touch, and Maines' own steel guitar work is especially striking."[\[10\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bloomed#cite_note-auto2-10) In a 1997 concert review, _[The New York Times](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_New_York_Times "The New York Times")_ noted the "dark, poetic lining to his sparse songs, an edge that made his album _Bloomed_ one of 1995's most compelling debuts."[\[19\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bloomed#cite_note-19) Reviewing a 1999 reissue, _[Pitchfork](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pitchfork_(website) "Pitchfork (website)")_ wrote that "when Buckner veers closer to traditional folk, he invests his songs with an almost apocalyptic urgency."[\[13\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bloomed#cite_note-auto-13)
