Musical artist

**Richard Slaid Cleaves** (born June 9, 1964) is an American [folk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Contemporary_folk_music "Contemporary folk music") singer-songwriter who has been performing extensively and recording albums in the [Americana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Americana_(music) "Americana (music)") genre since the early 1990s.[\[1\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slaid_Cleaves#cite_note-1) Raised in [South Berwick, Maine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Berwick,_Maine "South Berwick, Maine"), he has lived in the [Austin, Texas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austin,_Texas "Austin, Texas"), area since 1992. [\[2\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slaid_Cleaves#cite_note-2)

Cleaves's musical roots extend back prior to his days playing in a high school "garage band" with his childhood friend [Rod Picott](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rod_Picott "Rod Picott"). The two shared a love of music, especially [Bruce Springsteen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bruce_Springsteen "Bruce Springsteen") and named their band [The Magic Rats](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Magic_Rats?action=edit&redlink=1 "The Magic Rats"), after a character in Springsteen's song "Jungleland."

He brought his love of American artists such as [Woody Guthrie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Woody_Guthrie "Woody Guthrie"), [Johnny Cash](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johnny_Cash "Johnny Cash"), [Hank Williams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hank_Williams "Hank Williams"), [Chuck Berry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chuck_Berry "Chuck Berry"), [Bruce Springsteen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bruce_Springsteen "Bruce Springsteen"), [Tom Petty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tom_Petty "Tom Petty"), [Tom Waits](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tom_Waits "Tom Waits"), [Creedence Clearwater Revival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Creedence_Clearwater_Revival "Creedence Clearwater Revival"), and more with him to [Cork, Ireland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cork_(city) "Cork (city)"), where he spent his junior year of college. To help pass the time he learned how to play the songs on guitar and on November 18, 1985, he made his debut as a [busker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Busker "Busker") in Cork City, Ireland.

After several false starts he started to gain notice around Portland, Maine and in 1990 released his debut cassette, _The Promise._ Only a few songs off this album, "Sweet Summertime", "Lonesome Highway" and "Wrecking Ball" still occasionally get played in concert. The original tape has unfortunately been lost, and only copies remain.

That was followed a year later by _Looks Good from the Road_, recorded with his rock band, [The Moxie Men](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Moxie_Men?action=edit&redlink=1 "The Moxie Men"), which featured Cleaves on lead vocals and acoustic guitar, his brother J. on bass, [Mark Cousins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mark_Cousins_(musician)?action=edit&redlink=1 "Mark Cousins (musician)") on drums and [Pip Walter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pip_Walter?action=edit&redlink=1 "Pip Walter") on electric guitar, acoustic guitar, and vocals. By the end of the year they were the darlings of the Portland press and touted as one of the bands "most likely to succeed."

However, Cleaves's solo acoustic side took over and in 1991 he moved with his wife, Karen, to Austin, Texas. In 1992, he was a winner of the prestigious New Folk competition at the [Kerrville Folk Festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kerrville_Folk_Festival "Kerrville Folk Festival"), an award previously given to such artists as [Nanci Griffith](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nanci_Griffith "Nanci Griffith"), [Robert Earl Keen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Earl_Keen "Robert Earl Keen") and [Steve Earle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steve_Earle "Steve Earle").

Cleaves continued to work hard in Austin, playing various clubs around the city, touring, and continuing to hone his craft as a songwriter. In 1997, he recorded and released his first national album, _No Angel Knows_ for the Rounder-Philo label, which has been his home ever since.

In 2000, Cleaves had an Americana charts hit with his album _Broke Down_ and song of the same name. The title track was co-written with his childhood friend Picott, whom he grew up with in Maine.[\[3\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slaid_Cleaves#cite_note-3)

Cleaves continued to gain notice[\[4\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slaid_Cleaves#cite_note-4) with his follow-up album, 2004's _Wishbones_, appearing on the ESPN2 show _Cold Pizza_, and his music is praised by Nicholson Baker in his 2009 novel, The Anthologist.[\[5\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slaid_Cleaves#cite_note-5)

In 2006 Cleaves released _Unsung_, a collection of songs written by other singer-songwriters including [Graham Weber](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Graham_Weber?action=edit&redlink=1 "Graham Weber"), [J. J. Baron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/J._J._Baron?action=edit&redlink=1 "J. J. Baron"), [Michael O'Connor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_O'Connor_(musician)?action=edit&redlink=1 "Michael O'Connor (musician)") (who often tours with Cleaves), [Adam Carroll](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adam_Carroll_(American_musician) "Adam Carroll (American musician)"), and [Nicole St. Pierre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nicole_St._Pierre?action=edit&redlink=1 "Nicole St. Pierre").

Dreamer: A Tribute to [Kent Finlay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kent_Finlay "Kent Finlay"), released in early 2016 on Austin-based Eight 30 Records, features Cleaves' version of "Lost," his co-write with Finlay.[\[6\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slaid_Cleaves#cite_note-6)

Highway Prayer: A Tribute to Adam Carroll, released in late 2016 on Austin-based Eight 30 Records, features Cleaves' version of "South of Town."

Cleaves tours nationally performing solo and with a band in many configurations.
