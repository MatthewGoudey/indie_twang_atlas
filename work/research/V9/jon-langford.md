Musical artist

**Jonathan Denis Langford** (born 11 October 1957) is a Welsh[\[1\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jon_Langford#cite_note-VVoice-Christgau-2003-1)[\[2\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jon_Langford#cite_note-AllMusic-Langford-Bio-2) musician and artist based in Chicago, Illinois, United States.[\[3\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jon_Langford#cite_note-CoS-SweetHomeChicago-2014-3)

Langford is a founder member of the [punk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Punk_rock "Punk rock") band [The Mekons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Mekons "The Mekons"), the [post-punk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Post-punk "Post-punk") group [The Three Johns](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Three_Johns "The Three Johns"), and the [alternative country](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alternative_country "Alternative country") ensembles [The Waco Brothers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Waco_Brothers "The Waco Brothers") and [Pine Valley Cosmonauts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pine_Valley_Cosmonauts "Pine Valley Cosmonauts").

## Early life

Langford was born in [Newport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Newport,_Wales "Newport, Wales"), Wales, the youngest son of Kit Langford and Denis Langford, a registered chartered accountant for Lloyd's Brewery and [Deloitte & Touche](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deloitte "Deloitte").[\[4\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jon_Langford#cite_note-SouthWalesArgus-FirstPerson-2013-4)[\[5\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jon_Langford#cite_note-VerseChorus-DenisLangford-5) Langford's older brother is science-fiction author and critic [David Langford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Langford "David Langford"), who lives in [Reading, Berkshire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reading,_Berkshire "Reading, Berkshire").[\[4\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jon_Langford#cite_note-SouthWalesArgus-FirstPerson-2013-4)

When he was young, Langford would visit his grandparents in [Croesyceiliog](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Croesyceiliog "Croesyceiliog"), whose family friend ran two pubs, the Cambrian Arms and The Six in Hand.[\[4\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jon_Langford#cite_note-SouthWalesArgus-FirstPerson-2013-4) He attended Gaer Infants School and Gaer Junior School, then Brynglas Primary School, the [Newport High School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Newport_High_School,_Bettws_Lane "Newport High School, Bettws Lane") middle school on Queen's Hill.[\[4\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jon_Langford#cite_note-SouthWalesArgus-FirstPerson-2013-4) In 1972–1973, after playing rugby and football, at the age of 15 Langford decided he liked playing music better. He played a lot of [David Bowie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Bowie "David Bowie") and was listening to a lot of [Man](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Man_(band) "Man (band)").[\[4\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jon_Langford#cite_note-SouthWalesArgus-FirstPerson-2013-4)

Langford attended art school at [University of Leeds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Leeds "University of Leeds") as a painter.[\[6\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jon_Langford#cite_note-AustinChronicle-StillPunk-2012-6) He left school temporarily when The Mekons were founded, but later went back to college and finished his degree.[\[4\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jon_Langford#cite_note-SouthWalesArgus-FirstPerson-2013-4)

## Music

Since the mid-1980s, Langford has been one of the leaders in incorporating [folk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Folk_music "Folk music") and [country music](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Country_music "Country music") into punk rock. He has released a number of solo recordings as well as recordings with other bands outside of [The Mekons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Mekons "The Mekons"), most notably the [Waco Brothers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Waco_Brothers "Waco Brothers"), which he co-founded after moving to Chicago in the early 1990s.

In a 2010 interview, Langford said his earliest influences were [Tom Jones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tom_Jones_(singer) "Tom Jones (singer)"), [Slade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slade "Slade"), [T. Rex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/T._Rex_(band) "T. Rex (band)"), [The Kinks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Kinks "The Kinks"), [Johnny Cash](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johnny_Cash "Johnny Cash"), [Man](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Man_(band) "Man (band)") and [Black Sabbath](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_Sabbath "Black Sabbath").[\[7\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jon_Langford#cite_note-Blurt-FatWelshBastard-2010-7)

### The Mekons

Main article: [The Mekons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Mekons "The Mekons")

Langford was originally the drummer for the [punk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Punk_rock "Punk rock") band " [The Mekons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Mekons_(band) "The Mekons (band)")" when it formed at the [University of Leeds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Leeds "University of Leeds") in 1977, but he later took up the guitar as other band members left.[\[8\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jon_Langford#cite_note-AllMusic-Mekons-Bio-8) The Mekons were signed to [Virgin Records](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Virgin_Records "Virgin Records") but according to Langford they "got fired."[\[4\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jon_Langford#cite_note-SouthWalesArgus-FirstPerson-2013-4) They played their first United States appearance on New Year's Eve in 1980, gave up live performances for a while, and released 1982's _The Mekons Story_. They began performing again in public in 1984, playing their first shows as benefits for the British miners' union.[\[9\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jon_Langford#cite_note-NYTimes-SoGoodHurts-1988-9) After being signed by major American label [A&M Records](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A&M_Records "A&M Records") in the late 1980s, label shuffling resulted in the band trying to leave the label. In response, they recorded _[Curse of the Mekons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Curse_of_the_Mekons "Curse of the Mekons"),_ which the label refused to release, becoming available in the U.S. only as an import.[\[10\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jon_Langford#cite_note-NYTimes-ShedLabel-1991-10) A documentary called _The Revenge of the Mekons_ was released in 2014 by director Joe Angio.[\[11\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jon_Langford#cite_note-NYTimes-RevengeDoc-2014-11) The Mekons continue to record and perform live, as of 2022 and 2026.

### The Three Johns

Main article: [The Three Johns](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Three_Johns "The Three Johns")

With John Hyatt and Phillip Brennan, Langford released several albums of drum-machine-fueled punk between 1982 and 1987. A retrospective box set was released in August 2015.[\[12\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jon_Langford#cite_note-12) Also, in 1988 [Tom Greenhalgh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tom_Greenhalgh "Tom Greenhalgh"), Langford's colleague in the Mekons, collaborated with members of the Three Johns as the Jelly Bishops to release the five-song EP "Kings Of Barstool Mountain".[\[13\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jon_Langford#cite_note-13)

### Dim Subooteyo

As "Dim Subooteyo", [Brendan Croker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brendan_Croker "Brendan Croker") and Langford released the album _Lakeside_ (1990, SNAT Records).[\[14\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jon_Langford#cite_note-14)

### The Killer Shrews

The "Killer Shrews" were a group composed of Langford (lead vocals), [Gary Lucas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gary_Lucas "Gary Lucas"), and [Tony Maimone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tony_Maimone "Tony Maimone"). They released one self-titled album on Enemy Records in 1993.[\[15\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jon_Langford#cite_note-15)

### The Waco Brothers

Main article: [The Waco Brothers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Waco_Brothers "The Waco Brothers")

The "Waco Brothers" make country-punk music, and are a Chicago-based amalgam of players from the [Pine Valley Cosmonauts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pine_Valley_Cosmonauts "Pine Valley Cosmonauts") family and others, who have been recording since 1995, as of 2021. For their first albums, they included Dean Schlabowske (guitar/vocal), Tracey Dear (mandolin/vocal), [Alan Doughty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alan_Doughty "Alan Doughty") (bass/vocal), [Mark Durante](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mark_Durante "Mark Durante") (pedal steel guitar), and Mekons drummer [Steve Goulding](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steve_Goulding "Steve Goulding")). By 2015, Goulding, now based in New York City, had been replaced by Joe Camarillo (drums), and Durante had left. Camarillo died in January 2021, and was replaced by Dan Massey. Jean Cook (violin, vocal) joined in 2020.

### Pine Valley Cosmonauts

Main article: [Pine Valley Cosmonauts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pine_Valley_Cosmonauts "Pine Valley Cosmonauts")

Langford initiated another project, the " [Pine Valley Cosmonauts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pine_Valley_Cosmonauts "Pine Valley Cosmonauts")", which performs the music of other country music groups. Several alternative country musicians have guested alongside a revolving assortment of Chicago musicians who have backed both Langford and other musicians such as [Kelly Hogan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kelly_Hogan "Kelly Hogan").

### Wee Hairy Beasties

Main article: [Wee Hairy Beasties](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wee_Hairy_Beasties "Wee Hairy Beasties")

The "Wee Hairy Beasties" were a children's music group based in Chicago, composed of Jon Langford, [Sally Timms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sally_Timms "Sally Timms"), [Kelly Hogan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kelly_Hogan "Kelly Hogan"), and [Devil in a Woodpile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Devil_in_a_Woodpile "Devil in a Woodpile"). They played their first gig together at the [Brookfield Zoo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brookfield_Zoo "Brookfield Zoo") near Chicago, and released two albums in 2006 and 2008.[\[16\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jon_Langford#cite_note-eMusic-Int-16)[\[17\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jon_Langford#cite_note-17)

### Men of Gwent

The "Men of [Gwent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Gwent "Kingdom of Gwent")" are mainly Newport-based musicians, including members of Give Me Memphis and [The Darling Buds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Darling_Buds "The Darling Buds"). Previously known as LL, the group have written and recorded intermittently for over 20 years\[ _[when?](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Manual_of_Style/Dates_and_numbers#Chronological_items "Wikipedia:Manual of Style/Dates and numbers")_\], and have been playing live since 2007.[\[18\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jon_Langford#cite_note-18)[\[19\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jon_Langford#cite_note-19) As LL, their only release was a demo track ("Rechem") on the 1999 compilation _Fear of a Red Planet_.[\[20\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jon_Langford#cite_note-20) Their debut album _The Legend of LL_ was released on Country Mile Records in 2015 and included reworkings of several songs from the same LL demos, as well as a new version of " [Pill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pillgwenlly "Pillgwenlly") Sailor", first released on _Skull Orchard_ in 1998.[\[21\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jon_Langford#cite_note-21) Their second album _President of Wales_ was released in November 2019, also on Country Mile.[\[22\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jon_Langford#cite_note-22)

### Solo, Skull Orchard, etc.

Langford's first official solo album, _[Skull Orchard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Skull_Orchard "Skull Orchard")_, a look back at his hometown of Newport, Wales, was released in 1998. He followed it with _All the Fame of Lofty Deeds_, in 2004, and _Gold Brick_ in 2006.

Ever since releasing the original 2008 album, Langford continued to intermittently use the "Skull Orchard" band name, usually when recording or performing live with accompanying electric instruments. Skull Orchard has almost always included bassist [Alan Doughty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alan_Doughty "Alan Doughty"), also of the Waco Brothers.[\[23\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jon_Langford#cite_note-LATimes-OldDevils-2010-23) In 2010 and 2011 for example, frequent members also included Joe Camarillo (of the Waco Brothers), Jim Elkington, Jean Cook, and [Tawny Newsome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tawny_Newsome "Tawny Newsome").[\[24\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jon_Langford#cite_note-24)[\[25\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jon_Langford#cite_note-25) Recordings credited to Jon Langford and the Skull Orchard include his 2010 _Old Devils_ album, his 2014 _Here Be Monsters_ album, and, starting in 2020, some of the songs in his _Jon Langford's Lucky Seven Series_ of singles.[\[26\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jon_Langford#cite_note-JLLucky7Pt1_bandcamp-26)[\[27\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jon_Langford#cite_note-JLLucky7Pt2_bandcamp-27)

In the late 2000s Langford met the " [Burlington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Burlington,_Ontario "Burlington, Ontario") \[Ontario, Canada\] Welsh Male Chorus", who are based near [Toronto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toronto "Toronto"). He invited them first to accompany him at a CeltFest in Chicago in 2007, and then to re-record the whole of the _Skull Orchard_. The album _Skull Orchard Revisited_ (credited to Jon Langford and the Burlington Welsh Male Chorus) was released on 3 June 2011 by Bloodshot Records.[\[28\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jon_Langford#cite_note-Dusted-SkullOrchardRevisited-2011-28) Langford continued to intermittently perform live with the chorus.[\[29\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jon_Langford#cite_note-JL_HSB-29)

Circa 2003, Langford started the band "Ship & Pilot", to perform his songs, especially in and near [New York City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_City "New York City"). It continued to perform into 2006, and very occasionally since, including in 2022. Ship & Pilot also included [Tony Maimone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tony_Maimone "Tony Maimone"), [Jean Cook](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ida_(band) "Ida (band)"), usually Sally Timms, and on drums variously [Steve Goulding](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steve_Goulding "Steve Goulding") or Dan Massey.[\[30\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jon_Langford#cite_note-30)[\[31\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jon_Langford#cite_note-31)[\[32\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jon_Langford#cite_note-32)[\[33\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jon_Langford#cite_note-33)[\[34\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jon_Langford#cite_note-34)

At the 2014 [Hideout](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hideout_Inn "Hideout Inn") Block Party in Chicago, Langford debuted the band "Bad Luck Jonathan" (a name likely inspired by that of then-president of Nigeria, [Goodluck Jonathan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Goodluck_Jonathan "Goodluck Jonathan")).[\[35\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jon_Langford#cite_note-35)[\[36\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jon_Langford#cite_note-36) The band, described as "socialist voodoo space boogie",[\[37\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jon_Langford#cite_note-37) featured [Alan Doughty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alan_Doughty "Alan Doughty") and Joe Camarillo from the [Waco Brothers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Waco_Brothers "Waco Brothers"), [Phil Wandscher](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phil_Wandscher "Phil Wandscher") from [Whiskeytown](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Whiskeytown "Whiskeytown"), and Martin Billheimer from Chicago's former Devil Bell Hippies (and the [Pritzker Military Museum and Library](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pritzker_Military_Museum_and_Library "Pritzker Military Museum and Library")).[\[38\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jon_Langford#cite_note-38)[\[39\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jon_Langford#cite_note-39) According to Langford, "Bad Luck Jonathan is very loud and unashamedly 1971."[\[40\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jon_Langford#cite_note-LoneStarMusic2016_JLangford-40)

"Four Lost Souls" was a collaboration between Langford, [John Szymanski](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Szymanski?action=edit&redlink=1 "John Szymanski"), [Tawny Newsome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tawny_Newsome "Tawny Newsome") and [Bethany Thomas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bethany_Thomas?action=edit&redlink=1 "Bethany Thomas"). Their eponymous debut album was produced by [Norbert Putnam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norbert_Putnam "Norbert Putnam") and released in 2017, followed by live performances.[\[41\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jon_Langford#cite_note-41)[\[42\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jon_Langford#cite_note-42)

"Jon Langford & The Bright Shiners", with [Tamineh Gueramy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tamineh_Gueramy?action=edit&redlink=1 "Tamineh Gueramy") (fiddle), [Alice Spencer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alice_Spencer_(musician)?action=edit&redlink=1 "Alice Spencer (musician)") ( [Mellotron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mellotron "Mellotron")), and John Szymanski (guitar) - and all on vocals - was formed circa 2022. By 2024 they were touring occasionally around the US and in the UK. They released an album in 2024.[\[43\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jon_Langford#cite_note-43)[\[44\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jon_Langford#cite_note-44)[\[45\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jon_Langford#cite_note-45)[\[46\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jon_Langford#cite_note-JL&BS_WhereItReallyStarts2024-46)[\[47\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jon_Langford#cite_note-R&RGlobe-interview_2024June-47)[\[48\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jon_Langford#cite_note-48)

Aside from the above-mentioned bands, Langford has performed with many different musicians over the years, but his most constant live collaborator has been singer and fellow Mekon [Sally Timms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sally_Timms "Sally Timms"), ever since they both moved to Chicago. As of 2024, they continue to collaborate on various recording and performance projects.[\[49\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jon_Langford#cite_note-49)[\[50\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jon_Langford#cite_note-CoffeeGalleryBackstage2022-50)

## Visual art

Langford is also a painter and graphic designer. Langford has painted portraits of figures from country music, including [Hank Williams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hank_Williams "Hank Williams"), [Johnny Cash](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johnny_Cash "Johnny Cash"), [Elvis Presley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elvis_Presley "Elvis Presley"), and " [The Cuckoo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Cuckoo_(song) "The Cuckoo (song)")", often based on well known photographs. Many of his paintings and prints are available from the Yard Dog Art Gallery in Austin, TX, and LeMieux Galleries in New Orleans, LA, and [Hatch Show Print](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hatch_Show_Print "Hatch Show Print") Gallery in Nashville, TN as of 2021.[\[61\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jon_Langford#cite_note-61)[\[62\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jon_Langford#cite_note-62) _Nashville Radio_, a collection of his artwork and writings, was published in 2006.[\[63\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jon_Langford#cite_note-63)

Langford has designed and painted the covers for many music recordings. These include, but are not limited to, most of the recordings on which he has been the musical leader, and many recordings of his other bands.[\[2\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jon_Langford#cite_note-AllMusic-Langford-Bio-2) Other examples include the cover art of _The Sandinista! Project – A Tribute to The Clash_[\[64\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jon_Langford#cite_note-[[Sandanista!Project_AllMusic]]-64) and "Commercial Suicide Man" (2018),[\[65\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jon_Langford#cite_note-65) a collaborative single by [the Nightingales](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Nightingales "The Nightingales") and [Vic Godard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vic_Godard "Vic Godard").

In 2015, Langford was commissioned by the [Country Music Hall of Fame and Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Country_Music_Hall_of_Fame_and_Museum "Country Music Hall of Fame and Museum") to paint a series of portraits for its " [Dylan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bob_Dylan "Bob Dylan"), Cash, and the Nashville Cats: A New Music City" exhibition, which opened on 27 March 2015.[\[66\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jon_Langford#cite_note-66)[\[67\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jon_Langford#cite_note-67) Of that exhibit, per Langford, "I said to my wife, 'They’ve got ‘The Death of Country Music’ on the wall at the Country Music Hall of Fame', and she just went, 'Well, I guess you won, then'.”[\[40\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jon_Langford#cite_note-LoneStarMusic2016_JLangford-40) That commission lead to a collaboration between Langford and [Hatch Show Print](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hatch_Show_Print "Hatch Show Print") Master Printer [Jim Sherraden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jim_Sherraden?action=edit&redlink=1 "Jim Sherraden").[\[68\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jon_Langford#cite_note-68) Their artwork was then adapted for the album covers of the 2015 double-LP compilation _[Dylan, Cash, and the Nashville Cats: A New Music City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dylan,_Cash,_and_the_Nashville_Cats:_A_New_Music_City "Dylan, Cash, and the Nashville Cats: A New Music City")_ and of the 2016 triple-LP _[Trio: Farther Along](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Complete_Trio_Collection "The Complete Trio Collection")_ by [Dolly Parton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dolly_Parton "Dolly Parton"), [Linda Ronstadt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linda_Ronstadt "Linda Ronstadt"), and [Emmylou Harris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emmylou_Harris "Emmylou Harris").

Langford has designed the graphics for [T-shirts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/T-shirt "T-shirt"), [tote bags](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tote_bag "Tote bag"), and other items to support various organisations, including: Chicago's [Giveashirt](https://www.giveashirt.net/), which benefits [StreetWise](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/StreetWise "StreetWise") magazine (which is sold by people who are homeless or at risk of being homeless);[\[69\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jon_Langford#cite_note-69) Chicago's [Hideout Inn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hideout_Inn "Hideout Inn") during the [COVID-19 pandemic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/COVID-19_pandemic "COVID-19 pandemic");[\[70\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jon_Langford#cite_note-70) Chicago's [Old Town School of Folk Music](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_Town_School_of_Folk_Music "Old Town School of Folk Music");\[ _[citation needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed "Wikipedia:Citation needed")_\] and San Francisco's [Hardly Strictly Bluegrass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hardly_Strictly_Bluegrass "Hardly Strictly Bluegrass") festival.\[ _[citation needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed "Wikipedia:Citation needed")_\] Langford's paintings appear on bottles and other items for the [Dogfish Head Brewery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dogfish_Head_Brewery "Dogfish Head Brewery"),[\[71\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jon_Langford#cite_note-Dogfish-Q&A-2013-71) and Few Spirits. Since 2015, Langford has designed covers for a series of novels by author [Jay Spencer Green](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jay_Spencer_Green?action=edit&redlink=1 "Jay Spencer Green"), including _Breakfast at Cannibal Joe's_ and _Ivy Feckett is Looking for Love_.[\[72\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jon_Langford#cite_note-72)[\[73\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jon_Langford#cite_note-73)

For over 10 years, Langford illustrated the pop-music parody comic strip _[Great Pop Things](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Pop_Things "Great Pop Things")_ under the pseudonym Chuck Death with a friend from his hometown, Newport, Wales, Colin B. Morton, who wrote the text.[\[74\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jon_Langford#cite_note-NYTimes-GreatPopThings-1998-74) The cartoon strip was published in music and alternative weekly newspapers in [London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/London "London"), [Los Angeles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Los_Angeles "Los Angeles"), and Chicago, and was a pen-and-ink history of rock-and-roll.[\[74\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jon_Langford#cite_note-NYTimes-GreatPopThings-1998-74) An anthology of the best strips was published in a book of the same name.[\[75\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jon_Langford#cite_note-Langford-GreatPopThings-2010-75)

## Theatre

- In 2005, Langford's multimedia music/spoken-word/video performance, _The Executioner's Last Songs,_ premiered at [Alverno College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alverno_College "Alverno College"), and has been performed in several other cities.
- In January and February 2009, Chicago's Walkabout Theater Company and Collaboraction premiered a stage adaptation of Langford's _Goldbrick_ that featured a live band, two actors and video projections.[\[78\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jon_Langford#cite_note-Walkabout-Goldbrick-2009-78)
- In November and December 2009, [The House Theatre of Chicago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_House_Theatre_of_Chicago "The House Theatre of Chicago") staged a production of _All the Fame of Lofty Deeds,_ written by rock journalist Mark Guarino and based on Langford's art and 2004 solo album of the same name.[\[79\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jon_Langford#cite_note-HouseTheatre-AllFameLofty-2009-79)
- In November 2018, scored Mark Guarino's play _Take Me_, produced at [Straw Dog Theater Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strawdog_Theatre_Company "Strawdog Theatre Company"). [Chris Jones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chris_Jones_(drama_critic) "Chris Jones (drama critic)"), theatre critic for The [Chicago Tribune](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chicago_Tribune "Chicago Tribune") stated "if you're a fan of Langford’s work, you'll find plenty here to merit a trip to Strawdog: At times, I was tempted to close my eyes, getting lost in the [Bowie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Bowie "David Bowie")-esque longing you can find in these lush and potent melodies, true and vital feeling hidden in pastiche."[\[80\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jon_Langford#cite_note-80)

## Politics and service

Langford considers himself "working class socialist."[\[4\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jon_Langford#cite_note-SouthWalesArgus-FirstPerson-2013-4)

In 1988, Langford co-produced (with [Mark Riley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marc_Riley "Marc Riley")) a [Johnny Cash](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johnny_Cash "Johnny Cash") tribute album, ' _Til Things are Brighter..._,[\[85\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jon_Langford#cite_note-85) to raise funds for the [Terrence Higgins Trust](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Terrence_Higgins_Trust "Terrence Higgins Trust"). The album was endorsed and admired by Cash himself who is featured alongside Langford and Riley on its cover. He and his fellow musicians have done many other musical fundraisers to support various causes, including striking British coal miners and [Doctors Without Borders](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctors_Without_Borders "Doctors Without Borders") ( [The Mekons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Mekons "The Mekons")); and the [Kentuckians for the Commonwealth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kentuckians_for_the_Commonwealth "Kentuckians for the Commonwealth") ( [Freakons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Freakwater#Freakons "Freakwater")).
[\[86\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jon_Langford#cite_note-Freakons_BS2013-86)[\[87\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jon_Langford#cite_note-Freakons_ChicagoReader2017-87)[\[88\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jon_Langford#cite_note-Freakons_ShittyBarn&BrownPaper2017-88)

Langford produced _The Executioner's Last Songs, Vols. 1,[\[89\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jon_Langford#cite_note-89) 2, & 3_[\[90\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jon_Langford#cite_note-90) record compilations of 2002 and 2003, by various singers backed by the [Pine Valley Cosmonauts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pine_Valley_Cosmonauts "Pine Valley Cosmonauts"), to benefit charitable organisations working to end the death penalty ( [Artists Against the Death Penalty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Artists_Against_the_Death_Penalty?action=edit&redlink=1 "Artists Against the Death Penalty"), the Illinois Death Penalty Moratorium Project,[\[91\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jon_Langford#cite_note-91) and the National Coalition To Abolish the Death Penalty). In 2006, Langford was commissioned to develop a performance[\[92\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jon_Langford#cite_note-92) based on the compilations for the [Museum of Contemporary Art](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Museum_of_Contemporary_Art,_Chicago "Museum of Contemporary Art, Chicago") in Chicago; the show was also performed at the Walker Art Center in Minneapolis.

As mentioned above, Langford has designed and contributed graphics to a variety of organisations. Langford was an honorary board member of the Chicago-based nonprofit organisation [Rock for Kids](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rock_for_Kids "Rock for Kids").

## Personal life

Langford is married to architect and jewellery designer, Helen Tsatsos. They were introduced in 1986 at a party after a Three Johns gig in her home town of Chicago.[\[4\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jon_Langford#cite_note-SouthWalesArgus-FirstPerson-2013-4) They've lived in Chicago ever since their marriage in 1992. They have two children.[\[93\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jon_Langford#cite_note-TimeOut-Langford-Kids-2006-93)
