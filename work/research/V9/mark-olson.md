Musical artist

**Mark John Olson**[\[1\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mark_Olson_(musician)#cite_note-1) (born September 18, 1961) is an American musician and singer-songwriter. He was a founding member of [alternative country](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alternative_country "Alternative country") bands [The Jayhawks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Jayhawks "The Jayhawks") and the [Original Harmony Ridge Creekdippers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Original_Harmony_Ridge_Creekdippers "Original Harmony Ridge Creekdippers").

## Career

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Mark_Olson_(musician)&action=edit&section=1 "Edit section: Career")\]

Olson was born in [Minneapolis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minneapolis "Minneapolis"), Minnesota. He formed the [Jayhawks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Jayhawks "The Jayhawks") in 1985 with singer and guitarist [Gary Louris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gary_Louris "Gary Louris") and was originally the principal singer-songwriter in the group.[\[2\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mark_Olson_(musician)#cite_note-NYT-2)

Their first album for Def American was the Drakoulias-produced _[Hollywood Town Hall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hollywood_Town_Hall "Hollywood Town Hall")_ in 1992.[\[3\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mark_Olson_(musician)#cite_note-The_Great_Rock_Discography-3) After a successful single, "Waiting for the Sun", and extensive touring the band went back in the studio and released the follow-up, _[Tomorrow the Green Grass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tomorrow_the_Green_Grass "Tomorrow the Green Grass")_ in 1995, which yielded the radio hit "Blue". The same year Olson quit the Jayhawks to look after his wife, [Victoria Williams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Victoria_Williams "Victoria Williams"), after she was diagnosed with multiple sclerosis, and the band continued without him, releasing three more albums before going on hiatus in 2005.[\[2\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mark_Olson_(musician)#cite_note-NYT-2)[\[4\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mark_Olson_(musician)#cite_note-BB-4)

For his post-Jayhawks career, Olson returned to his folk and country roots and with Williams and multi-instrumentalist Mike "Razz" Russell released the well-received album _[The Original Harmony Ridge Creek Dippers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Original_Harmony_Ridge_Creek_Dippers "The Original Harmony Ridge Creek Dippers")_ in 1997. The trio teamed up again for the 1998 album _[Pacific Coast Rambler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pacific_Coast_Rambler "Pacific Coast Rambler")_ and 1999's _[Zola and the Tulip Tree](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zola_and_the_Tulip_Tree "Zola and the Tulip Tree")_.

[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/3/38/Mark-olson-2010-10-23.jpg/330px-Mark-olson-2010-10-23.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Mark-olson-2010-10-23.jpg) Mark Olson in Örebro, Sweden, October 23, 2010

By 2000, Olson releasing the autobiographical _[My Own Jo Ellen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/My_Own_Jo_Ellen "My Own Jo Ellen")_ as Mark Olson and the Original Harmony Ridge Creekdippers. Two more albums recorded with the Creekdippers followed, _[December's Child](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/December's_Child "December's Child")_ in 2002 (which saw him reunited with Louris for the first time in seven years on the track "Say You'll Be Mine") and _[Mystic Theatre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mystic_Theatre_(album) "Mystic Theatre (album)")_ (which also featured Williams) in 2004. The same year saw the release of another Creekdippers album, _[Political Manifest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Political_Manifest "Political Manifest")_.

After splitting from Williams in 2005, Olson paired up once again with Louris for two short tours in the winter of 2005 and the spring of 2006.[\[5\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mark_Olson_(musician)#cite_note-5) He released his acclaimed solo album _[The Salvation Blues](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Salvation_Blues "The Salvation Blues")_ in June 2007. The album, written while he was staying with Cardiff-based folk singer and writer Charlotte Greig and her husband, novelist John Williams, was inspired by his divorce.[\[6\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mark_Olson_(musician)#cite_note-6)[\[7\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mark_Olson_(musician)#cite_note-7)

After the release of _The Salvation Blues_, Olson toured the United States and Europe with Italian violinist [Michele Gazich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michele_Gazich?action=edit&redlink=1 "Michele Gazich (page does not exist)") and Norwegian multi-instrumentalist Ingunn Ringvold ( [Sailorine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sailorine "Sailorine")) playing [djembe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Djembe "Djembe"), percussion and piano and singing [harmony vocals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harmony_vocals "Harmony vocals"). They played more than 300 shows together.

An album with Louris, _[Ready for the Flood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ready_for_the_Flood "Ready for the Flood")_, produced by [Chris Robinson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chris_Robinson_(singer) "Chris Robinson (singer)") of the Black Crowes, was released in November 2008 (January 2009 in the U.S.).[\[8\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mark_Olson_(musician)#cite_note-8)

In 2011 the Jayhawks reunited and recorded a new album. The lineup consisted of Olson, Louris, Perlman, Karen Grotberg, and Tim O'Reagan, and, as Louris says, "Our goal is to make the best Jayhawks album that's ever been done". The album, _Mockingbird Time_, was released September 20. The band toured in support. In 2012 Olson again left the band,[\[9\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mark_Olson_(musician)#cite_note-9) stating "I don't ever want to see Gary Louris again, nor do I want him singing my songs".[\[10\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mark_Olson_(musician)#cite_note-10)
