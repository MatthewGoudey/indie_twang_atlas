Musical artist

**Jay Stuart Farrar** (born December 26, 1966) is an American songwriter and musician based in [St. Louis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Louis "St. Louis"). A member of two critically acclaimed music groups, [Uncle Tupelo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uncle_Tupelo "Uncle Tupelo") and [Son Volt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Son_Volt "Son Volt"), he began his solo music career in 2001. Beyond being a songwriter, Farrar plays guitar, piano, harmonica, and sings.

## Uncle Tupelo

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Jay_Farrar&action=edit&section=1 "Edit section: Uncle Tupelo")\]

Farrar formed [Uncle Tupelo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uncle_Tupelo "Uncle Tupelo") with [Jeff Tweedy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jeff_Tweedy "Jeff Tweedy") and [Mike Heidorn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mike_Heidorn "Mike Heidorn") in 1987 after the lead singer of their previous band, The Primitives, left to attend college. The trio recorded three albums for Rockville Records, before signing with [Sire Records](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sire_Records "Sire Records") and expanding to being a five-piece. Shortly after the release of the band's major label debut album [_Anodyne_](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anodyne_(album) "Anodyne (album)"), Farrar announced his decision to leave the band owing to a soured relationship with his co-songwriter Tweedy. Uncle Tupelo is often mentioned as one of the originators of the [alt-country](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alternative_country "Alternative country") genre of music or subgenre of country music which combines country, folk, and/or bluegrass with punk rock, alt-rock, indie rock, or other genres.

## Son Volt

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Jay_Farrar&action=edit&section=2 "Edit section: Son Volt")\]

See also: [Son Volt §Discography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Son_Volt#Discography "Son Volt")

After the dissolution of Uncle Tupelo in 1994, Farrar formed the rock group [Son Volt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Son_Volt "Son Volt"), whose original lineup released three albums in the late 1990s, before undergoing a hiatus in 1999. In 1999, Farrar was invited to participate in the [tribute album](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tribute_album "Tribute album") for [Moby Grape](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moby_Grape "Moby Grape") co-founder [Skip Spence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Skip_Spence "Skip Spence"), who was terminally ill with cancer. The album, _[More Oar: A Tribute to the Skip Spence Album](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/More_Oar:_A_Tribute_to_the_Skip_Spence_Album "More Oar: A Tribute to the Skip Spence Album")_ ( [Birdman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Birdman_Records "Birdman Records"), 1999), was an album of [cover versions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cover_version "Cover version") of the songs on Spence's only solo album, _[Oar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oar_(Skip_Spence_album) "Oar (Skip Spence album)")_ ( [Columbia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Columbia_Records "Columbia Records"), 1999). In 2005, the band re-formed with a different lineup and has released seven additional albums.

## Other

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Jay_Farrar&action=edit&section=3 "Edit section: Other")\]

See also: [Jay Farrar discography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jay_Farrar_discography "Jay Farrar discography")

In 1995, Farrar collaborated with Kelly Willis on the song "Rex's Blues", which appeared on [Red Hot + Bothered](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Red_Hot_+_Bothered "Red Hot + Bothered"), an [AIDS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HIV/AIDS "HIV/AIDS") benefit album produced by the [Red Hot Organization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Red_Hot_Organization "Red Hot Organization"). As a solo artist, he has released two full-length albums, two EPs, one film score, and various live recordings. His full-length albums are _[Sebastopol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sebastopol_(album) "Sebastopol (album)")_ (2001) and _[Terroir Blues](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Terroir_Blues "Terroir Blues")_ (2003), the first released on the [independent record label](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Independent_record_label "Independent record label") Artemis Records and the second released on his own label, Transmit Sound.

An EP of songs from the _Sebastopol_ sessions, entitled _ThirdShiftGrottoSlack_ was released in 2002. His score for the [independent film](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Independent_film "Independent film") _The Slaughter Rule_ (2002, directors Alex and Andrew Smith) was released in 2003 on the independent record label Bloodshot Records. Farrar formed his own independent record label in 2003, **Transmit Sound** (formerly called "Act/Resist Records"). In 2004, Farrar released a six-song live acoustic EP entitled _Live EP_. Also 2004 marked the release of the live CD/DVD _[Stone, Steel & Bright Lights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stone,_Steel_&_Bright_Lights "Stone, Steel & Bright Lights")_.

Farrar worked closely with keyboardist [Steven Drozd](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steven_Drozd "Steven Drozd") of [The Flaming Lips](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Flaming_Lips "The Flaming Lips") during the recording of _Sebastopol_. Eric Heywood, [Mark Spencer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mark_Spencer_(guitarist) "Mark Spencer (guitarist)") from the [Blood Oranges](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blood_Oranges_(band) "Blood Oranges (band)"), and the rock group [Canyon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canyon_(indie_rock_band) "Canyon (indie rock band)") have often accompanied Farrar in his solo recordings and performances. Spencer is now a full-time member of Son Volt. In 2006, Farrar announced the formation of a new band, [Gob Iron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gob_Iron "Gob Iron"), with Varnaline's Anders Parker. The songs which would make up their debut album were recorded in autumn 2004, while Farrar was in the process of recording a new Son Volt album. In April 2019, Parker and Farrar released a [7-inch single](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Single_(music) "Single (music)") using the Gob Iron moniker.

Farrar collaborated with [Ben Gibbard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ben_Gibbard "Ben Gibbard") (of the indie rock band [Death Cab for Cutie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Death_Cab_for_Cutie "Death Cab for Cutie")) to create all of the music for the soundtrack for the 2009 documentary film _One Fast Move or I'm Gone_, about [Jack Kerouac](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jack_Kerouac "Jack Kerouac")'s time spent at [Big Sur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Big_Sur "Big Sur"). The [soundtrack](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/One_Fast_Move_or_I'm_Gone "One Fast Move or I'm Gone") was released on October 20, 2009.[\[2\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jay_Farrar#cite_note-2) In 2012, Farrar along with [WillJohnson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Will_Johnson_(musician) "Will Johnson (musician)"), [YimYames](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jim_James "Jim James"), and [Anders Parker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anders_Parker "Anders Parker") collaborated on the [Woody Guthrie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Woody_Guthrie "Woody Guthrie") archive project, _[New Multitudes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Multitudes "New Multitudes")_.

## Style

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Jay_Farrar&action=edit&section=4 "Edit section: Style")\]

His musical style ranges from sparse, unaccompanied [folk music](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Folk_music "Folk music") to full [rock and roll](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rock_and_roll "Rock and roll") band arrangements comparable to [Neil Young](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neil_Young "Neil Young") or [Dinosaur Jr.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dinosaur_Jr. "Dinosaur Jr.") His solo recordings also often include sound experiments, reminiscent of psychedelia, with a distinct [Eastern](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_world "Eastern world") bent. One of the hallmarks of his sound is the use of alternate tunings on the [guitar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guitar "Guitar"). His love for [Woody Guthrie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Woody_Guthrie "Woody Guthrie") inspired a custom guitar made by [Creston Lea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Creston_Electric_Instruments "Creston Electric Instruments") of [Burlington, Vermont](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Burlington,_Vermont "Burlington, Vermont"). The guitar was made from artifacts Farrar gathered from the site of Guthrie's childhood home.
