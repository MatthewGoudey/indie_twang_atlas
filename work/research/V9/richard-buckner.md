Musical artist

**Richard Carl Buckner** is an American singer-songwriter born in California, United States. After living in [Edmonton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edmonton "Edmonton"), Alberta, for a number of years, he relocated to [Brooklyn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brooklyn "Brooklyn"), New York.

## Background

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Richard_Buckner_(musician)&action=edit&section=1 "Edit section: Background")\]

Buckner's solo career began with _[Bloomed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bloomed "Bloomed")_ (1994), a [lyrically](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lyrics "Lyrics") dense suite of songs recorded in [Lubbock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lubbock,_Texas "Lubbock, Texas"), Texas, and produced by [Lloyd Maines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lloyd_Maines "Lloyd Maines"). It was released when Buckner was the frontman of the band The Doubters, which was not achieving very much success at the time.[\[2\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_Buckner_(musician)#cite_note-2) In January 1996, while living in San Francisco, he recorded an album's worth of acoustic songs, all of which reappeared in more fully realized forms on his second and third albums. The CD was self-produced and self-released, and was sold exclusively at his early shows.

Later that year, he [signed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Recording_contract "Recording contract") with [MCA Records](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MCA_Inc. "MCA Inc."), for whom he recorded two albums, both produced by [J.D. Foster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/J.D._Foster "J.D. Foster"). _[Devotion + Doubt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Devotion_+_Doubt "Devotion + Doubt")_ was released in 1997, displaying a more adventurous, almost [avant-garde](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Avant-garde "Avant-garde") approach to songwriting and [arranging](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arrangement "Arrangement"), and featuring backing from members of the band [Giant Sand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Giant_Sand "Giant Sand"), as well as Maines and [Marc Ribot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marc_Ribot "Marc Ribot"), among others. Its follow-up, 1998's _Since_, continues in this style, with an even greater emphasis on detailed production, this time featuring contributions from [John McEntire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_McEntire "John McEntire"), [Dave Schramm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dave_Schramm_(musician) "Dave Schramm (musician)"), [David Grubbs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Grubbs "David Grubbs"), [Syd Straw](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syd_Straw "Syd Straw"), and others. Although these albums garnered considerable critical approval for Buckner, they did not perform well enough for his label, and he was released from his contract with [MCA Records](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MCA_Records "MCA Records") (whom he then nicknamed "Musical Career Assassins") shortly thereafter.[\[3\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_Buckner_(musician)#cite_note-3)

His 2000 album, _[The Hill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Hill_(Richard_Buckner_Album) "The Hill (Richard Buckner Album)"),_ was released by [Overcoat Recordings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Overcoat_Recordings "Overcoat Recordings") and features poems from [Edgar Lee Masters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edgar_Lee_Masters "Edgar Lee Masters")'s _[Spoon River Anthology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spoon_River_Anthology "Spoon River Anthology")_, which Buckner set to music. Also in 2000, his self-released, self-titled album (recorded in 1996) was issued by Overcoat, his final release for the label. He returned in 2002, first with the EP _Impasse-ette_, then with a full-length entitled _[Impasse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Impasse_(album) "Impasse (album)")_ one month later. In 2004, an edited version of Buckner's song "Ariel Ramirez", from the album _[Since](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Since_(album) "Since (album)")_ (1998), was featured in a television ad for the [Volkswagen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volkswagen "Volkswagen") [Touareg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Touareg "Touareg"). The song, in its original form, was also featured in the 2008 horror film [_The Strangers_](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Strangers_(2008_film) "The Strangers (2008 film)") _._[\[4\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_Buckner_(musician)#cite_note-4)

He is currently aligned with the [North Carolina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Carolina "North Carolina")–based indie label [Merge Records](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Merge_Records "Merge Records"), who released 2004's _[Dents and Shells](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dents_and_Shells "Dents and Shells")_ and 2006's _Meadow_. Between the two, he released an album with [Jon Langford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jon_Langford "Jon Langford") called _Sir Dark Invader vs. The Fanglord_ (2005, originally recorded in [Sally Timms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sally_Timms "Sally Timms")'s house in 2002) on Buried Treasure Records. Merge Records released Buckner's album _[Our Blood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Our_Blood "Our Blood")_ on August 2, 2011.[\[5\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_Buckner_(musician)#cite_note-5) The album was licensed in Europe and Oceania to Decor records.[\[6\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_Buckner_(musician)#cite_note-6)

Buckner's album _[Surrounded](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Surrounded_(Richard_Buckner_album) "Surrounded (Richard Buckner album)")_ was released in 2013 by Merge Records.[\[7\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_Buckner_(musician)#cite_note-7)

Buckner has been cited as an inspiration by [Justin Vernon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Justin_Vernon "Justin Vernon") of [Bon Iver](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bon_Iver "Bon Iver").[\[8\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_Buckner_(musician)#cite_note-8) In an interview, Vernon stated that _[22, A Million](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/22,_A_Million "22, A Million")_ was dedicated to Buckner and [Bernice Johnson Reagon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bernice_Johnson_Reagon "Bernice Johnson Reagon").[\[9\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_Buckner_(musician)#cite_note-9)

In December 2020, Buckner released his first book, _Cuttings from the Tangle_, with [Black Sparrow Press](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_Sparrow_Press "Black Sparrow Press"). NPR's _Morning Edition_ said of the book: “During a career spent crisscrossing the country, Buckner has seen plenty. In all those hotels between here and there, at those bars and truck stops and lounges, he would sit and listen . . . Buckner puts that power of observation to good use.”[\[10\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_Buckner_(musician)#cite_note-10) Writing for _Literary Hub_, author Nina MacLaughlin said: “ _Cuttings from the Tangle_ is not the work of a road-weary musician dabbling in another form. This book confirms a truth hinted at all these years in the language of his lyrics: Buckner is a writer.”[\[11\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_Buckner_(musician)#cite_note-11)
