**Souled American** is an American [alternative country](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alternative_country "Alternative country") band from [Chicago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chicago "Chicago") that was active mostly in the late 1980s and early 1990s.[\[1\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Souled_American#cite_note-1) The band started in 1986 with a quartet of musicians from Illinois. The initial lineup consisted of rhythm guitar/singer Chris Grigoroff, bassist/singer Joe Adducci, lead guitarist [Scott Tuma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scott_Tuma "Scott Tuma"), and drummer Jamey Barnard.[\[2\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Souled_American#cite_note-2) The band made six records between 1988 and 1995; Barnard left the group during the making of the fourth record [Sonny](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sonny_(album) "Sonny (album)"), while Tuma departed during the making of the sixth record, _Notes Campfire_. Since then, Souled American has survived as a duo featuring the original songwriters Adducci and Grigoroff, aided and accompanied by a variety of other musicians.

In 2023, [Grammy Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grammy_Awards "Grammy Awards")-winning musician [Jeff Tweedy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jeff_Tweedy "Jeff Tweedy") wrote about Souled American in his book, _World Within a Song_. Though Souled American, he explained, were contemporaries of his first band [Uncle Tupelo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uncle_Tupelo "Uncle Tupelo"), they never achieved widespread popularity; instead, as [Uncle Tupelo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uncle_Tupelo "Uncle Tupelo") shot to stardom in the burgeoning [alternative country](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alternative_country "Alternative country") movement of the early 1990s, Souled American fell from sight. Consequently, Tweedy wrote, this Souled American song he was celebrating, "was likely the hardest track to find of any" he wrote about.[\[3\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Souled_American#cite_note-3)

Nor was this Tweedy's first effort to bring attention to this forgotten band. In 2016, in a video for [Pitchfork Magazine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pitchfork_Magazine "Pitchfork Magazine") entitled, "The One Song I Wish I'd Written," Tweedy nominated Souled American's "Before Tonight."[\[4\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Souled_American#cite_note-4)

In 2025, to rectify this imbalance, [Omnivore Recordings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Omnivore_Recordings "Omnivore Recordings") issued _Rise Above It_, a compilation of twenty Souled American songs. In a highly-starred review published April 25, 2025, Jon Dale of [Uncut](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uncut_(magazine) "Uncut (magazine)") pointed out how the band used [country music](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Country_music "Country music") as "the shell, of sorts, for a quixotic combination that pulled from all kinds of genres – the heartbreak and melodicism of [country](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Country_music "Country music") and [folk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Folk_music "Folk music"); the groove of [reggae](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reggae "Reggae") and [dub](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dub_music "Dub music"); and touches of [R&B](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/R&B "R&B") in some of the singing and playing, and in their choice of covers."[\[5\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Souled_American#cite_note-5)

This followed an earlier attempt by [tUMULt Records](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tumult_Records "Tumult Records") to revive interest through the 1999 reissue of their first four records in a remastered CD box set _Framed_, which inspired reviews and features in [Rolling Stone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rolling_Stone "Rolling Stone"), [Pitchfork](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pitchfork_(website) "Pitchfork (website)"), and others.[\[6\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Souled_American#cite_note-6)

Novelist/songwriter [John Darnielle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Darnielle "John Darnielle") penned an essay ("How Souled American's Flubber Changed My Life")[\[7\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Souled_American#cite_note-7) which underscored what songwriter/musician (Camper Van Beethoven/Cracker) [David Lowery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Lowery_(musician) "David Lowery (musician)") told a radio DJ in 1990 that "...in the way [The Pixies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Pixies "The Pixies") sorta summed up all the sort of musical undercurrents in underground rock over the last ten years, Souled American did it for a different set of musical undercurrents (namely the American [folk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Folk_music "Folk music") roots tradition). They took it and stood it on its head, in maybe the way [Beefheart](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Captain_Beefheart "Captain Beefheart") did, but in a more pop/folky way..."[\[8\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Souled_American#cite_note-8)

## History

Mark Guarino, in his book _Country & Midwestern_, describes Chicago's developing music scene of the late 1980s that helped bring attention to this new style of roots music. "Souled American was likely playing Phyllis's Musical Inn, a corner dive on Division...\[they were\] drawn to the fatalism and soul of the early twentieth century string-band music."[\[9\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Souled_American#cite_note-9) In 1988, they were signed by the newly-formed Rough Trade US, a division of [Rough Trade Records](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rough_Trade_Records "Rough Trade Records"), which released three Souled American records over the course of the next eighteen months: _[Fe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fe_(Souled_American_album) "Fe (Souled American album)")_ (1988), _[Flubber](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flubber_(album) "Flubber (album)")_ (1989), and _[Around the Horn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Around_the_Horn_(album) "Around the Horn (album)")_ (1990). Rough Trade US folded soon after.

In 1992, Souled American recorded an album of American country covers called [Sonny](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sonny_(album) "Sonny (album)") that was released only in Europe by [Rough Trade Records](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rough_Trade_Records "Rough Trade Records").

The band re-emerged in 1994 with _Frozen_ and 1996's _Notes Campfire_, both released on the small German label Moll Tonträger.

In 1997, guerrilla artist [Camden Joy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Camden_Joy "Camden Joy") created a poster project with his best friend Mark Lerner which they called, "Fifty Posters About Souled American," consisting of typewritten comments and stories by various artists and musicians; Joy then plastered the posters around New York City.[\[10\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Souled_American#cite_note-10)

In the 30 years following their 1996 release of _Notes Campfire_, the band released three songs. One was a cover of [Kris Kristofferson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kris_Kristofferson "Kris Kristofferson")'s "Please Don't Tell Me How the Story Ends," on a 2002 tribute album. The second was "Ringside Suite," included on a 2006 compilation CD in issue No.4 of [_Yeti_](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yeti_(magazine) "Yeti (magazine)") magazine. The last was "Sorry State," released in 2024 via the group's Bandcamp page. [\[11\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Souled_American#cite_note-11)

In early 2026, the band announced that _Sanctions_, their first album-length release of new material in 30 years, would be released April 17, 2026, on Jealous Butcher Records, with band members Joe Adducci and Chris Grigoroff writing, performing, and producing.[\[12\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Souled_American#cite_note-12) The band also announced a set of US tour dates for spring and summer of 2026.[\[13\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Souled_American#cite_note-13)
