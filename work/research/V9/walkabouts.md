**The Walkabouts** were an [American](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States "United States") [rock band](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rock_band "Rock band") formed in [Seattle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seattle "Seattle"), [Washington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Washington_(state) "Washington (state)") in 1984. The core members were vocalist Carla Torgerson and vocalist and songwriter Chris Eckman. Although the rest of the lineup changed occasionally, for most of the time the other members were Michael Wells, Glenn Slater and Terri Moeller.[\[1\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Walkabouts#cite_note-Larkin80-1)

The band drew inspiration from [folk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Folk_music "Folk music") and [country music](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Country_music "Country music"), particularly [Townes Van Zandt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Townes_Van_Zandt "Townes Van Zandt"), [Neil Young](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neil_Young "Neil Young") and [Johnny Cash](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johnny_Cash "Johnny Cash"), but also from other types of artists and musical styles such as [Scott Walker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scott_Walker_(singer) "Scott Walker (singer)"), [Leonard Cohen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leonard_Cohen "Leonard Cohen"), French [chanson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chanson "Chanson") and [Jacques Brel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jacques_Brel "Jacques Brel"). Their sound was typically rich, with string arrangements and keyboards in addition to the standard rock instruments.

The Walkabouts achieved commercial success and a strong fanbase in [Europe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Europe "Europe"), where they had done promotion and extensive touring starting from the early 1990s. They occasionally even made it high on the [record charts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Record_chart "Record chart") in countries such as [Greece](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greece "Greece") and [Norway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norway "Norway").\[ _[citation needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed "Wikipedia:Citation needed")_\]

## History

Carla Torgerson and Chris Eckman met and began playing music together in 1983 while attending [Whitman College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Whitman_College "Whitman College") in [Walla Walla, Washington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walla_Walla,_Washington "Walla Walla, Washington"). In 1984, they moved to Seattle, and the band was born when they joined forces with Chris' two younger brothers, drummer Grant and bassist Curt. The Eckman brothers had been playing in various [punk rock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Punk_rock "Punk rock") and [pop](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pop_music "Pop music") groups during their college years, and Carla came from a folk and street singing background. The band took their name from Nicolas Roeg's cult film, _[Walkabout](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walkabout_(film) "Walkabout (film)")_.[\[2\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Walkabouts#cite_note-bandsite-2)

[![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/0/0b/The_Walkabouts_-_The_Rocket_%281986%29.jpg/500px-The_Walkabouts_-_The_Rocket_%281986%29.jpg)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:The_Walkabouts_-_The_Rocket_(1986).jpg) The Walkabouts in 1986

They released their first EP, _22 Disasters_, in early 1985.[\[1\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Walkabouts#cite_note-Larkin80-1) Curt Eckman then left the band, to be replaced by Michael Wells. Their second EP, _Linda Evans/Cyclone_, was released in 1987. The following year they released their first album, _See Beautiful Rattlesnake Gardens_, on the [PopLlama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PopLlama_Records "PopLlama Records") label, before being signed by [Sub Pop](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sub_Pop "Sub Pop") as that label's first non-grunge band.[\[1\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Walkabouts#cite_note-Larkin80-1) Adding keyboard player and multi-instrumentalist Glenn Slater, they released three albums on Sub Pop over the next few years - _Cataract_ (1989), _Rag & Bone_ (1990), and _[Scavenger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scavenger_(Walkabouts_album) "Scavenger (Walkabouts album)")_ (1991).[\[1\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Walkabouts#cite_note-Larkin80-1) They also toured extensively, particularly in Europe. In 1992 they left Sub Pop Seattle, but remained with Sub Pop Europe who issued three more albums, _[New West Motel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_West_Motel "New West Motel")_ (1993), _[Satisfied Mind](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Satisfied_Mind "Satisfied Mind")_ (1993), and _[Setting the Woods on Fire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Setting_the_Woods_on_Fire "Setting the Woods on Fire")_ (1994).[\[1\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Walkabouts#cite_note-Larkin80-1) Drummer Terri Moeller replaced Grant Eckman in 1992, when he left to care for his newborn son, Dakota Eckman.[\[3\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Walkabouts#cite_note-fansite-3)

_[Satisfied Mind](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Satisfied_Mind "Satisfied Mind")_ was their first of several albums largely or wholly comprising songs originally written and recorded by an eclectic variety of other artists, including [Nick Cave](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nick_Cave "Nick Cave"), [Charlie Rich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charlie_Rich "Charlie Rich"), [Johnny Rivers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johnny_Rivers "Johnny Rivers"), [Patti Smith](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Patti_Smith "Patti Smith"), [Mary Margaret O'Hara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mary_Margaret_O'Hara "Mary Margaret O'Hara"), and [Gene Clark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gene_Clark "Gene Clark").[\[1\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Walkabouts#cite_note-Larkin80-1) Their 1996 collection of unreleased songs, _Death Valley Days_, also included songs by [Neil Young](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neil_Young "Neil Young"), [Nick Drake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nick_Drake "Nick Drake") and [Bob Dylan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bob_Dylan "Bob Dylan"), and in 2000 they issued _Train Leaves at Eight_, which broadened the approach further by including songs by European artists including [Mikis Theodorakis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mikis_Theodorakis "Mikis Theodorakis"), [Goran Bregović](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Goran_Bregovi%C4%87 "Goran Bregović"), [Jacques Brel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jacques_Brel "Jacques Brel") and [Neu!](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neu! "Neu!").[\[3\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Walkabouts#cite_note-fansite-3)

In 1995, the band signed with [Virgin Records](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Virgin_Records "Virgin Records") in Germany and released _[Devil's Road](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Devil's_Road "Devil's Road")_ (1996) - recorded in part with the [Warsaw Philharmonic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warsaw_Philharmonic "Warsaw Philharmonic") \- and _Nighttown_ (1997), leading to new levels of success in Europe.[\[1\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Walkabouts#cite_note-Larkin80-1) The video for "The Light Will Stay On", the lead single from _Devil's Road_ was in heavy rotation on [MTV Europe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MTV_Europe "MTV Europe").\[ _[citation needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed "Wikipedia:Citation needed")_\] Starting in 1996, Michael Wells left the band for several years, rejoining in 2003. He was replaced in quick succession by bassists [John Baker Saunders](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Baker_Saunders "John Baker Saunders"), [Fred Chalenor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fred_Chalenor "Fred Chalenor") and Joe Skyward. In 1999, they moved to the German record label, [Glitterhouse Records](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glitterhouse_Records "Glitterhouse Records") (the successor to Sub Pop Europe), and released _Trail of Stars_ (1999), _Train Leaves at Eight_ (2000), _Ended Up a Stranger_ (2001),[\[1\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Walkabouts#cite_note-Larkin80-1) _Slow Days with Nina_ (2003) - an EP tribute to [Nina Simone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nina_Simone "Nina Simone") \- and _[Acetylene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Acetylene_(album) "Acetylene (album)")_ (2005). During that period of time, Terri Moeller took a break from the band in 2000, and was replaced by [Brian Young](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brian_Young_(drummer) "Brian Young (drummer)") until she returned in 2001.

In 2010, guitarist Paul Austin joined the band and work began on a new album entitled _Travels in the Dustland,_ released the following year to strong reviews that also hailed the band's return to activity after a six year absence. During the subsequent European tour, the live album _Berlin_ was recorded and released.

In 2015, Eckman confirmed in an interview with _[Uncut](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uncut_(magazine) "Uncut (magazine)")_ magazine, and in a subsequent Facebook post, that The Walkabouts had disbanded.[\[4\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Walkabouts#cite_note-fb-4) Eckman continues to write and record with a number of projects, as well as serve as label manager for the global sounds imprint Glitterbeat.

## Side projects

Eckman and Torgerson have released albums as side projects under the name "Chris & Carla" and as solo artists.[\[1\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Walkabouts#cite_note-Larkin80-1)

Carla Torgerson has worked with [Greek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greeks "Greeks") musician Akis Boyatzis and his band Sigmatropic on their album _16 [Haiku](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haiku "Haiku") & Other Stories_ (2001). Later Torgerson released a solo album _Saint Stranger_ (2004) with help of Akis Boyatzis.

Between 1996 and 2003, Chris Eckman worked with Norwegian band [Midnight Choir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Midnight_Choir "Midnight Choir") and teamed with Al DeLoner of Midnight Choir in [electronica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electronica "Electronica")-project called "Höst" for an album _The Damage Suite_ (2001). Also Eckman has worked with [Willard Grant Conspiracy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Willard_Grant_Conspiracy "Willard Grant Conspiracy") on the albums _[Regard the End](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regard_the_End "Regard the End")_ (2003) and _[Let It Roll](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Let_It_Roll_(Willard_Grant_Conspiracy_album) "Let It Roll (Willard Grant Conspiracy album)")_, as well as with [The Bambi Molesters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Bambi_Molesters "The Bambi Molesters"). Eckman is also a member of the trio Dirtmusic along with [Chris Brokaw](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chris_Brokaw "Chris Brokaw") and [Hugo Race](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hugo_Race "Hugo Race") and he has worked with the band [Tamikrest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tamikrest "Tamikrest") from [Mali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mali "Mali"). These two acts toured Europe as a double bill in the spring of 2010. Eckman has worked with [Tosca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tosca_(band) "Tosca (band)") on projects including the song "John Lee Huber", and with [Rupert Huber](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rupert_Huber_(composer) "Rupert Huber (composer)") of Tosca on the album _L/O/N/G_.

Terri Moeller has formed The Beltanes with Eric Alton, formerly of [X-15](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/X-15_(band) "X-15 (band)"), releasing one cassette EP, entitled _Love Punks_ (1990), and [The Transmissionary Six](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Transmissionary_Six "The Transmissionary Six") with Paul Austin, formerly of [Willard Grant Conspiracy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Willard_Grant_Conspiracy "Willard Grant Conspiracy"). She released a solo CD under the pseudonym Terri Tarantula in 2010.
