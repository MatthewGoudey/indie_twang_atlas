**The Waco Brothers** are an American [alternative country](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alternative_country "Alternative country"), or [country-punk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Country-punk "Country-punk") rock, band based in [Chicago, Illinois](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chicago,_Illinois "Chicago, Illinois"), United States.

## History

The Waco Brothers was formed by [Jon Langford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jon_Langford "Jon Langford") of [mekons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mekons "Mekons") and Dean Schlabowske of [Wreck (band)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wreck_(band) "Wreck (band)"). The group grew out of Langford's wish to play more country-influenced music as the Mekons concentrated more on a [punk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Punk_rock "Punk rock") sound. They were originally put together simply for local Chicago shows, but the success of their [Bloodshot Records](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bloodshot_Records "Bloodshot Records") albums allowed them to tour the US occasionally. Some of the members also participated in Langford's [Pine Valley Cosmonauts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pine_Valley_Cosmonauts "Pine Valley Cosmonauts") project.

The band recorded the first of its studio albums in 1995. Their album, _Waco Express: Live & Kickin' at Schuba's Tavern_ is a concert recording which [Ken Tucker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ken_Tucker "Ken Tucker"), the pop music critic for [NPR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NPR "NPR")'s _[Fresh Air](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fresh_Air "Fresh Air")_ and editor-at-large at _[Entertainment Weekly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Entertainment_Weekly "Entertainment Weekly")_, described as "country as it should be written and played, with a long memory for roadhouse honky-tonks rather than TV-ready music videos."[\[1\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Waco_Brothers#cite_note-1) Author and music critic [Sarah Vowell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sarah_Vowell "Sarah Vowell") told the _[Chicagoist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chicagoist "Chicagoist")_, "I’ve never been able to find a live band in New York as consistently thrilling and funny and fun as the Waco Brothers."[\[2\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Waco_Brothers#cite_note-2) Until the COVID-19 epidemic, the Waco Brothers made a point of performing every year in [Austin, Texas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austin,_Texas "Austin, Texas"), during the [South by Southwest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_by_Southwest "South by Southwest") (SXSW) festival.[\[3\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Waco_Brothers#cite_note-LoneStarMusic2016_JLangford-3) In 2020, their annual December performance at [Schubas Tavern](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Schubas_Tavern?action=edit&redlink=1 "Schubas Tavern") in Chicago had to be livestreamed without the usual live audience.\[ _[citation needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed "Wikipedia:Citation needed")_\]

In 2012, the Waco Brothers collaborated with [Nashville](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nashville "Nashville") mainstay [Paul Burch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_Burch "Paul Burch") to produce _Great Chicago Fire_. The _[Chicago Tribune](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chicago_Tribune "Chicago Tribune")_ said of the album, "If the [Rolling Stones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rolling_Stones "Rolling Stones") were still making great records, this would be it."[\[4\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Waco_Brothers#cite_note-4)[\[5\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Waco_Brothers#cite_note-GreatChicagoFire_CTribune-5)

## Members

For their first albums, the Waco Brothers included Dean Schlabowske (guitar/vocal), Tracey Dear (mandolin/vocal), [Alan Doughty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alan_Doughty "Alan Doughty") (bass/vocal), [Mark Durante](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mark_Durante "Mark Durante") (pedal steel guitar), and Mekons drummer [Steve Goulding](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steve_Goulding "Steve Goulding")). Joe Camarillo (drums) often filled in for Goulding, who had moved to New York City.[\[6\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Waco_Brothers#cite_note-Nobby_WB&UncleDave-6) By 2015, Camarillo had fully replaced Goulding, and Durante had left. Camarillo died of complications of congenital cardiac disease in January, 2021, after surviving [COVID-19](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/COVID-19 "COVID-19").[\[7\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Waco_Brothers#cite_note-JCamarillo-death_CReader-7)[\[8\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Waco_Brothers#cite_note-JCamarillo-death_CTribune-8)[\[9\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Waco_Brothers#cite_note-JCamarillo-memorialJoeShow_CReader-9) Dan Massey, who had previously substituted on drums whenever Camarillo had been unavailable, became the Waco Brothers permanent new drummer, starting with the band's 2021 concerts. Jean Cook, who had played violin intermittently with the band for years, officially joined the band in 2020.[\[10\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Waco_Brothers#cite_note-MenThatGodForgot_bandcamp2023-10)[\[11\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Waco_Brothers#cite_note-CedarLandfordQA-11) AS of 2024 and since approximately 2017, [John Szymanski](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Szymanski?action=edit&redlink=1 "John Szymanski") has been a regular substitute Waco Brother on guitar or bass, plus vocals, whenever one of the regular members was unavailable for a particular performance;\[ _[citation needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed "Wikipedia:Citation needed")_\] Szymanski has been a regular member of several of Jon Langford's other musical projects.
