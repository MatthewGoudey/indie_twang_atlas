**Southern Culture on the Skids**, also sometimes known as **SCOTS**, is an American [rock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rock_music "Rock music") band from [Chapel Hill, North Carolina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chapel_Hill,_North_Carolina "Chapel Hill, North Carolina").

The band released their debut EP _Voodoo Beach Party_ in 1984, followed by their self-titled debut album the following year. After these early releases, the band's line-up shifted due to a perceived lack of direction, and the band re-emerged with a new sound that encompassed a multitude of genres.

Guitarist and founding member Rick Miller grew up dividing his time between his father's home and business in [Henderson, North Carolina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henderson,_North_Carolina "Henderson, North Carolina") and his mother's home in southern California. Miller completed an art degree at the [University of North Carolina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_North_Carolina "University of North Carolina") in [Chapel Hill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chapel_Hill,_North_Carolina "Chapel Hill, North Carolina"). The first incarnation of Southern Culture on the Skids formed in 1983 and featured Miller on guitar, with Stan Lewis (vocals), Leslie Land (bass) and Chip Shelby (drums).[\[1\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_Culture_on_the_Skids#cite_note-Huey-1) Of the band's name, Miller later said "(We wanted) some kind of name that would get us some attention, ya know? We were listening to the UNC radio (station) there and they were playing an [REM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/R.E.M. "R.E.M.") song. I like REM fine, but at the end of it, the DJ says, 'Ya that was REM, the sound of the new South.' I looked at my roommate and we said, 'Gawd, if that's the sound of the 'new South' I preferred it when it was on the skids.' That's how we got the name."[\[2\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_Culture_on_the_Skids#cite_note-Jumpin-2) This line-up released the EP _Voodoo Beach Party_, followed later in 1985 by a full-length album entitled _Southern Culture on the Skids_ on local indie label Lloyd Street.[\[1\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_Culture_on_the_Skids#cite_note-Huey-1)

After these releases, the band felt that they lacked direction.[\[3\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_Culture_on_the_Skids#cite_note-VirginianPilot-3) This led to Lewis leaving in 1987, soon followed by Land. Miller took over on lead vocals, with Mary Huff joining on bass and vocals. Huff suggested Dave Hartman as new drummer, whom she had played with in another band previously. Both Huff and Hartman were from [Roanoke, Virginia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roanoke,_Virginia "Roanoke, Virginia"). The band explored different styles before deciding on a " [swamp rock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swamp_rock "Swamp rock") thing".[\[3\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_Culture_on_the_Skids#cite_note-VirginianPilot-3) Eventually the three-piece SCOTS released _Too Much Pork for Just One Fork_ on Moist Records in 1991, with SCOTS' evolving signature sound, and humorous lyrical tales concerning southern US culture, particularly sex and food ('Eight Piece Box'). The rockier, rawer-sounding album _For Lovers Only_ followed in 1992 on [Safe House Records](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Safe_House_Records "Safe House Records"), featuring Huff on lead vocals for 'Daddy Was a Preacher But Mama Was a Go-Go Girl'. SCOTS released two EPS in 1993, the half-studio half-live _Peckin' Party_ (Feedbag Records) and _Girlfight_ on [Sympathy for the Record Industry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sympathy_for_the_Record_Industry "Sympathy for the Record Industry"). 1994 saw the release of the more country-flavoured album _Ditch Diggin'_, again on Safe House, with cleaner production and slightly shorter, tighter songs. The album also featured covers of the [Louvin Brothers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Louvin_Brothers "The Louvin Brothers") ('The Great Atomic Power') and [Link Wray](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Link_Wray "Link Wray") ('Jack the Ripper Pts. 1 & 2') which had been part of live shows.[\[2\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_Culture_on_the_Skids#cite_note-Jumpin-2) In 1994 they began convening and headlining an annual roots-rock music festival entitled _Sleazefest_, held late summer in Chapel Hill.[\[4\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_Culture_on_the_Skids#cite_note-Encyclo-4)

SCOTS' increasing popularity created major label interest and they signed to [Geffen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geffen_Records "Geffen Records") subsidiary [DGC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DGC_Records "DGC Records") in 1995. The following year saw the first recorded version of 'Camel Walk' appear, on a Mexican wrestling-themed EP entitled _Santo Swings!_ SCOTS' first DGC album, _[Dirt Track Date](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dirt_Track_Date "Dirt Track Date")_ featured re-recorded versions of popular songs from previous releases ('Eight Piece Box'; 'Camel Walk'; 'Voodoo Cadillac'; 'Firefly') amongst new material, including 'Soul City'. The album was a critical and commercial success, selling over a quarter-million copies.[\[1\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_Culture_on_the_Skids#cite_note-Huey-1) 'Camel Walk' and 'Red Beans 'n' Reverb' featured on the soundtrack to the 1996 movie _[Flirting with Disaster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flirting_with_Disaster_(film) "Flirting with Disaster (film)")_, while the band appeared in the 1997 movie _[I Know What You Did Last Summer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/I_Know_What_You_Did_Last_Summer "I Know What You Did Last Summer")_, playing 'My Baby's Got The Strangest Ways' in the opening beach party scene. SCOTS added keyboard player Chris 'Cousin Crispy' Bess to the line-up for the 1997 album _[Plastic Seat Sweat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plastic_Seat_Sweat?action=edit&redlink=1 "Plastic Seat Sweat (page does not exist)")_, creating a fuller, richer sound. _Plastic Seat Sweat_ was also well-reviewed but Geffen were disappointed by sales, and they parted ways with SCOTS. Miller later commented that the record recouped its costs, but Geffen wanted 'hits' and offered contract renewal conditional on production of a radio-friendly song within the next 3 months, plus touring with [ska-punk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ska_punk "Ska punk") bands popular at the time. SCOTS declined.[\[4\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_Culture_on_the_Skids#cite_note-Encyclo-4)

Without a label, SCOTS toured steadily for a few years. _Zombified_ (1998) was a limited, independent release themed around horror movies from drive-ins and late-night TV. SCOTS' next widely released album appeared in 2000, _Liquored Up and Lacquered Down_, on [TVT Records](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TVT_Records "TVT Records"). The multiple alcohol-themed songs reflected the band's previous two years, said Miller.[\[4\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_Culture_on_the_Skids#cite_note-Encyclo-4) The deal with TVT was short-lived and it took another four years for the next original SCOTS record to appear. 2002 saw the release of SCOTS’ first live album, at least an ‘official’ release amongst several bootlegs. _Live at El Sol_, on their own Kudzu Records label, featured 16 tracks recorded at shows in [Madrid, Spain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Madrid "Madrid") in 1997. SCOTS next album, a 2004 release on North Carolina's [Yep Roc](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yep_Roc_Records "Yep Roc Records") Records, was entitled _[Mojo Box](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mojo_Box "Mojo Box")_ and was recorded and produced by Miller at the Kudzu Ranch. _Mojo Box_ featured typical songs about trailer parks (‘Doublewide’), muscle cars (‘’69 El Camino’) and Miller's love of surf rock (‘The Wet Spot’) plus versions of [Jody Reynolds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jody_Reynolds "Jody Reynolds")’ rockabilly classic ‘Fire of Love’ (featuring Bess on keyboards) and [The Creations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Creation_(band) "The Creation (band)")’ ‘Biff Bang Pow’. A second live release followed in 2006, _Doublewide and Live_, plus a deluxe edition with an extra three songs. The recording was made in front of an enthusiastic crowd at [Local 506](https://local506.com/) in Chapel Hill. The band then surprised a little with 2007's _Countrypolitan Favourites_, an album of diverse cover songs which mixed country standards including ‘ [Oh Lonesome Me](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oh_Lonesome_Me "Oh Lonesome Me")’ and ‘ [Tobacco Road](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tobacco_Road_(song) "Tobacco Road (song)")’ with versions of tunes by [T-Rex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/T._Rex_(band) "T. Rex (band)"), [The Kinks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Kinks "The Kinks") and [The Byrds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Byrds "The Byrds").

Album _The Kudzu Ranch_, released in 2010 on Kudzu Records (as would all future albums), represented the first original recorded material from SCOTS in six years. But it allayed any thought the band had lost their mojo with tracks like ‘Pig Pickin’’, ‘Bone Dry Dirt’ and ‘My Neighbor Burns Trash’ alongside an imaginative [Nirvana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nirvana_(band) "Nirvana (band)")/ [Pink Floyd](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pink_Floyd "Pink Floyd") mashup (‘Come As You Are/’Lucifer Sam’). The previous albums _Too Much Pork…_ and _Zombified_ were re-released in 2011, the latter with 5 bonus tracks, as new, younger fans searched for SCOTS’ earlier recorded material, much of which had either been deleted by now-defunct record labels or lost in the [Universal fire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2008_Universal_fire "2008 Universal fire"). Perhaps to this end, SCOTS re-recorded the songs from 1994 album _Ditch Diggin’_ in 2013, released as _Dig This! (Ditch Diggin’ Vol. 2)_, with modest variations on the original songs and without the two cover versions. Miller claimed the recording was inspired by having to dig out the Kudzu Ranch's blocked drains.[\[5\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_Culture_on_the_Skids#cite_note-Ditch-5)

In their earlier days, SCOTS occasionally performed as their own opening act under the name The Pinecones, playing songs inspired by 1960s country-rock and psychedelia typical of artists like [Gram Parsons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gram_Parsons "Gram Parsons"), The Byrds, [The Seeds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Seeds "The Seeds") and [The Chocolate Watchband](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Chocolate_Watchband "The Chocolate Watchband") (amongst others).[\[6\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_Culture_on_the_Skids#cite_note-Cones-6)[\[7\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_Culture_on_the_Skids#cite_note-Cobb-7) This led to SCOTS’ 2016 album _The Electric Pinecones_, which mixed original songs in this style (‘Grey Skies’, ‘I Aint Gonna Hang Around’) together with more typical SCOTS songs (‘Swamp Fox’, ‘Rice and Beans’). More than anything, the record exemplified Miller, Huff and Hartman's musicianship and versatility. Following on from the _Dig This_ album, and the demise of DGC Records, SCOTS released _Bootlegger’s Choice_ in 2018, featuring new recordings of 15 songs from DGC albums _Dirt Track Date_ and _Plastic Seat Sweat_, plus a remastered version of the original ‘Camel Walk’. Continuing in this vein, 2020 saw a re-release of the very rare 2003 _Kudzu Records Presents_, a collection of six songs focussed on [NOLA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Orleans "New Orleans") artists including [Jessie Hill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jessie_Hill "Jessie Hill") and [Smiley Lewis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smiley_Lewis "Smiley Lewis"). The 2020 version featured four extra tracks including songs by [Doug Sahm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doug_Sahm "Doug Sahm") and [Slim Whitman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slim_Whitman "Slim Whitman"), plus two original instrumentals.

The advent of the [COVID pandemic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/COVID-19_pandemic "COVID-19 pandemic") in 2019/20 halted SCOTS touring and performing. The band managed to write and record some songs in a makeshift set-up in Miller's living room, these forming the basis of 2021's _At Home with Southern Culture on the Skids_ album. Stripped-back arrangements of necessarily more intimate songs directly addressed the isolation and frustration caused by the pandemic (‘Call Me’) while ‘Run Baby Run’ was a garage rock stomper. SCOTS eventually returned to the road in late 2021. The 2000 album _Liquored Up and Lacquered Down_ was re-released on Kudzu Records in 2023, with three bonus tracks plus a cover of Doug Sahm's 'Nitty Gritty'.

## Performance style

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Southern_Culture_on_the_Skids&action=edit&section=2 "Edit section: Performance style")\]

_[AllMusic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AllMusic "AllMusic")_ said that Southern Culture on the Skids started as a straightforward [roots rock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roots_rock "Roots rock") group before morphing into "a raucous, tongue-in-cheek party band obsessed with [sex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_sexual_activity "Human sexual activity") and [fried chicken](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fried_chicken "Fried chicken") in the early '90s".[\[1\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_Culture_on_the_Skids#cite_note-Huey-1) _Stomp and Stammer_ described the band as " _[Green Acres](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Green_Acres "Green Acres")_ meets [Green Onions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Green_Onions "Green Onions")".[\[8\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_Culture_on_the_Skids#cite_note-Clark-8) According to the _[Lexington Herald Leader](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lexington_Herald_Leader "Lexington Herald Leader")_, "The trio has so persistently masked its obviously schooled [swamp-rock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swamp_rock "Swamp rock") gumbo sound with enough onstage [trailer-park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trailer_park "Trailer park") shtick to make many dismiss — or embrace, depending on your entertainment vantage point — the group as a novelty act. What is really going on is a band with roots-rock chops to spare zeroing on material and performance perspectives that keep its music fun."[\[9\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_Culture_on_the_Skids#cite_note-9) _Charleston Grit_ says that the band makes " [white trash](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/White_trash "White trash") fashion and Southern twang an art form."[\[10\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_Culture_on_the_Skids#cite_note-CharlestonGrit-10) The band is known for their live shows, which often include throwing fried chicken and [banana pudding](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Banana_pudding "Banana pudding") into the audience, and audience members invited to dance and eat onstage.[\[10\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_Culture_on_the_Skids#cite_note-CharlestonGrit-10)

The _Shepherd Express_ said that the band's music was classified under "genre labels a country mile long", including [rockabilly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rockabilly "Rockabilly"), [R&B](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rhythm_and_blues "Rhythm and blues") and [surf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Surf_music "Surf music").[\[11\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_Culture_on_the_Skids#cite_note-ShepherdExpress-11) _WNRN_ described the band's style as " [Dixie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dixie "Dixie")-fried [Southern rock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_rock "Southern rock")".[\[12\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_Culture_on_the_Skids#cite_note-WNRN-12) _The Newtown Bee_ described their music as "surfin' southern fried [psychobilly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Psychobilly "Psychobilly")".[\[13\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_Culture_on_the_Skids#cite_note-NewtownBee-13) _Elmore_ magazine wrote that the band's musical style encompasses "an eclectic range of [Americana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Americana_(music) "Americana (music)") including rockabilly, surf rock, [country](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Country_music "Country music") and R&B, with a [punk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Punk_rock "Punk rock") edge and heaps of humor".[\[7\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_Culture_on_the_Skids#cite_note-Cobb-7) _AllMusic_ described the band's sound as a "wild, careening brand of [rock & roll](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rock_and_roll "Rock and roll") \[...\] a quintessentially Southern-fried amalgam of rockabilly, boogie, country, [blues](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blues "Blues"), [swamp pop](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swamp_pop "Swamp pop"), and vintage R&B, plus a liberal dose of California surf guitar, a hint of punk attitude, and the occasional mariachi horns".[\[1\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_Culture_on_the_Skids#cite_note-Huey-1) _Stomp and Stammer_ described the band's music as a mix of country, [garage rock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garage_rock "Garage rock"), surf, rockabilly and [soul](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soul_music "Soul music").[\[8\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_Culture_on_the_Skids#cite_note-Clark-8) According to the _Virginian-Pilot_, the band fuses blues, psychobilly and rock and roll.[\[3\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_Culture_on_the_Skids#cite_note-VirginianPilot-3)

The band's influences include [the Cramps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Cramps "The Cramps"),[\[1\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_Culture_on_the_Skids#cite_note-Huey-1) [International Submarine Band](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Submarine_Band "International Submarine Band"), [Buck Owens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buck_Owens "Buck Owens"), [Merle Haggard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Merle_Haggard "Merle Haggard"), [the Byrds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Byrds "The Byrds"), [the Seeds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Seeds "The Seeds") and [the Chocolate Watchband](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Chocolate_Watchband "The Chocolate Watchband").[\[7\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_Culture_on_the_Skids#cite_note-Cobb-7) Guitarist and vocalist Rick Miller says that the band's music "is a lot like a Southern plate lunch. Every item on the menu has been cooking for a while and has its own flavor. But they all run together when you put 'em on the plate and start to eat."[\[3\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_Culture_on_the_Skids#cite_note-VirginianPilot-3)
