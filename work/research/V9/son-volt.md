**Son Volt** is an American rock band formed in 1994 by [Jay Farrar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jay_Farrar "Jay Farrar") after the breakup of [Uncle Tupelo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uncle_Tupelo "Uncle Tupelo"). The band's current line-up consists of Farrar (vocals, guitar), Andrew DuPlantis (bass guitar), John Horton (guitar), Mark Patterson (drums), and Mark Spencer (keyboard, steel guitar). In addition to playing [alternative rock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alternative_rock "Alternative rock"), the band is considered a staple of the [alternative country](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alternative_country "Alternative country") rock movement of the 1990s. The band's sound also is rooted in [folk rock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Folk_rock "Folk rock") and Americana. The band went on an indefinite hiatus in 2001,[\[1\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Son_Volt#cite_note-1) before reforming in 2004.[\[2\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Son_Volt#cite_note-2)

## History

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Son_Volt&action=edit&section=1 "Edit section: History")\]

### Early years, _Trace_, _Straightaways_, and _Wide Swing Tremolo_

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Son_Volt&action=edit&section=2 "Edit section: Early years, Trace, Straightaways, and Wide Swing Tremolo")\]

The group formed after the [alternative country rock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alternative_country "Alternative country") act [Uncle Tupelo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uncle_Tupelo "Uncle Tupelo") broke up due to tensions between Farrar and bandmate [Jeff Tweedy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jeff_Tweedy "Jeff Tweedy"). After Uncle Tupelo split, Tweedy formed the alternative rock act [Wilco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wilco "Wilco"), while Farrar decided to form another act. While forming Son Volt, Farrar met Jim and Dave Boquist during the final Uncle Tupelo tour and teamed up with former Uncle Tupelo drummer Mike Heidorn to create the band. The group performed and recorded in the Minneapolis area in late 1994 and performed its first concert at the 7th Street Entry in Minneapolis on June 16, 1995. While half of the band was rooted in the Minneapolis area, Farrar and Heidorn lived in the St. Louis area, and the band used both cities as bases for its operations during the first couple of years.

Son Volt's first album, _[Trace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trace_(Son_Volt_album) "Trace (Son Volt album)")_, met with critical acclaim and topped many "best-of" lists in 1995. It was a moderate commercial success; the first track "Windfall" became very popular in the alt-country scene, while the band released "Drown" as a single which charted No. 10 on the mainstream rock charts and No. 25 on the modern rock charts. By 2009, _Trace_ had sold 297,000 copies in the United States.[\[3\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Son_Volt#cite_note-:1-3)

1997's _[Straightaways](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Straightaways "Straightaways")_ followed in the same vein as _Trace_ with even a more acoustic feel, leading some music critics to give negative reviews, but strong and positive reviews came from outlets such as the [Orlando Sentinel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orlando_Sentinel "Orlando Sentinel") and the [Chicago Tribune](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chicago_Tribune "Chicago Tribune"). 1998's _[Wide Swing Tremolo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wide_Swing_Tremolo "Wide Swing Tremolo")_ featured a heavier rock sound and received mostly positive reviews from music outlets. _[Entertainment Weekly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Entertainment_Weekly "Entertainment Weekly")_ wrote that "many of the songs ... return to the power and purity of the band’s brilliant 1995 debut, _Trace_."

### Hiatus and return

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Son_Volt&action=edit&section=3 "Edit section: Hiatus and return")\]

Farrar announced a hiatus from Son Volt after their 1999 tour. Beginning in 2001, Jay Farrar released several solo efforts that postponed further releases from Son Volt. Farrar reformed with the original members of Son Volt to record a song for a tribute album for [Alejandro Escovedo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alejandro_Escovedo "Alejandro Escovedo"). The sessions reportedly went so well that Farrar and the other band members intended to record once again in the autumn of 2004. Just prior to the sessions, however, Farrar and the other band members abruptly ended negotiations.[\[4\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Son_Volt#cite_note-4) Farrar formed a new version of the band with a different line-up and released an album on Transmit Sound/Sony Legacy, _[Okemah and the Melody of Riot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Okemah_and_the_Melody_of_Riot "Okemah and the Melody of Riot")_, a folk-rock album based on protest music that had been influenced by [Woody Guthrie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Woody_Guthrie "Woody Guthrie") and [Bob Dylan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bob_Dylan "Bob Dylan").[\[5\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Son_Volt#cite_note-5) in 2005. That same year also saw the release of _[A Retrospective: 1995-2000](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A_Retrospective:_1995-2000 "A Retrospective: 1995-2000")_, which gathered highlights from this era, along with previously unreleased recordings. 2006 saw the release of a live DVD, _Six String Belief_, which was recorded at The Orange Peel in [Asheville, NC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asheville,_NC "Asheville, NC").

### Band reformed and new music

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Son_Volt&action=edit&section=4 "Edit section: Band reformed and new music")\]

In 2007, the band returned to an alternative rock and alt-country sound and released a studio album called _[The Search](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Search_(Son_Volt_album) "The Search (Son Volt album)")_. The Americana- and folk-influenced album _[American Central Dust](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Central_Dust "American Central Dust")_ followed, released by Rounder Records on July 7, 2009. Their next project was a [Bakersfield Sound](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bakersfield_Sound "Bakersfield Sound")-influenced album aptly named _[Honky Tonk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Honky_Tonk_(album) "Honky Tonk (album)")_, which was released March 5, 2013, also by Rounder Records. A large scale tour followed the release of the album.[\[6\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Son_Volt#cite_note-6) On February 17, 2017, the band released _[Notes of Blue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Notes_of_Blue "Notes of Blue")_ on Farrar's label, Transmit Sound.

### _Union_ and _Electro Melodier_

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Son_Volt&action=edit&section=5 "Edit section: Union and Electro Melodier")\]

The band's ninth studio album, _[Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Union_(Son_Volt_album) "Union (Son Volt album)")_, was released on March 28, 2019 on Farrar's Transmit Sound label and distributed by Thirty Tigers Records.[\[7\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Son_Volt#cite_note-7) The album consisted of songs that were highly critical of the election of US President [Donald Trump](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Donald_Trump "Donald Trump") and his administration. Many of the songs were commentaries on middle-class economics, freedom of the press, and immigration. Son Volt's tenth album, _[Electro Melodier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electro_Melodier "Electro Melodier")_, was released on July 30, 2021. In early summer 2021, guitarist Chris Frame announced that he would be leaving the band to pursue other interests and was replaced by former [The Bottle Rockets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Bottle_Rockets "The Bottle Rockets") guitarist John Horton. The tribute album _[Day of the Doug](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Day_of_the_Doug "Day of the Doug")_ followed in 2023.

## Musical style

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Son_Volt&action=edit&section=6 "Edit section: Musical style")\]

Son Volt's music ranges from quiet [folk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Folk_music "Folk music") ballads reminiscent of _[The Freewheelin' Bob Dylan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Freewheelin'_Bob_Dylan "The Freewheelin' Bob Dylan")_, to heartland rock in the spirit of [Neil Young](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neil_Young "Neil Young") with [Crazy Horse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crazy_Horse_(band) "Crazy Horse (band)"). The band's sound features a heavy alternative rock sound in many places, all while basing their music in mostly an Americana style.[\[8\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Son_Volt#cite_note-8) Reviews refer to the band as [alternative country](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alternative_country "Alternative country") pioneers, a "staple in the alt-country scene" or "a cult favorite", with their music "spanning a few musical niches", but based in [Americana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Americana_(music) "Americana (music)").[\[9\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Son_Volt#cite_note-9)[\[10\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Son_Volt#cite_note-10)[\[11\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Son_Volt#cite_note-11)
