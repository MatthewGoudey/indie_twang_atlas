_**The Lioness**_ is the fourth studio album by [Songs: Ohia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Songs:_Ohia "Songs: Ohia").[\[8\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Lioness_(album)#cite_note-8) It was released by [Secretly Canadian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Secretly_Canadian "Secretly Canadian") on January 17, 2000. A reissue of the album, _**Love & Work: The Lioness Sessions**_, was released on November 23, 2018.[\[9\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Lioness_(album)#cite_note-9)

## Production

The album was recorded by [Andy Miller](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andy_Miller_(record_producer) "Andy Miller (record producer)") at Chem 19 Studios in [Hamilton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hamilton,_South_Lanarkshire "Hamilton, South Lanarkshire"), near [Glasgow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glasgow "Glasgow"), Scotland, with [Alasdair Roberts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alasdair_Roberts_(musician) "Alasdair Roberts (musician)"), Geof Comings, Jonathan Cargill, and members of [Arab Strap](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arab_Strap_(band) "Arab Strap (band)").\[ _[citation needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed "Wikipedia:Citation needed")_\]

## Critical reception

_The Lioness_ was met with "generally favorable" reviews from critics. At [Metacritic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metacritic "Metacritic"), which assigns a weighted average rating out of 100 to reviews from mainstream publications, this release received an [average](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weighted_arithmetic_mean "Weighted arithmetic mean") score of 74, based on 4 reviews.[\[1\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Lioness_(album)#cite_note-MC-1)

_[Exclaim!](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exclaim! "Exclaim!")_ thought that "the transatlantic recording session inspired Molina to produce what is both his darkest and cheerful record to date."[\[10\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Lioness_(album)#cite_note-10) _[The Austin Chronicle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Austin_Chronicle "The Austin Chronicle")_ called the album "quiet and reflective, numbing and resolute, with writing as simple and straightforward as an ice pick under the ribs."[\[11\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Lioness_(album)#cite_note-11) _[The Sunday Times](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Sunday_Times "The Sunday Times")_ wrote that "Songs: Ohia's slug- paced folk-blues owe an obvious debt to the spectral yowling of Will Oldham's Palace Brothers, but _The Lioness_ still has the power to turn the most brightly lit room a delicate shade of blue."[\[12\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Lioness_(album)#cite_note-12)
