_**The Magnolia Electric Co.**_ is the seventh and final album by [Songs: Ohia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Songs:_Ohia "Songs: Ohia"). It was recorded by [Steve Albini](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steve_Albini "Steve Albini") at [Electrical Audio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electrical_Audio "Electrical Audio") in Chicago and released by [Secretly Canadian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Secretly_Canadian "Secretly Canadian") on March 4, 2003. It was critically acclaimed on release.

## Title

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=The_Magnolia_Electric_Co.&action=edit&section=1 "Edit section: Title")\]

The title of the album and comments by [Jason Molina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jason_Molina "Jason Molina") have led to discussions whether it is not simultaneously, in fact, the debut album by Molina's new band, also named [Magnolia Electric Co](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magnolia_Electric_Co "Magnolia Electric Co").

The artwork for the album does not contain the name Songs: Ohia anywhere, though its hype sticker and the center label on later pressings of the vinyl record do say "Songs: Ohia." On the other hand, the album was recorded with different musicians than the later members of Magnolia Electric Co., and the decision to take on the new name was not announced until the tour following the release in the spring of 2003. Molina later declared _[Didn't It Rain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Didn't_It_Rain_(Songs:_Ohia_album) "Didn't It Rain (Songs: Ohia album)")_ to be the final Songs: Ohia album.[\[14\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Magnolia_Electric_Co.#cite_note-14)

## Composition

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=The_Magnolia_Electric_Co.&action=edit&section=2 "Edit section: Composition")\]

_Magnolia_ digs into [alt-country](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alt-country "Alt-country") and "red-blood, full-throated" [country rock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Country_rock "Country rock"), as well as seeing the band draw from both “orthodox” [Americana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Americana_(music) "Americana (music)") and “otherworldly atmosphere”.[\[10\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Magnolia_Electric_Co.#cite_note-PitchforkReview#2-10)[\[15\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Magnolia_Electric_Co.#cite_note-Paste-15) It also works in [roots rock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roots_rock "Roots rock"), while the arrangements and songwriting echo [70s](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Music_in_the_1970s "Music in the 1970s") [outlaw country](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Outlaw_country "Outlaw country").[\[5\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Magnolia_Electric_Co.#cite_note-Consequence-5)[\[8\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Magnolia_Electric_Co.#cite_note-NOW-8) In her 2026 book _Won't Back Down_, Erin Osmon called _The Magnolia Electric Co._ "...one of [counterculture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Counterculture "Counterculture")'s most celebrated and defining [heartland rock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heartland_rock "Heartland rock") offerings".[\[16\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Magnolia_Electric_Co.#cite_note-Osmon_2026-16)
