_**Molina and Johnson**_ is a collaborative album recorded by American songwriters [Jason Molina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jason_Molina "Jason Molina") and [Will Johnson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Will_Johnson_(musician) "Will Johnson (musician)"). It was released on 2 November 2009 by [Secretly Canadian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Secretly_Canadian "Secretly Canadian").[\[1\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Molina_and_Johnson#cite_note-SC-1)

The cover art for the album was taken from a photograph by [Jack Delano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jack_Delano "Jack Delano") for the [Office of War Information](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Office_of_War_Information "Office of War Information"): _[Indiana Harbor Belt RR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indiana_Harbor_Belt_Railroad "Indiana Harbor Belt Railroad"), [switchman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Switchman "Switchman") demonstrating signal with a " [fusee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flare_(pyrotechnic) "Flare (pyrotechnic)")" \- used at twilight and dawn - when visibility is poor. This signal means "stop." [Calumet City, Ill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Calumet_City,_Illinois "Calumet City, Illinois")._ from 1943.

"All Gone, All Gone" features back-up vocals by [Sarah Jaffe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sarah_Jaffe "Sarah Jaffe").

It received "generally favorable reviews" according to review aggregator [Metacritic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metacritic "Metacritic"), with an aggregate score of 75 as of 25 November 2009.[\[2\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Molina_and_Johnson#cite_note-MC-2) According to the _[Austin Chronicle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austin_Chronicle "Austin Chronicle")_ reviewer, "this debut collaboration is a testament to just how deeply these two songwriters sympathize with each other's work, revealing a shared penchant for evocatively detailed images that blossom into visceral narratives."[\[3\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Molina_and_Johnson#cite_note-AC-3) The _[Pitchfork Media](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pitchfork_Media "Pitchfork Media")_ reviewer was less sanguine: "The 14 tracks on _Molina and Johnson_ comprise an especially slow drive through somber countryside, windows up and speed limit carefully maintained."[\[4\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Molina_and_Johnson#cite_note-PF-4)
