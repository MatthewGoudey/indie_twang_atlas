Musical artist

**Matthew Stephen** **Ward** (born October 4, 1973) is an American singer-songwriter, Grammy nominated producer and guitarist from [Glendale, California](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glendale,_California "Glendale, California"). Ward's solo work is a mixture of [folk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Folk_music "Folk music") and [blues](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blues "Blues")-inspired [Americana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Americana_(music) "Americana (music)") [analog recordings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Analog_recording "Analog recording"). He has released 12 studio albums since 1999, primarily through the independent label [Merge Records](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Merge_Records "Merge Records"). In addition to his solo work, he is a member of indie pop duo [She & Him](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/She_&_Him "She & Him") and folk-rock [supergroup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supergroup_(music) "Supergroup (music)") [Monsters of Folk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monsters_of_Folk "Monsters of Folk"), and also participates in recording, producing, and playing with multiple other artists.

## Early life

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=M._Ward&action=edit&section=1 "Edit section: Early life")\]

Ward was born on October 4, 1973, in [Glendale, California](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glendale,_California "Glendale, California"), a suburb of [Los Angeles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Los_Angeles "Los Angeles").[\[1\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M._Ward#cite_note-1) He later moved to [Ventura County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ventura_County,_California "Ventura County, California"), and attended [Newbury Park High School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Newbury_Park_High_School "Newbury Park High School"). He went to college at [Cal Poly San Luis Obispo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/California_Polytechnic_State_University,_San_Luis_Obispo "California Polytechnic State University, San Luis Obispo"), and later moved to [Portland, Oregon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portland,_Oregon "Portland, Oregon"), to pursue his music career.[\[2\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M._Ward#cite_note-2) Growing up, Ward taught himself songs by [the Beatles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Beatles "The Beatles") on his brother's guitar, and began recording demos on a [four-track analog tape](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/4-track_(multitrack) "4-track (multitrack)") recorder when he was about fifteen.[\[3\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M._Ward#cite_note-:0-3) Ward continues to record only analog, and starts all of his songs as demos on the same recorder he has had since his teens.[\[3\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M._Ward#cite_note-:0-3) In a 2007 interview with [Exclaim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exclaim! "Exclaim!") magazine, Noel Gallagher of [Oasis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oasis_(band) "Oasis (band)") called M. Ward’s “Post War” album “One of the best albums I’ve ever heard actually.” [link](https://exclaim.ca/music/article/noel_gallagher-oasis)

## Solo recordings

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=M._Ward&action=edit&section=2 "Edit section: Solo recordings")\]

### _Duets for Guitars \#2_ and _End of Amnesia_ (1999–2001)

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=M._Ward&action=edit&section=3 "Edit section: Duets for Guitars #2 and End of Amnesia (1999–2001)")\]

Ward's solo debut, _[Duet for Guitars #2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duet_for_Guitars_No._2 "Duet for Guitars No. 2")_, was released by Co-Dependent Records in 1999, then reissued by [Howe Gelb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Howe_Gelb "Howe Gelb")'s Ow Om record label in 2000.[\[4\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M._Ward#cite_note-:1-4) Described by Joshua Klein of _[Pitchfork](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pitchfork_Media "Pitchfork Media")_ as "ragged and lo-fi ... recorded on a shoestring and not necessarily worse for it",[\[5\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M._Ward#cite_note-5) _Duet for Guitars #2_ soon went out of print for a second time, before being reissued by Merge in 2007.[\[4\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M._Ward#cite_note-:1-4)

Ward's second album, _[End of Amnesia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/End_of_Amnesia "End of Amnesia")_, was put out by [Future Farmer Records](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Future_Farmer_Records "Future Farmer Records") and [Loose Music](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Loose_Music "Loose Music") (Europe) in 2001. In a retrospective review, Ryan Kearney of _Pitchfork_ compared the album to a contemporary band, [Sparklehorse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sparklehorse "Sparklehorse"), saying that "both [Linkous](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mark_Linkous "Mark Linkous") and Ward are country-and folk-influenced artists who scratch unavoidable, but nominally disruptive marks on the traditional blueprint".[\[6\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M._Ward#cite_note-6) Sparklehorse had released _[It's a Wonderful Life](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/It's_a_Wonderful_Life_(album) "It's a Wonderful Life (album)")_ to critical acclaim earlier in the year.

A collection of live recordings, _Live Music & The Voice of Strangers_, was a self-released disc that was sold at his shows in 2001.

### _Transfiguration of Vincent_ and _Transistor Radio_ (2003–05)

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=M._Ward&action=edit&section=4 "Edit section: Transfiguration of Vincent and Transistor Radio (2003–05)")\]

Ward released his third album, _[Transfiguration of Vincent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transfiguration_of_Vincent "Transfiguration of Vincent")_, on [Merge Records](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Merge_Records "Merge Records") in 2003 to critical success. _Transfiguration of Vincent_ received a weighted average score of 82 out of 100 by review aggregator website Metacritic, based on 13 critical reviews, indicating "universal acclaim".[\[7\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M._Ward#cite_note-7) The title alludes to the 1965 album _[The Transfiguration of Blind Joe Death](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Transfiguration_of_Blind_Joe_Death "The Transfiguration of Blind Joe Death")_ by [John Fahey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Fahey_(musician) "John Fahey (musician)"), and refers to the life and death of Vincent O'Brien, a close friend to Ward.[\[8\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M._Ward#cite_note-:2-8) Fahey's [pre-war](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_II "World War II") style of folk music and production techniques, using basic equipment and simple arrangements, greatly influenced Ward's own sound and recording practices.[\[8\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M._Ward#cite_note-:2-8)

_[Transistor Radio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transistor_Radio_(album) "Transistor Radio (album)")_, Ward's fourth album, was released on Merge in 2005, and he served as the opening act for [The White Stripes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_White_Stripes "The White Stripes") that fall.[\[9\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M._Ward#cite_note-9) The album consists of Ward's own compositions as well as three covers, [The Beach Boys](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Beach_Boys "The Beach Boys")' " [You Still Believe in Me](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/You_Still_Believe_in_Me "You Still Believe in Me")", [Carmen Lombardo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carmen_Lombardo "Carmen Lombardo")'s pop standard " [Sweethearts on Parade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carmen_Lombardo#Compositions_by_Carmen_Lombardo "Carmen Lombardo")", and [Bach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johann_Sebastian_Bach "Johann Sebastian Bach")'s " [Well-Tempered Clavier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Well-Tempered_Clavier "The Well-Tempered Clavier")". _Transistor Radio_ received a score of 78 out of 100 on Metacritic, indicating "generally favorable reviews".[\[10\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M._Ward#cite_note-10) Josh Terry of [Consequence of Sound](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Consequence_of_Sound "Consequence of Sound"), writing of _Transistor Radio_ in 2014, described the album as "one of the finer examples of ramshackle and intimate mid-aughts folk."[\[11\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M._Ward#cite_note-:3-11) A writer for _Alternative Press_, however, said that "Most of Ward's quiet, contemporary folk songs are mere sketches, mediocre if not unmemorable."[\[12\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M._Ward#cite_note-12) _Transistor Radio_ was reissued by Merge in December 2014 with four previously unreleased tracks.[\[11\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M._Ward#cite_note-:3-11)

### _Post-War_ and _Hold Time_ (2006–09)

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=M._Ward&action=edit&section=5 "Edit section: Post-War and Hold Time (2006–09)")\]

In August 2006, Ward released _[Post-War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Post-War "Post-War")_ on Merge Records. _Post-War_ was Ward's first album with a full backing band, with players including [Howe Gelb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Howe_Gelb "Howe Gelb"), [Jim James](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jim_James "Jim James"), and [Neko Case](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neko_Case "Neko Case"). _Post-War_ was described by _[Vanity Fair](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vanity_Fair_(magazine) "Vanity Fair (magazine)")_ in its August 2006 issue as thematic on the question "How will America heal once this [craziness in Iraq](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iraq_War "Iraq War") is over?" Ward said in that article that he looked to the post-war music of the late 1940s and 1950s. "I had the naive, simplistic idea that producers and writers and artists of the time helped in a minuscule way to change the mind-set of America." In addition to this inspired material, the album also contains a cover of [Daniel Johnston](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daniel_Johnston "Daniel Johnston")'s "To Go Home", which was subsequently re-released on the _[To Go Home](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/To_Go_Home "To Go Home")_ [EP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Extended_play "Extended play") in 2007. A critical success, _Post-War_ received "universal acclaim" of 81 out of 100, aggregated by Metacritic[\[13\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M._Ward#cite_note-13) and reached No. 146 on [Billboard's Top 200](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Billboard_200 "Billboard 200").[\[14\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M._Ward#cite_note-Billboard-14) Ward spent most of 2007 touring with [Norah Jones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norah_Jones "Norah Jones") in support of the album, as well as occasionally playing in Norah's touring band, "The Handsome Band". Ward played on Jones's 2007 release _[Not Too Late](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Not_Too_Late_(album) "Not Too Late (album)")_.

_[Hold Time](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hold_Time_(album) "Hold Time (album)")_, the followup to _Post-War_, was released on Merge in February 2009. _Hold Time_ includes guest performances by [Jason Lytle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jason_Lytle "Jason Lytle") of the band [Grandaddy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grandaddy "Grandaddy"), [Lucinda Williams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lucinda_Williams "Lucinda Williams"), Tom Hagerman of [DeVotchKa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DeVotchKa "DeVotchKa"), and [She & Him](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/She_&_Him "She & Him") co-contributor [Zooey Deschanel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zooey_Deschanel "Zooey Deschanel"). The album includes a cover of the [Buddy Holly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buddy_Holly "Buddy Holly") song " [Rave On!](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rave_On! "Rave On!")" Described by Autumn De Wilde _[Entertainment Weekly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Entertainment_Weekly "Entertainment Weekly")_ as "\[feeling\] timeless, a musical wanderer's dusty, train-hopping tour through folk, blues, and country,"[\[15\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M._Ward#cite_note-15) _Hold Time_ received an aggregated 79 out of 100 on Metacritic, for "generally favorable reviews"[\[16\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M._Ward#cite_note-16) and reached No. 31 on Billboard's Top 200.[\[14\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M._Ward#cite_note-Billboard-14)

### _A Wasteland Companion_ and _More Rain_ (2012–16)

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=M._Ward&action=edit&section=6 "Edit section: A Wasteland Companion and More Rain (2012–16)")\]

After several years of touring and releasing albums with pop project She & Him and folk [supergroup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supergroup_(music) "Supergroup (music)") [Monsters of Folk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monsters_of_Folk "Monsters of Folk"), Ward released his seventh studio album, _[A Wasteland Companion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A_Wasteland_Companion "A Wasteland Companion")_, in 2012 on Merge and [Bella Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bella_Union "Bella Union"). Reaching number 21 in the US charts,[\[14\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M._Ward#cite_note-Billboard-14) _A Wasteland Companion_ received an aggregated score of 75 out of 100 on Metacritic, indicating "generally favorable reviews".[\[17\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M._Ward#cite_note-17) Will Hermes of _[Rolling Stone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rolling_Stone "Rolling Stone")_ gave the album 3.5 of 5 stars, reflective of the aggregated score, and commented that "\[ _A Wasteland Companion_\] is his most vivid and varied yet, full of exquisite guitar work,...gem-like songcraft...and inspired covers."[\[18\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M._Ward#cite_note-18) Matthew McFarland of _Prefix_ offered the criticism, "What's missing, though, is the familiar sense of deft control over the album's arc, the lyrical intrigues, and the instrumental detail that make his other work so indispensable \[sic\] to the indie folk canon of last decade."[\[19\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M._Ward#cite_note-19)

After another multi-year break from solo recording, M. Ward released his eighth album, _[More Rain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/More_Rain "More Rain")_, on March 4, 2016, via Merge[\[20\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M._Ward#cite_note-20) and [Bella Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bella_Union "Bella Union").[\[21\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M._Ward#cite_note-21) Ward began working on _More Rain_ in 2012, initially experimenting with layering his own vocals to create a [doo-wop](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doo-wop "Doo-wop") record. After collaborating with other artists on the record such as [R.E.M.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/R.E.M. "R.E.M.")'s [Peter Buck](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_Buck "Peter Buck"), [k.d. lang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K.d._lang "K.d. lang"), [Neko Case](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neko_Case "Neko Case"), and others, the sound of the album went in a different direction, described as a "true gotta-stay-indoors, rainy-season record that looks upwards through the weather while reflecting on his past."[\[22\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M._Ward#cite_note-22)

[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/4/47/She_%26_Him_at_NFF.jpg/250px-She_%26_Him_at_NFF.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:She_&_Him_at_NFF.jpg) Ward (right) and [Zooey Deschanel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zooey_Deschanel "Zooey Deschanel") performing as [She & Him](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/She_&_Him "She & Him") at the [Newport Folk Festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Newport_Folk_Festival "Newport Folk Festival") (August 2, 2008)

### _What a Wonderful Industry_, _Migration Stories_, _Think of Spring_ and _Supernatural Thing_ (2017–present)

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=M._Ward&action=edit&section=7 "Edit section: What a Wonderful Industry, Migration Stories, Think of Spring and Supernatural Thing (2017–present)")\]

On June 8, 2018, Ward self-released _What a Wonderful Industry_, his ninth studio album. Using a variety of anecdotes and metaphors, the album examines the complex challenges of working within the cutthroat music industry. As Ward stated in an interview with NPR, "You quickly learn there's a perfectly imperfect balance of cold-blooded and warm-blooded animals in the zoo....This record visits the most memorable characters. There's a lot of very inspirational people I've had the pleasure to work with but there are also a few I wish I'd never met."[\[23\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M._Ward#cite_note-23)

Ward's tenth studio album, _Migration Stories_, was released on April 3, 2020, on Anti Records.[\[24\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M._Ward#cite_note-24)

On December 11, 2020 Ward also released _[Think of Spring](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Think_of_Spring "Think of Spring")_ which is a tribute to the live album _[Lady in Satin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lady_in_Satin "Lady in Satin")_ by [Billie Holiday](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Billie_Holiday "Billie Holiday").[\[25\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M._Ward#cite_note-25) The album features solo acoustic versions most of the songs from _Lady in Satin_ (excluding _Glad to Be Unhappy_ and _The End of a Love Affair_ and including _All The Way_, which was recorded by Holiday and released on her 1959 album _[Last Recording](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Last_Recording "Last Recording")_[\[26\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M._Ward#cite_note-auto-26)). Ward recorded the album from home during the [COVID-19](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/COVID-19 "COVID-19") pandemic on a [Tascam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tascam "Tascam") [Portastudio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portastudio "Portastudio") four-track cassette recorder.[\[27\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M._Ward#cite_note-27)

In June 2023, Anti Records released Ward's twelfth solo album _Supernatural Thing_.

### She and Him

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=M._Ward&action=edit&section=9 "Edit section: She and Him")\]

Main article: [She & Him](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/She_&_Him "She & Him")

In 2006, Ward was working on the soundtrack for the film _[The Go-Getter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Go-Getter_(2007_film) "The Go-Getter (2007 film)")_, co-starring actress [Zooey Deschanel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zooey_Deschanel "Zooey Deschanel"), when Director [Martin Hynes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martin_Hynes "Martin Hynes") suggested Ward and Deschanel record a duet for the movie. Ward later referred to the collaboration as "a fruitful, creative experience."[\[28\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M._Ward#cite_note-28) Deschanel soon sent Ward demos of songs she had written, and the two formed the pop duo [She & Him](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/She_&_Him "She & Him") to record together, with Deschanel writing the songs.[\[3\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M._Ward#cite_note-:0-3) The duo's first album _[Volume One](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volume_One_(She_and_Him_album) "Volume One (She and Him album)")_ — which Ward produced – was released on [Merge Records](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Merge_Records "Merge Records") on March 18, 2008. _Volume One_ found critical and commercial success for Deschanel and Ward's pop music. The album reached No. 31 on Billboard's Top 200,[\[29\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M._Ward#cite_note-:4-29) and Metacritic has the critical album reviews aggregated to 76 out of 100, or "generally favorable reviews".[\[30\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M._Ward#cite_note-30)

She & Him's second album, _[Volume Two](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volume_Two_(She_&_Him_album) "Volume Two (She & Him album)")_, was released on Merge in 2010. _Volume Two_ peaked at No. 6 on Billboard's Top 200, outperforming their first release. _[A Very She & Him Christmas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A_Very_She_&_Him_Christmas "A Very She & Him Christmas")_ was released October 25, 2011, on Merge Records, peaking at No. 12.[\[29\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M._Ward#cite_note-:4-29) After a break, She & Him released their third album of original songs, _[Volume 3](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volume_3_(She_&_Him_album) "Volume 3 (She & Him album)")_ in 2013 followed by a collection of [standards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Standard_(music) "Standard (music)"), _[Classics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Classics_(She_&_Him_album) "Classics (She & Him album)")_, in 2014. Both albums also charted and were released on Merge.[\[29\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M._Ward#cite_note-:4-29) In 2016 they released their second christmas album, [Christmas Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christmas_Party_(She_&_Him_album) "Christmas Party (She & Him album)").

### Monsters of Folk

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=M._Ward&action=edit&section=10 "Edit section: Monsters of Folk")\]

Main article: [Monsters of Folk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monsters_of_Folk "Monsters of Folk")

[Monsters of Folk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monsters_of_Folk "Monsters of Folk") is a project formed by Ward, [Jim James](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jim_James "Jim James") of [My Morning Jacket](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/My_Morning_Jacket "My Morning Jacket"), and [Conor Oberst](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conor_Oberst "Conor Oberst") and [Mike Mogis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mike_Mogis "Mike Mogis") of [Bright Eyes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bright_Eyes_(band) "Bright Eyes (band)"). The four had been touring and playing together at various points throughout the 2000s and regularly talked of making an album together.[\[31\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M._Ward#cite_note-31) The result, the eponymous _[Monsters of Folk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monsters_of_Folk_(album) "Monsters of Folk (album)")_, was released in September 2009. _Monsters of Folk_ received "generally favorable reviews" with an aggregate score of 80 out of 100 on Metacritic.[\[32\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M._Ward#cite_note-32) _Prefix_'s Dave Clark said of the [supergroup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supergroup_(music) "Supergroup (music)") that "The players on Monsters of Folk complement each other extremely well. There is definitely something to be said for group chemistry. These songs don't always shine the way they could, but the album is a great effort."[\[33\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M._Ward#cite_note-33) _Monsters of Folk_ was a commercial success, peaking at No. 15 on Billboard's Top 200.[\[34\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M._Ward#cite_note-34)

### Geckøs

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=M._Ward&action=edit&section=11 "Edit section: Geckøs")\]

An impromptu performance at the wedding of a mutual friend lead to a long-distance collaboration under the name **Geckøs** with [Howe Gelb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Howe_Gelb "Howe Gelb") of [Giant Sand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Giant_Sand "Giant Sand") and McKowski of [The Lost Brothers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Lost_Brothers "The Lost Brothers"). The group released its first eponymous album in September 2025.[\[35\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M._Ward#cite_note-35)

### Other artists

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=M._Ward&action=edit&section=12 "Edit section: Other artists")\]

M. Ward has produced all his solo work and every album by [She & Him](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/She_&_Him "She & Him"). In addition he has produced albums for [Jenny Lewis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jenny_Lewis "Jenny Lewis"), [Mavis Staples](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mavis_Staples "Mavis Staples") and most recently [Valerie June](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Valerie_June "Valerie June").

He has performed on recordings by [Brian Wilson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brian_Wilson "Brian Wilson"), [Kim Deal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kim_Deal "Kim Deal"), Giant Sand, [Cat Power](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cat_Power "Cat Power"), [Neko Case](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neko_Case "Neko Case"), [Beth Orton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beth_Orton "Beth Orton") (with whom he co-wrote the title track to her album _[Comfort of Strangers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Comfort_of_Strangers "Comfort of Strangers")_), [The Court & Spark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Court_&_Spark "The Court & Spark"), [Bright Eyes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bright_Eyes_(band) "Bright Eyes (band)") (with whom he toured on the 2004 [Vote for Change](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vote_for_Change "Vote for Change") tour with [R.E.M.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/R.E.M. "R.E.M.") and [Bruce Springsteen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bruce_Springsteen "Bruce Springsteen")), [Jenny Lewis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jenny_Lewis "Jenny Lewis") (whose debut solo album _[Rabbit Fur Coat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rabbit_Fur_Coat "Rabbit Fur Coat")_ he co-produced), and [My Morning Jacket](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/My_Morning_Jacket "My Morning Jacket").

In 2005, Ward was – along with [Aaron Burtch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aaron_Burtch?action=edit&redlink=1 "Aaron Burtch (page does not exist)"), [Jason Lytle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jason_Lytle "Jason Lytle") and [Jim Fairchild](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jim_Fairchild "Jim Fairchild") from [Grandaddy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grandaddy "Grandaddy"), [Scout Niblett](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scout_Niblett "Scout Niblett"), [Marie Frank](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marie_Frank?action=edit&redlink=1 "Marie Frank (page does not exist)") or [Jeremy Gara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jeremy_Gara "Jeremy Gara") – involved in [Howe Gelb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Howe_Gelb "Howe Gelb")'s project called [Arizona Amp and Alternator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arizona_Amp_and_Alternator?action=edit&redlink=1 "Arizona Amp and Alternator (page does not exist)"),[\[36\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M._Ward#cite_note-36)[\[37\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M._Ward#cite_note-37) which had officially no band members[\[38\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M._Ward#cite_note-38) but a lot of guest musicians (M. Ward's voice can be recognized on the track "Aaaa(3)").

In 2006, he helped produce and contributed a song to the [John Fahey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Fahey_(musician) "John Fahey (musician)") tribute album _[I Am the Resurrection](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/I_Am_the_Resurrection_(album) "I Am the Resurrection (album)")_. He also appears on [Norah Jones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norah_Jones "Norah Jones")' album _[Not Too Late](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Not_Too_Late_(album) "Not Too Late (album)")_, performing backing vocals and guitar on " [Sinkin' Soon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sinkin'_Soon "Sinkin' Soon")", and toured as the opener and a member of her "Handsome Band" for the album in the spring of 2007. Also his cover of [David Bowie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Bowie "David Bowie")'s song " [Let's Dance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Let's_Dance_(David_Bowie_song) "Let's Dance (David Bowie song)")" is featured on the soundtrack of the 2007 [New Zealand film](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Zealand_film "New Zealand film") _[Eagle vs Shark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eagle_vs_Shark "Eagle vs Shark")_. Ward was previously a member of the band [Rodriguez](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rodriguez_(band) "Rodriguez (band)") with [Kyle Field](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kyle_Field_(musician) "Kyle Field (musician)") of [Little Wings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Little_Wings "Little Wings") and Mike Funk of [Echodrone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Echodrone?action=edit&redlink=1 "Echodrone (page does not exist)"). Their album _Swing Like a Metronome_ was released in 2000 and produced by Jason Lytle of [Grandaddy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grandaddy "Grandaddy").

Songs or compositions written by M. Ward have been recorded or performed by: Bright Eyes ("Seashell Tale"), Feist ("Post War"), Cat Power ("Sad Sad Song"), Carrie Rodriguez ("Eyes on the Prize"), Grandaddy ("Fishing Boat Song"), Lloyd Cole ("Chinese Translation"), Gillian Welch and Dave Rawlings ("One Life Away" "Fuel for Fire"), Norah Jones ("Lullaby + Exile"), and She and Him ("Magic Trick").

In 2013, Ward contributed guitar work to the [Neko Case](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neko_Case "Neko Case") album _The Worse Things Get, The Harder I Fight, The Harder I Fight, The More I Love You_.
