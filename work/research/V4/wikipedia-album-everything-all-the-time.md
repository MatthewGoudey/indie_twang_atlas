_**Everything All the Time**_ is the debut album of [indie rock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indie_rock "Indie rock") band [Band of Horses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Band_of_Horses "Band of Horses") and was released on March 21, 2006, on [Sub Pop Records](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sub_Pop_Records "Sub Pop Records"). It features new versions of five of the six songs from the band's _[Tour EP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tour_EP_(Band_of_Horses) "Tour EP (Band of Horses)")_, some with different titles.

The album is the only one to feature original band members [Mat Brooke](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mat_Brooke "Mat Brooke"), Chris Early and Tim Meinig. Vocalist Ben Bridwell, guitarist Mat Brooke and drummer Sera Cahoone were all members of [Carissa's Wierd](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carissa's_Wierd "Carissa's Wierd") when the band broke up in 2003.

## History

[![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/6/66/Joe_ArnoneEdit.jpg/500px-Joe_ArnoneEdit.jpg)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Joe_ArnoneEdit.jpg) Joe Arnone (with Barrett behind)

_Everything All the Time_ features the band's original four-piece lineup, although both Tim Meinig and [Sera Cahoone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sera_Cahoone "Sera Cahoone") receive drumming credits.[\[1\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Everything_All_the_Time#cite_note-1) The band recorded this album in the heart of Seattle for Sub Pop Records. Discussing the album's sound, band leader [Ben Bridwell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ben_Bridwell "Ben Bridwell") said, "I thought before recording that I really wanted an ELO-sounding record, with strings and keyboards and synths, but then, as we got closer to it, we wanted to take a more raw approach." Regarding its lyrics, he added, "A lot of these songs didn't really come from any lyric writing, let alone any singing ability. A lot of the ways the words are sung were meant to hide or mask what's being said. But there are definitely words. I wrote 'em down on paper and everything."[\[2\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Everything_All_the_Time#cite_note-2)

The band performed the first single from the album, " [The Funeral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Funeral_(Band_of_Horses_song) "The Funeral (Band of Horses song)")", on the _[Late Show with David Letterman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Late_Show_with_David_Letterman "Late Show with David Letterman")_.[\[3\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Everything_All_the_Time#cite_note-3) By that time Brooke, Meinig and Early had all left the band and had been replaced by Joe Arnone (keyboards, guitar), Rob Hampton (guitar, bass) and Creighton Barrett (drums). "The Funeral" has been used extensively in [film, TV, video games and advertisements.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Funeral_(Band_of_Horses_song)#Appearances_in_other_media "The Funeral (Band of Horses song)")

The two tracks with lyrics written by Mat Brooke, "I Go to the Barn..." and "St. Augustine" feature co-lead vocals by Brooke and Bridwell although Brooke's vocals are much quieter than Bridwell's. A demo version of "I Go to the Barn..." titled "I'd Like to Think" was recorded by Brooke and Bridwell as **Nov 16**, a short lived project between [Carissa's Wierd](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carissa's_Wierd "Carissa's Wierd") and [Band of Horses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Band_of_Horses "Band of Horses"). On the Nov 16 version, Bridwell's vocals are much quieter than Brooke's. Neither "I Go to the Barn..." nor "St. Augustine" were played live after Brooke's 2006 departure from the band until December 2012, when both songs reappeared in their live setlist.

The album was a minor hit in Scandinavia, appearing in the lower reaches of both the Swedish and Norwegian album charts.[\[4\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Everything_All_the_Time#cite_note-ultratop-4)

Professional ratings

The album was generally well received by critics. It has a score of 78 out of 100 from 24 critics on the website [Metacritic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metacritic "Metacritic").[\[5\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Everything_All_the_Time#cite_note-MC-5) _[Pitchfork](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pitchfork_(website) "Pitchfork (website)")_ placed _Everything All the Time_ at number 109 on their list of top 200 albums of the 2000s.[\[16\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Everything_All_the_Time#cite_note-16)
