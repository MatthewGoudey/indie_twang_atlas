**Calexico** is an American [indie rock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indie_rock "Indie rock") band based in [Tucson, Arizona](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tucson,_Arizona "Tucson, Arizona").[\[2\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Calexico_(band)#cite_note-:1-2)[\[3\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Calexico_(band)#cite_note-3)[\[4\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Calexico_(band)#cite_note-4) Founded in 1996, the band's two main members, Joey Burns and John Convertino, first played together in [Los Angeles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Los_Angeles "Los Angeles") as part of the group [Giant Sand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Giant_Sand "Giant Sand"). They have recorded a number of albums on [Quarterstick Records](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quarterstick_Records "Quarterstick Records") and [City Slang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City_Slang "City Slang"), and their 2005 EP, _[In the Reins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/In_the_Reins "In the Reins")_, recorded with [Iron & Wine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iron_&_Wine "Iron & Wine"), reached the [Billboard 200](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Billboard_200 "Billboard 200") albums chart. Their musical style is influenced by traditional [Latin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latin_American_music "Latin American music") sounds of [mariachi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mariachi "Mariachi"), [conjunto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conjunto "Conjunto"), [cumbia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cumbia "Cumbia"), and [tejano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tejano_music "Tejano music") mixed with [country](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Country_music "Country music"), [jazz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jazz "Jazz"), and [post-rock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Post-rock "Post-rock").

The band is named for the border town of [Calexico, California](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Calexico,_California "Calexico, California"), and has been described by some as "desert noir".[\[5\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Calexico_(band)#cite_note-5)

## History

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Calexico_(band)&action=edit&section=1 "Edit section: History")\]

### Formation

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Calexico_(band)&action=edit&section=2 "Edit section: Formation")\]

[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/5/51/Calexico_Joey_Burns.jpg/250px-Calexico_Joey_Burns.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Calexico_Joey_Burns.jpg) Joey Burns

Calexico had its origins in 1990 when Joey Burns, who was studying music at the [University of California, Irvine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_Irvine "University of California, Irvine"), met up with John Convertino, who was playing drums with [Howe Gelb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Howe_Gelb "Howe Gelb") in [Giant Sand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Giant_Sand "Giant Sand"). Burns also joined Giant Sand,[\[2\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Calexico_(band)#cite_note-:1-2) after first playing upright bass on a European tour.\[ _[citation needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed "Wikipedia:Citation needed")_\]

Around this time, Burns and Convertino also played in the [Friends of Dean Martinez](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Friends_of_Dean_Martinez "Friends of Dean Martinez") along with Bill Elm.[\[2\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Calexico_(band)#cite_note-:1-2) This group signed a record deal with [Sub Pop](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sub_Pop "Sub Pop"). However, the pair split up with Bill Elm, the co-founder of The Friends of Dean Martinez in 1996.\[ _[citation needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed "Wikipedia:Citation needed")_\] The band subsequently became a kind of indie [rhythm section](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rhythm_section "Rhythm section") for hire, working with the likes of [Victoria Williams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Victoria_Williams "Victoria Williams"), [Bill Janovitz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bill_Janovitz "Bill Janovitz"), [Barbara Manning](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barbara_Manning "Barbara Manning") and [Richard Buckner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_Buckner_(musician) "Richard Buckner (musician)") before forming Calexico.

Calexico first recorded [_Spoke_](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spoke_(album) "Spoke (album)") in 1995 for German independent label _[Hausmusik](https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hausmusik%20(Label) "de:Hausmusik (Label)")_ with a limited edition of 2,000 copies. At that point the band was still called Spoke and the album was technically self-titled. After the band signed with Quarterstick Records (a subsidiary of [Touch and Go Records](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Touch_and_Go_Records "Touch and Go Records")) and changed their name to Calexico, _Spoke_ was reissued by that label in 1997. Burns and Convertino also collaborated with Gelb and [Lisa Germano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lisa_Germano "Lisa Germano") on the album [_Slush_](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slush_(album) "Slush (album)") released under the name [OP8](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/OP8 "OP8") that same year.\[ _[citation needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed "Wikipedia:Citation needed")_\]

### Early years (1998–2005)

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Calexico_(band)&action=edit&section=3 "Edit section: Early years (1998–2005)")\]

Their second album, _[The Black Light](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Black_Light_(Calexico_album) "The Black Light (Calexico album)")_, was released in 1998. This was a concept album about the desert of Arizona and northern Mexico and received excellent reviews, with the critic from the _Wall Street Journal_ rating it as one of the best records of the year.

The band built their profile by touring as support acts for bands such as [Pavement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pavement_(band) "Pavement (band)"), the [Dirty Three](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dirty_Three "Dirty Three") and [Lambchop](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lambchop_(band) "Lambchop (band)"). Calexico has regularly played festivals such as the [Bonnaroo Music Festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bonnaroo_Music_Festival "Bonnaroo Music Festival"), the [Hurricane Festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hurricane_Festival "Hurricane Festival") and [All Tomorrow's Parties](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/All_Tomorrow's_Parties_(music_festival) "All Tomorrow's Parties (music festival)"). The _Road Map_ album was a limited recording released in 1999 for sale only at Calexico's live shows.

Calexico released its third album _[Hot Rail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hot_Rail "Hot Rail")_ in May 2000 featuring the addition of horns and violin to their sound. The duo was busy in 2000 as they also appeared on a Giant Sand record called _Chore of Enchantment_ as well as a tour only record _Travelall_.

At the end of 2000, Burns and Convertino joined with two French friends [Naïm Amor and Thomas Belhôm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amor_Belhom_Duo "Amor Belhom Duo") to record "Tête A Tête" (Wabana Records, 2001) released under moniker ABBC (Amor/Belhom/Burns/Convertino). Calexico released two collections of rarities during 2001. _Even My Sure Things Fall Through_ collected outtakes from previous albums, B-sides, remixes and material previously unreleased in the US. The album also featured Mariachi Luz de Luna who frequently played live with the duo. The _[Aerocalexico](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aerocalexico "Aerocalexico")_ album was sold exclusively at their gigs in 2001. Their songs "Ballad Of Cable Hogue" and "Service And Repair" were featured in the 2001 German comedy movie _[Lammbock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lammbock "Lammbock")_.

Calexico released _[Feast of Wire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Feast_of_Wire "Feast of Wire")_ in 2003. The album is considered their breakthrough album; further, it was praised by [Mojo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mojo_(magazine) "Mojo (magazine)").\[ _[citation needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed "Wikipedia:Citation needed")_\]

### _Garden Ruin_ to _Edge of the Sun_ (2006 to 2017)

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Calexico_(band)&action=edit&section=4 "Edit section: Garden Ruin to Edge of the Sun (2006 to 2017)")\]

Released in 2006, _[Garden Ruin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garden_Ruin "Garden Ruin")_ is Calexico's fifth studio album and was produced by [JD Foster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/JD_Foster "JD Foster"). The sound strays slightly from earlier works, focusing less on the horn section and placing more emphasis on guitar and vocal tracks, giving the whole album a more contemporary Indie sound.

Their sixth studio LP is called _[Carried to Dust](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carried_to_Dust "Carried to Dust")_, featuring Sam Beam of [Iron & Wine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iron_&_Wine "Iron & Wine"), Douglas McCombs of [Tortoise](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tortoise_(band) "Tortoise (band)") and Pieta Brown. It was released in the US via Touch & Go Records on September 9, 2008.[\[6\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Calexico_(band)#cite_note-6) The album was positively reviewed.[\[7\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Calexico_(band)#cite_note-7) On October 16, 2008, a three-song live acoustic video performance premiered on LiveDaily Sessions, featuring Joey Burns performing the songs "Two Silver Trees", "Writer's Minor Holiday" and "Man Made Lake."[\[8\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Calexico_(band)#cite_note-LiveDailySessions-8) In 2009, the song "Banderilla" was featured in an episode of [AMC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AMC_(TV_channel) "AMC (TV channel)")'s [Breaking Bad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Breaking_Bad "Breaking Bad").[\[9\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Calexico_(band)#cite_note-BreakingBad-9)

In 2010, Calexico released the soundtrack of the documentary _Circo_. The band also spent part of 2010 touring with Arcade Fire along the US west coast. In May 2011, their song "Slowness" was dedicated by [Gabby Giffords](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gabby_Giffords "Gabby Giffords") to her husband [astronaut](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Astronaut "Astronaut") [Mark Kelly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mark_Kelly_(astronaut) "Mark Kelly (astronaut)") as the wake up song aboard the [Space Shuttle _Endeavour_](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Space_Shuttle_Endeavour "Space Shuttle Endeavour") on its final flight.[\[10\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Calexico_(band)#cite_note-10) _[Algiers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Algiers_(Calexico_album) "Algiers (Calexico album)")_ was released 11 September 2012 on [Anti-Records](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anti-_(record_label) "Anti- (record label)") and [City Slang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City_Slang "City Slang") (Europe). Algiers is titled after the neighborhood of New Orleans where it was recorded, [Algiers, New Orleans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Algiers,_New_Orleans "Algiers, New Orleans"). This was Calexico's seventh studio album.

In April 2013, Calexico released the album _[Edge of the Sun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edge_of_the_Sun "Edge of the Sun")_. The album features guest appearances by [Neko Case](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neko_Case "Neko Case"), Sam Beam ( [Iron & Wine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iron_&_Wine "Iron & Wine")), Ben Bridwell ( [Band of Horses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Band_of_Horses "Band of Horses")), [Gaby Moreno](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gaby_Moreno "Gaby Moreno"), [Carla Morrison](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carla_Morrison "Carla Morrison") and members of the Greek instrumental group Takim.[\[11\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Calexico_(band)#cite_note-11) Burns commented how happy he was to work with Sergio Mendoza on writing the songs for the album. Mendoza also co-produced the album along with Burns and Convertino.[\[12\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Calexico_(band)#cite_note-:2-12) This album was also released on Anti-Records. In 2016, the band performed on the main stage at the [Edmonton Folk Music Festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edmonton_Folk_Music_Festival "Edmonton Folk Music Festival").[\[13\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Calexico_(band)#cite_note-13)

### _The Thread That Keeps Us_ (2018 to present)

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Calexico_(band)&action=edit&section=5 "Edit section: The Thread That Keeps Us (2018 to present)")\]

In 2018, Calexico released _The Thread That Keeps Us_. The album was recorded in Northern California in a home-turned-studio called [Panoramic House](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Panoramic_House?action=edit&redlink=1 "Panoramic House (page does not exist)"). The band called this space “The Phantom Ship”.[\[14\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Calexico_(band)#cite_note-:0-14) The album was co-produced by their longtime engineer Craig Schumacher. Calexico and Schumacher recruited musicians from all across the globe to find an earthy yet expansive sound.[\[15\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Calexico_(band)#cite_note-15) “There's a little more chaos and noise in the mix than what we've done in the past,” Burns points out.[\[14\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Calexico_(band)#cite_note-:0-14) The rugged coastline of northern California impacted the making of _The Thread That Keeps Us_.[\[14\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Calexico_(band)#cite_note-:0-14) This sound is heard throughout the album. The album was released by [Anti-Records](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anti-_(record_label) "Anti- (record label)") and [City Slang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City_Slang "City Slang") on January 26, 2018.[\[14\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Calexico_(band)#cite_note-:0-14)

_The Thread That Keeps Us_ was No. 2 on the Alternative Albums retail chart and No. 5 on the Americana / Folk Chart in the USA in its first week of sales ( _[Billboard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Billboard_(Magazine) "Billboard (Magazine)")_).[\[16\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Calexico_(band)#cite_note-16) _The Thread That Keeps Us_ also entered the charts in Europe at no. 7 in Germany, No. 13 in Belgium, No. 21 in Netherlands, No. 47 in France, No. 58 in the United Kingdom, and No. 60 in Italy.

On March 21, 2019, Calexico and [Iron & Wine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iron_&_Wine "Iron & Wine") announced _Years to Burn_, their first full-length collaboration album. It was released on June 14, 2019 via Sub Pop.[\[17\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Calexico_(band)#cite_note-ironwine-17)

Then, on December 4, 2020, they released their eleventh album _[Seasonal Shift](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seasonal_Shift "Seasonal Shift")_ via [Anti-](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anti-_(record_label) "Anti- (record label)") and [City Slang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City_Slang "City Slang").[\[18\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Calexico_(band)#cite_note-18)

In February 2022, Calexico announced that their thirteenth studio album, _El Mirador_, would be released on April 8, 2022.[\[19\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Calexico_(band)#cite_note-PitchforkMirador-19)

The band reissued and toured _Feast of Wire_ in 2023; the album's twentieth anniversary.[\[2\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Calexico_(band)#cite_note-:1-2)[\[20\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Calexico_(band)#cite_note-20)

## Critical acclaim

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Calexico_(band)&action=edit&section=14 "Edit section: Critical acclaim")\]

Their debut album _Spoke_ received three stars from [AllMusic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AllMusic "AllMusic"), which called the album "a Santa Fe rummage sale of sounds."[\[22\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Calexico_(band)#cite_note-22) Their second album _The Black Light_, released in 1998, gained the band notoriety and attention. AllMusic praised this sophomore effort and gave the album 4.5 stars. The album was praised for being "deeper and richer than their debut."[\[23\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Calexico_(band)#cite_note-23)

_Hot Rail_ was released in 2000 and continued off the success of previous albums. Pitchfork gave the album a 7.9.[\[24\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Calexico_(band)#cite_note-24) The band then took a brief hiatus and released their next studio album in 2003, _Feast of Wire_. The album received an 8.9 from Pitchfork, which described it as "the album we always knew they had in them but feared they would never make."[\[25\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Calexico_(band)#cite_note-25)

_Feast of Wire_ was well received by [Mojo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mojo_(magazine) "Mojo (magazine)"), describing the 2003 album as, “Seductive, stirring songs about crushed hope and the corruption of beauty and some of their most ambitious arrangements make this their most fully-realised and accomplished album”.\[ _[citation needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed "Wikipedia:Citation needed")_\]

Again after another hiatus, Calexico returned in 2006 with _Garden Ruin_. Amazon gave their album a 90/100 and described it as "what [Sigur Ros](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sigur_R%C3%B3s "Sigur Rós") might sound like if they came from Arizona, and it's truly excellent."[\[26\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Calexico_(band)#cite_note-26) In 2008, came _Carried to Dust_. By now Calexico was already established as a band who meticulously created beautiful albums. This success continued with _Carried to Dust_ receiving 4.5 stars from AllMusic. Describing the album as "their most balanced, channeling their experience and potential into a subtly dramatic, chiaroscuro tour de force."[\[27\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Calexico_(band)#cite_note-27)

2012 was a busy year for Calexico, giving birth to a new album titled _Algiers_. _[The A.V. Club](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_A.V._Club "The A.V. Club")_ gave the album an A−.[\[28\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Calexico_(band)#cite_note-28) Another studio album came in 2015; titled _Edge of the Sun_. This album also received high ranks from reviewers. [Rough Trade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rough_Trade_Records "Rough Trade Records") called it the twenty-first best album of 2015.[\[29\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Calexico_(band)#cite_note-29) Their next studio album came in 2018, _The Thread That Keeps Us_. [Exclaim!](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exclaim! "Exclaim!") rated the album a 4 out of 10.[\[30\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Calexico_(band)#cite_note-30)
