_**Nixon**_ is the fifth [studio album](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Studio_album "Studio album") by American [rock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rock_music "Rock music") band [Lambchop](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lambchop_(band) "Lambchop (band)"). It was released on February 7, 2000, and was issued by [Merge Records](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Merge_Records "Merge Records") and [City Slang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City_Slang "City Slang").

_Nixon_ became a breakthrough release for Lambchop in the United Kingdom, where it received critical acclaim and was named among the best albums of 2000 by numerous publications.

## Composition

_Nixon_ has been described as a merging of [chamber pop](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chamber_pop "Chamber pop"), [countrypolitan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Countrypolitan "Countrypolitan"), and [R&B](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/R&B "R&B") sounds alongside "sweet" [soul](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soul_music "Soul music") music.[\[1\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nixon_(album)#cite_note-AllMusic-1)[\[2\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nixon_(album)#cite_note-Q-2)

## Artwork and title

The title _Nixon_ alludes to [Richard Nixon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_Nixon "Richard Nixon") and was derived from the album's cover artwork, which is a painting by [Wayne White](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wayne_White_(artist) "Wayne White (artist)"), a friend of Lambchop frontman [Kurt Wagner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kurt_Wagner_(musician) "Kurt Wagner (musician)").[\[3\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nixon_(album)#cite_note-Kathimerini-3) "He always plays around with slogans or words. He considered the material on the _Nixon_ album to be [tragicomic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tragicomedy "Tragicomedy"), and an image of Nixon came to mind," Wagner explained.[\[3\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nixon_(album)#cite_note-Kathimerini-3)

## Release

_Nixon_ was released on February 7, 2000, by Lambchop's European label [City Slang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City_Slang "City Slang"),[\[4\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nixon_(album)#cite_note-4) and on February 8, 2000, by the band's American label [Merge Records](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Merge_Records "Merge Records").[\[5\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nixon_(album)#cite_note-5) It peaked at number 60 on the [UK Albums Chart](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UK_Albums_Chart "UK Albums Chart").[\[6\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nixon_(album)#cite_note-UK-6) "Up with People" was released as a single from the album on May 2, 2000,[\[7\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nixon_(album)#cite_note-7) reaching number 66 on the [UK Singles Chart](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UK_Singles_Chart "UK Singles Chart").[\[8\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nixon_(album)#cite_note-8) According to _[The Guardian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Guardian "The Guardian")_'s John Aizlewood, _Nixon_ established Lambchop as "standard bearers" for [alternative country](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alternative_country "Alternative country") music, and "proved that the genre could be commercially viable if it painted its off-kilter pictures of redemption and loss in glorious [Technicolor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Technicolor "Technicolor") rather than mealy-mouthed monochrome."[\[9\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nixon_(album)#cite_note-9)

Professional ratings

_Nixon_ was released to highly positive reviews from music critics, receiving a score of 84 out of 100 on the review aggregation website [Metacritic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metacritic "Metacritic"), indicating "universal acclaim".[\[10\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nixon_(album)#cite_note-Metacritic-10) The album was particularly well received by the British music press.[\[16\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nixon_(album)#cite_note-Pitchfork2-16) _[NME](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NME "NME")_ critic [Gavin Martin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gavin_Martin "Gavin Martin") deemed it Lambchop's best record and said that its "sheer sonorous delight" justified comparisons to [The Beach Boys](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Beach_Boys "The Beach Boys")' 1966 album _[Pet Sounds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pet_Sounds "Pet Sounds")_,[\[14\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nixon_(album)#cite_note-NME-14) while [Allan Jones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Allan_Jones_(editor) "Allan Jones (editor)") of _[Uncut](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uncut_(magazine) "Uncut (magazine)")_ praised _Nixon_ as "one of the first great records of the new millennium".[\[19\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nixon_(album)#cite_note-Uncut-19) At the end of 2000, _Nixon_ was named one of the year's best albums by numerous British publications, including _Uncut_ (who ranked it as the best album of 2000),[\[20\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nixon_(album)#cite_note-20) _[Mojo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mojo_(magazine) "Mojo (magazine)")_,[\[21\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nixon_(album)#cite_note-21) _NME_,[\[22\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nixon_(album)#cite_note-22) and _[Q](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Q_(magazine) "Q (magazine)")_.[\[23\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nixon_(album)#cite_note-23)

_Nixon_ was later included in the book _[1001 Albums You Must Hear Before You Die](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1001_Albums_You_Must_Hear_Before_You_Die "1001 Albums You Must Hear Before You Die")_.[\[24\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nixon_(album)#cite_note-24)
