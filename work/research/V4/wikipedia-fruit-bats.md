**Fruit Bats** is an American [indie rock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indie_rock "Indie rock") band formed in 1997 in [Chicago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chicago "Chicago"), Illinois, as the project of singer and songwriter [Eric D. Johnson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eric_D._Johnson "Eric D. Johnson"). Johnson is the band's sole permanent member, with various musicians joining the band in live and studio settings.[\[2\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fruit_Bats_(band)#cite_note-2)

## History

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Fruit_Bats_(band)&action=edit&section=1 "Edit section: History")\]

### Early years and _Echolocation_

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Fruit_Bats_(band)&action=edit&section=2 "Edit section: Early years and Echolocation")\]

In the late 1990s and early 2000s, Johnson was teaching guitar and banjo at Chicago's [Old Town School of Folk Music](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_Town_School_of_Folk_Music "Old Town School of Folk Music"), and fronting an indie rock band called I Rowboat. In early 2000, he joined the experimental folk group [Califone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Califone "Califone") as a multi-instrumentalist.[\[3\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fruit_Bats_(band)#cite_note-auto1-3) He had also been making homemade cassettes since 1997 under the name Fruit Bats. He describes coming up with the band name as a joke of sorts, describing it as "this dumb fake punk rock name that I put on a four-track tape."[\[4\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fruit_Bats_(band)#cite_note-4)

The members of Califone ran a small record label called Perishable, and after hearing some of the Fruit Bats recordings, agreed to release a full length album. The subsequent album, _Echolocation_, was produced by [Brian Deck](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brian_Deck "Brian Deck") and released in the fall of 2001. Fruit Bats made their live debut at Chicago's [Empty Bottle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Empty_Bottle "Empty Bottle") in March 2001.[\[5\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fruit_Bats_(band)#cite_note-5) They went on to tour frequently in 2001 and 2002, often as a support act for such bands as [The Sadies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Sadies "The Sadies"), [Gorky's Zygotic Mynci](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gorky's_Zygotic_Mynci "Gorky's Zygotic Mynci"), [Modest Mouse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Modest_Mouse "Modest Mouse") and [The Shins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Shins "The Shins").

### Sub Pop years

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Fruit_Bats_(band)&action=edit&section=3 "Edit section: Sub Pop years")\]

In 2002, Fruit Bats signed with [Sub Pop Records](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sub_Pop_Records "Sub Pop Records"). It was through friendships Johnson had developed with members of [Modest Mouse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Modest_Mouse "Modest Mouse") and [The Shins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Shins "The Shins") that he was able to get an introduction with the label.[\[3\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fruit_Bats_(band)#cite_note-auto1-3) Fruit Bats' Sub Pop debut, _Mouthfuls_, was released in April 2003. The band continued to tour regularly, traveling with label-mates [Iron and Wine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iron_and_Wine "Iron and Wine") and [Holopaw](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holopaw "Holopaw"), as well as playing some headlining dates. That album cycle also saw the band make their major festival debut at Washington state's [Sasquatch! Music Festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sasquatch!_Music_Festival "Sasquatch! Music Festival") in 2004. _Mouthfuls_’ closing track "When U Love Somebody," of which Johnson has said "I wrote it in the time it takes to sing it" has become a signature song for the band.[\[3\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fruit_Bats_(band)#cite_note-auto1-3) It was featured in the film [Youth in Revolt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Youth_in_Revolt "Youth in Revolt"), and has been covered by bands such as [The Decemberists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Decemberists "The Decemberists"),[\[6\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fruit_Bats_(band)#cite_note-6) [Guster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guster "Guster") and [Said the Whale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Said_the_Whale "Said the Whale").

In 2005, Fruit Bats released their second album for Sub Pop, _Spelled in Bones_. That same year they made their network television debut on _[Last Call with Carson Daly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Last_Call_with_Carson_Daly "Last Call with Carson Daly")_. They also embarked on a co-headline tour with label-mates [Rogue Wave](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rogue_Wave_(band) "Rogue Wave (band)"), and supported acts such as [Son Volt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Son_Volt "Son Volt"), [Ray Lamontagne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ray_Lamontagne "Ray Lamontagne"), and Guster, and also appeared for a performance at [Sundance Film Festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sundance_Film_Festival "Sundance Film Festival").

Johnson became a member of The Shins in late 2006 and toured with that band until 2010.[\[3\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fruit_Bats_(band)#cite_note-auto1-3) He has described his time in that band as "a total game changer" and influential on Fruit Bats' work creatively and professionally.[\[7\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fruit_Bats_(band)#cite_note-7)

In 2009, after a three-year break while Johnson toured with The Shins, Fruit Bats released the album _The Ruminant Band_. This album marked a sonic departure for the band, with a ragged, guitar heavy folk-rock sound. Johnson described this album "a better use of my influences" and has also stated the album was a creative and stylistic watershed for him.[\[8\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fruit_Bats_(band)#cite_note-8) The album was also something of a critical breakthrough for Fruit Bats, getting positive reviews from [Pitchfork](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pitchfork_(website) "Pitchfork (website)"), _[The A.V. Club](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_A.V._Club "The A.V. Club")_ and [Robert Christgau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Christgau "Robert Christgau").[\[9\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fruit_Bats_(band)#cite_note-9) The band also made another TV appearance, this time on _[The Late Late Show with Craig Ferguson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Late_Late_Show_with_Craig_Ferguson "The Late Late Show with Craig Ferguson")_. It was also on this album cycle that the band made the first of many appearances at Portland's [Pickathon Music Festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pickathon "Pickathon").[\[10\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fruit_Bats_(band)#cite_note-10)

Fruit Bats' final album for Sub Pop, _Tripper_, came out in August 2011. It was the first of several releases by the band produced by [Thom Monahan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thom_Monahan "Thom Monahan"). It featured appearances by [J. Mascis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/J._Mascis "J. Mascis"), [Neal Casal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neal_Casal "Neal Casal") and [Richard Swift](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_Swift_(musician) "Richard Swift (musician)"), and made it to the top 15 of the [_Billboard_](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Billboard_(magazine) "Billboard (magazine)") Heatseekers chart, the first Fruit Bats record to make that list.[\[11\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fruit_Bats_(band)#cite_note-11) The album's single "You’re Too Weird" is often remembered for its very 1980s music video, in which Johnson plays a heavily styled version of himself.[\[12\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fruit_Bats_(band)#cite_note-12) _Tripper_'s subsequent tours would see Fruit Bats make appearances at [Bonnaroo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bonnaroo "Bonnaroo") and [The Dave Matthews Band Caravan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dave_Matthews_Band_Caravan "Dave Matthews Band Caravan").

Fruit Bats were featured on the 2011 soundtrack for the [Jesse Peretz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jesse_Peretz "Jesse Peretz") film _[Our Idiot Brother](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Our_Idiot_Brother "Our Idiot Brother")_, starring [Paul Rudd](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_Rudd "Paul Rudd"). Johnson served as the soundtrack's producer, and also composed the score for the film.[\[13\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fruit_Bats_(band)#cite_note-13)

In November 2013, Johnson announced he would be dissolving the Fruit Bats. That month they played what was to be their farewell show at a sold out Aladdin Theatre in Portland, Oregon.[\[14\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fruit_Bats_(band)#cite_note-14) The following year Johnson released an album under the name EDJ, which he has subsequently described as "essentially a Fruit Bats album."[\[15\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fruit_Bats_(band)#cite_note-15)

### Reformation 2015–2018

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Fruit_Bats_(band)&action=edit&section=4 "Edit section: Reformation 2015–2018")\]

Johnson had described the retirement of the Fruit Bats name as "a career-stalling move"[\[3\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fruit_Bats_(band)#cite_note-auto1-3) and in 2015 announced on Instagram that he was reviving the band, declaring "I miss these old songs" via a handwritten letter.[\[16\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fruit_Bats_(band)#cite_note-16) That same year [My Morning Jacket](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/My_Morning_Jacket "My Morning Jacket") had invited Johnson's EDJ project to open several tour dates. Johnson instead asked if he could bring the reformed Fruit Bats instead. The tour was a major breakthrough for the band as a live act.[\[3\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fruit_Bats_(band)#cite_note-auto1-3)

In 2016, Fruit Bats released their sixth official full-length LP, _Absolute Loser_, via newly formed Los Angeles–based label Easy Sound. It was the band's first release in five years. The album featured the single "Humbug Mountain Song," which has garnered over fifty-nine million streams on Spotify.[\[17\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fruit_Bats_(band)#cite_note-17) The accompanying comedic music video for that song featured Johnson as a modern-day frontiersman fighting a bear.[\[18\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fruit_Bats_(band)#cite_note-18) The band's tours on the album featured performances at [Nelsonville Music Festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nelsonville_Music_Festival "Nelsonville Music Festival"), [Green Man Festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Green_Man_Festival "Green Man Festival"), [Newport Folk Festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Newport_Folk_Festival "Newport Folk Festival"), and Pickathon. _Absolute Loser_ was voted as one of the best albums of 2016 by _[Paste](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paste_(magazine) "Paste (magazine)")_ magazine.[\[19\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fruit_Bats_(band)#cite_note-19)

### Merge Records and breakthrough

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Fruit_Bats_(band)&action=edit&section=5 "Edit section: Merge Records and breakthrough")\]

Fruit Bats signed to North Carolina–based independent label [Merge Records](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Merge_Records "Merge Records") in 2018.[\[20\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fruit_Bats_(band)#cite_note-20)

Also in 2018, Johnson formed the folk group [Bonny Light Horseman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bonny_Light_Horseman_(band) "Bonny Light Horseman (band)") with [Josh Kaufman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Josh_Kaufman_(musician) "Josh Kaufman (musician)") and [Anais Mitchell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anais_Mitchell "Anais Mitchell"). Their subsequent debut album was nominated for two [Grammy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grammy "Grammy") Awards.[\[21\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fruit_Bats_(band)#cite_note-21)

In 2019, Fruit Bats' Merge debut _Gold Past Life_ was released. It charted on Billboard's Heatseeker and Independent album charts. It features several songs which have become streaming hits and fan favorites, including "The Bottom of It," "A Lingering Love," and "Cazadera." It was the last appearance on a Fruit Bats record by Neal Casal before his death.[\[22\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fruit_Bats_(band)#cite_note-22) Of the album, _[Variety](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Variety_(magazine) "Variety (magazine)")_ magazine said, "there won’t be many albums in 2019 with as many quotable, cut-to-the-marrow lines as this one has."[\[23\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fruit_Bats_(band)#cite_note-23) _Gold Past Life_ was voted the seventh best album of 2019 by [No Depression](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/No_Depression_(magazine) "No Depression (magazine)") magazine.[\[24\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fruit_Bats_(band)#cite_note-24)

In 2020, Fruit Bats released a full-album cover of [The Smashing Pumpkins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Smashing_Pumpkins "The Smashing Pumpkins")' 1993 record _[Siamese Dream](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siamese_Dream "Siamese Dream")_. Johnson recorded the album at his home in Los Angeles, playing every instrument himself.[\[25\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fruit_Bats_(band)#cite_note-25)

Fruit Bats' 2021 album _The Pet Parade_ was produced by Johnson's Bonny Light Horseman bandmate Josh Kaufman and recorded remotely in different cities in the early days of the COVID-19 pandemic. The title of the album is a reference to an actual pet parade that Johnson used to attend as a child in [La Grange, Illinois](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/La_Grange,_Illinois "La Grange, Illinois")[\[26\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fruit_Bats_(band)#cite_note-26)—and a metaphor for the "beauty and absurdity of existence."[\[27\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fruit_Bats_(band)#cite_note-27) The album was nominated for Best Americana Album at the 2021 [Libera Awards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Libera_Awards "Libera Awards").[\[28\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fruit_Bats_(band)#cite_note-28)

The video for _The Pet Parade_'s single "The Balcony", which features a cast composed entirely of puppets, was co-directed by Johnson—his directorial debut—and his wife, Annie Beedy. The song was one of the most-played singles of 2021 on [SiriusXMU](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SiriusXMU "SiriusXMU") radio,[\[29\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fruit_Bats_(band)#cite_note-29) and that year Fruit Bats made their first network television appearance since 2009 performing it on _[Late Night with Seth Meyers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Late_Night_with_Seth_Meyers "Late Night with Seth Meyers")_.[\[30\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fruit_Bats_(band)#cite_note-30)

Fruit Bats' double-disc, 20-year career retrospective _Sometimes A Cloud Is Just a Cloud: Slow Growers, Sleeper Hits and Lost Songs_ was released in 2022. The first disc featured a curated assortment of popular songs from through the years, and the second was filled with B-sides, unreleased singles, and some of the early four track recordings from the 1990s.[\[31\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fruit_Bats_(band)#cite_note-31) The collection's title comes from a lyric in the 2019 song "Cazadera".

Fruit Bats toured extensively in 2022 after the COVID-19 pandemic derailed plans for 2020 and 2021 dates.[\[32\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fruit_Bats_(band)#cite_note-32) They kicked off the year of touring reuniting with My Morning Jacket at that band's One Big Holiday festival in [Cancún](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canc%C3%BAn "Cancún"), Mexico.[\[33\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fruit_Bats_(band)#cite_note-33) That year also saw Fruit Bats embark on their first proper tour of the United Kingdom since 2011, including an appearance at [End of the Road Festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/End_of_the_Road_Festival "End of the Road Festival").[\[34\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fruit_Bats_(band)#cite_note-34)

"Humbug Mountain Song" was featured in an episode of _[Better Things](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Better_Things_(TV_series) "Better Things (TV series)")_ in April 2022.[\[35\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fruit_Bats_(band)#cite_note-35)

## Musical style and influences

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Fruit_Bats_(band)&action=edit&section=6 "Edit section: Musical style and influences")\]

Fruit Bats sound has changed over the years, ranging from lo-fi indie Americana on _Echolocation_[\[36\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fruit_Bats_(band)#cite_note-36) to the often disco-influenced sounds of _Gold Past Life_.[\[37\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fruit_Bats_(band)#cite_note-37)

Johnson has often cited Top 40 radio of the 1970s and 1980s as influences.[\[38\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fruit_Bats_(band)#cite_note-38) Other professed early songwriting influences include [The Velvet Underground](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Velvet_Underground "The Velvet Underground"), [Uncle Tupelo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uncle_Tupelo "Uncle Tupelo"), [Big Star](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Big_Star "Big Star"), [the Beatles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Beatles "The Beatles")' [White Album](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/White_Album "White Album"),[\[39\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fruit_Bats_(band)#cite_note-auto-39) [Joni Mitchell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joni_Mitchell "Joni Mitchell"), and [Talk Talk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Talk_Talk "Talk Talk").[\[40\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fruit_Bats_(band)#cite_note-40) Johnson has described discovering the burgeoning mid-90s indie rock scene in his late teens, a discovery he would refer to as "the light at the end of the tunnel."[\[39\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fruit_Bats_(band)#cite_note-auto-39) He went on to say, "basically I kinda became a singer/songwriter right out of the gate, but not by choice. I was a [Deadhead](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deadhead "Deadhead") but not a good enough player to be in a jam band, and then indie rock came along and showed me that there were all kinds of possibilities, even if you couldn't shred."[\[41\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fruit_Bats_(band)#cite_note-41) Johnson has spoken frequently of his love for [Prince](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prince_(musician) "Prince (musician)"), whom he has referenced several times in his song lyrics.[\[42\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fruit_Bats_(band)#cite_note-42)

Johnson is a longtime fan of [The Grateful Dead](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Grateful_Dead "The Grateful Dead").[\[43\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fruit_Bats_(band)#cite_note-43) Fruit Bats have covered the songs "Wharf Rat" and "They Love Each Other" in concert.[\[44\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fruit_Bats_(band)#cite_note-44)[\[45\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fruit_Bats_(band)#cite_note-45) Johnson has appeared several times as a guest on several Grateful Dead related projects. In August 2019 he joined Joe Russo's Almost Dead for several songs at Los Angeles’ [Greek Theatre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greek_Theatre "Greek Theatre"),[\[46\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fruit_Bats_(band)#cite_note-46) and once again with that band in July 2021 at their set at Westville Music Bowl in New Haven, Connecticut.[\[47\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fruit_Bats_(band)#cite_note-47) In October 2021 he played three shows at the [Capitol Theatre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Capitol_Theatre_(Port_Chester,_New_York) "Capitol Theatre (Port Chester, New York)") in Port Chester, New York, as part of [Phil Lesh and Friends](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phil_Lesh_and_Friends "Phil Lesh and Friends").[\[48\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fruit_Bats_(band)#cite_note-48)
