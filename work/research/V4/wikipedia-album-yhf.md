_**Yankee Hotel Foxtrot**_ is the fourth [studio album](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Studio_album "Studio album") by the American [rock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rock_music "Rock music") band [Wilco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wilco "Wilco"), originally self-released on September 18, 2001, on Wilco's website as streamed audio, and later as a retail release on April 23, 2002, through [Nonesuch Records](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nonesuch_Records "Nonesuch Records"). Recording sessions for the album began in late 2000. These sessions, which were documented for the film _[I Am Trying to Break Your Heart](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/I_Am_Trying_to_Break_Your_Heart:_A_Film_About_Wilco "I Am Trying to Break Your Heart: A Film About Wilco")_, were marred by conflicts including a switch in drummers and disagreements among the band members and engineers about songs. Despite this, the album was mostly completed by early 2001. The album showcased a more atmospheric and experimental sound than the band's previous work, and has been described as [art rock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Art_rock "Art rock")[\[4\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yankee_Hotel_Foxtrot#cite_note-6)[\[5\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yankee_Hotel_Foxtrot#cite_note-7) and [indie rock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indie_rock "Indie rock")[\[6\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yankee_Hotel_Foxtrot#cite_note-8) by music critics. It was the band's first album with drummer [Glenn Kotche](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glenn_Kotche "Glenn Kotche"), and last with multi-instrumentalist and songwriter [Jay Bennett](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jay_Bennett "Jay Bennett").

[Reprise Records](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reprise_Records "Reprise Records"), Wilco's record label at the time, refused to release the album as they felt unhappy about the result; this would lead to Wilco's departure from Reprise. The band subsequently acquired the rights to the album and later [streamed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Streaming_media "Streaming media") the entire album for free on their website on September 29, 2001, 11 days after the planned release date. In November of that year, Wilco signed with [Nonesuch Records](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nonesuch_Records "Nonesuch Records"), who gave the album its first official retail release on April 23, 2002. _Yankee Hotel Foxtrot_ received widespread acclaim from music critics at release, and is widely regarded as one of the greatest albums of the 2000s and of all time.[\[7\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yankee_Hotel_Foxtrot#cite_note-9) It is also Wilco's best-selling work, having reached number 13 on the [_Billboard_ 200](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Billboard_200 "Billboard 200") chart.[\[8\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yankee_Hotel_Foxtrot#cite_note-10) In 2020, _[Rolling Stone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rolling_Stone "Rolling Stone")_ ranked the album at number 225 on its updated " [500 Greatest Albums of All Time](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rolling_Stone's_500_Greatest_Albums_of_All_Time "Rolling Stone's 500 Greatest Albums of All Time")" list.[\[9\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yankee_Hotel_Foxtrot#cite_note-rollingstone.com-11)

[Wilco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wilco "Wilco") was touring to promote _[Mermaid Avenue Vol. II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mermaid_Avenue_Vol._II "Mermaid Avenue Vol. II")_ in May 2000 when lead singer [Jeff Tweedy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jeff_Tweedy "Jeff Tweedy") was invited to play at the [Noise Pop Festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Noise_Pop_Festival "Noise Pop Festival") in [Chicago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chicago "Chicago"). The festival promoter offered to pair Tweedy with a collaborator of his choosing, and Tweedy decided to perform with [Jim O'Rourke](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jim_O'Rourke_(musician) "Jim O'Rourke (musician)"). Tweedy frequently played O'Rourke's album _[Bad Timing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bad_Timing_(album) "Bad Timing (album)")_ in his car while he traveled during the previous winter. O'Rourke was an accomplished producer as well as a musician, and had produced over 200 albums by the time that Tweedy requested the collaboration. O'Rourke offered the services of drummer [Glenn Kotche](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glenn_Kotche "Glenn Kotche"), and the trio performed at [Double Door](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Double_Door "Double Door") for the festival on May 14, 2000. Tweedy enjoyed the performance so much that he suggested that the trio record an album together. They chose the name [Loose Fur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Loose_Fur "Loose Fur"), and recorded six songs during the following summer.[\[11\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yankee_Hotel_Foxtrot#cite_note-FOOTNOTEKot2004176%E2%80%93184-13)

By the end of the year, Wilco had recorded enough demo tracks to release a fourth studio album. Its working title was _Here Comes Everybody_, which was later scrapped in favour of _Yankee Hotel Foxtrot_. Australian band [Spacey Jane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spacey_Jane "Spacey Jane") later released an album titled _[Here Comes Everybody](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Here_Comes_Everybody_(album) "Here Comes Everybody (album)")_ in 2022 in tribute to the working title, for which they sought approval from Wilco prior to release.[\[12\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yankee_Hotel_Foxtrot#cite_note-14) Despite having enough songs for an album, the band was unhappy with some of the demo takes. This was attributed to the inflexibility of [Ken Coomer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ken_Coomer "Ken Coomer")'s drumming. According to _[American Songwriter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Songwriter "American Songwriter")_, "virtually every attempt \[Tweedy\] made to steer Coomer toward the percussive sound he had envisioned for the record sparked a fight."[\[13\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yankee_Hotel_Foxtrot#cite_note-15) The band decided to bring Kotche into the studio to record with the band. Wilco officially replaced Coomer with Kotche in January 2001, a decision originally proposed by Tweedy and almost immediately approved by the rest of the band.[\[14\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yankee_Hotel_Foxtrot#cite_note-FOOTNOTEKot2004185%E2%80%93188-16)

[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/5/5f/Marina_City%2C_Chicago%2C_Illinois%2C_Estados_Unidos%2C_2012-10-20%2C_DD_01.jpg/250px-Marina_City%2C_Chicago%2C_Illinois%2C_Estados_Unidos%2C_2012-10-20%2C_DD_01.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Marina_City,_Chicago,_Illinois,_Estados_Unidos,_2012-10-20,_DD_01.jpg) The [Marina City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marina_City "Marina City") complex on the [Chicago River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chicago_River "Chicago River"). The album cover features a photograph of the two towers.

Guitarist [Jay Bennett](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jay_Bennett "Jay Bennett") and Chris Brickley served as the audio engineers, and agreed with Tweedy that O'Rourke would be a good choice to [mix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Audio_mixing_(recorded_music) "Audio mixing (recorded music)") the album, after a failed attempt to mix a few of the songs at the [Chicago Recording Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chicago_Recording_Company "Chicago Recording Company") and after hearing O'Rourke's "audition mix". One of the conflicts, exhibited in the film _[I Am Trying to Break Your Heart: A Film About Wilco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/I_Am_Trying_to_Break_Your_Heart:_A_Film_About_Wilco "I Am Trying to Break Your Heart: A Film About Wilco")_, was over the ten-second transition between "Ashes of American Flags" and " [Heavy Metal Drummer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heavy_Metal_Drummer "Heavy Metal Drummer")". Bennett attempted to explain to Tweedy that there were several slightly different ways to approach the transition, each of which would yield slightly different results, but Tweedy explained that he just wanted the problem fixed, and was not concerned with understanding the different approaches. Bennett focused on the individual songs, while Tweedy focused on larger conceptual and thematic issues—a tried and true division of labor that had worked well on the four releases on which they co-wrote the material.[\[15\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yankee_Hotel_Foxtrot#cite_note-film-17)

In order to achieve the band's musical goals, Tweedy invited Jim O'Rourke into the studio to mix "I Am Trying to Break Your Heart", and the results impressed the band members. He recorded 6 of the 12 tracks on the album. O'Rourke was then asked to mix the rest of the album.[\[16\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yankee_Hotel_Foxtrot#cite_note-FOOTNOTEKot2004195%E2%80%93199-18) After the album's completion, Tweedy decided to remove Bennett from the band. The album was completed in 2001, and Tweedy believed it to be ready for release.[\[17\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yankee_Hotel_Foxtrot#cite_note-FOOTNOTEKot2004199%E2%80%93200-19) The album was given the title _Yankee Hotel Foxtrot_, referencing a series of letters in the [phonetic alphabet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NATO_phonetic_alphabet "NATO phonetic alphabet") that Tweedy had heard on the Irdial box set _[The Conet Project: Recordings of Shortwave Numbers Stations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Conet_Project:_Recordings_of_Shortwave_Numbers_Stations "The Conet Project: Recordings of Shortwave Numbers Stations")_. A clip from this [Numbers Station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Numbers_Station "Numbers Station") transmission was placed in the _Yankee Hotel Foxtrot_ song "Poor Places"; Irdial later sued Wilco for [copyright infringement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Copyright_infringement "Copyright infringement"), and a settlement was reached out of court.[\[18\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yankee_Hotel_Foxtrot#cite_note-20) The cover of the album is a picture of the two [Marina City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marina_City "Marina City") complex towers in the band's adopted hometown of [Chicago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chicago "Chicago").

[Los Angeles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Los_Angeles "Los Angeles") photographer [Sam Jones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sam_Jones_(Photographer/Director) "Sam Jones (Photographer/Director)") contacted Wilco in 2000 about producing a [documentary film](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Documentary_film "Documentary film") about the creation of _Yankee Hotel Foxtrot_. Jones shot over 80 hours of footage for _I Am Trying to Break Your Heart: A Film About Wilco_ beginning on the day that Coomer was dismissed from the band. The footage was edited down to 92 minutes, and the film was released to theaters in 2002.[\[19\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yankee_Hotel_Foxtrot#cite_note-Liner_notes-21) The documentary has received generally positive reviews.[\[20\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yankee_Hotel_Foxtrot#cite_note-22)

## Release

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Yankee_Hotel_Foxtrot&action=edit&section=2 "Edit section: Release")\]

### Original release

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Yankee_Hotel_Foxtrot&action=edit&section=3 "Edit section: Original release")\]

In 2001, [AOL](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AOL "AOL") merged with [Time Warner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Time_Warner "Time Warner") to form AOL Time Warner. Time Warner's market share of the music industry had dropped almost five percent from the mid-1990s, and the new executives ordered the termination of 600 jobs. One of those jobs was [Reprise Records](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reprise_Records "Reprise Records") president [Howie Klein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Howie_Klein "Howie Klein"), who had been a big supporter of Wilco on the label. Klein's dismissal placed head [A&R](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Artist_and_repertoire "Artist and repertoire") representative [David Kahne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Kahne "David Kahne") in charge of the decision whether to release _Yankee Hotel Foxtrot_. Kahne assigned A&R representative Mio Vukovic to monitor the progress of the album. Vukovic was unhappy about the album because he felt that his suggestions were not being considered. Kahne wanted a radio single from the album, but he felt that none of the songs were suitable for commercial release. In June 2001, the album was officially rejected and Vukovic suggested that the band independently release the album.[\[21\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yankee_Hotel_Foxtrot#cite_note-FOOTNOTEKot2004201%E2%80%93206-23)

Josh Grier, Wilco's lawyer, was able to negotiate a buy-out of the band from Reprise. The band would keep the rights to the album if they paid Reprise $50,000. Before Wilco could accept the deal, Reprise called the band and changed their offer to give the band the rights to _Yankee Hotel Foxtrot_ for free. Despite Reprise's efforts to accommodate Wilco's departure, the process marred public relations after an article in the _[Chicago Tribune](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chicago_Tribune "Chicago Tribune")_ described what had happened.[\[22\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yankee_Hotel_Foxtrot#cite_note-24)

Wilco had planned on releasing _Yankee Hotel Foxtrot_ on September 11, 2001, but Tweedy did not want a change in record labels to significantly delay the release of the album. Within weeks of being released from the label and Jay Bennett leaving the band, MP3s of all tracks from the album began to appear on file sharing networks. In a decision aimed at discouraging the downloading of lower quality unlicensed [MP3s](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MP3 "MP3") and having some control over how the album was distributed, on September 29, 2001, Wilco began streaming the entirety of the album on their official website.[\[1\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yankee_Hotel_Foxtrot#cite_note-SU-RELEASE-1) The wilcoworld.net website registered over 50,000 hits that day, eight times as much as typical daily traffic.[\[23\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yankee_Hotel_Foxtrot#cite_note-25) Traffic to the website quadrupled the normal traffic over the next few months. The following tour was a success financially, and members of Wilco observed that fans sang along with songs from the unreleased album.[\[24\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yankee_Hotel_Foxtrot#cite_note-FOOTNOTEKot2004223%E2%80%93226-26)

### Retail release

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Yankee_Hotel_Foxtrot&action=edit&section=4 "Edit section: Retail release")\]

Both major and independent record labels made bids to release _Yankee Hotel Foxtrot_, including [Artemis Records](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Artemis_Records "Artemis Records") and [Nonesuch Records](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nonesuch_Records "Nonesuch Records"). Tweedy denied the bids of record labels that did not have a roster of signed artists that he liked. He also decided to ignore small, independent labels because he wanted to be able to put the album out for a large audience and felt that very small labels would be unable to produce more than 100,000 copies. Wilco decided to sign with [Warner Music](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warner_Music_Group "Warner Music Group") subsidiary Nonesuch Records in November 2001, basing the decision on the label's affiliation with a large company, but also Nonesuch's artist-friendly atmosphere. In the end, Wilco recorded and produced _Yankee Hotel Foxtrot_ with Reprise Records (a Warner label), received the rights to the album from Reprise for free, and then had it released by a different Warner label, Nonesuch Records.[\[25\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yankee_Hotel_Foxtrot#cite_note-FOOTNOTEKot2004227%E2%80%93228-27)

The _[More Like the Moon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/More_Like_the_Moon "More Like the Moon")_ EP (also called _Bridge_ and _Australian_ EP) was originally released as a bonus disc to the Australian version of _Yankee Hotel Foxtrot_. The EP comprised six songs that were recorded but not released during the _Yankee Hotel Foxtrot_ sessions including a re-working of "Kamera". On the one-year anniversary of the release of _Yankee Hotel Foxtrot_, Wilco uploaded the EP onto their official website, and offered it for free to anyone who purchased the album. The band would later allow anyone to download the EP for free off the website, regardless of whether they had purchased the full-length album.[\[26\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yankee_Hotel_Foxtrot#cite_note-FOOTNOTEKot2004237-28)[\[27\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yankee_Hotel_Foxtrot#cite_note-29)

### 20th anniversary reissue

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Yankee_Hotel_Foxtrot&action=edit&section=5 "Edit section: 20th anniversary reissue")\]

In celebration of the 20th anniversary of the album's release, Wilco performed the album in its entirety for four nights at the [United Palace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Palace "United Palace") in New York City and three nights at the [Auditorium Theater](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Auditorium_Theater "Auditorium Theater") in Chicago. The band was joined by strings and horns sections in an attempt to perform the album as it was recorded. The band also announced reissues of the album, including a Super Deluxe version spanning 11 LPs which includes the original remastered album, demos, rare live recordings, and other alternate versions of the songs. These reissues were released on September 30, 2022. Pitchfork gave the reissue a perfect 10 rating, and naming it "Best New Reissue".[\[28\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yankee_Hotel_Foxtrot#cite_note-30)

Professional ratings

_Yankee Hotel Foxtrot_ was acclaimed by critics upon release.[\[29\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yankee_Hotel_Foxtrot#cite_note-Metacritic-31) Brent Sirota of _[Pitchfork](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pitchfork_(website) "Pitchfork (website)")_ gave the album a [perfect 10 rating](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_albums_awarded_10_by_Pitchfork "List of albums awarded 10 by Pitchfork"), noting that the album was "simply a masterpiece."[\[35\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yankee_Hotel_Foxtrot#cite_note-PitchforkReview-37) [David Fricke](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Fricke "David Fricke") of _[Rolling Stone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rolling_Stone "Rolling Stone")_ praised its resemblance to [psychedelia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Psychedelia "Psychedelia") while [AllMusic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AllMusic "AllMusic") writer Zac Johnson lauded its musical "complexity".[\[30\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yankee_Hotel_Foxtrot#cite_note-AllmusicReview-32)[\[37\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yankee_Hotel_Foxtrot#cite_note-RollingStoneReview-39) [E! Online](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/E! "E!") said that its "rich, exotic flavor gets more intense the longer you chew on it",[\[29\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yankee_Hotel_Foxtrot#cite_note-Metacritic-31) while _[Stylus Magazine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stylus_Magazine "Stylus Magazine")_ called it "a great album, and an outstanding place for prospective new Wilco fans to start."[\[40\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yankee_Hotel_Foxtrot#cite_note-StylusReview-42) _[Billboard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Billboard_(magazine) "Billboard (magazine)")_ gave it a favorable review and called it "a more adventurous and rewarding release".[\[41\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yankee_Hotel_Foxtrot#cite_note-BillboardReview-43) _[The Austin Chronicle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Austin_Chronicle "The Austin Chronicle")_ gave it four stars out of five and said that "After a while -- a familiarity period if you will -- it becomes clear that these songs are not only fully realized, they're damn near brilliant."[\[42\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yankee_Hotel_Foxtrot#cite_note-AustinChronicleReview-44) _[Playlouder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Playlouder "Playlouder")_ gave it four stars out of five and called it "The most worth-the-wait long-awaited album in the world... ever? Could be..."[\[43\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yankee_Hotel_Foxtrot#cite_note-45) _[Uncut](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uncut_(magazine) "Uncut (magazine)")_ also gave the album four stars out of five and said, "The most common description of this much-discussed album over the past few months is that YHF is Americana's _[Kid A](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kid_A "Kid A")_. In truth, it's more successful than that."[\[29\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yankee_Hotel_Foxtrot#cite_note-Metacritic-31) _[Blender](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blender_(magazine) "Blender (magazine)")_ likewise gave it four stars out of five and stated: "Tweedy whittles down the arrangements and drops in enough experimental nuances to make the whole thing sound refreshingly lo-fi."[\[29\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yankee_Hotel_Foxtrot#cite_note-Metacritic-31) _[Q](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Q_(magazine) "Q (magazine)")_ likewise gave it four stars and called it "battered, bonkers and bewitching in equal parts" and that it "at last finds Wilco's 'interesting' phase become downright fascinating."[\[29\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yankee_Hotel_Foxtrot#cite_note-Metacritic-31) [Yahoo! Music UK](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yahoo!_Music "Yahoo! Music") gave it eight stars out of ten and said, "Tweedy takes conventional songforms birthed on his acoustic guitar and scrambles them completely, reassembled into fractured, dissonant epics with the help of the reliably brilliant Jim O'Rourke."[\[44\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yankee_Hotel_Foxtrot#cite_note-YahooMusicUKReview-46)

_[Trouser Press](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trouser_Press "Trouser Press")_ was one of the few major media outlets that did not give the album a good review, instead giving it an average review and stating that "more time spent in the songwriting lab might have yielded material more suitable to the evident studio effort invested and brought Wilco closer to making a truly great album."[\[45\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yankee_Hotel_Foxtrot#cite_note-47) [Robert Christgau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Christgau "Robert Christgau") gave the album a one-star honorable mention rating, describing the music as "purty" but stating that he found the lyrics and vocals in general to be boring.[\[46\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yankee_Hotel_Foxtrot#cite_note-ChristgauReview-48)

Though _Yankee Hotel Foxtrot_ was recorded before the [September 11, 2001 attacks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/September_11,_2001_attacks "September 11, 2001 attacks"), critics noted a relevance in light of the attacks. For example, Jeff Gordinier of _[Entertainment Weekly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Entertainment_Weekly "Entertainment Weekly")_ compared the two towers of [Marina City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marina_City "Marina City") to the [World Trade Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Trade_Center_(1973%E2%80%932001) "World Trade Center (1973–2001)") towers.[\[32\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yankee_Hotel_Foxtrot#cite_note-EWReview-34) Also containing similar themes are the songs "War on War" and "Ashes of American Flags" which contains the line "I would like to salute the ashes of American flags." The song "Jesus, Etc." also contains these lyrics:
"Tall buildings shake, Voices escape singing sad sad songs... Voices whine, Skyscrapers are scraping together, your voice is smoking."[\[47\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yankee_Hotel_Foxtrot#cite_note-49)

### Commercial

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Yankee_Hotel_Foxtrot&action=edit&section=8 "Edit section: Commercial")\]

_Yankee Hotel Foxtrot_ was released by Nonesuch on April 23, 2002. The album sold 55,573 copies during its first week of release, peaking on the US [_Billboard_ 200](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Billboard_200 "Billboard 200") album chart at number 13.[\[48\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yankee_Hotel_Foxtrot#cite_note-50)[\[49\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yankee_Hotel_Foxtrot#cite_note-FOOTNOTEKot2004228%E2%80%93229-51) The album was [certified Gold](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RIAA_certification "RIAA certification") by the [Recording Industry Association of America](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Recording_Industry_Association_of_America "Recording Industry Association of America") and has sold over 590,000 units.[\[50\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yankee_Hotel_Foxtrot#cite_note-52)[\[51\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yankee_Hotel_Foxtrot#cite_note-53)

### Accolades

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Yankee_Hotel_Foxtrot&action=edit&section=9 "Edit section: Accolades")\]

_Yankee Hotel Foxtrot_ was voted as the best album of the year in _[The Village Voice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Village_Voice "The Village Voice")_ [Pazz & Jop](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pazz_&_Jop "Pazz & Jop") critics poll.[\[52\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yankee_Hotel_Foxtrot#cite_note-54) In 2008, _Rolling Stone_ critic Tom Moon listed _Yankee Hotel Foxtrot_ among the _[1,000 Recordings to Hear Before You Die](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1,000_Recordings_to_Hear_Before_You_Die "1,000 Recordings to Hear Before You Die")_. The album was also included in the book _[1001 Albums You Must Hear Before You Die](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1001_Albums_You_Must_Hear_Before_You_Die "1001 Albums You Must Hear Before You Die")_.[\[53\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yankee_Hotel_Foxtrot#cite_note-55)

_Yankee Hotel Foxtrot_ found a place on many lists of the greatest albums of the 2000s. _Rolling Stone_ ranked the album at number three on its list of the 100 Best Albums of the Decade.[\[54\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yankee_Hotel_Foxtrot#cite_note-56) _[Pitchfork](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pitchfork_(website) "Pitchfork (website)")_ put the album at number four on the Top 200 Albums of the 2000s.[\[55\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yankee_Hotel_Foxtrot#cite_note-57) The alternative music website also named "Poor Places" and "Jesus, Etc." as the 147th and 61st best songs of the decade, respectively.[\[56\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yankee_Hotel_Foxtrot#cite_note-58) _[Paste](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paste_(magazine) "Paste (magazine)")_ named the album the second-best album of the decade.[\[57\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yankee_Hotel_Foxtrot#cite_note-59) Some music outlets have ranked _Yankee Hotel Foxtrot_ as one of the greatest albums of all time. In 2006, readers of _[Q Magazine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Q_(magazine) "Q (magazine)")_ voted it the 100th "Greatest Album Ever".[\[58\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yankee_Hotel_Foxtrot#cite_note-60) In 2012, _[Rolling Stone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rolling_Stone "Rolling Stone")_ ranked it #493 on its list of [The 500 Greatest Albums of All Time](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rolling_Stone's_500_Greatest_Albums_of_All_Time "Rolling Stone's 500 Greatest Albums of All Time"), saying, "Wilco's great leap forward was a mix of rock tradition, electronics, oddball rhythms and experimental gestures.",[\[59\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yankee_Hotel_Foxtrot#cite_note-61) and in the 2020 reboot of the list, they elevated the album's position to #225.[\[9\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yankee_Hotel_Foxtrot#cite_note-rollingstone.com-11)
