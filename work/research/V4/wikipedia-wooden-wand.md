**Wooden Wand** is the stage name of singer-songwriter **James Jackson Toth**,[\[1\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wooden_Wand#cite_note-Stafford-1)[\[2\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wooden_Wand#cite_note-Loftus-2) who has recorded under his given name as well as the name WAND. The style of music recorded by Toth and his many incarnations has drawn on a variety of both conventional and experimental folk and rock influences, including [psychedelic folk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Psychedelic_folk "Psychedelic folk"), [freak folk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Freak_folk "Freak folk") and [indie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Independent_music "Independent music").\[ _[citation needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed "Wikipedia:Citation needed")_\] Though he was a significant player in the [New Weird America](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Weird_America "New Weird America") trend of the early to mid-2000s along with [Devendra Banhart](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Devendra_Banhart "Devendra Banhart"), [Akron/Family](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Akron/Family "Akron/Family"), [Joanna Newsom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joanna_Newsom "Joanna Newsom"), and collaborators The Vanishing Voice, Toth has been difficult to pigeonhole in one genre; recent releases have been identified as acid folk, [free jazz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Free_jazz "Free jazz"), [outlaw country](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Outlaw_country "Outlaw country"), and [country-tinged rock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Country_rock "Country rock").\[ _[citation needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed "Wikipedia:Citation needed")_\] Toth has appeared on labels including [Kill Rock Stars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kill_Rock_Stars "Kill Rock Stars"), [Ecstatic Peace!](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ecstatic_Peace! "Ecstatic Peace!"), [Rykodisc](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rykodisc "Rykodisc"), and [Young God](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Young_God_Records "Young God Records").

Wooden Wand's collaborations have been nearly as wandering and nomadic as Toth himself, a New York native who attended [Purchase College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SUNY_Purchase "SUNY Purchase") before relocating to [Knoxville, Tennessee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Knoxville,_Tennessee "Knoxville, Tennessee"), [Murfreesboro, Tennessee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Murfreesboro,_Tennessee "Murfreesboro, Tennessee"), and most recently [Lexington, Kentucky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lexington,_Kentucky "Lexington, Kentucky"). He has recorded with the Vanishing Voice (including ex-wife Jessica Bowen and other bandmates Jarvis Taniere and G. Lucas Crane, who went on to form ( [Jex Thoth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jex_Thoth "Jex Thoth")) and ( [Woods](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Woods_(band) "Woods (band)")), respectively, and Heidi Diehl), the Sky High Band, the Omen Bones Band, and the Briarwood Virgins (a [Birmingham, Alabama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Birmingham,_Alabama "Birmingham, Alabama") supergroup featuring members of the Through the Sparks, Plate Six, Delicate Cutters, and Verbena). In January 2013, Fire Records in the UK released Wooden Wand's latest record _[Blood Oaths of the New Blues](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blood_Oaths_of_the_New_Blues "Blood Oaths of the New Blues")_, which was the second album recorded with the Briarwood Virgins band in Birmingham in February 2012.

Swans frontman and head of Young God Records Michael Gira stated, James Jackson Toth's “got that picaresque quality that Dylan had in his heyday, wherein the shambolic narrator undergoes various travails and epiphanies — harrowing, bleak and darkly comical — in the course of a narrative, then leaves you mystified, both smiling and sad.”\[ _[citation needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed "Wikipedia:Citation needed")_\]
