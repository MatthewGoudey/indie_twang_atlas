**Great Lake Swimmers** is a Canadian [folk rock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Folk_rock "Folk rock") band from [Wainfleet, Ontario](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wainfleet,_Ontario "Wainfleet, Ontario"),[\[1\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Lake_Swimmers#cite_note-1) and currently based in [Toronto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toronto "Toronto").

The current touring line-up is [Tony Dekker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tony_Dekker "Tony Dekker") on lead vocals, acoustic guitar and harmonica, Erik Arnesen on banjo, electric guitar and [harmonium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pump_organ "Pump organ"), Ryan Granville-Martin on drums and backing vocals, Bret Higgins on [upright bass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Upright_bass "Upright bass") and Kelsey McNulty on keyboards and backing vocals. Past members included [Julie Fader](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Julie_Fader "Julie Fader") on backing vocals, [Sandro Perri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sandro_Perri "Sandro Perri") on guitar, and Greg Millson and Colin Huebert on drums.

The band's style has been compared to [Red House Painters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Red_House_Painters "Red House Painters"), [Nick Drake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nick_Drake "Nick Drake"), [Iron & Wine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iron_&_Wine "Iron & Wine") and [Neil Young](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neil_Young "Neil Young"),[\[2\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Lake_Swimmers#cite_note-2) as well as [Will Oldham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Will_Oldham "Will Oldham")[\[3\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Lake_Swimmers#cite_note-3) ( [Bonnie "Prince" Billy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bonnie_%22Prince%22_Billy "Bonnie \"Prince\" Billy")) and [Sufjan Stevens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sufjan_Stevens "Sufjan Stevens").[\[4\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Lake_Swimmers#cite_note-4) Dekker has cited influences including Gordon Lightfoot, [Gram Parsons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gram_Parsons "Gram Parsons") and [Hank Williams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hank_Williams "Hank Williams").[\[5\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Lake_Swimmers#cite_note-5)

## History

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Great_Lake_Swimmers&action=edit&section=1 "Edit section: History")\]

The band released two albums, _[Great Lake Swimmers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Lake_Swimmers_(album) "Great Lake Swimmers (album)")_ in 2003 and _[Bodies and Minds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bodies_and_Minds "Bodies and Minds")_ in 2005, on the independent label [(weewerk)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weewerk "Weewerk") before signing to the larger [Nettwerk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nettwerk "Nettwerk") in 2007.

The band released its third full-length album, _[Ongiara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ongiara "Ongiara")_, on March 27, 2007, in Canada and in May for the rest of the world. Although signing to Nettwerk early in 2007, Great Lake Swimmers continue to be managed by (weewerk). In September 2007, (weewerk) released a limited edition vinyl version of _Ongiara_. It was available in Australia through native indie label [Speak N Spell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Speak_N_Spell "Speak N Spell").

Their fourth album, _[Lost Channels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lost_Channels "Lost Channels")_, was released on March 31, 2009. It was shortlisted for the [2009 Polaris Music Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2009_Polaris_Music_Prize "2009 Polaris Music Prize"),[\[6\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Lake_Swimmers#cite_note-6) and was nominated for a [Juno Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Juno_Awards "Juno Awards"), in the category of [Roots & Traditional Album of the Year – Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Juno_Award_for_Roots_&_Traditional_Album_of_the_Year_%E2%80%93_Group "Juno Award for Roots & Traditional Album of the Year – Group"), and a [Canadian Folk Music Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canadian_Folk_Music_Award "Canadian Folk Music Award").

In 2009, Great Lake Swimmers took part in an interactive documentary series called _[City Sonic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City_Sonic "City Sonic")_. The series, which featured 20 Toronto artists, had Tony Dekker talk about his daily underground commute along Toronto’s subway system.[\[7\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Lake_Swimmers#cite_note-7) In 2011, Dekker participated in the documentary series _[National Parks Project](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Parks_Project "National Parks Project")_, visiting [Cape Breton Highlands National Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cape_Breton_Highlands_National_Park "Cape Breton Highlands National Park") in [Nova Scotia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nova_Scotia "Nova Scotia") with filmmaker [Keith Behrman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Keith_Behrman "Keith Behrman") and musicians [Daniela Gesundheit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daniela_Gesundheit "Daniela Gesundheit") and [Old Man Luedecke](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_Man_Luedecke "Old Man Luedecke").

The band's fifth studio album, _[New Wild Everywhere](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Wild_Everywhere "New Wild Everywhere")_, was released on April 3, 2012.[\[8\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Lake_Swimmers#cite_note-easycome-8) The band also composed an instrumental soundtrack for photographer Ian Coristine's _One in a Thousand_, an e-book of photography from the [Thousand Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thousand_Islands "Thousand Islands") region of Ontario, also released in April 2012.[\[9\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Lake_Swimmers#cite_note-9)

Dekker released a solo album, _Prayer of the Woods_, in October 2013.[\[10\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Lake_Swimmers#cite_note-prayer-10) The album includes eight original songs as well as covers of [Gordon Lightfoot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gordon_Lightfoot "Gordon Lightfoot")'s " [Carefree Highway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carefree_Highway_(song) "Carefree Highway (song)")" and [Human Sexual Response](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_Sexual_Response_(band) "Human Sexual Response (band)")'s "Land of the Glass Pinecones".[\[10\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Lake_Swimmers#cite_note-prayer-10) In 2014, he followed up with _Sings 10 Years of Zunior_, an album of covers of other Canadian artists — including [Old Man Luedecke](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_Man_Luedecke "Old Man Luedecke"), [Chad VanGaalen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chad_VanGaalen "Chad VanGaalen"), [Christine Fellows](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christine_Fellows "Christine Fellows"), [Ohbijou](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ohbijou "Ohbijou"), [Rae Spoon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rae_Spoon "Rae Spoon"), [Matt Mays](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matt_Mays "Matt Mays"), [Martin Tielli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martin_Tielli "Martin Tielli"), [Jennifer Castle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jennifer_Castle "Jennifer Castle") and [Cadence Weapon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cadence_Weapon "Cadence Weapon") — released to mark the 10th anniversary of Canadian web music store [Zunior](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zunior "Zunior").[\[11\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Lake_Swimmers#cite_note-11)

The sixth studio album by the full band, _[A Forest of Arms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A_Forest_of_Arms "A Forest of Arms")_, was released on April 21, 2015.[\[12\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Lake_Swimmers#cite_note-12) In 2017, they followed up with the [Christmas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christmas "Christmas")-themed EP _They Don't Make Them Like That Anymore_.[\[13\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Lake_Swimmers#cite_note-13)

In February 2018, the band released the single "Falling Apart"/"The Talking Wind", as an advance preview of the forthcoming album _The Waves, the Wake_.[\[14\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Lake_Swimmers#cite_note-14)

Their eighth album, _Uncertain Country_, was released on April 28, 2023.[\[15\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Lake_Swimmers#cite_note-15) In 2024, they appeared on several tracks from [Abigail Lapell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abigail_Lapell "Abigail Lapell")'s album _Anniversary_.[\[16\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Lake_Swimmers#cite_note-16) The 5th track on the album, "Swimming Like Flying", was written in memory of Bonnie O'Donnell, a passionate swimmer and close friend of [Tony Dekker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tony_Dekker "Tony Dekker") and [Serena Ryder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Serena_Ryder "Serena Ryder"). O'Donnell managed Ryder from 2004 until her sudden death in January 2008 at age 32. [\[17\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Lake_Swimmers#cite_note-17) Ryder sang the guest vocals on the track.

Their ninth album, _Caught Light_, was released in October 2025.[\[18\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Lake_Swimmers#cite_note-18)

### Other

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Great_Lake_Swimmers&action=edit&section=11 "Edit section: Other")\]

In 2006, the band released a "digital box set" on [Zunior](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zunior "Zunior"), consisting of their first two albums, the limited edition _Hands in Dirty Ground_ EP, several digital tour photos and the video for their song "To Leave It Behind" on a 512 MB [USB flash drive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USB_flash_drive "USB flash drive").[\[23\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Lake_Swimmers#cite_note-23)

In 2008, Dekker appeared on [Jenny Omnichord](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jenny_Omnichord "Jenny Omnichord")'s album _Charlotte or Otis: Duets for Children, Their Parents and Other People Too_, performing a duet vocal on the song "Do You Know Karate".

In 2009, the band released a limited double 7" vinyl split single with the Dutch band Audiotransparent. This release includes a cover of the [Elvis Presley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elvis_Presley "Elvis Presley") classic "Don't Be Cruel" and the original song "Send Me a Letter". Dekker and Arnesen also appear on the Audiotransparent song "You Are a Movie".

In 2010, the band contributed four tracks to the online music community [Swim Drink Fish Music](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swim_Drink_Fish_Music "Swim Drink Fish Music"), including live versions of their songs "Your Rocky Spine", "I Could Be Nothing" and "Everything Is Moving So Fast" and a previously unreleased track, "Ballad of a Fisherman's Wife".

The songs “I Became Awake” and “Various Stages” are heard in the 2010 independent Canadian horror film _[The Corridor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Corridor_(2010_film) "The Corridor (2010 film)")_.

The song "Moving Pictures Silent Films" was featured in the August 8, 2011 episode of _[Warehouse 13](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warehouse_13 "Warehouse 13")_, entitled "3...2...1..."

In 2012, the band contributed the Christmas song "Hang a String of Lights" to _Paste_ magazine's annual online Holiday Sampler music collection.
