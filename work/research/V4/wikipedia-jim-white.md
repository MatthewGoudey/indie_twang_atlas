Musical artist

**Michael Davis Pratt** (born March 9, 1957), known professionally as **Jim White**, is an American singer-songwriter, record producer, visual artist and author.

## Early life

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Jim_White_(guitarist)&action=edit&section=1 "Edit section: Early life")\]

White was born in California but moved to [Pensacola, Florida](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pensacola,_Florida "Pensacola, Florida") at the age of 5. He was deeply influenced in his childhood by the spectacle of Pentecostal religion and [gospel music](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gospel_music "Gospel music").[\[3\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jim_White_(guitarist)#cite_note-ankeny-3) According to various sources, he has been a ditch digger, a suntan oil salesman, a landscaper, a dishwasher, a fry cook, a fashion model, a fashion photographer, a professional surfer and a New York City cab driver.[\[3\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jim_White_(guitarist)#cite_note-ankeny-3)[\[4\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jim_White_(guitarist)#cite_note-4) Before embarking on a music career, White attended film school at [New York University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_University "New York University"). Soon after finishing his lengthy thesis at the university, White entered a self-described "deep hole of sickness and depression and poverty".[\[5\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jim_White_(guitarist)#cite_note-henderson-5) It was during this period that White began writing songs again after a long hiatus, many of which would appear on his debut album _Wrong Eyed Jesus_, released by [David Byrne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Byrne "David Byrne")'s boutique label [Luaka Bop](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luaka_Bop "Luaka Bop").[\[6\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jim_White_(guitarist)#cite_note-6)

## Musical career and performances

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Jim_White_(guitarist)&action=edit&section=2 "Edit section: Musical career and performances")\]

[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/1/1f/Jim_White_1.jpg/250px-Jim_White_1.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Jim_White_1.jpg) Jim White at Nottingham's Bodega Social Club, 2007

White's live shows, particularly when touring solo, can be characterized as off-beat, blending his playlist with open discussion with the audience, anecdotal storytelling derived from his own life experiences, all of which is typically humorous and insightful, with a deep sense of his feeling for the broken beauty of humanity.

White's albums often feature unlikely collaborations. On _Wrong-Eyed Jesus_, folk singer [Victoria Williams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Victoria_Williams "Victoria Williams") sings on the track "Angel-Land"; British electronica trio [Morcheeba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Morcheeba "Morcheeba") produced and played on three tracks on _No Such Place_ alongside [Sade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sade_(band) "Sade (band)") keyboardist Andrew Hale, who produced three tracks on the same album. [Aimee Mann](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aimee_Mann "Aimee Mann"), [Barenaked Ladies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barenaked_Ladies "Barenaked Ladies"), and jazz guitarist [Bill Frisell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bill_Frisell "Bill Frisell") appear on _[Drill a Hole in That Substrate and Tell Me What You See](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drill_a_Hole_in_That_Substrate_and_Tell_Me_What_You_See "Drill a Hole in That Substrate and Tell Me What You See")_, which was produced by [Joe Henry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joe_Henry "Joe Henry"), [Tucker Martine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tucker_Martine "Tucker Martine"), and Jim White himself. In an interview with David Byrne, White described the making of the album.[\[7\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jim_White_(guitarist)#cite_note-7)

White's 2012 album _[Where It Hits You](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Where_It_Hits_You "Where It Hits You")_ marked his first effort after an amicable parting of ways with Byrne's Luaka Bop. The record features guest appearances by members the roots group Olabelle, Terri Binion and Shak Nasty. _Where It Hits You_ was released on the Yep Roc label.[\[8\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jim_White_(guitarist)#cite_note-8)

In 2017 White's album _Waffles, Triangles & Jesus_ came out via the PIAPTK label[\[9\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jim_White_(guitarist)#cite_note-9) in the US and Loose in the UK. White was joined by musical group Hog Eyed Man, Holly Golightly,[\[10\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jim_White_(guitarist)#cite_note-10) and Cicada Rhythm.

White's 2020 album _Misfit's Jubilee_[\[11\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jim_White_(guitarist)#cite_note-11) marked a departure from his more solemn introspective previous work, featuring and upbeat collaboration with Belgian recording artists Geert Hellings and Nicolas Rombouts. White's longtime drummer Marlon Patton played drums on _Misfit's Jubilee_, as he did on White's previous four solo records.[\[12\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jim_White_(guitarist)#cite_note-12)

White's debut album _Wrong Eyed Jesus_ is considered a minor classic in the Americana genre, and his fan base includes various entertainment luminaries such as [David Byrne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Byrne "David Byrne"), [Matt Groening](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matt_Groening "Matt Groening"), and [Vince Gilligan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vince_Gilligan "Vince Gilligan").[\[13\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jim_White_(guitarist)#cite_note-13) The song "Wordmule" appeared in the _[Breaking Bad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Breaking_Bad "Breaking Bad")_ episode " [Blood Money](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blood_Money_(Breaking_Bad) "Blood Money (Breaking Bad)")", while "Static on the Radio" appeared in the end credits of _[El Camino: A Breaking Bad Movie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/El_Camino:_A_Breaking_Bad_Movie "El Camino: A Breaking Bad Movie")_.

In 2025, White released 'Precious Bane', a collaboration with Trey Blake, a neurodivergent artist who resides in Brighton, UK. [Stereolab](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stereolab "Stereolab") keyboard player Joe Watson co-engineered the album.

## Side projects

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Jim_White_(guitarist)&action=edit&section=3 "Edit section: Side projects")\]

In 2003 White appeared as de facto tour guide in the documentary film _[Searching for the Wrong Eyed Jesus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Searching_for_the_Wrong_Eyed_Jesus "Searching for the Wrong Eyed Jesus")_, exploring oral and song traditions of the poor white US south.[\[14\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jim_White_(guitarist)#cite_note-14) White appeared alongside writer Harry Crews and musicians [Johnny Dowd](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johnny_Dowd "Johnny Dowd"), [The Handsome Family](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Handsome_Family "The Handsome Family") and [David Eugene Edwards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Eugene_Edwards "David Eugene Edwards"). Before achieving cult status, the film was featured in festivals around the world, winning Best Documentary at the Seattle Film Festival.[\[15\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jim_White_(guitarist)#cite_note-15)

In 2006, Jim White collaborated with Johnny Dowd and Willie B and formed a group called Hellwood; in 2006, the band toured throughout Europe to promote the album _Chainsaw of Life_. White served as producer and songwriter on a collaboration alongside [Tucker Martine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tucker_Martine "Tucker Martine") and blues singer [Linda Delgado](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linda_Delgado?action=edit&redlink=1 "Linda Delgado (page does not exist)") on a project titled [Mama Lucky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mama_Lucky?action=edit&redlink=1 "Mama Lucky (page does not exist)"). Mama Lucky's first release _Permanent Stranger_ was released in a limited fashion in February 2009.[\[16\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jim_White_(guitarist)#cite_note-16)

In 2010 White created Sounds of the Americans, an experimental collaboration with guitarist Dan Nettles based on the soundtrack they composed for a Juilliard Music School theater production.[\[17\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jim_White_(guitarist)#cite_note-17)

Continuing his pattern of alternating between solo projects and collaborations, in 2014 White teamed up with the Packway Handle Band to release the bluegrass hybrid _Take It Like A Man_, which peaked at #3 on the Billboard bluegrass chart.[\[18\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jim_White_(guitarist)#cite_note-18)[\[19\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jim_White_(guitarist)#cite_note-19)

In 2023, White released Permanent Stranger under the band name "Mama Lucky," his side project with Portland-based producer [Tucker Martine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tucker_Martine "Tucker Martine").[\[20\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jim_White_(guitarist)#cite_note-20) He also co-produced Ane Diaz's "Despechada" album[\[21\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jim_White_(guitarist)#cite_note-21) and produced Ben De La Cour's critically acclaimed record Sweet Anhedonia (Jullian Records),[\[22\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jim_White_(guitarist)#cite_note-22) which appeared on several "Best of 2023" lists.[\[23\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jim_White_(guitarist)#cite_note-23)
