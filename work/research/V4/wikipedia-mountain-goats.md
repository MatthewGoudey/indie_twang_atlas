**The Mountain Goats** are an American [indie folk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indie_folk "Indie folk") band formed in [Claremont](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Claremont,_California "Claremont, California"), [California](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/California "California"), by singer-songwriter [John Darnielle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Darnielle "John Darnielle"). The band is currently\[ _[when?](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Manual_of_Style/Dates_and_numbers#Chronological_items "Wikipedia:Manual of Style/Dates and numbers")_\] based in [Durham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Durham,_North_Carolina "Durham, North Carolina"), [North Carolina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Carolina "North Carolina"). For many years, the sole member of the Mountain Goats was Darnielle, despite the plural moniker. Although he remains the core member of the band, he has worked with a variety of collaborators over time, including bassist and vocalist [Peter Hughes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_Hughes_(musician) "Peter Hughes (musician)"), drummer [Jon Wurster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jon_Wurster "Jon Wurster"), multi-instrumentalist Matt Douglas, singer-songwriter [Franklin Bruno](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Franklin_Bruno "Franklin Bruno"), bassist and vocalist Rachel Ware, singer-songwriter/ [producer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Record_producer "Record producer") [John Vanderslice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Vanderslice "John Vanderslice"), guitarist [Kaki King](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kaki_King "Kaki King"), and multi-instrumentalist [Annie Clark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Vincent_(musician) "St. Vincent (musician)").[\[2\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Mountain_Goats#cite_note-tmg_bio-2)

Throughout the 1990s, the Mountain Goats were known for producing [low-fidelity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lo-fi_music "Lo-fi music") [home recordings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Home_recording "Home recording") (most notably, on a [cassette deck](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cassette_deck "Cassette deck") [boombox](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boombox "Boombox")) and releasing recordings in [cassette](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Compact_Cassette "Compact Cassette") or [vinyl 7-inch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/7-inch_single "7-inch single") formats.[\[3\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Mountain_Goats#cite_note-am_bio-3) Since 2002, the Mountain Goats have adopted a more polished approach, often recording studio albums with a full band.[\[3\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Mountain_Goats#cite_note-am_bio-3)[\[4\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Mountain_Goats#cite_note-4ad-4)

## History

### 1991–1995: Early years

The band's name is a reference to the [Screamin' Jay Hawkins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Screamin'_Jay_Hawkins "Screamin' Jay Hawkins") song "Yellow Coat".[\[3\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Mountain_Goats#cite_note-am_bio-3) Darnielle released his first recording as the Mountain Goats ( _[Taboo VI: The Homecoming](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taboo_VI:_The_Homecoming "Taboo VI: The Homecoming")_, on Shrimper Records) in 1991.[\[3\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Mountain_Goats#cite_note-am_bio-3) Many of his first recordings and performances featured Darnielle accompanied by members of the all-girl reggae band the Casual Girls, who became known as the Bright Mountain Choir. One of this group's members, Rachel Ware, continued to accompany Darnielle on bass, both live until 1995 and studio til 1996.[\[5\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Mountain_Goats#cite_note-tmgn_faq-5)

The first five years of the Mountain Goats' career saw a prolific output of songs on cassette, vinyl and CD. These releases spanned multiple labels and countries of origin, often released in limited numbers. The focus of the Mountain Goats project was the urgency of writing.[\[6\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Mountain_Goats#cite_note-6) Songs not recorded adequately to tape within days of being written were often forgotten.\[ _[citation needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed "Wikipedia:Citation needed")_\] Cassette releases during this time include _The Hound Chronicles_, _Transmissions to Horace_, _Hot Garden Stomp_, _Taking the Dative_, and _Yam, the King of Crops_.

In 1994, the Mountain Goats released their first full-length [studio album](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Studio_album "Studio album"), _[Zopilote Machine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zopilote_Machine "Zopilote Machine")_, on Ajax Records. It is the band's only full album featuring the entirety of the Bright Mountain Choir.

[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/8/80/Peter_hughes_9_23_07.jpg/500px-Peter_hughes_9_23_07.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Peter_hughes_9_23_07.jpg) [Peter Hughes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_Hughes_(musician) "Peter Hughes (musician)") in 2007

By 1995, most of what could be considered classic Mountain Goats conventions (boom-box recording, song series, Latin quotes, and mythological themes) were abandoned in favor of a more thematically focused and experimental sound.\[ _[citation needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed "Wikipedia:Citation needed")_\] This period was marked by Darnielle's collaborations with other artists including [Alastair Galbraith](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alastair_Galbraith_(musician) "Alastair Galbraith (musician)") and [Simon Joyner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simon_Joyner "Simon Joyner"). In November 1996, Darnielle announced a vow to "clear his musical tendency for profanity" to promote a more optimistic reception to the ideas outlined in his material.\[ _[citation needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed "Wikipedia:Citation needed")_\]

In 1995, the album _[Sweden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sweden_(album) "Sweden (album)")_ was released. Soon after its recording, a sequel titled _[Hail and Farewell, Gothenburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hail_and_Farewell,_Gothenburg "Hail and Farewell, Gothenburg")_ was recorded, but never released. It remained unheard by the general public until 2007, when it was leaked against Darnielle's wishes. In 1996, the Mountain Goats released the album _[Nothing for Juice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nothing_for_Juice "Nothing for Juice")_, and _[Full Force Galesburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Full_Force_Galesburg "Full Force Galesburg")_ the following year. Rachel Ware left the band in 1996 after the recording of _[Nothing for Juice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nothing_for_Juice "Nothing for Juice")_, and bassist [Peter Hughes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_Hughes_(musician) "Peter Hughes (musician)") then played bass for the band on some tours, and Hughes later officially joined the band in 2002.

Between 1998 and 2000, the Mountain Goats slowed down their prolific output, releasing _[The Coroner's Gambit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Coroner's_Gambit "The Coroner's Gambit")_ in October 2000. The album partially returned to the band's roots, as most songs were sporadically recorded on Darnielle's old [Panasonic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Panasonic "Panasonic") RX-FT500 [cassette deck](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cassette_deck "Cassette deck") [Boombox](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boombox "Boombox"), which produced a loud background noise to the songs.

### 2001–2003: _All Hail West Texas_ and _Tallahassee_

2002 saw the release of two Mountain Goats albums: _[All Hail West Texas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/All_Hail_West_Texas "All Hail West Texas")_ and _[Tallahassee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tallahassee_(album) "Tallahassee (album)")_. These albums mark a distinct change in focus for the Mountain Goats project, being the first in a series of concept albums that explore aspects of The Mountain Goats' canon in depth. _All Hail West Texas_ featured the resurrection of Darnielle's early boom box recording for a complete album. Darnielle considers this album to be the culmination of his lo-fi recording style. _Tallahassee_, the first Mountain Goats album to be recorded with a full band and in a studio,[\[7\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Mountain_Goats#cite_note-7) explores and concludes the relationship of a couple whose lives were the subject of the song cycle known as the Alpha Series.[\[8\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Mountain_Goats#cite_note-8) It was the first album to be released on a major label, [4AD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/4AD "4AD"), marking the start of the 4AD years of the band.

Also released that year was _[Martial Arts Weekend](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martial_Arts_Weekend "Martial Arts Weekend")_, attributed to [The Extra Glenns](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Extra_Glenns "The Extra Glenns"), a collaboration with [Franklin Bruno](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Franklin_Bruno "Franklin Bruno") on several previously unreleased Mountain Goats songs.[\[5\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Mountain_Goats#cite_note-tmgn_faq-5) Following that recording, Bruno joined Darnielle in the studio along with bassist [Peter Hughes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_Hughes_(musician) "Peter Hughes (musician)"), who was the second official member of the band and accompanied Darnielle on tour. These three musicians formed what was considered the Mountain Goats studio band.

### 2004–2009: 4AD years

[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/8/88/John_Vanderslice.jpg/500px-John_Vanderslice.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:John_Vanderslice.jpg) [Record producer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Record_producer "Record producer") [John Vanderslice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Vanderslice "John Vanderslice") in 2006

In 2004, the Mountain Goats released _[We Shall All Be Healed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/We_Shall_All_Be_Healed "We Shall All Be Healed")_. The album marked a number of changes for the Mountain Goats, as it was the first time Darnielle worked with producer [John Vanderslice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Vanderslice "John Vanderslice"), and the first album of directly autobiographical material. _We Shall All Be Healed_ chronicles Darnielle's life with a group of friends and acquaintances addicted to [methamphetamine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Methamphetamine "Methamphetamine") in [Portland, Oregon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portland,_Oregon "Portland, Oregon"),[\[9\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Mountain_Goats#cite_note-9) though the album is set in [Pomona, California](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pomona,_California "Pomona, California").\[ _[citation needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed "Wikipedia:Citation needed")_\] The following year, the band's second Vanderslice-produced album, _[The Sunset Tree](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Sunset_Tree "The Sunset Tree")_, was released. Again autobiographical, Darnielle tackled the subject of his early childhood spent with an abusive stepfather.[\[10\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Mountain_Goats#cite_note-10)[\[11\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Mountain_Goats#cite_note-11) Darnielle had previously dealt with this subject in what he often refers to as the only "extensively autobiographical" song he had written before 2004, the 1999 unreleased song "You're in Maya".[\[12\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Mountain_Goats#cite_note-12) The Mountain Goats relocated to [Durham, North Carolina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Durham,_North_Carolina "Durham, North Carolina") in 2006, and issued _[Get Lonely](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Get_Lonely "Get Lonely")_, which was produced by [Scott Solter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scott_Solter "Scott Solter"), who had worked with Vanderslice on engineering for prior Mountain Goats records.

[Jon Wurster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jon_Wurster "Jon Wurster") joined the group in 2007, playing drums on the last leg of the _Get Lonely_ tour. The band recorded tracks for its next album at Prairie Sun studios.[\[13\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Mountain_Goats#cite_note-13) Entitled _[Heretic Pride](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heretic_Pride "Heretic Pride")_, the album was released on 19 February 2008.[\[14\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Mountain_Goats#cite_note-tmg_hh-14) Produced by John Vanderslice and Scott Solter, the album saw Darnielle, Hughes, and Wurster joined by Franklin Bruno, Erik Friedlander, [Annie Clark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Vincent_(musician) "St. Vincent (musician)") (better known by her stage name, St. Vincent), and members of The Bright Mountain Choir.[\[14\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Mountain_Goats#cite_note-tmg_hh-14) American [alternative hip hop](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alternative_hip_hop "Alternative hip hop") artist [Aesop Rock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aesop_Rock "Aesop Rock") released a remix of the track " [Lovecraft in Brooklyn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lovecraft_in_Brooklyn "Lovecraft in Brooklyn")" from the album, and in return Darnielle contributed vocals to his album _[None Shall Pass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/None_Shall_Pass "None Shall Pass")_, in the song "Coffee". In addition, the Mountain Goats released two other EPs in 2008, a collaboration with Kaki King entitled _[Black Pear Tree](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_Pear_Tree "Black Pear Tree")_ and the four-song Satanic Messiah EP.

In 2009, Darnielle and Vanderslice collaborated on the record _[Moon Colony Bloodbath](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moon_Colony_Bloodbath "Moon Colony Bloodbath")_. Released in a limited vinyl run of 1000 and sold during their "Gone Primitive" tour, the EP was a concept record about [organ harvesting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Organ_harvesting "Organ harvesting") colonies on the [Moon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moon "Moon"). This was followed by the next full Mountain Goats album, _[The Life of the World to Come](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Life_of_the_World_to_Come_(album) "The Life of the World to Come (album)")_, which released in October of the same year.[\[15\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Mountain_Goats#cite_note-15) The album is composed of twelve tracks, each one inspired by (and titled after) a single verse of the Christian [Bible](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bible "Bible"). In publicizing the record, the band made their first ever television appearance, performing "Psalms 40:2" on _[The Colbert Report](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Colbert_Report "The Colbert Report")_, hosted by professed Mountain Goats fan [Stephen Colbert](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stephen_Colbert "Stephen Colbert").[\[16\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Mountain_Goats#cite_note-16)

### 2010–2024: Merge Records years

[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/5/52/John_Darnielle_The_Mountain_Goats_HOH_Fest_2010.JPG/500px-John_Darnielle_The_Mountain_Goats_HOH_Fest_2010.JPG?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:John_Darnielle_The_Mountain_Goats_HOH_Fest_2010.JPG) John Darnielle playing a solo show under the name The Mountain Goats at Harvest of Hope Festival in [St. Augustine, FL](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Augustine,_FL "St. Augustine, FL") in 2010

The Mountain Goats signed to [Merge Records](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Merge_Records "Merge Records"), home to drummer Jon Wurster's other band, [Superchunk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Superchunk "Superchunk"), in 2010.[\[17\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Mountain_Goats#cite_note-17)[\[18\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Mountain_Goats#cite_note-merge-18) The label issued a new record by [The Extra Lens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Extra_Lens "The Extra Lens"), formerly The Extra Glenns, entitled _[Undercard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Undercard_(album) "Undercard (album)")_, followed by another Mountain Goats LP, _[All Eternals Deck](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/All_Eternals_Deck "All Eternals Deck")_, in 2011.[\[18\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Mountain_Goats#cite_note-merge-18)[\[19\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Mountain_Goats#cite_note-19) They were also chosen by [Jeff Mangum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jeff_Mangum "Jeff Mangum") of [Neutral Milk Hotel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neutral_Milk_Hotel "Neutral Milk Hotel") to perform at the [All Tomorrow's Parties](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/All_Tomorrow's_Parties_(music_festival) "All Tomorrow's Parties (music festival)") festival that he was due to curate in December 2011 in Minehead, England, but were unable to appear due to a rescheduling.[\[20\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Mountain_Goats#cite_note-20)

The band's fourteenth studio album, _[Transcendental Youth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transcendental_Youth "Transcendental Youth")_, was released in late 2012,[\[21\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Mountain_Goats#cite_note-21) and in early 2013 they played at Carnegie Hall in support of [John Green](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Green "John Green") and [Hank Green](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hank_Green "Hank Green") in their "Evening of Awesome" performance.[\[22\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Mountain_Goats#cite_note-22)[\[23\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Mountain_Goats#cite_note-23) In July 2013, _[All Hail West Texas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/All_Hail_West_Texas "All Hail West Texas")_ was re-released on vinyl. During an interview with [Stereogum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stereogum "Stereogum") in August 2012, John Darnielle said that [Amy Grant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amy_Grant "Amy Grant") was his favorite pop artist, and noted that " [Rich Mullins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rich_Mullins "Rich Mullins") is one of the best songwriters I know of."[\[24\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Mountain_Goats#cite_note-24) Mullins was the songwriter who penned many of Grant's hits.

The Mountain Goats released their 15th album, _[Beat the Champ](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beat_the_Champ "Beat the Champ")_, on 7 April 2015, again with [Merge Records](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Merge_Records "Merge Records").[\[25\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Mountain_Goats#cite_note-25) According to [Pitchfork Media](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pitchfork_Media "Pitchfork Media"), the album concentrates on the [professional wrestlers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Professional_wrestling "Professional wrestling") Darnielle admired when he was a child and tries to develop and imagine their lives.[\[26\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Mountain_Goats#cite_note-26) Multi-instrumentalist Matt Douglas assisted the group in recording the album, and soon thereafter became a full-time member.[\[27\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Mountain_Goats#cite_note-27)

In January 2017, the Mountain Goats recorded a humorous song per request of director [Rian Johnson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rian_Johnson "Rian Johnson"), depicting an alternate story of his upcoming movie, _[Star Wars: The Last Jedi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Star_Wars:_The_Last_Jedi "Star Wars: The Last Jedi")_. The song, titled "The Ultimate Jedi Who Wastes All the Other Jedi and Eats Their Bones", was published on Johnson's [SoundCloud](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SoundCloud "SoundCloud") page.[\[28\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Mountain_Goats#cite_note-28)

In May 2017, they released their sixteenth studio album, _[Goths](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Goths_(album) "Goths (album)")_.[\[29\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Mountain_Goats#cite_note-29) The band has stated that _Goths_ was inspired by an adolescence listening to [The Cure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Cure "The Cure"), [Bauhaus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bauhaus_(band) "Bauhaus (band)"), [Siouxsie and the Banshees](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siouxsie_and_the_Banshees "Siouxsie and the Banshees"), and [Joy Division](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joy_Division "Joy Division"), as well as hearing songs on the Californian radio station [KROQ-FM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KROQ-FM "KROQ-FM").[\[30\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Mountain_Goats#cite_note-30)

In January 2019, the band announced the April 26 release of their _[Dungeons & Dragons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dungeons_&_Dragons "Dungeons & Dragons")_ inspired album, _[In League with Dragons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/In_League_with_Dragons "In League with Dragons")_ (and released initial single, "Younger"), via Merge Records. The announcement was accompanied by a live music stream from the [Wizards of the Coast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wizards_of_the_Coast "Wizards of the Coast") headquarters.[\[31\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Mountain_Goats#cite_note-RS-31)

In March 2020, while the [COVID-19 pandemic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/COVID-19_pandemic "COVID-19 pandemic") left the band unable to tour, Darnielle retrieved his old Panasonic RX-FT500 tape deck and recorded 10 new songs, using the direct-to-boombox method for the first time since 2002's _All Hail West Texas_. The resulting album, entitled _[Songs for Pierre Chuvin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Songs_for_Pierre_Chuvin "Songs for Pierre Chuvin")_, was inspired by Darnielle's reading of [Pierre Chuvin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pierre_Chuvin "Pierre Chuvin")'s 1990 book _A Chronicle of the Last Pagans_, and was released digitally on April 10, 2020, with a limited physical release on cassette through Merge Records.[\[32\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Mountain_Goats#cite_note-32)[\[33\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Mountain_Goats#cite_note-33)

The Mountain Goats announced in August 2020 that the band's nineteenth studio album, _[Getting Into Knives](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Getting_Into_Knives "Getting Into Knives")_, would be released on October 23, 2020, on CD, vinyl, cassette, and digital.[\[34\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Mountain_Goats#cite_note-bandcamp-gik-34)

In April 2021, the Mountain Goats announced their album _[Dark in Here](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dark_in_Here "Dark in Here")_, released on June 25, alongside the release of its first single "Mobile". The album was recorded at [FAME Studios](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FAME_Studios "FAME Studios").[\[35\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Mountain_Goats#cite_note-35) The band's 21st album, _[Bleed Out](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bleed_Out_(Mountain_Goats_album) "Bleed Out (Mountain Goats album)")_, was released on August19, 2022.[\[36\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Mountain_Goats#cite_note-36)

In July 2023, the band announced their 22nd album _[Jenny from Thebes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jenny_from_Thebes "Jenny from Thebes")_, a "rock opera about a woman named Jenny",[\[37\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Mountain_Goats#cite_note-37) would be released on October27, 2023. The character "Jenny" has appeared in several previous songs by the band, notably in several from _[All Hail West Texas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/All_Hail_West_Texas "All Hail West Texas")_.

Peter Hughes announced his departure from the band in August 2024, citing "health and sanity" concerns among other reasons.[\[38\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Mountain_Goats#cite_note-38)[\[39\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Mountain_Goats#cite_note-39)

### 2025–present: Independent years

In September 2025, Darnielle announced a new album, _[Through This Fire Across from Peter Balkan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Through_This_Fire_Across_from_Peter_Balkan "Through This Fire Across from Peter Balkan")_, to be released on the Mountain Goats' own independent label, Cadmean Dawn, after fifteen years with Merge Records. The album was released on November7, 2025. Darnielle explained that _Through This Fire Across from Peter Balkan_ was originally inspired by a dream Darnielle had. He wrote, "I transcribed what I could remember of it into the note-taking app I keep on my phone. The note reads: 'through this fire across from peter balkan #dream. It was the title of a work, not sure which form.' The next time I sat at the piano to see if I had ideas, I got the notion of writing a work that proceeded from its title, and that tried to make real the dreamlike grammar of that title."[\[40\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Mountain_Goats#cite_note-40)

The album _[Days](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Days_(The_Mountain_Goats_album) "Days (The Mountain Goats album)")_ was released on August7, 2026, also via Cadmean Dawn.

## In other media

In the [Showtime](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Showtime_(TV_network) "Showtime (TV network)") television series _[Weeds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weeds_(TV_series) "Weeds (TV series)"),_ the band performed the Theme to Weeds (Malvina Reynolds’ " [Little Boxes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Little_Boxes "Little Boxes")") during the opening credits of Season 8, Episode 5.[\[49\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Mountain_Goats#cite_note-49) The [Adult Swim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adult_Swim "Adult Swim") show _[Moral Orel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moral_Orel "Moral Orel")_ featured "Love, Love, Love", "Old College Try", and " [No Children](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/No_Children "No Children")" on several episodes of Season 3.[\[50\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Mountain_Goats#cite_note-50)

The band made their television debut on October 6, 2009, playing "Psalms 40:2" on _[The Colbert Report](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Colbert_Report "The Colbert Report")_. On January 19, 2010, they played "Genesis 3:23" on _[Late Night with Jimmy Fallon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Late_Night_with_Jimmy_Fallon "Late Night with Jimmy Fallon")_.[\[51\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Mountain_Goats#cite_note-51) On February 23, 2011, they played "Birth of Serpents" in support of their album, _[All Eternals Deck](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/All_Eternals_Deck "All Eternals Deck")_, on the _[Late Show with David Letterman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Late_Show_with_David_Letterman "Late Show with David Letterman")_. On April 6, 2015, the band performed "Foreign Object" on _[Late Night with Seth Meyers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Late_Night_with_Seth_Meyers "Late Night with Seth Meyers")_ while promoting _[Beat the Champ](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beat_the_Champ "Beat the Champ")_.[\[52\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Mountain_Goats#cite_note-52) In July 2019, the band performed on _[The Late Show with Stephen Colbert](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Late_Show_with_Stephen_Colbert "The Late Show with Stephen Colbert")_, with [Stephen Colbert](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stephen_Colbert "Stephen Colbert") joining in to sing "This Year".[\[53\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Mountain_Goats#cite_note-53)
