_**Z**_ is the fourth studio album by [psychedelic rock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Psychedelic_rock "Psychedelic rock") band [My Morning Jacket](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/My_Morning_Jacket "My Morning Jacket"). This collection features a much spacier and more polished sound than previous releases, making heavy use of [synthesizers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Synthesizer "Synthesizer") throughout and incorporating [reggae](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reggae "Reggae") and [dub](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dub_music "Dub music") influences. The heavy reverb that was a defining characteristic of the band's prior recordings is largely absent. The songs on the album are more focused and shorter compared to the band's previous albums.

A double-live album, _[Okonokos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Okonokos "Okonokos")_, was recorded at the end of the Z Tour and was released on September 26, 2006; it features live versions of eight of _Z'_ s 10 songs.

In 2012, _[Rolling Stone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rolling_Stone "Rolling Stone")_ ranked the album at number 457 on its list of [The 500 Greatest Albums of All Time](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rolling_Stone's_500_Greatest_Albums_of_All_Time "Rolling Stone's 500 Greatest Albums of All Time"). The magazine has also ranked the album 31st on its list of the 100 best albums of the 2000s and 23rd of the 40 greatest stoner albums ever.

## Background and production

In September 2003, My Morning Jacket released its third album _[It Still Moves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/It_Still_Moves "It Still Moves")_.[\[4\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Z_(My_Morning_Jacket_album)#cite_note-Biography-4) It was the band's first album to be released by [ATO Records](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ATO_Records "ATO Records").[\[5\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Z_(My_Morning_Jacket_album)#cite_note-LA_Times-5) _It Still Moves_ was well received by critics and peaked at number two on the [Heatseekers Albums](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Top_Heatseekers "Top Heatseekers") in the United States.[\[4\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Z_(My_Morning_Jacket_album)#cite_note-Biography-4)[\[6\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Z_(My_Morning_Jacket_album)#cite_note-Billboard_Review-6) During the subsequent tour, guitarist Johnny Quaid and keyboardist Danny Cash quit the band on amicable terms, and were replaced by guitarist [Carl Broemel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carl_Broemel "Carl Broemel") and keyboardist Bo Koster.[\[7\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Z_(My_Morning_Jacket_album)#cite_note-7) Drummer Patrick Hallahan said that while the arrival of new members put the band in an awkward position, it was also a liberating experience. "We thought, 'Let's take ourselves out of our element.'"[\[5\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Z_(My_Morning_Jacket_album)#cite_note-LA_Times-5)

For the next My Morning Jacket album, the band members wanted to experiment with unique arrangements and a greater usage of musical [soundscapes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soundscape#In_music "Soundscape") when compared to their previous albums.[\[8\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Z_(My_Morning_Jacket_album)#cite_note-Marquee-8) Vocalist [Jim James](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jim_James "Jim James") said the band went out of their comfort zone, and decided to record the album at the remote Allaire Studios in the [Catskill Mountains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catskill_Mountains "Catskill Mountains") instead of in their hometown of [Louisville, Kentucky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louisville,_Kentucky "Louisville, Kentucky"). According to bassist [Tom Blankenship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tom_Blankenship "Tom Blankenship"): "We had been talking for years about really locking ourselves in when we did an album, and now there was no excuse but to be in the studio all the time. You'd wake up and have breakfast and grab your coffee and walk into the control room."[\[9\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Z_(My_Morning_Jacket_album)#cite_note-RS_Rise_Up-9)

_Z_ was the first My Morning Jacket album to have a professional producer. James had previously produced every album, which he said was out of pride.[\[8\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Z_(My_Morning_Jacket_album)#cite_note-Marquee-8) Before the recording sessions began, James met producer [John Leckie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Leckie "John Leckie"), who was known for his work with bands such as [Pink Floyd](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pink_Floyd "Pink Floyd") and [Radiohead](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radiohead "Radiohead"). Leckie was also a tape operator on the [George Harrison](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Harrison "George Harrison") album _[All Things Must Pass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/All_Things_Must_Pass "All Things Must Pass")_, one of James' favorite albums.[\[10\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Z_(My_Morning_Jacket_album)#cite_note-10) James said Leckie's perspective as an outsider played a pivotal role in the production of the album. According to James: "Sometimes when everybody in the band is just there, you know, people don't want to hurt anyone's feelings, or you might think that it was a good take because everyone is tired. We really learned the value of having another ear there."[\[8\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Z_(My_Morning_Jacket_album)#cite_note-Marquee-8)

## Composition

This album was seen by reviewers as a shift in the musical genre of the band and vocal style of James, in part due to working with [John Leckie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Leckie "John Leckie") and the inclusion of genres such as [reggae](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reggae "Reggae") and [dub](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dub_music "Dub music") on " [Off the Record](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Off_the_Record_(My_Morning_Jacket_song) "Off the Record (My Morning Jacket song)")".[\[11\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Z_(My_Morning_Jacket_album)#cite_note-pm-11)[\[12\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Z_(My_Morning_Jacket_album)#cite_note-pop-12)

## Digital rights management

The CD was one of the more high-profile releases in 2005 featuring [digital rights management](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Digital_rights_management "Digital rights management") technology to prohibit owners from playing the music on a computer or creating digital copies. [United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States "United States") pressings of this CD contain [MediaMax CD-3](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MediaMax_CD-3 "MediaMax CD-3") by [SunnComm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SunnComm "SunnComm").[\[13\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Z_(My_Morning_Jacket_album)#cite_note-drm-13) The band's manager, Mike Martinovich, expressed their dissatisfaction with the technology and it was abandoned on subsequent releases.[\[14\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Z_(My_Morning_Jacket_album)#cite_note-rollingstone-14) The band themselves provided information on their website about how to bypass the software, and also offered to burn individual copies of the album for fans, free of copy-protection software.[\[15\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Z_(My_Morning_Jacket_album)#cite_note-MTV.com-15)

Professional ratings

The album was the second highest rated album of 2005 according to [Metacritic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metacritic "Metacritic"), behind [Sufjan Stevens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sufjan_Stevens "Sufjan Stevens")' _[Illinois](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Illinois_(Sufjan_Stevens_album) "Illinois (Sufjan Stevens album)")_; the album has a total score of 90 out of 100.[\[25\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Z_(My_Morning_Jacket_album)#cite_note-metacritic-25)

Technically, both _Z_ and _Illinois_ were tied for the highest [Metacritic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metacritic "Metacritic") rating in 2005, with a score of 90 each. However, the score for _Illinois_ was based on a larger overall number of reviews: 40, compared to 31 for _Z_.[\[26\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Z_(My_Morning_Jacket_album)#cite_note-26)

[Online music magazine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Online_magazine "Online magazine") [Pitchfork Media](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pitchfork_Media "Pitchfork Media") placed _Z_ at number 146 on their list of top 200 albums of the 2000s, calling it My Morning Jacket's _[OK Computer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/OK_Computer "OK Computer")_.[\[27\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Z_(My_Morning_Jacket_album)#cite_note-27) In 2012, _[Rolling Stone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rolling_Stone "Rolling Stone")_ ranked the album at number 457 on its list of [The 500 Greatest Albums of All Time](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rolling_Stone's_500_Greatest_Albums_of_All_Time "Rolling Stone's 500 Greatest Albums of All Time").[\[28\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Z_(My_Morning_Jacket_album)#cite_note-auto-28) The magazine has also ranked the album 31st on its list of the 100 best albums of the 2000s[\[29\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Z_(My_Morning_Jacket_album)#cite_note-auto1-29) and 23rd of the 40 greatest stoner albums ever.[\[30\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Z_(My_Morning_Jacket_album)#cite_note-auto2-30)
In March 2022, the album ranked at number 1 on [WFPK Louisville's](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/WFPK "WFPK") "Top 500 Albums of the 2000's\[ _[sic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sic "Sic")_\] Countdown" which was conducted by a public poll.[\[31\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Z_(My_Morning_Jacket_album)#cite_note-31)
