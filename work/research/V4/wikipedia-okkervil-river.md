**Okkervil River** is an American rock band led by singer-songwriter [Will Sheff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Will_Sheff "Will Sheff"). Formed in [Austin, Texas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austin,_Texas "Austin, Texas"), in 1998, the band takes its name from a short story by Russian author [Tatyana Tolstaya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tatyana_Tolstaya "Tatyana Tolstaya") set on the river [Okkervil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Okkervil "Okkervil") in [Saint Petersburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Petersburg "Saint Petersburg").[\[1\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Okkervil_River#cite_note-1) They began as a trio made up of Sheff and friends he had met in his native state of [New Hampshire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Hampshire "New Hampshire") but, over time, have gone through many lineups.

Okkervil River self-released their first album, _Stars Too Small to Use,_ which led them to the [South by Southwest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_by_Southwest "South by Southwest") music festival. After recording their first album in a garage, they signed with [Jagjaguwar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jagjaguwar "Jagjaguwar"). They continued by releasing four more albums, including the critically lauded concept album _[Black Sheep Boy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_Sheep_Boy "Black Sheep Boy")_.[\[2\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Okkervil_River#cite_note-2)

After a period of touring for _Black Sheep Boy_, Okkervil River followed up with _[The Stage Names](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Stage_Names "The Stage Names")_. The album sold 10,000 in its opening week in the United States. The group released a free covers album, _[Golden Opportunities Mixtape](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golden_Opportunities_Mixtape "Golden Opportunities Mixtape")_ from their live performances.[\[3\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Okkervil_River#cite_note-3)

The band has garnered positive critical reception.[\[4\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Okkervil_River#cite_note-4)
They have appeared on the talk show _[Late Night with Conan O'Brien](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Late_Night_with_Conan_O'Brien "Late Night with Conan O'Brien")_ and have performed with acts such as [The Decemberists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Decemberists "The Decemberists"), [The New Pornographers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_New_Pornographers "The New Pornographers"), [The National](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_National_(band) "The National (band)"), and [Lou Reed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lou_Reed "Lou Reed").

Okkervil River released the album _[The Stand Ins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Stand_Ins "The Stand Ins")_ in 2008. They promoted the release with a series of cover songs from the album on [YouTube](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/YouTube "YouTube") by people they've met as a band.[\[5\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Okkervil_River#cite_note-5) Their 2010 collaboration as a backing band with psychedelic rocker [Roky Erickson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roky_Erickson "Roky Erickson") yielded _[True Love Cast Out All Evil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/True_Love_Cast_Out_All_Evil "True Love Cast Out All Evil")_. In 2011, Okkervil River released their sixth full-length album, _[I Am Very Far](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/I_Am_Very_Far "I Am Very Far")_, which peaked at No. 32 on the [_Billboard_ 200](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Billboard_200 "Billboard 200"). The band released their seventh album, _[The Silver Gymnasium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Silver_Gymnasium "The Silver Gymnasium")_, in 2013. The album peaked at No. 66 on the [_Billboard_ 200](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Billboard_200 "Billboard 200"). This was followed by the album, _[Away](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Away_(Okkervil_River_album) "Away (Okkervil River album)"),_ which was released in 2016 on [ATO Records](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ATO_Records "ATO Records").[\[6\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Okkervil_River#cite_note-6) Their ninth album, _[In the Rainbow Rain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/In_the_Rainbow_Rain "In the Rainbow Rain")_, was released by ATO in 2018.

## History

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Okkervil_River&action=edit&section=1 "Edit section: History")\]

### Formation

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Okkervil_River&action=edit&section=2 "Edit section: Formation")\]

[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/4/47/Okkervil_River_Scott_Brackett.jpg/250px-Okkervil_River_Scott_Brackett.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Okkervil_River_Scott_Brackett.jpg) Scott Brackett playing trumpet at the 2007 Fun Fun Fest in Austin, Texas

Okkervil River's founding members became friends at [Kimball Union Academy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kimball_Union_Academy "Kimball Union Academy") in [Meriden, New Hampshire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meriden,_New_Hampshire "Meriden, New Hampshire"), and after parting ways for college moved to Austin, Texas to live together and start a band. The band consisted of singer-songwriter [Will Sheff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Will_Sheff "Will Sheff"), Zach Thomas on bass and mandolin, and Seth Warren on drums. Their first gig was at Steamboat in Austin on January 11, 1999.[\[7\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Okkervil_River#cite_note-7)

### _Bedroom EP_ and _Stars Too Small to Use_

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Okkervil_River&action=edit&section=3 "Edit section: Bedroom EP and Stars Too Small to Use")\]

In 1998, the group self-released their first disc, _Bedroom EP_. Over the course of two weekends in the summer of 1999, they recorded a seven-song self-released album titled _[Stars Too Small to Use](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stars_Too_Small_to_Use "Stars Too Small to Use")_, with recording engineer Jeff Hoskins.

They met [Jonathan Meiburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jonathan_Meiburg "Jonathan Meiburg") at a gig with his band Whu Gnu at the Waterloo Brewing Company on December 3, 1999. Meiburg subsequently joined the band on accordion and later on pianos and organs.

On the strength of _Stars Too Small to Use_, Okkervil River was admitted into the 2000 [SXSW](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SXSW "SXSW") music festival. Their first major press was a SXSW feature article[\[8\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Okkervil_River#cite_note-8) in the _[Austin Chronicle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austin_Chronicle "Austin Chronicle")_ on March 3, 2000.

### _Don't Fall in Love with Everyone You See_ and _Down the River of Golden Dreams_

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Okkervil_River&action=edit&section=4 "Edit section: Don't Fall in Love with Everyone You See and Down the River of Golden Dreams")\]

[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/a/ac/Okkervil_river2.jpg/250px-Okkervil_river2.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Okkervil_river2.jpg) Okkervil guitarist Brian Cassidy at Fun Fun Fun fest in 2007

The band met recording engineer and producer Brian Beattie at their SXSW showcase on March 18, 2000, and soon agreed to make a record together. They spent much of the rest of that year working on _[Don't Fall in Love with Everyone You See](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Don't_Fall_in_Love_with_Everyone_You_See "Don't Fall in Love with Everyone You See")_. Warren moved to [Berkeley, California](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berkeley,_California "Berkeley, California") in December and was replaced on drums by Mark Pedini.

By the time of that year's SXSW festival, Okkervil River had received interest in their new record from the [Bloomington, Indiana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bloomington,_Indiana "Bloomington, Indiana")-based record label Jagjaguwar. The record was released on Jagjaguwar on January 22, 2002.

One year later the band traveled to San Francisco and reunited with Warren to record their third album at [Tiny Telephone Studios](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tiny_Telephone_Studios "Tiny Telephone Studios") with engineer [Scott Solter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scott_Solter "Scott Solter") at the console. Jagjaguwar released _[Down the River of Golden Dreams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Down_the_River_of_Golden_Dreams "Down the River of Golden Dreams")_ on September 2, 2003.

In 2003, Pedini left the band to pursue his [graphic design](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Graphic_design "Graphic design") work, leaving Okkervil River without a drummer for that year's SXSW. They invited [Travis Nelsen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Travis_Nelsen "Travis Nelsen"), fresh off a tour filling in on drums for sister labelmates [Secretly Canadian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Secretly_Canadian "Secretly Canadian")'s [Swearing at Motorists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swearing_at_Motorists "Swearing at Motorists"), to perform with them. He soon became the band's full-time drummer. The next year, during a long bout of touring, the band added keyboardist and lap-steel player Howard Draper as a fifth member.

### _Black Sheep Boy_, _The Stage Names_ and _The Stand Ins_

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Okkervil_River&action=edit&section=5 "Edit section: Black Sheep Boy, The Stage Names and The Stand Ins")\]

[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/6/6c/Okkervil_River_%28Lauren_Gurgiolo%29.jpg/250px-Okkervil_River_%28Lauren_Gurgiolo%29.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Okkervil_River_(Lauren_Gurgiolo).jpg) Lauren Gurgiolo

In August 2004, the band began recording with Beattie again, finally putting the finishing touches on their third full-length album, _[Black Sheep Boy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_Sheep_Boy "Black Sheep Boy")_ in November. It was released on April 5, 2005. As a result of the album's critical success, Okkervil River followed up with an [EP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Extended_play "Extended play") entitled _[Black Sheep Boy Appendix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_Sheep_Boy_Appendix "Black Sheep Boy Appendix")_ on November 22, 2005. This was the first recording with Draper, horn and keyboardist Scott Brackett, guitarist Brian Cassidy, and touring bassist Pat Pestorius, who eventually replaced Zach Thomas in the band. The band signed to [Virgin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Virgin_Records "Virgin Records")/ [EMI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/EMI "EMI") in Europe and the label re-released _[Black Sheep Boy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_Sheep_Boy "Black Sheep Boy")_ and its follow-up _[Black Sheep Boy Appendix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_Sheep_Boy_Appendix "Black Sheep Boy Appendix")_ as a double disc on April 28, 2006. Jagjaguwar eventually followed suit, releasing the _Definitive Edition_ with extra songs and videos.[\[9\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Okkervil_River#cite_note-9)

_[The Stage Names](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Stage_Names "The Stage Names")_, their fourth full-length studio album (produced again by Beattie), was released on August 7, 2007. The disc features the line-up that toured extensively on _Black Sheep Boy_ and the _Black Sheep Boy Appendix_, with Cassidy replacing Draper who joined [Shearwater](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shearwater_(band) "Shearwater (band)"). The album was met with critical acclaim[\[10\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Okkervil_River#cite_note-metacritic-10) and debuted at number 62 on the [_Billboard_ 200](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Billboard_200 "Billboard 200") with 10,000 copies sold.[\[11\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Okkervil_River#cite_note-billboard-11)

Okkervil River released their fifth album _[The Stand Ins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Stand_Ins "The Stand Ins")_ on September 9, 2008.[\[12\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Okkervil_River#cite_note-12) In its first week, the album charted at No.42 with 11,000 copies sold, according to the [_Billboard_ 200](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Billboard_200 "Billboard 200").[\[13\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Okkervil_River#cite_note-13) On December 12, 2007, the band freely released a nine-song [mixtape](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mixtape "Mixtape") entitled _[Golden Opportunities Mixtape](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golden_Opportunities_Mixtape "Golden Opportunities Mixtape")_ via their website.[\[14\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Okkervil_River#cite_note-14) These recordings, along with the upcoming appendix, are the first to feature contributions from new touring keyboardist, Justin Sherburn, who joined the band in November 2007.

In 2008 guitarist Brian Cassidy stepped down from the band as a full-time touring member and was temporarily replaced by Charles Bissell of [The Wrens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Wrens_(band) "The Wrens (band)") for their spring and summer tours.[\[15\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Okkervil_River#cite_note-15) Bissell was later replaced by Lauren Gurgiolo, singer and songwriter of the Austin, Texas band The Dialtones. After performing on the _[Late Show with David Letterman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Late_Show_with_David_Letterman "Late Show with David Letterman")_ in early 2009, the "Pop Lie" single was released backed with the b-sides "Millionaire" and "Pop Lie (One Man Band Version)".

### _I Am Very Far_ & _The Silver Gymnasium_

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Okkervil_River&action=edit&section=6 "Edit section: I Am Very Far & The Silver Gymnasium")\]

[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/8/89/Will_Sheff_2008.jpg/250px-Will_Sheff_2008.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Will_Sheff_2008.jpg) [Will Sheff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Will_Sheff "Will Sheff"), lead singer and songwriter (2008)

Okkervil River's sixth album, _[I Am Very Far](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/I_Am_Very_Far "I Am Very Far")_, was released on May 10, 2011.[\[16\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Okkervil_River#cite_note-16) To promote the album, the band went on tour in 2011 with [Titus Andronicus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Titus_Andronicus_(band) "Titus Andronicus (band)"), [Julianna Barwick](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Julianna_Barwick "Julianna Barwick"), [Future Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Future_Islands "Future Islands") and NewVillager.[\[17\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Okkervil_River#cite_note-17) In September 2013 Okkervil River released their seventh album, _[The Silver Gymnasium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Silver_Gymnasium "The Silver Gymnasium")_, the content of which is inspired by Sheff's childhood and hometown.[\[18\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Okkervil_River#cite_note-18)

### _Away_

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Okkervil_River&action=edit&section=7 "Edit section: Away")\]

[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/5/5f/Will_Scheff_%28okkervil_river%29.jpg/250px-Will_Scheff_%28okkervil_river%29.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Will_Scheff_(okkervil_river).jpg) Will Sheff (Okkervil river)

_[Away](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Away_(Okkervil_River_album) "Away (Okkervil River album)")_, the band's eighth album, was released on September 9, 2016. The album features a new backing band and sonic direction, including contributions from Marissa Nadler, Jonathan Meiburg and members of the classical group yMusic.[\[19\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Okkervil_River#cite_note-19) The content of the album is largely based on Will Sheff's life during the 2013–2015 period after the release of The Silver Gymnasium, during which many members of the band left and his grandfather died.

### _In the Rainbow Rain_

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Okkervil_River&action=edit&section=8 "Edit section: In the Rainbow Rain")\]

_[In the Rainbow Rain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/In_the_Rainbow_Rain "In the Rainbow Rain")_ was released on April 27, 2018, and marks the ninth album for the band. Will Sheff produced the album while [Shawn Everett](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shawn_Everett "Shawn Everett") (Perfume Genius, Alabama Shakes, The War on Drugs) assumed mixing responsibilities.
The writing process incorporated Sheff's songwriting craftsmanship with an interplay of his new bandmates, [Benjamin Lazar Davis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Benjamin_Lazar_Davis "Benjamin Lazar Davis") (bass), Will Graefe (guitar), Lip Talk (Sarah K. Pedinotti) (keys) and Cully Symington (percussion) – the same iteration of Okkervil River that joined Sheff on the Away tour.[\[20\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Okkervil_River#cite_note-20)

Sheff and the band started work on the new album shortly after the end of that tour – and the presidential election. "If December 2016 was good for anything, it was good for writing songs", he said. Sheff took a different approach to the album, sometimes co-writing with his new band, and occasionally attending local Quaker meetings.

## Other projects

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Okkervil_River&action=edit&section=9 "Edit section: Other projects")\]

[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/4/42/Okkervil_river1.jpg/250px-Okkervil_river1.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Okkervil_river1.jpg) [Jonathan Meiburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jonathan_Meiburg "Jonathan Meiburg"), former Okkervil River member, at a festival in 2006

Main article: [Shearwater (band)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shearwater_(band) "Shearwater (band)")

In 2001, Meiburg and Sheff founded a second band known as [Shearwater](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shearwater_(band) "Shearwater (band)"). Originally an outlet for Meiburg songs and some Sheff songs that didn't fit the Okkervil mold, Shearwater has gradually evolved into a full-fledged band. The band's 2006 release, _[Palo Santo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palo_Santo_(Shearwater_album) "Palo Santo (Shearwater album)")_ (sans Sheff's vocals, but including his musical input), has seen Meiburg receive great critical acclaim. The groups still share many fans, and, while on tour together in 2004, they released a limited edition split-CD entitled _[Sham Wedding/Hoax Funeral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sham_Wedding/Hoax_Funeral "Sham Wedding/Hoax Funeral")_. In 2008, Meiburg left Okkervil River to focus exclusively on Shearwater.

On April 20, 2010, former [13th Floor Elevators](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/13th_Floor_Elevators "13th Floor Elevators") singer-guitarist [Roky Erickson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roky_Erickson "Roky Erickson") released the album _True Love Cast Out All Evil_, produced by Will Sheff and backed by Okkervil River.

Okkervil River's "Unless it Kicks" was featured in the 2013 film _A Birder's Guide to Everything._[\[21\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Okkervil_River#cite_note-21)

Okkervil River also performed " [Candle on the Water](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Candle_on_the_Water "Candle on the Water")" for the 2016 remake of " [Pete's Dragon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pete's_Dragon_(2016_film) "Pete's Dragon (2016 film)")".[\[22\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Okkervil_River#cite_note-22)
