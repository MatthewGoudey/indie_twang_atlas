Musical artist

**Jeffrey Scot Tweedy** (born August 25, 1967) is an American musician, author, and record producer, best known as the lead vocalist and guitarist of the band [Wilco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wilco "Wilco"). Tweedy, originally from [Belleville, Illinois](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belleville,_Illinois "Belleville, Illinois"), began his music career in high school with his band The Plebes along with [Jay Farrar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jay_Farrar "Jay Farrar"), also in the band. The Plebes later became the [alternative country](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alternative_country "Alternative country") band [Uncle Tupelo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uncle_Tupelo "Uncle Tupelo").

After Uncle Tupelo broke up, Tweedy formed Wilco which found critical and commercial success, most notably with _[Yankee Hotel Foxtrot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yankee_Hotel_Foxtrot "Yankee Hotel Foxtrot")_ and _[A Ghost Is Born](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A_Ghost_Is_Born "A Ghost Is Born")_. The latter received a [Grammy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grammy_Awards "Grammy Awards") for [Best Alternative Album](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grammy_Award_for_Best_Alternative_Music_Album "Grammy Award for Best Alternative Music Album") in 2005.

During his career Tweedy has released 20 studio albums including [four with Uncle Tupelo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uncle_Tupelo#Discography "Uncle Tupelo"), [twelve with Wilco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wilco_discography#Studio_albums "Wilco discography"), [one with his son Spencer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sukierae "Sukierae"), [a solo acoustic album](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Together_at_Last "Together at Last"), three solo studio albums, along with numerous collaborations with other musicians, most notably _[Mermaid Avenue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mermaid_Avenue "Mermaid Avenue")_ with [Billy Bragg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Billy_Bragg "Billy Bragg").[\[1\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jeff_Tweedy#cite_note-Kot-LHTD-2004-1)[\[2\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jeff_Tweedy#cite_note-NYTimes-RockStar-2009-2)

## Early life

Tweedy was born in [Belleville](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belleville,_Illinois "Belleville, Illinois") on August 25, 1967,[\[3\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jeff_Tweedy#cite_note-FamilySearch-3) the fourth child of Bob and JoAnn Tweedy (née Werkmeister).[\[4\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jeff_Tweedy#cite_note-SunTimes-GregTweedy-obit-2013-4) Bob Tweedy (died August 4, 2017)[\[5\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jeff_Tweedy#cite_note-5) worked for [Alton & Southern Railroad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alton_and_Southern_Railroad "Alton and Southern Railroad") in [East St. Louis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_St._Louis,_Illinois "East St. Louis, Illinois"); JoAnn was a housemaker.[\[1\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jeff_Tweedy#cite_note-Kot-LHTD-2004-1):11–15 Tweedy grew up with three siblings, older brother Greg Tweedy (died in 2013), brother Steven Tweedy, and sister Debbie Voll.[\[4\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jeff_Tweedy#cite_note-SunTimes-GregTweedy-obit-2013-4)

Tweedy's mother bought him his first guitar when he was six years old, although he did not begin to play it seriously until he was twelve. Apparently Tweedy told people that he knew how to play the guitar once he got his first guitar, even though he did not know how to play.

When he was twelve, Tweedy was injured in a bicycle accident and was laid up for the summer. He decided to learn how to play a few chords before somebody "called him out" on the lie.[\[1\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jeff_Tweedy#cite_note-Kot-LHTD-2004-1) On an appearance of _[The Late Show with Stephen Colbert](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Late_Show_with_Stephen_Colbert "The Late Show with Stephen Colbert")_, he remembered attending an [X](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/X_(American_band) "X (American band)") concert as a youngster in [St. Louis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Louis "St. Louis"). [The Replacements](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Replacements_(band) "The Replacements (band)") opened, and [Paul Westerberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_Westerberg "Paul Westerberg"), their guitarist and vocalist, fell off the stage while performing. Tweedy recalls thinking "That looks like fun!"[\[6\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jeff_Tweedy#cite_note-6)

In 1981, when Tweedy was fourteen years old, he befriended [Jay Farrar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jay_Farrar "Jay Farrar") during an English class at [Belleville Township High School West](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belleville_Township_High_School_West "Belleville Township High School West").[\[1\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jeff_Tweedy#cite_note-Kot-LHTD-2004-1):10 All of the members of Farrar's family enjoyed playing music; he already knew [rock and roll](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rock_and_roll "Rock and roll") music very well. By that time, Tweedy was a fan of [The Ramones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Ramones "The Ramones") and [country music](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Country_music "Country music") while Farrar enjoyed [The Sex Pistols](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Sex_Pistols "The Sex Pistols").[\[7\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jeff_Tweedy#cite_note-NYTimes-LHTD-2004-7) Tweedy attended [Belleville Area College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belleville_Area_College "Belleville Area College") and [Southern Illinois University Edwardsville](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_Illinois_University_Edwardsville "Southern Illinois University Edwardsville").[\[1\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jeff_Tweedy#cite_note-Kot-LHTD-2004-1)

## Career

### Early music

In the early 1980s, Tweedy joined The Plebes, a [rockabilly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rockabilly "Rockabilly") band, with brothers Wade and Dade Farrar which Tweedy joined in order to qualify for a battle of the bands competition. They won.[\[1\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jeff_Tweedy#cite_note-Kot-LHTD-2004-1):10,17[\[8\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jeff_Tweedy#cite_note-OptionMag-HeartCountry-1993-8) Tweedy pushed The Plebes away from the rockabilly music that they had been playing, which caused Dade Farrar to leave the band. The band renamed themselves The Primitives in 1984, taking their name from a song by [garage rock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garage_rock "Garage rock") band The Groupies.[\[1\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jeff_Tweedy#cite_note-Kot-LHTD-2004-1):18[\[9\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jeff_Tweedy#cite_note-9) Wade Farrar sang lead vocals and played harmonica, Jay Farrar played guitar, Tweedy played bass guitar, and [Mike Heidorn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mike_Heidorn "Mike Heidorn") played drums. In late 1986, the band decided to change their name to Uncle Tupelo, because a more popular British band was also using the name " [The Primitives](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Primitives "The Primitives")".[\[10\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jeff_Tweedy#cite_note-NoDepression-liner-notes-2003-10) The Primatives went on hiatus in 1986 after Wade Farrar left the band to finish his engineering degree at Southern Illinois University.[\[1\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jeff_Tweedy#cite_note-Kot-LHTD-2004-1):22 While waiting for Wade to return from campus, Farrar, Tweedy, and Heidorn formed Uncle Tupelo.

### Uncle Tupelo (1987–1994)

Main article: [Uncle Tupelo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uncle_Tupelo "Uncle Tupelo")

At his parents' request, Tweedy attended several universities, but dropped out of all of them to concentrate on Uncle Tupelo. While [moonlighting](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/moonlight "wiktionary:moonlight") as a record store clerk at Euclid Records in St. Louis,[\[11\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jeff_Tweedy#cite_note-RiverfrontTimes-SolidSoundFest-2011-11)[\[12\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jeff_Tweedy#cite_note-StLouisMag-Redemption-2011-12) Tweedy met Tony Margherita. After Margherita saw the band perform at an acoustic concert in 1988, he decided to become the band's manager. The band began playing regular shows at Cicero's basement bar in the [Delmar Loop](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Delmar_Loop "Delmar Loop") near [Washington University in St. Louis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Washington_University_in_St._Louis "Washington University in St. Louis") with other bands playing in a similar style.[\[1\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jeff_Tweedy#cite_note-Kot-LHTD-2004-1):24–26[\[10\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jeff_Tweedy#cite_note-NoDepression-liner-notes-2003-10) Uncle Tupelo recorded a ten-track demo tape entitled _Not Forever, Just For Now_ in 1989, attracting the attention of [Giant/Rockville Records](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Giant_Records_(Warner) "Giant Records (Warner)"). The independent label signed the band, and Uncle Tupelo's first album, _[No Depression](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/No_Depression_(album) "No Depression (album)")_, was released the next year.[\[10\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jeff_Tweedy#cite_note-NoDepression-liner-notes-2003-10) The [title song](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/No_Depression_in_Heaven "No Depression in Heaven"), originally performed by the [Carter Family](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carter_Family "Carter Family"), became strongly associated with the [alternative country](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alternative_country "Alternative country") scene, and became the name of an influential alternative country periodical called _[No Depression](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/No_Depression_(magazine) "No Depression (magazine)")_.

During times when Uncle Tupelo was not touring, Tweedy and Farrar played as Coffee Creek, a short-lived cover band with [The Bottle Rockets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Bottle_Rockets "The Bottle Rockets")' [Brian Henneman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brian_Henneman "Brian Henneman") and Mark Ortmann.[\[13\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jeff_Tweedy#cite_note-Request-AreYouReady-1993-13) Around this time, Tweedy began developing problems with alcohol, leading to tensions between Tweedy and Farrar. While he never refused to play a gig, Tweedy was forced to sit out in place of Henneman at some performances. Tweedy quit drinking entirely after meeting future wife Sue Miller, although he replaced this habit with smoking [marijuana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cannabis_(drug) "Cannabis (drug)"). However, after developing a dependence on marijuana, he quickly quit using it, as well.[\[1\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jeff_Tweedy#cite_note-Kot-LHTD-2004-1):51–53,95 After releasing _[Still Feel Gone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Still_Feel_Gone "Still Feel Gone")_, the band formed a friendship with [Peter Buck](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_Buck "Peter Buck") of [R.E.M.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/R.E.M. "R.E.M."), who produced their third album _[March 16–20, 1992](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/March_16%E2%80%9320,_1992 "March 16–20, 1992")_ for free.[\[1\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jeff_Tweedy#cite_note-Kot-LHTD-2004-1) Uncle Tupelo left the Rockville label in favor of [Sire Records](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sire_Records "Sire Records") ( [Warner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warner_Brothers "Warner Brothers")) later in 1992 because Rockville refused to pay the band any royalties for their albums.[\[1\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jeff_Tweedy#cite_note-Kot-LHTD-2004-1):72 After the signing, [Max Johnston](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Max_Johnston_(musician) "Max Johnston (musician)") and [John Stirratt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Stirratt "John Stirratt") joined the band as Mike Heidorn was replaced by Bill Belzer who was later replaced by [Ken Coomer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ken_Coomer "Ken Coomer").[\[1\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jeff_Tweedy#cite_note-Kot-LHTD-2004-1):74–75 The five-piece band recorded _[Anodyne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anodyne_(album) "Anodyne (album)")_, which sold over 150,000 copies and debuted at number 18 on the _[Billboard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Billboard_(magazine) "Billboard (magazine)")_ [Heatseekers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Top_Heatseekers "Top Heatseekers") chart, but was the last album Uncle Tupelo released.[\[1\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jeff_Tweedy#cite_note-Kot-LHTD-2004-1):80[\[14\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jeff_Tweedy#cite_note-Billboard-Heatseekers-1993-14)

#### Breakup

In January 1994, Farrar called Tony Margherita to tell him that the band was breaking up, saying that he was not having any fun in the band anymore and was not getting along with Tweedy. Tweedy was enraged that Farrar decided to break up the band without notifying him, and this led to a series of harsh verbal exchanges. Farrar and Tweedy agreed to a final Uncle Tupelo tour, but the concerts were marred by the two not participating in each other's songs. The band decided to play Tweedy's "The Long Cut" on _[Late Night with Conan O'Brien](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Late_Night_with_Conan_O'Brien "Late Night with Conan O'Brien")_, which further distanced Farrar and Tweedy.[\[1\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jeff_Tweedy#cite_note-Kot-LHTD-2004-1):80–84 Farrar began to assemble a new band named [Son Volt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Son_Volt "Son Volt") with Mike Heidorn, bassist Jim Boquist, and his brother Dave Boquist. At the same time, Jeff Tweedy formed [Wilco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wilco "Wilco") with Stirratt, Johnston, and Coomer.[\[1\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jeff_Tweedy#cite_note-Kot-LHTD-2004-1):88,90

### Wilco (1995–present)

Main article: [Wilco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wilco "Wilco")

In 1994, Tweedy formed [Wilco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wilco "Wilco") with [John Stirratt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Stirratt "John Stirratt"), [Max Johnston](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Max_Johnston_(musician) "Max Johnston (musician)"), and [Ken Coomer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ken_Coomer "Ken Coomer"). Wilco has released twelve albums and found commercial success with their albums _[Yankee Hotel Foxtrot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yankee_Hotel_Foxtrot "Yankee Hotel Foxtrot")_, _[A Ghost Is Born](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A_Ghost_Is_Born "A Ghost Is Born")_, _[Sky Blue Sky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sky_Blue_Sky "Sky Blue Sky")_ and _[Wilco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wilco_(The_Album) "Wilco (The Album)")_. The band also released two collaboration albums with [Billy Bragg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Billy_Bragg "Billy Bragg") and one with [The Minus 5](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Minus_5 "The Minus 5"). Jeff Tweedy has been the recipient of two [Grammy Awards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grammy_Awards "Grammy Awards"), including Best Alternative Album for _A Ghost Is Born_. Tweedy has also participated in a number of side groups including [Golden Smog](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golden_Smog "Golden Smog") and [Loose Fur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Loose_Fur "Loose Fur"), published a book of poems, and released a DVD of solo performances. He was originally influenced by punk and country music, but has later reflected more experimental themes in his music.

[![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/5/5f/Wilco_-_Roskilde_Festival_2007.jpg/500px-Wilco_-_Roskilde_Festival_2007.jpg)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Wilco_-_Roskilde_Festival_2007.jpg) Tweedy singing with Wilco in 2007

Wilco signed with [Reprise](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reprise_Records "Reprise Records")/ [Warner Bros. Records](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warner_Records "Warner Records") and began recording _[AM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A.M._(Wilco_album) "A.M. (Wilco album)")_ almost as soon as the band was formed.[\[1\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jeff_Tweedy#cite_note-Kot-LHTD-2004-1):89–91 After recording, Tweedy was introduced to [Jay Bennett](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jay_Bennett "Jay Bennett"), who then joined the band. Also during this time, Tweedy quit smoking marijuana after a particularly bad experience with some [cannabis brownies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cannabis_brownie "Cannabis brownie").[\[1\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jeff_Tweedy#cite_note-Kot-LHTD-2004-1):94–96_A.M._ did not fare as well commercially in comparison to Son Volt's first album, only reaching number 27 on the Heatseekers chart while Son Volt's debut _[Trace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trace_(Son_Volt_album) "Trace (Son Volt album)")_ hit the [_Billboard_ 200](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Billboard_200 "Billboard 200").[\[15\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jeff_Tweedy#cite_note-Billboard-Heatseekers-1995-15)[\[16\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jeff_Tweedy#cite_note-Billboard-SonVolt-16) [Dan Murphy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dan_Murphy_(musician) "Dan Murphy (musician)") of [Soul Asylum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soul_Asylum "Soul Asylum") invited Tweedy to join him in a supergroup named [Golden Smog](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golden_Smog "Golden Smog") with [Gary Louris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gary_Louris "Gary Louris") and Marc Perlman of the [Jayhawks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jayhawks_(band) "Jayhawks (band)"), Kraig Johnson of Run Westy Run, and Noah Levy of [The Honeydogs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Honeydogs "The Honeydogs"). Under the pseudonym Scott Summit, Tweedy released _[Down by the Old Mainstream](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Down_by_the_Old_Mainstream "Down by the Old Mainstream")_ with Golden Smog in 1996.[\[1\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jeff_Tweedy#cite_note-Kot-LHTD-2004-1):105–106

Tweedy and Wilco began to explore new styles and broke from the style of previous recordings on the seminal sprawling double album _[Being There](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Being_There_(Wilco_album) "Being There (Wilco album)")_ in 1996. Tweedy did not write music for many of the songs ahead of time, and welcomed unexpected sounds into the recording. Wilco recorded nineteen songs for the double-CD album, and wanted the label to release it with a retail price comparable to a single-CD release.[\[1\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jeff_Tweedy#cite_note-Kot-LHTD-2004-1):113,116_Being There_ was a commercial success, selling 300,000 copies and peaking in the top half of the _Billboard_ 200.[\[17\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jeff_Tweedy#cite_note-17) Reprise records invested $100,000 in the single "Outta Mind (Outtasite)", but received little radio exposure.[\[1\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jeff_Tweedy#cite_note-Kot-LHTD-2004-1):125[\[18\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jeff_Tweedy#cite_note-18) While on tour, Tweedy began to spend time reading books by [William H. Gass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_H._Gass "William H. Gass"), [Henry Miller](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_Miller "Henry Miller"), and [John Fante](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Fante "John Fante"). As he read their books, Tweedy decided to place more of an emphasis on writing.[\[1\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jeff_Tweedy#cite_note-Kot-LHTD-2004-1):136 Representatives in the A&R department of Reprise wanted a radio single from _Summerteeth_, and Wilco reluctantly agreed to a re-working of " [Can't Stand It](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Can't_Stand_It "Can't Stand It")". The single was a top five hit on [adult album alternative](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adult_album_alternative "Adult album alternative") radio stations, but failed to cross over to a larger audience.[\[1\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jeff_Tweedy#cite_note-Kot-LHTD-2004-1):162–167

Before the release of _Summerteeth_, the daughter of the late folk legend Woody Guthrie contacted [folk rock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Folk_rock "Folk rock") singer [Billy Bragg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Billy_Bragg "Billy Bragg"), who in turn contacted Tweedy about recording an album of unreleased [Woody Guthrie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Woody_Guthrie "Woody Guthrie") songs. Tweedy was indifferent to the idea of working with Bragg, but Jay Bennett's enthusiasm about the idea convinced Tweedy to get the band involved in the project. As a result of Tweedy's feelings on the political nature of some of the lyrics, Bragg recorded mostly political songs while Wilco recorded more neutral songs.\[ _[citation needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed "Wikipedia:Citation needed")_\] Almost all of the songs that appeared on _[Mermaid Avenue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mermaid_Avenue "Mermaid Avenue")_ and _[Mermaid Avenue Vol. II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mermaid_Avenue_Vol._II "Mermaid Avenue Vol. II")_ were recorded over a six-day period in December 1997.[\[1\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jeff_Tweedy#cite_note-Kot-LHTD-2004-1):142–145 The first _Mermaid Avenue_ album and a second Golden Smog album ( _[Weird Tales](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weird_Tales_(album) "Weird Tales (album)")_) were released in 1998, _Summerteeth_ was released in early 1999, and _Mermaid Avenue Vol. II_ was released in 2000. Tweedy received his first Grammy nomination when _Mermaid Avenue_ was nominated for [Grammy Award for Best Contemporary Folk Album](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grammy_Award_for_Best_Contemporary_Folk_Album "Grammy Award for Best Contemporary Folk Album") in 1999.[\[19\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jeff_Tweedy#cite_note-MilwaukeeJournalSentinel-Grammys-1999-19)

#### _Yankee Hotel Foxtrot_

[![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/8/85/Wilco_MassMoca_AustinNelson.jpg/500px-Wilco_MassMoca_AustinNelson.jpg)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Wilco_MassMoca_AustinNelson.jpg) Wilco at MASS MoCA

Jeff Tweedy was invited to play at Chicago's Noise Pop festival, and was told that he could collaborate with a musician of his choosing. Tweedy chose [Jim O'Rourke](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jim_O'Rourke_(musician) "Jim O'Rourke (musician)") based on his fascination with O'Rourke's _[Bad Timing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bad_Timing_(Jim_O'Rourke_album) "Bad Timing (Jim O'Rourke album)")_ album. O'Rourke offered to bring drummer [Glenn Kotche](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glenn_Kotche "Glenn Kotche") to the festival, and the trio formed a side project named [Loose Fur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Loose_Fur "Loose Fur"). The other band members of Wilco had written a number of songs for _[Yankee Hotel Foxtrot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yankee_Hotel_Foxtrot "Yankee Hotel Foxtrot")_, but Tweedy was unsatisfied with them because he believed that the songs did not sound like the ones he played with Loose Fur. Tweedy became such a fan of Kotche's playing style that he decided to dismiss Ken Coomer from the band in favor of Kotche.[\[1\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jeff_Tweedy#cite_note-Kot-LHTD-2004-1):176–183,188 Tweedy had strong feelings about how songs should be sequenced, which clashed with Jay Bennett's focus on the songs themselves. Because Bennett was mixing the album, this led to a series of arguments between songs about how the album should sound. Tweedy asked O'Rourke to remix several songs on the album that had been mixed by Bennett, which caused tensions within the band to escalate. The album was completed in June 2001, and Tweedy was insistent that it was in its final form.[\[1\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jeff_Tweedy#cite_note-Kot-LHTD-2004-1):197–200[\[20\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jeff_Tweedy#cite_note-IATTBYH-Documentary-2006-20) Tweedy also fired Bennett around this time, believing (according to Bennett) that Wilco should only have one core member.[\[20\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jeff_Tweedy#cite_note-IATTBYH-Documentary-2006-20) The band maintains that the firing of Bennett was a collective decision.[\[21\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jeff_Tweedy#cite_note-Pitchfork-Interview-2007-21)

Reprise Records' parent company [Time Warner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Time_Warner "Time Warner") merged with [America Online](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/America_Online "America Online") in 2001, and the recording company was asked to cut costs. [Howie Klein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Howie_Klein "Howie Klein"), the CEO of Reprise Records, considered Wilco to be one of the label's core bands, but was offered a lucrative buy-out by AOL Time Warner. Reprise did not consider the album commercially viable and was not interested in releasing the album. David Kahne (Head of A&R) agreed to release Wilco from Reprise under the condition that Wilco got to keep all legal entitlements to the _Yankee Hotel Foxtrot_ album.[\[1\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jeff_Tweedy#cite_note-Kot-LHTD-2004-1):201–207[\[20\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jeff_Tweedy#cite_note-IATTBYH-Documentary-2006-20) After an article in the _[Chicago Tribune](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chicago_Tribune "Chicago Tribune")_ publicly described these managerial practices, CEO Gary Briggs quit.[\[1\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jeff_Tweedy#cite_note-Kot-LHTD-2004-1):208–209 Shortly after leaving the label, Briggs remarked:

> It \[dropping Wilco from the label\] should never have happened. One of the most embarrassing moments in my career at Warner Brothers was the day they let Wilco go. It broke my heart, and it told me that I no longer have a home there.[\[1\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jeff_Tweedy#cite_note-Kot-LHTD-2004-1):208–209

_Yankee Hotel Foxtrot_ was originally scheduled to be released on Reprise on September 11, 2001, prior to the band's departure from Reprise. Seven days later, Tweedy decided that he would stream the entirety of _Yankee Hotel Foxtrot_ on Wilco's official website.[\[1\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jeff_Tweedy#cite_note-Kot-LHTD-2004-1):225 Over thirty record labels offered to release _Yankee Hotel Foxtrot_ after the departure from Reprise was official.[\[22\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jeff_Tweedy#cite_note-22) One of the thirty was Warner Brothers affiliate [Nonesuch Records](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nonesuch_Records "Nonesuch Records"), who signed Wilco in November 2001. AOL Time Warner paid Wilco to make the album on Reprise, gave them the record for free, and then bought it back on the Nonesuch label.[\[1\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jeff_Tweedy#cite_note-Kot-LHTD-2004-1):209–210 The album was released on April 23, 2002 to significant critical acclaim, including being named the best album of the year by _[The Village Voice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Village_Voice "The Village Voice")_.[\[23\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jeff_Tweedy#cite_note-Pitchfork-YHF-2002-23)[\[24\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jeff_Tweedy#cite_note-VillageVoice-PazzJop-2002-24) The album became the biggest hit of Jeff Tweedy's career and was certified gold by the [Recording Industry Association of America](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Recording_Industry_Association_of_America "Recording Industry Association of America") for selling over 500,000 copies.[\[25\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jeff_Tweedy#cite_note-RIAA-DBsearch-2006-25)

#### _A Ghost Is Born_

[![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/e/e0/Jefftweedyps07.jpg)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Jefftweedyps07.jpg) Tweedy in 2007

[Scott McCaughey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scott_McCaughey "Scott McCaughey") contacted Tweedy about recording an album together for a [The Minus 5](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Minus_5 "The Minus 5") release. They scheduled a meeting for September 11, 2001, but were reluctant to enter the recording studio after the [terrorist attacks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/September_11,_2001_attacks "September 11, 2001 attacks"). At night, McCaughey and Tweedy decided to begin recording songs as a way to calm down. A few more tracks were later added to the album with the rest of Wilco, and it was released with the name _[Down with Wilco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Down_with_Wilco "Down with Wilco")_ in 2003.[\[1\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jeff_Tweedy#cite_note-Kot-LHTD-2004-1):220–222

In November 2003, Wilco began recording a fifth studio album. Unlike their previous albums, all of the songs were originally performed in the studio and then later adapted for playing at concerts. Wilco released _[A Ghost Is Born](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A_Ghost_Is_Born "A Ghost Is Born")_ on June 22, 2004, and it attained a top ten peak on the [_Billboard_ 200](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Billboard_200 "Billboard 200").[\[26\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jeff_Tweedy#cite_note-MTVNews-Charts-2004-26) The album was awarded with Grammy Awards for [Best Alternative Music Album](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Best_Alternative_Music_Album "Best Alternative Music Album") and [Best Recording Package](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Best_Recording_Package "Best Recording Package") in 2005.[\[27\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jeff_Tweedy#cite_note-AP-Grammys-2005-27) A few weeks before the album's release, Tweedy released a book of forty-three poems entitled _Adult Head_ on Zoo Press.[\[28\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jeff_Tweedy#cite_note-ChicagoSunTimes-Poetic-2004-28) The following year, the band released their first live album, a two-disc set entitled _[Kicking Television: Live in Chicago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kicking_Television:_Live_in_Chicago "Kicking Television: Live in Chicago")_, recorded at [The Vic Theater](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Vic_Theater "The Vic Theater").[\[29\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jeff_Tweedy#cite_note-29)

#### Later Wilco albums

Wilco recorded twelve tracks for a sixth studio album entitled _[Sky Blue Sky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sky_Blue_Sky "Sky Blue Sky")_, which was released on May 15, 2007.[\[30\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jeff_Tweedy#cite_note-Billboard-SBS-2007-30) _Sky Blue Sky_ debuted at number four on the _Billboard_ 200, the band's highest debut yet. It sold over 87,000 copies in its first week of release.[\[31\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jeff_Tweedy#cite_note-Billboard-Chart-2007-31)

In early May 2009, former Wilco member [Jay Bennett](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jay_Bennett "Jay Bennett") sued Tweedy for breach of contract.[\[32\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jeff_Tweedy#cite_note-MemphisFlyer-LivingLife-2009-32) Bennett died later that month of an accidental overdose of the painkiller [fentanyl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fentanyl "Fentanyl").[\[33\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jeff_Tweedy#cite_note-ChicagoTribune-JayBennett-2009-33) In June 2009 Wilco released their seventh studio album _[Wilco (The Album)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wilco_(The_Album) "Wilco (The Album)")_, followed by _[The Whole Love](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Whole_Love "The Whole Love")_ in 2011, [_Star Wars_](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Star_Wars_(Wilco_album) "Star Wars (Wilco album)") in 2015, _[Schmilco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Schmilco "Schmilco")_ in 2016, _[Ode to Joy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ode_to_Joy_(Wilco_album) "Ode to Joy (Wilco album)")_ in 2019, _[Cruel Country](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cruel_Country "Cruel Country")_ in May 2022, and _[Cousin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cousin_(album) "Cousin (album)")_ in 2023.

### Other work

Jeff Tweedy has performed several solo tours, on which he typically plays acoustic music. He also recorded the song "Simple Twist of Fate" for the soundtrack to _[I'm Not There](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/I'm_Not_There "I'm Not There")_.[\[34\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jeff_Tweedy#cite_note-RollingStone-LoungeAx-1998-34) On October 24, 2006, Nonesuch Records released _[Sunken Treasure: Live in the Pacific Northwest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sunken_Treasure:_Live_in_the_Pacific_Northwest "Sunken Treasure: Live in the Pacific Northwest")_, a live DVD by Tweedy. The disc includes performances and conversations gathered over five nights on Tweedy's February 2006 solo acoustic tour, with footage from concerts at Seattle's Moore Theater, Portland's [Crystal Ballroom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crystal_Ballroom_(Portland,_Oregon) "Crystal Ballroom (Portland, Oregon)"), Eugene's McDonald Theatre, Arcata's Humboldt State University, and [The Fillmore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Fillmore "The Fillmore") in San Francisco. The DVD was directed by Christoph Green and [Fugazi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fugazi "Fugazi")'s Brendan Canty, the creators of the documentary series _[Burn to Shine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Burn_to_Shine_(DVD_series) "Burn to Shine (DVD series)")_.[\[35\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jeff_Tweedy#cite_note-35)

Tweedy has partnered with [Mavis Staples](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mavis_Staples "Mavis Staples") on three acclaimed albums. In 2010 they released _[You Are Not Alone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/You_Are_Not_Alone_(Mavis_Staples_album) "You Are Not Alone (Mavis Staples album)")_, in 2013, _[One True Vine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/One_True_Vine "One True Vine")_, and in 2017 _[If All I Was Was Black](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/If_All_I_Was_Was_Black "If All I Was Was Black")_. Tweedy played an array of instruments on these albums and wrote many of the songs.[\[36\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jeff_Tweedy#cite_note-RollingStone-OneTrueVine-2013-36)

Tweedy worked with the psychedelic-influenced garage rock group [White Denim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/White_Denim "White Denim") on their record _[Corsicana Lemonade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corsicana_Lemonade "Corsicana Lemonade")_, recording some songs at Wilco's Chicago studio The Loft.[\[37\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jeff_Tweedy#cite_note-Relix-WhiteDenim-2013-37) In 2015 the album _[Still](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Still_(Richard_Thompson_album) "Still (Richard Thompson album)")_ by [Richard Thompson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_Thompson_(musician) "Richard Thompson (musician)") was released. The album was produced by Tweedy in The Loft Studios and features Tweedy on guitar, keyboards and backing vocals.[\[38\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jeff_Tweedy#cite_note-Aqua-38)

Tweedy has appeared as a fictional singer-songwriter on 2014 episodes of _[Parks and Recreation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parks_and_Recreation "Parks and Recreation")_ and _[Portlandia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portlandia_(TV_series) "Portlandia (TV series)")_.[\[39\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jeff_Tweedy#cite_note-39)

On June 4, 2014, it was announced that he had formed a new band called [Tweedy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tweedy_(band) "Tweedy (band)") with his son Spencer. The band's debut album _[Sukierae](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sukierae "Sukierae")_ was released on September 16. The release was followed by a world tour in which half of the set consisted of new songs off _Sukierae_ performed by a touring band including Spencer. The latter half of the set Tweedy plays solo, typically performing Wilco and Uncle Tupelo classics.[\[40\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jeff_Tweedy#cite_note-Pitchfork-Sukierae-2014-40)[\[41\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jeff_Tweedy#cite_note-Grantland-Sukierae-2014-41)

In June 2017 Jeff Tweedy released a solo acoustic album of eleven songs spanning his career from [Wilco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wilco "Wilco"), [Loose Fur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Loose_Fur "Loose Fur"), and [Golden Smog](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golden_Smog "Golden Smog") titled _[Together at Last](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Together_at_Last "Together at Last")_.[\[42\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jeff_Tweedy#cite_note-42) In November 2018 Jeff Tweedy released a memoir titled _[Let's Go (So We Can Get Back)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Let's_Go_(So_We_Can_Get_Back) "Let's Go (So We Can Get Back)")_ and his first solo album of new material, titled _[Warm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warm_(Jeff_Tweedy_album) "Warm (Jeff Tweedy album)")_.[\[43\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jeff_Tweedy#cite_note-:0-43) A companion album to _Warm_ titled _[Warmer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warmer_(Jeff_Tweedy_album) "Warmer (Jeff Tweedy album)")_ was released on April 13, 2019, as a [Record Store Day](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Record_Store_Day "Record Store Day") exclusive.[\[44\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jeff_Tweedy#cite_note-44)

P. Dutton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/E._P._Dutton "E. P. In September 2025, Tweedy released a triple-album of new solo material, _[Twilight Override](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twilight_Override "Twilight Override")_.[\[49\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jeff_Tweedy#cite_note-49) He wrote the album over a two year period and recorded the 30 tracks in collaboration with friends and both of his sons, Sammy and Spencer.[\[50\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jeff_Tweedy#cite_note-CSMOct25-50)

## Musical style

[![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/c/c9/Tweedy_Kotche_2012.jpg/500px-Tweedy_Kotche_2012.jpg)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Tweedy_Kotche_2012.jpg) Jeff Tweedy and Glenn Kotche Wilco at Susquehanna Bank Center XPoNential Music Festival 2012

Tweedy's musical style has varied over his music career. Tweedy's vocal style is considered nasal, emotional, and raspy, and has been compared to that of [Neil Young](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neil_Young "Neil Young").[\[7\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jeff_Tweedy#cite_note-NYTimes-LHTD-2004-7)[\[51\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jeff_Tweedy#cite_note-BBC-AGIB-2002-51) His first exposure to music was through [gramophone records](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gramophone_record "Gramophone record") that his siblings left behind when they attended college, and he particularly liked [The Beatles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Beatles "The Beatles")' [White Album](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Beatles_(album) "The Beatles (album)"). Tweedy would frequently read issues of magazines such as _[Rolling Stone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rolling_Stone "Rolling Stone")_, and began to purchase [punk rock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Punk_rock "Punk rock") albums such as [The Clash](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Clash "The Clash")'s _[London Calling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/London_Calling "London Calling")_ and [X](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/X_(U.S._band) "X (U.S. band)")'s _[Wild Gift](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wild_Gift "Wild Gift")_. Belleville crowds did not respond well to punk music, so while Tweedy was a member of The Primitives they played covers of [country](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Country_music "Country music") songs at much faster tempos.[\[1\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jeff_Tweedy#cite_note-Kot-LHTD-2004-1):13–19 When Uncle Tupelo formed, the band began composing its own songs influenced by [Jason & the Scorchers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jason_&_the_Scorchers "Jason & the Scorchers") and [The Minutemen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Minutemen_(band) "The Minutemen (band)").[\[1\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jeff_Tweedy#cite_note-Kot-LHTD-2004-1) Wilco's first album shared many musical similarities with the four previous Uncle Tupelo albums, but on _Being There_, Tweedy began introducing more experimental themes into his music. He claims that he wanted to rebel against the belief spread by the _No Depression_ magazine that Wilco was primarily a country band.[\[1\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jeff_Tweedy#cite_note-Kot-LHTD-2004-1):110–111 One of the most influential albums for Tweedy was _[Bad Timing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bad_Timing_(Jim_O'Rourke_album) "Bad Timing (Jim O'Rourke album)")_ by Jim O'Rourke, which helped to inspire _Yankee Hotel Foxtrot_ and _A Ghost Is Born_.[\[1\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jeff_Tweedy#cite_note-Kot-LHTD-2004-1):176–177 Tweedy uses a 1957 Gibson J-45 acoustic guitar, as well as a 1965 Fender Jazzmaster, at least three different Telecasters, an Epiphone Casino, a Rickenbacker 360, and a Gibson SG Standard. He has vintage SGs from '62 and '65 as well as a 2007 Custom Shop model and a 2008 Custom Shop Vintage Original Spec (V.O.S.) that are all rigged with Maestro tremolo bars.[\[52\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jeff_Tweedy#cite_note-Gibson-Sound-2009-52) He also has been known to use a Breedlove 000[\[53\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jeff_Tweedy#cite_note-GuitarWorld-RadioHead-2002-53) and even designed a limited edition 000 for Breedlove in 2007.[\[54\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jeff_Tweedy#cite_note-PremierGuitar-Breedlove-2007-54) His amplifier of choice is a Vox AC30.

## Personal life

Tweedy has been prone to having [migraines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Migraines "Migraines") throughout his life; he has missed as many as forty days of elementary school in one year with the condition.[\[1\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jeff_Tweedy#cite_note-Kot-LHTD-2004-1):50[\[20\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jeff_Tweedy#cite_note-IATTBYH-Documentary-2006-20) While he attempted to regulate the use of painkillers, he was never able to stop using them for more than five weeks. He attributes that to a [comorbidity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Comorbidity "Comorbidity") with [major depressive disorder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Major_depressive_disorder "Major depressive disorder") and severe [panic attacks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Panic_attacks "Panic attacks").[\[55\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jeff_Tweedy#cite_note-NYTimes-Migraine-2008-55) In 2004, he entered a [dual diagnosis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dual_diagnosis "Dual diagnosis") rehabilitation clinic in order to receive treatment for an addiction to prescription [painkiller](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Painkiller "Painkiller") [Vicodin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vicodin "Vicodin").[\[56\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jeff_Tweedy#cite_note-Filter-Interview-2004-56)[\[57\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jeff_Tweedy#cite_note-RollingStone-Rehab-2004-57)[\[58\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jeff_Tweedy#cite_note-58) Tweedy quit smoking the next year, which [John Stirratt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Stirratt "John Stirratt") claimed afterward significantly improved the focus of the band.[\[59\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jeff_Tweedy#cite_note-ChicagoTribune-Simpler-2007-59)[\[60\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jeff_Tweedy#cite_note-60)

Tweedy is married to former bookkeeper Sue Miller and lives in the [Irving Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irving_Park,_Chicago "Irving Park, Chicago") area of Chicago.[\[61\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jeff_Tweedy#cite_note-:1-61) He first met Miller when he was trying to get Uncle Tupelo booked at [Cubby Bear](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Cubby_Bear "The Cubby Bear"), where Miller worked. Miller later co-owned [Lounge Ax](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lounge_Ax "Lounge Ax"), which booked [Uncle Tupelo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uncle_Tupelo "Uncle Tupelo") for 16 shows over four years. They began dating in 1991 and were married on August 9, 1995.[\[1\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jeff_Tweedy#cite_note-Kot-LHTD-2004-1):53,96 She was diagnosed with cancer in 2014. During a 2015 interview with _Rolling Stone_, Tweedy said "she's doing great now." Tweedy also said that music is a healthy distraction in such difficult times.[\[62\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jeff_Tweedy#cite_note-62)

Tweedy and Miller have two sons: [Spencer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spencer_Tweedy "Spencer Tweedy") and Sammy. Spencer has been involved in numerous musical projects including The Blisters, Tully Monster, and a project with his father, [Tweedy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tweedy_(band) "Tweedy (band)").[\[63\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jeff_Tweedy#cite_note-ChicagoTribune-EricZorn-2005-63) In 2008, Spencer joined Wilco on stage at [Madison Square Garden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Madison_Square_Garden "Madison Square Garden") to play drums on their song _The Late Greats_ while opening for [Neil Young](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neil_Young "Neil Young").[\[64\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jeff_Tweedy#cite_note-RollingStone-BoyGenius-2008-64) Both Spencer and Sammy feature on Tweedy's 2025 [triple album](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Triple_album "Triple album") [Twilight Override](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twilight_Override "Twilight Override"),[\[65\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jeff_Tweedy#cite_note-65) as well as in the live band for Tweedy's 2026 [KEXP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KEXP "KEXP") show.[\[66\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jeff_Tweedy#cite_note-66)

Tweedy has [converted](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conversion_to_Judaism "Conversion to Judaism") to Judaism.[\[67\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jeff_Tweedy#cite_note-67) His wife is [Jewish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jewish_people "Jewish people"); their sons both had [Bar Mitzvah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bar_Mitzvah "Bar Mitzvah") ceremonies.[\[68\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jeff_Tweedy#cite_note-Uncut-Audience-2013-68) During the ceremony for his older son, Tweedy played an acoustic version of [Bob Dylan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bob_Dylan "Bob Dylan")'s " [Forever Young](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forever_Young_(Bob_Dylan_song) "Forever Young (Bob Dylan song)")".[\[69\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jeff_Tweedy#cite_note-NYT-RockofAges-2009-69)

In November 2019 Tweedy's home in Irving Park was shot at least seven times in an attack described by his son as "not targeted".[\[61\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jeff_Tweedy#cite_note-:1-61) No injuries were reported.
