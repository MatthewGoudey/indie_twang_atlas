**Sparklehorse** was an American [indie rock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indie_rock "Indie rock") band from Richmond, Virginia, led by singer and multi-instrumentalist [Mark Linkous](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mark_Linkous "Mark Linkous"). It was active from 1995 until Linkous's death in 2010. Linkous said he chose the name Sparklehorse because the two words sounded good together and could be a loose metaphor for a motorcycle. At its inception, members of Sparklehorse included Paul Watson (banjo, cornet, lap steel and electric guitar), Scott Minor (drums, chord organ, banjo), Johnny Hott (Wurlitzer organ, percussion, backing vocals), and Scot Fitzsimmons (standup bass).

## History

Sparklehorse's first album, _[Vivadixiesubmarinetransmissionplot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vivadixiesubmarinetransmissionplot "Vivadixiesubmarinetransmissionplot")_ (1995), produced by [Cracker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cracker_(band) "Cracker (band)") frontman [David Lowery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Lowery_(musician) "David Lowery (musician)") (credited as "David Charles" on the record), was a modest [college radio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/College_radio "College radio") success. In 1996, while touring Europe with [Radiohead](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radiohead "Radiohead") shortly after the album's release, Linkous consumed a combination of [anti-depressants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anti-depressant "Anti-depressant"), [valium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Valium "Valium"), [alcohol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alcohol_(drug) "Alcohol (drug)"), and [heroin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heroin "Heroin") in a [London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/London "London") hotel room.[\[5\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sparklehorse#cite_note-5) Unconscious and with his legs pinned beneath him for almost 14 hours, the resulting [potassium build-up](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crush_syndrome "Crush syndrome") caused his heart to stop for several minutes after his body was lifted up. The ensuing surgery almost caused him to [lose the use of both legs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paralysis "Paralysis") and, as a result, he needed to use a [wheelchair](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wheelchair "Wheelchair") for six months and required dialysis for acute kidney failure.

_[Good Morning Spider](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Good_Morning_Spider "Good Morning Spider")_ (1998) was recorded following this incident. Critics have conjectured that Linkous's brush with death inspired the album's somber tone, but Linkous said that much of _GMS_ had already been written.\[ _[citation needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed "Wikipedia:Citation needed")_\] One song that resulted from his affliction is "St. Mary", which is dedicated to the nurses at [the eponymous hospital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St_Mary's_Hospital,_London "St Mary's Hospital, London") in [Paddington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paddington "Paddington") where Linkous recuperated.

In 1999 Sparklehorse performed at the Horseshoe Tavern in Toronto.[\[6\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sparklehorse#cite_note-6) 2001 saw the release of _[It's a Wonderful Life](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/It's_a_Wonderful_Life_(album) "It's a Wonderful Life (album)")_, featuring appearances by [Tom Waits](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tom_Waits "Tom Waits"), [PJ Harvey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PJ_Harvey "PJ Harvey"), [Bob Rupe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bob_Rupe "Bob Rupe"), [Nina Persson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nina_Persson "Nina Persson") and [Dave Fridmann](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dave_Fridmann "Dave Fridmann").[\[7\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sparklehorse#cite_note-7) Much of _Vivadixie..._ and _Spider_ were recorded solely by Linkous on his Virginia farm, but the new album was more collaborative. Linkous expressed his satisfaction with the overall sound of _It's a Wonderful Life_, which was engineered by [Joel Hamilton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joel_Hamilton "Joel Hamilton"), while also saying he would have preferred to include more experimental and instrumental material.[\[8\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sparklehorse#cite_note-8)

On September 25, 2006, Sparklehorse released its fourth album, _[Dreamt for Light Years in the Belly of a Mountain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dreamt_for_Light_Years_in_the_Belly_of_a_Mountain "Dreamt for Light Years in the Belly of a Mountain")_, collaborating with [Danger Mouse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Danger_Mouse_(musician) "Danger Mouse (musician)"), [\[9\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sparklehorse#cite_note-9) [Christian Fennesz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christian_Fennesz "Christian Fennesz"), and [Steven Drozd](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steven_Drozd "Steven Drozd"). This album featured the radio release "Don't Take My Sunshine Away" and a remastered version of "Shade And Honey", which Linkous originally wrote for [Alessandro Nivola](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alessandro_Nivola "Alessandro Nivola") to sing in the 2003 movie _[Laurel Canyon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laurel_Canyon_(film) "Laurel Canyon (film)")_, as well as a virtually unchanged re-release of "Morning Hollow", the bonus track on _It's a Wonderful Life_.

In 2008, Sparklehorse recorded a cover of "Jack's Obsession" from _[Tim Burton's The Nightmare Before Christmas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tim_Burton's_The_Nightmare_Before_Christmas "Tim Burton's The Nightmare Before Christmas")_ for the official compilation album _[Nightmare Revisited](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nightmare_Revisited "Nightmare Revisited")_.

In 2009, Sparklehorse teamed up with [Danger Mouse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Danger_Mouse_(musician) "Danger Mouse (musician)") and [David Lynch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Lynch "David Lynch") on the project _[Dark Night of the Soul](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Danger_Mouse_and_Sparklehorse_Present:_Dark_Night_of_the_Soul "Danger Mouse and Sparklehorse Present: Dark Night of the Soul")_. Corliss et al. (2010) described _Dark Night of the Soul_ as “spooky, beautiful, (and)... bittersweet...considering Linkous’ untimely death.”[\[10\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sparklehorse#cite_note-10)

In 2009, Linkous collaborated with electronic [ambient-music](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ambient_music "Ambient music") artist [Christian Fennesz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christian_Fennesz "Christian Fennesz") to create _[In the Fishtank 15](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/In_the_Fishtank_15 "In the Fishtank 15")_, a wafting EP of experimentation and dreamy atmospherics. In October 2009, Linkous performed with Fennesz during a European tour.

Linkous died by suicide in [Knoxville, Tennessee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Knoxville,_Tennessee "Knoxville, Tennessee"), on March 6, 2010, effectively ending the band.[\[11\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sparklehorse#cite_note-11) In 2023, _[Bird Machine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bird_Machine "Bird Machine")_ – the Sparklehorse album Linkous was working on at the time of his death – was completed by Linkous' brother and sister-in-law, Matt and Melissa Linkous, and released on 8 September.[\[12\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sparklehorse#cite_note-announce-12)

## Legacy

In March 2020, [_Spin_ magazine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spin_(magazine) "Spin (magazine)") wrote that Linkous was more respected by his peers, such as [PJ Harvey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PJ_Harvey "PJ Harvey"), [Nina Persson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nina_Persson "Nina Persson") and [Tom Waits](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tom_Waits "Tom Waits"), than recognized by the record-buying public.[\[16\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sparklehorse#cite_note-16)
