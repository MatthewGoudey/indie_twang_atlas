_**GodWeenSatan: The Oneness**_ is the debut studio album by American rock band [Ween](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ween "Ween"), released on November 16, 1990, by [Twin/Tone Records](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twin/Tone_Records "Twin/Tone Records").[\[4\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GodWeenSatan:_The_Oneness#cite_note-:0-4) The album introduces several key themes for the group, including their eclecticism, gonzo sense of humor, and their demon god/ [mascot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mascot "Mascot"), the Boognish.

The album contains several tracks that are long time staples of Ween's live performances, such as "You Fucked Up", "Fat Lenny", "Marble Tulip Juicy Tree", and "L.M.L.Y.P."

## Background

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=GodWeenSatan:_The_Oneness&action=edit&section=1 "Edit section: Background")\]

### Writing

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=GodWeenSatan:_The_Oneness&action=edit&section=2 "Edit section: Writing")\]

The album was written and performed by [Aaron Freeman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gene_Ween "Gene Ween") and [Mickey Melchiondo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dean_Ween "Dean Ween"), who began writing and recording together when they were 14.[\[5\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GodWeenSatan:_The_Oneness#cite_note-5) They soon adopted the name Ween and began recording home tapes. The apparent earliest _GodWeenSatan_ track, "I Gots a Weasel," first appeared on their 1985 _Ween WAD_ EP in an extended form.[\[6\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GodWeenSatan:_The_Oneness#cite_note-6)

At age 16[\[7\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GodWeenSatan:_The_Oneness#cite_note-7) Ween released their first album-length tape, _The Crucial Squeegie Lip,_ on Birdo'pray Records, which featured a short version of "You Fucked Up."[\[8\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GodWeenSatan:_The_Oneness#cite_note-8)

On January 10, 1987, Ween played their earliest known live show at 'Weenstock,' a small event held in Freeman's basement. Five months later, they began playing at more prominent venues, such as [City Gardens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City_Gardens "City Gardens") in Trenton, NJ.[\[9\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GodWeenSatan:_The_Oneness#cite_note-9) Shortly afterward, Ween released their second album-length tape, _Ween II (Axis: Bold as Boognish),_ which featured a slower version of "Bumblebee."[\[10\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GodWeenSatan:_The_Oneness#cite_note-10)

Between September and November 1988, Ween recorded a home demo tape referred to as _Scraping the Palm for Guava_, which contained early versions of four _GodWeenSatan_ songs, "Mushroom Festival in Hell," _"_ Licking the Palm for Guava," "Up on the Hill" and "Don't Laugh (I Love You)."[\[11\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GodWeenSatan:_The_Oneness#cite_note-11)

On January 2, 1989,[\[12\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GodWeenSatan:_The_Oneness#cite_note-12) Ween played a show at Pranzatelli's Stereo and TV in [Bound Brook](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bound_Brook,_New_Jersey "Bound Brook, New Jersey").[\[13\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GodWeenSatan:_The_Oneness#cite_note-13) The show featured the earliest known versions of several _GodWeenSatan_ songs. These included fully formed versions of "Tick" and "Licking the Palm for Guava," shorter unfinished versions of "LMLYP" & "Nan," and extended versions of "Don't Laugh (I Love You)" and "El Camino."[\[14\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GodWeenSatan:_The_Oneness#cite_note-14) An [FM broadcast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FM_broadcasting "FM broadcasting") of Ween playing at [The Rathskellar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Rathskeller "The Rathskeller") on April 18, 1989, showcases Ween performing half of the album's tracks live. The recordings notably feature the earliest known versions of "Never Squeal," "Cold and Wet," "Common Bitch,'' "Old Queen Cole," "Papa Zit," "Squelch the Weasel," and "Fat Lenny."[\[15\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GodWeenSatan:_The_Oneness#cite_note-15)

In January 1990, Ween moved into the Pod, where they continued to record several home tapes. A majority of the released material from these tapes appeared on their second album, _[The Pod](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Pod "The Pod")_, but songs like "Birthday Boy," "Squelch the Weasel," "Blackjack," & "Puffy Cloud[\[16\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GodWeenSatan:_The_Oneness#cite_note-16) appeared on _GodWeenSatan_.

Dean Ween later referred to the album as a 'greatest hits' of material the duo had written during their first six years. He used a 1958 [Fender Musicmaster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fender_Musicmaster "Fender Musicmaster") during recording.[\[17\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GodWeenSatan:_The_Oneness#cite_note-:1-17)

### Release

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=GodWeenSatan:_The_Oneness&action=edit&section=3 "Edit section: Release")\]

By the time the album was released in November of 1990,[\[4\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GodWeenSatan:_The_Oneness#cite_note-:0-4) the duo had already recorded _[The Pod](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Pod "The Pod")_. _GodWeenSatan_ would be Ween's only release on [Twin/Tone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twin/Tone_Records "Twin/Tone Records").

[Restless Records](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Restless_Records "Restless Records") reissued the album on September 11, 2001,[\[17\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GodWeenSatan:_The_Oneness#cite_note-:1-17) jokingly referring to it as the "25th anniversary edition", despite the album only being eleven years old at the time. The reissue featured digitally remastered sound, new packaging, and the inclusion of three bonus tracks—"Bumblebee Part 2", "Stacey", and "Hippy Smell"—integrated into the original album's playlist. The band performed the album in its entirety on September 14, 2001.

In 2016, Ween released _[GodWeenSatan Live](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GodWeenSatan_Live "GodWeenSatan Live"),_ a live recording from a show recorded on September 14, 2001, during which the group performed the entirety of the album.[\[17\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GodWeenSatan:_The_Oneness#cite_note-:1-17)[\[18\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GodWeenSatan:_The_Oneness#cite_note-18)[\[19\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GodWeenSatan:_The_Oneness#cite_note-19) It was their first live release since 2008.[\[20\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GodWeenSatan:_The_Oneness#cite_note-20)[\[21\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GodWeenSatan:_The_Oneness#cite_note-21) The album was released near the time of _The Deaner Album_ by Melchiondo's side project, The Dean Ween Group.[\[22\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GodWeenSatan:_The_Oneness#cite_note-22)

Professional ratings

[David Browne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Browne_(journalist) "David Browne (journalist)"), writing for _[Entertainment Weekly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Entertainment_Weekly "Entertainment Weekly")_, praised the album's silliness and frequently ranting nature, writing: "As it veers uncontrollably from the stupid to the unlistenable, _God Ween Satan_ becomes the energizing sound of two street-corner nutjobs railing as best they can against the entire world. Equal opportunity for clever morons to punish the masses with the aid of modern recording equipment — it’s truly a wonderful thing."[\[24\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GodWeenSatan:_The_Oneness#cite_note-Browne-24) Andrew Perry of _[Select](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Select_(magazine) "Select (magazine)")_ wrote that Ween derived their "sonic dementia" from [post-hardcore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Post-hardcore "Post-hardcore"), but that the band's stylistic range was much wider, including parodies of [bubblegum pop](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bubblegum_pop "Bubblegum pop") and [lovers rock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lovers_rock "Lovers rock"). He concluded that the "anarchic" album was "unrealistically varied and never boring. You won't have heard the like of this before".[\[28\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GodWeenSatan:_The_Oneness#cite_note-Perry-28)

In a retrospective review, _[Pitchfork](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pitchfork_(website) "Pitchfork (website)")_'s Matt LeMay wrote: "Unafraid to say 'fuck' for no apparent reason, unafraid to rock out on cheesy [metal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heavy_metal_music "Heavy metal music") riffs, and unafraid to pick to pieces just about every variety of music, Ween managed to capture the essence of their sound on their debut as well, if not better than, on any later album."[\[26\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GodWeenSatan:_The_Oneness#cite_note-pitchfork-26) _[Uncut](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uncut_(magazine) "Uncut (magazine)")_ describe the album as containing pastiches of "anything on the rock planet", with Ween offending and delighting the listener "in equal measure".[\[31\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GodWeenSatan:_The_Oneness#cite_note-Uncut-31) Heather Phares of [AllMusic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AllMusic "AllMusic") described _GodWeenSatan_ as "almost as eclectic and inspired" as Ween's subsequent albums, with a palpable sense of fun that makes it "more than just a promising debut".[\[23\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GodWeenSatan:_The_Oneness#cite_note-AllMusic-23) Less favorable was [Robert Christgau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Christgau "Robert Christgau") in _[The Village Voice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Village_Voice "The Village Voice")_, who quipped of "L.M.L.Y.P." that Ween "went on about pussy for nine minutes (good idea) in a [Princey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prince_(musician) "Prince (musician)") blues-minstrel drawl (bad one)".[\[32\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GodWeenSatan:_The_Oneness#cite_note-Christgau-32)

In Punk News' review of _[The Mollusk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Mollusk "The Mollusk")_, _GodWeenSatan_ and _[The Pod](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Pod "The Pod")_ were said to have carved Ween's 'expansive niche in experimental fits that had numerous great ideas, but lacked focus.[\[33\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GodWeenSatan:_The_Oneness#cite_note-33) Far Out Magazine's retrospective on _The Mollusk_ similarly states that the duo's first two albums featured nothing that "even hinted at anything that could be seen as ‘commercial.’"[\[34\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GodWeenSatan:_The_Oneness#cite_note-34)
