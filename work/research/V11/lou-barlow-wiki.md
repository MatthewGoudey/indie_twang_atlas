Musical artist

**Louis Knox Barlow** (born July 17, 1966) is an American [alternative rock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alternative_rock "Alternative rock") musician and songwriter. A founding member of the groups [Dinosaur Jr.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dinosaur_Jr. "Dinosaur Jr."), [Sebadoh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sebadoh "Sebadoh") and [The Folk Implosion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Folk_Implosion "The Folk Implosion"), Barlow is credited[\[1\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lou_Barlow#cite_note-trouser-1) with helping to pioneer the [lo-fi style of rock music](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slacker_rock "Slacker rock") in the late 1980s and early 1990s. His first band, which was formed in [Amherst, Massachusetts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amherst,_Massachusetts "Amherst, Massachusetts"), was [Deep Wound](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deep_Wound "Deep Wound").

Barlow has released four solo albums.

## Early life

Barlow was born in [Dayton, Ohio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dayton,_Ohio "Dayton, Ohio") on July 17, 1966, and raised in [Jackson, Michigan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jackson,_Michigan "Jackson, Michigan"), and [Westfield, Massachusetts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Westfield,_Massachusetts "Westfield, Massachusetts").[\[2\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lou_Barlow#cite_note-2)[\[3\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lou_Barlow#cite_note-3)

## Dinosaur Jr.

See also: [Dinosaur Jr.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dinosaur_Jr. "Dinosaur Jr.")

Barlow attended high school in Westfield, Massachusetts, where he met [Scott Helland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scott_Helland "Scott Helland"). The two formed the Massachusetts-based [hardcore punk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hardcore_punk "Hardcore punk") band [Deep Wound](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deep_Wound "Deep Wound"). [J Mascis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/J_Mascis "J Mascis") joined the band after answering their ad for a "drummer wanted to play really fast".[\[4\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lou_Barlow#cite_note-4) After becoming disillusioned with the constraints of hardcore, Deep Wound broke up in 1984. Mascis and Barlow reunited that year to form Dinosaur, later [Dinosaur Jr.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dinosaur_Jr. "Dinosaur Jr.") Mascis and Barlow had personality conflicts throughout Dinosaur Jr.'s early existence, and after the 1988 release of their third album, _[Bug](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bug_(Dinosaur_Jr._album) "Bug (Dinosaur Jr. album)")_, and the initial supporting tour, Barlow was kicked out of the band.[\[5\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lou_Barlow#cite_note-5)

In 2005, Barlow rejoined the band alongside the original drummer, [Murph](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Murph_(drummer) "Murph (drummer)"). Since then, the band has reissued its first three records, toured extensively worldwide and released five new records, _[Beyond](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beyond_(Dinosaur_Jr._album) "Beyond (Dinosaur Jr. album)")_, _[Farm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Farm_(album) "Farm (album)")_, _[I Bet on Sky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/I_Bet_on_Sky "I Bet on Sky")_, _[Give a Glimpse of What Yer Not](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Give_a_Glimpse_of_What_Yer_Not "Give a Glimpse of What Yer Not")_, and _[Sweep It Into Space](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sweep_It_Into_Space "Sweep It Into Space")_.[\[6\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lou_Barlow#cite_note-:0-6)

## Sebadoh, Sentridoh and the Folk Implosion

See also: [Sebadoh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sebadoh "Sebadoh") and [The Folk Implosion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Folk_Implosion "The Folk Implosion")

After his dismissal from Dinosaur Jr., Barlow turned his attention to his band [Sebadoh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sebadoh "Sebadoh"), which he had formed earlier with multi-instrumentalist [Eric Gaffney](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eric_Gaffney "Eric Gaffney"). The project featured low [fidelity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fidelity "Fidelity") recording techniques and combined Barlow's introspective, confessional songwriting with Gaffney's discordant noise collages.[\[6\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lou_Barlow#cite_note-:0-6) Bassist and songwriter [Jason Loewenstein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jason_Loewenstein "Jason Loewenstein") was added to the line-up in 1989. Sebadoh's early releases include _[The Freed Man](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Freed_Man "The Freed Man")_ (1989) and _[Weed Forestin'](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weed_Forestin' "Weed Forestin'")_ (1990), the latter of which was originally self-released under the name Sentridoh in 1987. Both albums were officially released by [Homestead Records](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homestead_Records "Homestead Records"), as was the band's third album, _[Sebadoh III](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sebadoh_III "Sebadoh III")_ (1991), which helped establish the " [lo-fi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lo-fi_(music) "Lo-fi (music)")" subgenre and became a defining album of 90s [indie rock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indie_rock "Indie rock").[\[7\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lou_Barlow#cite_note-7) The band released several studio-recorded albums on [Sub Pop Records](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sub_Pop_Records "Sub Pop Records") throughout the 1990s.

As Sebadoh grew in popularity and critical acclaim, Barlow continued work on the Sentridoh side project, which featured mostly home-recorded material similar to his output on the first three Sebadoh albums, but often recorded solo and with a less consistent sound quality. Sentridoh released a trio of [cassette](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cassette_tape "Cassette tape")-only albums on Shrimper Records in the early 1990s, with the highlights later being collected on CD and [vinyl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vinyl_record "Vinyl record") compilations like _Winning Losers: A Collection of Home Recordings 89–93_ (1994) and _Another Collection of Home Recordings_ (1994). In 1993, Sentridoh released a popular [single](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Music_single "Music single") for the song "Losercore," on the label [Smells Like Records](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smells_Like_Records "Smells Like Records") founded by [Steve Shelley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steve_Shelley "Steve Shelley") of the American rock band, [Sonic Youth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sonic_Youth "Sonic Youth"). Barlow has called it "the most finely executed of all my releases" thanks to Shelley, who "made sure this looked and sounded great."[\[8\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lou_Barlow#cite_note-8) Numerous other Sentridoh releases on a variety of record labels followed, including releases on his own Loobiecore label.

In 1994, Barlow formed the [Folk Implosion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Folk_Implosion "Folk Implosion") with singer-songwriter John Davis. The band released several singles and [EPs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Extended_play "Extended play"), and in 1995 reached the [Top 40](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Top_40 "Top 40") with the song " [Natural One](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natural_One "Natural One")", from the soundtrack to the film _[Kids](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kids_(film) "Kids (film)")_ by [Larry Clark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Larry_Clark "Larry Clark"). It remains Barlow's biggest commercial hit.

After the release of Sebadoh's eighth album, _[The Sebadoh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Sebadoh "The Sebadoh")_, in 1999, the band went on hiatus and its members went on to pursue other projects. Barlow continued to work with the Folk Implosion, releasing _[One Part Lullaby](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/One_Part_Lullaby "One Part Lullaby")_ in 1999. Barlow took a break from the Folk Implosion in 2000 to collaborate on the album _Subsonic 6_ with [Belgian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belgians "Belgians") musician, [Rudy Trouvé](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rudy_Trouv%C3%A9 "Rudy Trouvé"). In 2003, Barlow released _[The New Folk Implosion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_New_Folk_Implosion "The New Folk Implosion")_ featuring [Imaad Wasif](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imaad_Wasif "Imaad Wasif") on guitar and Sebadoh drummer [Russ Pollard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russ_Pollard "Russ Pollard") on drums. The same year, he appeared as a musician in the film _[Laurel Canyon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laurel_Canyon_(film) "Laurel Canyon (film)")_. In the spring of 2004, Barlow briefly reunited with Loewenstein for the "Turbo Acoustic" Sebadoh tour. During this tour, he also reunited with J Mascis for a performance of the song "Video Prick" with former Deep Wound vocalist Charlie Nakajima. This performance led to a full-fledged [Dinosaur Jr.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dinosaur_Jr. "Dinosaur Jr.") reunion in 2005, with original members Barlow, Mascis and Murph performing "The Lung" on _[The Late Late Show with Craig Ferguson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Late_Late_Show_with_Craig_Ferguson "The Late Late Show with Craig Ferguson")_ on April 15, 2005, and a show at [Spaceland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spaceland "Spaceland") in Los Angeles the following night. The band then played well-received tours of the U.S. and Europe throughout the rest of the year, and in 2006 headed to Japan, Australia and [New Zealand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Zealand "New Zealand"). In 2007, Barlow reunited with Loewenstein and Gaffney to perform the first Sebadoh shows with the "classic" lineup in 14 years. In June 2013, Sebadoh released their first new music in 14 years; the music was released as an EP precursor to their new album, "Defend Yourself," which was released in September 2013. Both of the new EP and LP albums were released through [Joyful Noise Recordings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joyful_Noise_Recordings "Joyful Noise Recordings").[\[9\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lou_Barlow#cite_note-9)

## Solo work

Barlow released the first album under his own name, _[Emoh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emoh "Emoh")_, in January 2005 on [Merge Records](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Merge_Records "Merge Records"). It featured long-time collaborators such as Sebadoh members Jason Loewenstein and Russ Pollard and Lou's sister Abby Barlow, and featured a higher production value than many of his previous solo releases. Jason Crock of _[Pitchfork](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pitchfork_(website) "Pitchfork (website)")_ called it "the most consistently strong record he's released since the Folk Implosion's _One Part Lullaby_" and wrote that "even if much of it was recorded at home, _Emoh'_ s 14 unassuming [folk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Folk_music "Folk music") songs sound like they were created in a professional setting."[\[10\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lou_Barlow#cite_note-10) In November 2005, Barlow toured the [Iberian Peninsula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iberian_Peninsula "Iberian Peninsula") to promote the album.

Barlow released his second solo album, _[Goodnight Unknown](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Goodnight_Unknown "Goodnight Unknown")_, in October 2009 on Merge. It was produced by [Andrew Murdock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andrew_Murdock "Andrew Murdock") and featured numerous guest musicians, including Imaad Wasif on guitar and [Dale Crover](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dale_Crover "Dale Crover") of [the Melvins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Melvins "The Melvins") and Murph on drums. Barlow toured the album in the U.S. in the fall of 2009, opening for Dinosaur Jr. and backed by The Missingmen of [Mike Watt + the Missingmen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mike_Watt_+_the_Missingmen "Mike Watt + the Missingmen").

Barlow's third solo album, _Brace the Wave_, was released on September 4, 2015, on [Joyful Noise Recordings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joyful_Noise_Recordings "Joyful Noise Recordings"). It was recorded in six days with Justin Pizzoferrato, who also worked as a sound engineer for Dinosaur Jr. at Sonelab Studios in Massachusetts. The tracks from _Brace the Wave_ feature Barlow's ukulele playing. Barlow also took 100 original Polaroid [selfies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Selfies "Selfies") to accompany the VIP vinyl edition of the album.[\[11\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lou_Barlow#cite_note-joy-11)[\[12\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lou_Barlow#cite_note-minsker-12)

On October 28, 2016, Barlow released a 5 track EP entitled _Apocalypse Fetish_ on [Joyful Noise Recordings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joyful_Noise_Recordings "Joyful Noise Recordings")

In April 2021, it was announced that a solo album entitled _[Reason to Live](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reason_to_Live_(album) "Reason to Live (album)")_ would be released on May 28 via Joyful Noise.[\[13\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lou_Barlow#cite_note-13)

## Personal life

Barlow's first marriage was to Kathleen Billus, with whom he has two children, Hendrix and Hannelore.[\[6\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lou_Barlow#cite_note-:0-6)

In May 2015, he married knitwear designer Adelle Louise Burda, with whom he has a daughter, Izetta.[\[14\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lou_Barlow#cite_note-14)

Barlow has suffered from [social anxiety](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_anxiety "Social anxiety") and low [self-esteem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Self-esteem "Self-esteem").[\[15\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lou_Barlow#cite_note-15)
