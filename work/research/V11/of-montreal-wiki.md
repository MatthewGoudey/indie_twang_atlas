**Of Montreal** is an American [indie pop](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indie_pop "Indie pop") band from [Athens, Georgia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Athens,_Georgia "Athens, Georgia"). It was founded by frontperson [Kevin Barnes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kevin_Barnes "Kevin Barnes") in 1996, named after a failed romance between Barnes and a woman "of [Montreal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Montreal "Montreal")". The band is identified as part of the [Elephant 6](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elephant_6 "Elephant 6") collective.

Throughout its existence, of Montreal's[\[a\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Of_Montreal#cite_note-1) musical style has evolved considerably and drawn inspiration from 1960s [psychedelic pop](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Psychedelic_pop "Psychedelic pop") acts. The band's twentieth album was released in June 2026.

## History

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Of_Montreal&action=edit&section=1 "Edit section: History")\]

### 1996–1998

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Of_Montreal&action=edit&section=2 "Edit section: 1996–1998")\]

[Kevin Barnes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kevin_Barnes "Kevin Barnes") founded of Montreal, allegedly naming it for a failed romance with a woman from [Montreal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Montreal "Montreal"), [Quebec](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quebec "Quebec").[\[1\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Of_Montreal#cite_note-beepscalifornian-2) Barnes was the only member of the group prior to their[\[b\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Of_Montreal#cite_note-4) relocation to Athens, Georgia. There, they met [Derek Almstead](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Derek_Almstead "Derek Almstead") ( [Circulatory System](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Circulatory_System_(band) "Circulatory System (band)"), [Marshmallow Coast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marshmallow_Coast "Marshmallow Coast"), [Elf Power](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elf_Power "Elf Power")) and [Bryan Poole](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bryan_Poole "Bryan Poole"), who also performs as [The Late B.P. Helium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Late_B.P._Helium "The Late B.P. Helium"). Together, they recorded their debut album, _[Cherry Peel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cherry_Peel "Cherry Peel")_, and subsequent releases _[The Bird Who Ate the Rabbit's Flower](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Bird_Who_Ate_the_Rabbit's_Flower "The Bird Who Ate the Rabbit's Flower")_ and _[The Bedside Drama: A Petite Tragedy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Bedside_Drama:_A_Petite_Tragedy "The Bedside Drama: A Petite Tragedy")_.

After production on _[The Gay Parade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Gay_Parade "The Gay Parade")_, the band's third album, began in 1998, Poole left the band to focus on his involvement with Elf Power, another [Elephant Six](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elephant_Six "Elephant Six") band.[\[1\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Of_Montreal#cite_note-beepscalifornian-2) Barnes then recruited [James Husband](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Husband_(music_project) "James Husband (music project)") and [Dottie Alexander](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dottie_Alexander "Dottie Alexander"), who had been performing together as Lightning Bug vs. Firefly.[\[3\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Of_Montreal#cite_note-opticaldottie-5) Almstead moved from drums to bass, and Marshmallow Coast's Andy Gonzales joined soon after. Prior to the release of _The Gay Parade_ in 1999, of Montreal released a number of singles and a re-release of _The Bird Who Continues to Eat the Rabbit's Flower_.

### 1999–2006

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Of_Montreal&action=edit&section=3 "Edit section: 1999–2006")\]

Following the release of _The Gay Parade_, the band signed with [Kindercore Records](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kindercore_Records "Kindercore Records"), and in 2001, the [concept album](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Concept_album "Concept album") _[Coquelicot Asleep in the Poppies: A Variety of Whimsical Verse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coquelicot_Asleep_in_the_Poppies:_A_Variety_of_Whimsical_Verse "Coquelicot Asleep in the Poppies: A Variety of Whimsical Verse")_ was released.[\[4\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Of_Montreal#cite_note-6)

Kindercore Records folded in 2002, shortly after the release of _[Aldhils Arboretum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aldhils_Arboretum "Aldhils Arboretum")_, and Gonzales and Almstead left the band. Barnes took to writing and performing their 2004 album _[Satanic Panic in the Attic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Satanic_Panic_in_the_Attic "Satanic Panic in the Attic")_ mostly by themselves. It was released by [Polyvinyl Records](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polyvinyl_Records "Polyvinyl Records"). In 2005 _[The Sunlandic Twins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Sunlandic_Twins "The Sunlandic Twins")_ was released.

### 2007–2012

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Of_Montreal&action=edit&section=4 "Edit section: 2007–2012")\]

[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/6/69/OfMontrealLive.JPG/250px-OfMontrealLive.JPG?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:OfMontrealLive.JPG) of Montreal performing in [Athens, Georgia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Athens,_Georgia "Athens, Georgia") (2007)

Barnes recorded most of the band's 2007 release, _[Hissing Fauna, Are You the Destroyer?](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hissing_Fauna,_Are_You_the_Destroyer%3F "Hissing Fauna, Are You the Destroyer?")_, by themselves while living in [Norway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norway "Norway") and Athens, Georgia. They also released a companion CD to _Hissing Fauna, Are You the Destroyer?_ called _[Icons, Abstract Thee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Icons,_Abstract_Thee "Icons, Abstract Thee")._[\[5\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Of_Montreal#cite_note-AversionSchild-7) In 2007, Kevin Barnes performed five songs nude during a performance in Las Vegas.[\[6\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Of_Montreal#cite_note-8)

Of Montreal's next album, _[Skeletal Lamping](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Skeletal_Lamping "Skeletal Lamping")_, was released on October 21, 2008. It was released in ten different formats, including conventional CD and vinyl, as well as T-shirts, button sets, [wall decals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wall_decals "Wall decals"), tote bags, and a paper lantern, the latter formats complete with a digital download code for the album.[\[7\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Of_Montreal#cite_note-9) All items for the collection were designed by The Bee With Wheels (David Barnes) and Gemini Tactics (Nina Barnes). In an essay addressing the concept behind the album's release, Kevin Barnes stated, "We feel that there's no reason to produce another object that just sits on a shelf. We only want to produce objects that have a function and that can be treasured for their singularness."[\[8\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Of_Montreal#cite_note-10) In an interview with [NPR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Public_Radio "National Public Radio"), Kevin Barnes revealed that the concept of the CD may be going the way of the [8-track](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/8-track_tape "8-track tape") in favor of MP3 downloads. David Barnes joked that fans would buy the album via MP3 download and the version that comes with an amazing sandwich.[\[9\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Of_Montreal#cite_note-GANZ-11)

In August 2009, the band contributed a T-shirt design, which was printed and sold through the [Yellow Bird Project](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yellow_Bird_Project "Yellow Bird Project") website to raise money for the St. Judes Children's Hospital. The shirt was designed by David Barnes.[\[10\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Of_Montreal#cite_note-12)

During this era, the band lent its songs many times to commercials, television programs, and films. The song "Wraith Pinned to the Mist (And Other Games)" can be heard on radio and television commercials (with words changed) for an advertisement for [Outback Steakhouse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Outback_Steakhouse "Outback Steakhouse").[\[11\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Of_Montreal#cite_note-13) Similarly, their song "Every Day Feels Like Sunday" was used in television commercials for [NASDAQ](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NASDAQ "NASDAQ").[\[12\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Of_Montreal#cite_note-14) "A Sentence of Sorts In Kongsvinger" was used in a [Comcast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Comcast "Comcast") commercial.[\[13\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Of_Montreal#cite_note-15) "Gronlandic Edit" was used for a [T-Mobile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/T-Mobile_International_AG "T-Mobile International AG") commercial which also featured the band as actors.[\[14\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Of_Montreal#cite_note-16)

Teaming up with [Jon Brion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jon_Brion "Jon Brion"), Kevin Barnes traveled to [Ocean Way Recording](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ocean_Way_Recording "Ocean Way Recording") to record _[False Priest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/False_Priest "False Priest")_.[\[15\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Of_Montreal#cite_note-17)

On October 25, 2011, of Montreal released a Cassette Box Set of all 10 full-length albums via [Joyful Noise Recordings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joyful_Noise_Recordings "Joyful Noise Recordings"). The release was limited to 500 hand-numbered copies, and housed in a custom-built wooden box (screen-printed with original artwork from David Barnes). Each of the 10 cassettes were pressed on multi-colored tape shells and included original album art. Like their previous cassette release, MP3 download was included.[\[16\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Of_Montreal#cite_note-18)

Kevin Barnes had revealed some information on the following album in an interview with [Pitchfork Media](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pitchfork_Media "Pitchfork Media").[\[17\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Of_Montreal#cite_note-19) On October 20, 2011, Barnes announced that the next album was complete.[\[18\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Of_Montreal#cite_note-twitter2-20) In November 2011, the band released a new track titled "Wintered Debts" via the band's [SoundCloud](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SoundCloud "SoundCloud") site, a track off the new album _[Paralytic Stalks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paralytic_Stalks "Paralytic Stalks")_. In January 2012, of Montreal released "Dour Percentage," the first single from the new album. _Paralytic Stalks_ was released on February 7, 2012.[\[19\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Of_Montreal#cite_note-21)

On November 12, 2012, the band created a [Kickstarter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kickstarter "Kickstarter") page to help in the funding of a career-spanning, feature-length documentary called _[Song Dynasties](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Past_Is_a_Grotesque_Animal "The Past Is a Grotesque Animal")_. The film consists of footage collected throughout of Montreal's entire career, including touring and studio sessions, as well as other musicians such as [Andrew VanWyngarden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andrew_VanWyngarden "Andrew VanWyngarden") of [MGMT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MGMT "MGMT"). The tentative release window for _Song Dynasties_ was announced as March 2013.[\[20\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Of_Montreal#cite_note-Pitchfork_Kickstarter-22) An update regarding the documentary's progress was posted on the band's Facebook site in February.[\[21\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Of_Montreal#cite_note-23) The film was eventually released in June 2014 with the title "The Past Is a Grotesque Animal."[\[22\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Of_Montreal#cite_note-Pitchfork_Film-24)

### 2013–2018

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Of_Montreal&action=edit&section=5 "Edit section: 2013–2018")\]

[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/c/c7/Of_Montreal_%2C_Positivus_Music_Festival%2C_Latvia%2C_July_2014--64_%2817837297391%29.jpg/250px-Of_Montreal_%2C_Positivus_Music_Festival%2C_Latvia%2C_July_2014--64_%2817837297391%29.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Of_Montreal_,_Positivus_Music_Festival,_Latvia,_July_2014--64_(17837297391).jpg) of Montreal performing at the Positivus Music Festival in Riga last 2014

On April 25, 2013, of Montreal posted an announcement on their Facebook page stating that a new album titled _[Lousy with Sylvianbriar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lousy_with_Sylvianbriar "Lousy with Sylvianbriar")_ had been completed.[\[23\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Of_Montreal#cite_note-25) The post went into detail about the album's conception, including that Barnes' writing occurred while on a " self imposed isolation experiment in San Francisco" in early 2013. They were influenced by [Sylvia Plath](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sylvia_Plath "Sylvia Plath"), [the Grateful Dead](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Grateful_Dead "The Grateful Dead"), [Neil Young](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neil_Young "Neil Young"), and [the Flying Burrito Brothers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Flying_Burrito_Brothers "The Flying Burrito Brothers") in their writing. The album was recorded with a new lineup of musicians, including Jojo Glidewell, Nicolas Dobbratz, Bennett Lewis, and Bob Parins, with Clayton Rychlik and Kevin Barnes as the only returning members. The album and subsequent tour also featured Rebecca Cash on vocals. _Lousy with Sylvianbriar_ was released on October 8, 2013. On July 10, 2013, the band released the first single from the album, entitled "Fugitive Air."[\[24\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Of_Montreal#cite_note-26) On July 31, 2013, they announced that they would be touring their new album in October and November.[\[25\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Of_Montreal#cite_note-27)
This tour featured the same musicians that performed on the album, instead of longtime band members such as [Bryan Poole](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Late_B.P._Helium "The Late B.P. Helium") and [Dottie Alexander](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dottie_Alexander "Dottie Alexander").\[ _[citation needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed "Wikipedia:Citation needed")_\]

By means of a Facebook post and an interview with Barnes, conducted by _[Stereogum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stereogum "Stereogum")_, of Montreal's album _[Aureate Gloom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aureate_Gloom "Aureate Gloom")_ was announced. The record was influenced by "the mid-to-late 1970s music scene in New York," including bands such as [Talking Heads](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Talking_Heads "Talking Heads") and [Led Zeppelin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Led_Zeppelin "Led Zeppelin"). The album captures Barnes' emotions borne from their separation from their wife of 11 years and its aftermath. They described the album as being "all over the place musically", lending to its reflection of their mindset during the time of its creation. The album was released March 3, 2015.[\[26\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Of_Montreal#cite_note-stereogum.com-28)

In August 2016, of Montreal released _[Innocence Reaches](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Innocence_Reaches "Innocence Reaches")_, which incorporated new, [EDM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electronic_dance_music "Electronic dance music")-inspired sounds, as well as the progressive rock sounds of the previous two albums.[\[27\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Of_Montreal#cite_note-29) For the tour following the album's release, bassist Davey Pierce returned to the band, replacing Bob Parins.

On January 13, 2017, of Montreal released a new EP by surprise, entitled _[Rune Husk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rune_Husk "Rune Husk")_.[\[28\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Of_Montreal#cite_note-30) On March 9, 2018, the album _[White Is Relic/Irrealis Mood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/White_Is_Relic/Irrealis_Mood "White Is Relic/Irrealis Mood")_ was released on Polyvinyl, with singer Barnes explaining that the sound was influenced by "extended dance mixes" from the 1980s.[\[29\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Of_Montreal#cite_note-31)

### 2019–present

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Of_Montreal&action=edit&section=6 "Edit section: 2019–present")\]

The band released its sixteenth studio album, _[Ur Fun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ur_Fun "Ur Fun")_, on January 17, 2020.[\[30\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Of_Montreal#cite_note-urf-32) This was followed by the album, _[I Feel Safe with You, Trash](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/I_Feel_Safe_with_You,_Trash "I Feel Safe with You, Trash")_, on March 5, 2021,[\[31\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Of_Montreal#cite_note-beats-33) and _[Freewave Lucifer F<ck F^ck F>ck](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Freewave_Lucifer_Fck_Fck_Fck "Freewave Lucifer Fck Fck Fck")_ on July 29, 2022.

The band's nineteenth studio album, _[Lady on the Cusp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lady_on_the_Cusp "Lady on the Cusp")_, was released on May 17, 2024. It was preceded by three singles, "Yung Hearts Bleed Free", "Rude Girl on Rotation", and "Soporific Cell" released March 5, April 16, and May 14, 2024, respectively.

On April 2, 2026, the band released a single titled "When" and announced their twentieth studio album, _[aethermead](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aethermead "Aethermead")_, released on June 5 of the same year.[\[32\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Of_Montreal#cite_note-34) On May 5, they released their second single, "Already Dreaming" with a music video directed by Barnes daughter, Beatrice.[\[33\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Of_Montreal#cite_note-35) On June 2, the third single for _aethermead_ released, "Take the Form".[\[34\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Of_Montreal#cite_note-36)

## Side projects

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Of_Montreal&action=edit&section=9 "Edit section: Side projects")\]

True to the style of most Elephant Six recording artists, of Montreal's members have been in a variety of side projects with other bands:

- Kevin Barnes played keyboard for "The Difference in the Shades" on the [Bright Eyes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bright_Eyes_(band) "Bright Eyes (band)")' album _[Letting Off the Happiness](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Letting_Off_the_Happiness "Letting Off the Happiness")_.
- Kevin Barnes, Nina Barnes, and David Barnes did a comedy tour called " [A Pollinaire Rave](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A_Pollinaire_Rave "A Pollinaire Rave")".
- The band performed as the backing band for [Marshmallow Coast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marshmallow_Coast "Marshmallow Coast") on record and on tour.
- Dottie Alexander released the song "The You I Created" on the [Kindercore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kindercore_Records "Kindercore Records") singles club, under the pseudonym _My First Keyboard_; of Montreal acted as her backing band.
- Bryan Poole has a solo career as [the Late B.P. Helium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Late_B.P._Helium "The Late B.P. Helium").
- Davey Pierce and Travis Adams from the now-defunct emo band My Hotel Year, created the band [Inkwell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inkwell_(band) "Inkwell (band)") in 2004.[\[35\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Of_Montreal#cite_note-37)
- James Huggins released _[A Parallax I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A_Parallax_I "A Parallax I")_ under the name " [James Husband](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Husband_(music_project) "James Husband (music project)")" which featured a cover of The Beatles' " [We Can Work It Out](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/We_Can_Work_It_Out "We Can Work It Out")".
- Ahmed Gallab has a solo career as [Sinkane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sinkane "Sinkane"). He has played in the bands [Born Ruffians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Born_Ruffians "Born Ruffians"), [Caribou](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caribou_(musician) "Caribou (musician)"), [Yeasayer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yeasayer "Yeasayer"), and [Eleanor Friedberger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eleanor_Friedberger "Eleanor Friedberger").[\[36\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Of_Montreal#cite_note-38) He is also the music director and band leader of the [Atomic Bomb! Band](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atomic_Bomb!_Band "Atomic Bomb! Band").
- Derek Almstead has worked with many artists as a musician and engineer including his collaboration with Andy Gonzales [M Coast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M_Coast "M Coast"), [Elf Power](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elf_Power "Elf Power"), and [Circulatory System](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Circulatory_System_(band) "Circulatory System (band)").
- The group worked with artist [Janelle Monáe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Janelle_Mon%C3%A1e "Janelle Monáe") on a track called "Make The Bus" in her 2010 LP [The ArchAndroid (Suites II and III)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_ArchAndroid_(Suites_II_and_III) "The ArchAndroid (Suites II and III)").
- Kevin co-produced the 2012 [Solange Knowles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solange_Knowles "Solange Knowles") single " [Losing You](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Losing_You_(Solange_song) "Losing You (Solange song)")".[\[37\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Of_Montreal#cite_note-Village-39)
- K Ishibashi has a solo career as [Kishi Bashi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kishi_Bashi "Kishi Bashi") and toured as a supporting act with [Sondre Lerche](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sondre_Lerche "Sondre Lerche") in 2011.[\[38\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Of_Montreal#cite_note-40)
- Kevin Barnes produced and played on the album Marble Mouth by [Pillar Point](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pillar_Point_(band) "Pillar Point (band)").
