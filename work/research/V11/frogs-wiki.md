**The Frogs** are an alternative rock band founded in 1980, in [Milwaukee, Wisconsin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Milwaukee,_Wisconsin "Milwaukee, Wisconsin"), by brothers Jimmy and [Dennis Flemion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dennis_Flemion "Dennis Flemion"). The brothers mainly wrote and performed short, catchy [pop](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pop_music "Pop music") songs, but they are also known for their [improvised](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Improvised "Improvised") [home recordings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Home_recording "Home recording") that are delivered in a comedic style, and often include controversial issues such as [race](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Race_(classification_of_human_beings) "Race (classification of human beings)"), [religion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Religion "Religion"), and [sexuality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_sexual_behavior "Human sexual behavior") (notably [homoeroticism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homoeroticism "Homoeroticism")). A history of strife has hindered the band, although a devoted fan base that includes many well-known musicians has supported the group.

## History

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=The_Frogs_(band)&action=edit&section=1 "Edit section: History")\]

### Early years: 1980–1989

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=The_Frogs_(band)&action=edit&section=2 "Edit section: Early years: 1980–1989")\]

Brothers Jimmy Flemion and [Dennis Flemion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dennis_Flemion "Dennis Flemion") began playing as the "Gila Monsters" and "Stupid Frogs" in 1980, frequenting Milwaukee clubs and coffeehouses.[\[1\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Frogs_(band)#cite_note-encyclopedia-1)[\[2\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Frogs_(band)#cite_note-2)[\[3\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Frogs_(band)#cite_note-:0-3) In 1983, bassist Jay Tiller joined the group. That same year, Jimmy began wearing what was to become his trademark stage gimmick, a pair of six-foot [bat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bat "Bat") wings,[\[4\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Frogs_(band)#cite_note-allmusic-4) inspired by the band's set list, which at that time featured many [glam rock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glam_rock "Glam rock") songs about death and gloom. Soon the brothers also began wearing [wigs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wig "Wig") at their performances, and using [pyrotechnics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pyrotechnics "Pyrotechnics"). Hence, "wings and wigs" became synonymous with the band.

Although in their early days the Frogs played mostly in [coffeehouses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coffeehouse "Coffeehouse"),[\[3\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Frogs_(band)#cite_note-:0-3) they developed a larger following due mostly to their fan-friendly shows.\[ _[citation needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed "Wikipedia:Citation needed")_\]

In 1988, Brian Hill joined The Frogs as [bassist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bassist "Bassist") and would continue to make appearances with the group. That same year saw two significant events related to the Frogs' recording career. The first event was the release of the Frogs' debut [self-titled album](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Frogs_(album) "The Frogs (album)"), which they began recording in 1986.[\[5\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Frogs_(band)#cite_note-onion-5) Sources disagree on whether the album was [self-released](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Self-released "Self-released")[\[6\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Frogs_(band)#cite_note-thefrogsarchive-6) or released by Drag City Records;[\[1\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Frogs_(band)#cite_note-encyclopedia-1) it is undisputed, however, that only about 1,000 copies were pressed.[\[1\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Frogs_(band)#cite_note-encyclopedia-1)[\[6\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Frogs_(band)#cite_note-thefrogsarchive-6)

The second event ended up bringing the group to public attention: Jay Tiller gave a tape of the Frogs' improvised home recordings to his friend [Steve Albini](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steve_Albini "Steve Albini"), and the tape eventually made its way to [Gerard Cosloy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gerard_Cosloy "Gerard Cosloy"), then head of [Homestead Records](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homestead_Records_(1983) "Homestead Records (1983)"). Cosloy approached the band and offered to release an album. The Frogs were hoping that Homestead would re-release their first album, but Cosloy wanted to release an LP of the group's improvised home recordings, originally recorded on 4-track reel-to-reel with two microphones, which dealt with over-the-top homoeroticism. Since the tapes had not been intended to be released, the group hedged at first, but eventually they relented, and mixed a 14 track collection of songs. The result was _[It's Only Right and Natural](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/It's_Only_Right_and_Natural "It's Only Right and Natural")_, The Frogs' second LP, released in 1989. Songs like "Been a Month Since I Had a Man" and "Gather 'Round for Savior No.2" gave the album (and the band) a unique theme. Homestead announced that the Frogs were leaders in a "new gay supremacy movement," and the media ran with it.[\[1\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Frogs_(band)#cite_note-encyclopedia-1)

The album was received with mixed reactions; some listeners were ecstatic, while others, who either disliked the subject matter or misunderstood the concept, were not as enthusiastic.\[ _[citation needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed "Wikipedia:Citation needed")_\] In the gay press, writer Adam Block wrote, "I'd find the record a lot more remarkable if I believed the Frogs were actually gay."[\[7\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Frogs_(band)#cite_note-block89-7) _It's Only Right and Natural_ was even denounced by [Pat Robertson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pat_Robertson "Pat Robertson") on _[The 700 Club](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_700_Club "The 700 Club")_ and a number of [conservative](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conservative "Conservative") religious organizations as proof of [Satan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Satan "Satan")'s control over the entertainment industry.[\[8\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Frogs_(band)#cite_note-8)

### Grunge and success: 1990–2000

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=The_Frogs_(band)&action=edit&section=3 "Edit section: Grunge and success: 1990–2000")\]

In 1991, the Frogs produced a new album titled _[Racially Yours](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Racially_Yours "Racially Yours"),_ which included songs sung from the point of view of both blacks and whites who find themselves in various race-related situations and predicaments. The lyrics were equally serious and satirical. The Frogs' label [Homestead](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homestead_Records "Homestead Records") was unwilling to release the album (although the album was distributed freely by fans through bootlegs). It was eventually released in 2000 by the Four Alarm label. The Frogs' further plans for a live album went unfulfilled, as labels they pursued all seemed to be suffering through [bankruptcy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bankruptcy "Bankruptcy") and management problems. Jay Tiller left the band in 1992, to be replaced by Damian Strigens, although there were several substitutes in the meantime, including such notables as Eddie Roeser from [Urge Overkill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urge_Overkill "Urge Overkill") and [Kelley Deal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kelley_Deal "Kelley Deal") from [the Breeders](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Breeders "The Breeders").

In 1994, the Frogs followed [Gerard Cosloy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gerard_Cosloy "Gerard Cosloy") to a new label, [Matador Records](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matador_Records "Matador Records"), releasing two singles. They also recorded the [EP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Extended_play "Extended play") _[Starjob](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Starjob "Starjob"),_ produced by [Billy Corgan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Billy_Corgan "Billy Corgan") (who used the [pseudonym](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pseudonym "Pseudonym") _Johnny Goat_); however, the EP wouldn't be released until 1997.

In 1996, Matador released an album of old home-recorded and improvised Frogs material called _[My Daughter the Broad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/My_Daughter_the_Broad "My Daughter the Broad")._ It became a fan favorite of the band's material.

In 1997, _[Starjob](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Starjob "Starjob")_ was finally released, by [Scratchie Records](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scratchie_Records "Scratchie Records"), but the EP almost immediately went out of print when it failed to sell enough units to satisfy the label.\[ _[citation needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed "Wikipedia:Citation needed")_\] _[Starjob](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Starjob "Starjob")_ contains well-produced studio tracks, including "Lord Grunge" and one of the most well known Frogs songs, "I Only Play 4 Money."

In 1999, the Frogs covered " [Vacation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vacation_(The_Go-Go's_song) "Vacation (The Go-Go's song)")" by the Go-Go's for a tribute album.[\[9\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Frogs_(band)#cite_note-9) In that same year, the Four Alarm label issued another LP of the Frogs' old home-recorded and improvised material, called _[Bananimals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bananimals "Bananimals")_. To support these two releases, the Frogs played in [Canada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canada "Canada") for the first time. However, they were stopped at Canadian Customs as officials seized all of their [merchandise](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Merchandise "Merchandise"), declaring it "pornographic."\[ _[citation needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed "Wikipedia:Citation needed")_\] The Frogs had to play their Canadian shows without any merchandise to sell.\[ _[citation needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed "Wikipedia:Citation needed")_\]

The Frogs have gained a small but very devoted fan base. Although critics have attacked the Frogs for poor taste, well-known bands and performers have come to their defense.

#### Kurt Cobain and Nirvana

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=The_Frogs_(band)&action=edit&section=4 "Edit section: Kurt Cobain and Nirvana")\]

After meeting [Nirvana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nirvana_(band) "Nirvana (band)") singer [Kurt Cobain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kurt_Cobain "Kurt Cobain") in 1993, the Frogs wrote two songs about him and also made him a [videotape](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Videotape "Videotape"), _[Toy Porno](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toy_Porno "Toy Porno")._ The [VHS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/VHS "VHS") featured select live performances and [stop-motion animation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stop-motion_animation "Stop-motion animation") with painted [action figures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Action_figure "Action figure"). Action figures and dolls were used as sexually [promiscuous](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Promiscuous "Promiscuous") characters in various short sketches. The tape became constant viewing material on Nirvana's tour bus.\[ _[citation needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed "Wikipedia:Citation needed")_\] The videotape has since been made available to fans and has become a [cult classic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cult_classic "Cult classic").

#### Billy Corgan and The Smashing Pumpkins

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=The_Frogs_(band)&action=edit&section=5 "Edit section: Billy Corgan and The Smashing Pumpkins")\]

In 1993, [the Smashing Pumpkins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Smashing_Pumpkins "The Smashing Pumpkins")' frontman [Billy Corgan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Billy_Corgan "Billy Corgan") saw the Frogs at a small club in [Madison, Wisconsin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Madison,_Wisconsin "Madison, Wisconsin"), and invited them to open for The Pumpkins. The Frogs were soon opening for [Pearl Jam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pearl_Jam "Pearl Jam"), [Mudhoney](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mudhoney_(band) "Mudhoney (band)"), [Urge Overkill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urge_Overkill "Urge Overkill"), and other bands who were fans of the group, to mixed reaction.

In the [summer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Summer "Summer") of 1994, the Frogs played the second stage at [Lollapalooza](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lollapalooza "Lollapalooza"), with [Billy Corgan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Billy_Corgan "Billy Corgan") joining them for their encore of "I Only Play 4 Money" and "Lord Grunge." Corgan continued to support and promote the Frogs by producing a short film, "Meet the Frogs," which he included on the [Smashing Pumpkins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smashing_Pumpkins "Smashing Pumpkins")' 1994 _[Vieuphoria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vieuphoria "Vieuphoria")_ video compilation.

From August 1996 to February 1997, Dennis Flemion replaced [Smashing Pumpkins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smashing_Pumpkins "Smashing Pumpkins")' recently deceased keyboard player [Jonathan Melvoin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jonathan_Melvoin "Jonathan Melvoin") for the Pumpkins' Infinite Sadness Tour. During each night's encore, Jimmy Flemion performed " [1979](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1979_(song) "1979 (song)")" (which was influenced by an unreleased Frogs song, "Pleasure") with the [Smashing Pumpkins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smashing_Pumpkins "Smashing Pumpkins"), as well as selecting audience members to dance on stage. The Flemion brothers also appeared on the [Smashing Pumpkins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smashing_Pumpkins "Smashing Pumpkins")' " [Tonight, Tonight](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tonight,_Tonight_(The_Smashing_Pumpkins_song) "Tonight, Tonight (The Smashing Pumpkins song)")" single, and sang backing vocals on 1998's _[Adore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adore_(The_Smashing_Pumpkins_album) "Adore (The Smashing Pumpkins album)")._[\[10\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Frogs_(band)#cite_note-10)[\[11\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Frogs_(band)#cite_note-11)

#### Beck

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=The_Frogs_(band)&action=edit&section=6 "Edit section: Beck")\]

In 1995, [Beck](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beck "Beck") used a sample of the Frogs' song "I Don't Care If U Disrespect Me (Just So You Love Me)" on his song " [Where It's At](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Where_It's_At_(Beck_song) "Where It's At (Beck song)")", included on his 1996 release _[Odelay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Odelay "Odelay")._

#### Eddie Vedder and Pearl Jam

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=The_Frogs_(band)&action=edit&section=7 "Edit section: Eddie Vedder and Pearl Jam")\]

[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/e/ec/PearlJam-DC2000-6.jpg/250px-PearlJam-DC2000-6.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:PearlJam-DC2000-6.jpg) Flemion's bat wings on display at a 2000 [Pearl Jam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pearl_Jam "Pearl Jam") concert

In 1995, [Pearl Jam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pearl_Jam "Pearl Jam") included the Frogs' cover of " [Rearviewmirror](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rearviewmirror "Rearviewmirror")" (credited to all members of Pearl Jam, but largely written by lead singer Eddie Vedder) as the b-side to their "Immortality" single. The following year, [Pearl Jam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pearl_Jam "Pearl Jam") released a song called " [Smile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smile_(Pearl_Jam_song) "Smile (Pearl Jam song)")" on their _[No Code](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/No_Code "No Code")_ album. According to a story that Vedder told while performing in [Milwaukee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Milwaukee "Milwaukee") in 2006, the lyrics of "Smile" were taken directly from a note that Dennis Flemion hid inside Vedder's notebook when Vedder was on stage performing. The words used in the note are taken from the Frogs songs "This Is How I Feel" and "Now I Wanna Be Dead."[\[12\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Frogs_(band)#cite_note-12) Vedder told a similar story on Oct. 8, 2000, before performing "Smile" at Alpine Valley Music Theater in East Troy, Wis., although he didn't identify Dennis by name. In the segue between "Daughter" and "Smile," which you can hear on the bootleg from this show, Vedder says, "Someone wrote me a note in my notebook once. He's a good friend of mine. Jeff or Stone wrote this song and they needed words and these words were in this notebook. So every time a copy of 'No Code' is sold, my friend gets like 2/3 of a penny. Which adds up to, well 'No Code,' like $20."[\[13\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Frogs_(band)#cite_note-13)[\[14\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Frogs_(band)#cite_note-14) Dennis also gave Vedder a small pair of bat wings from the Frogs' early days, and Vedder has proudly displayed them behind his guitar amplifier. The wings reportedly can be seen on the front cover of Pearl Jam's _[Touring Band 2000](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Touring_Band_2000 "Touring Band 2000")._\[ _[citation needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed "Wikipedia:Citation needed")_\]

Eddie Vedder has sung "I Only Play For Money," "Starboy," and "The Longing Goes Away" with the Frogs on more than one occasion, and Pearl Jam played "I Only Play For Money" twice in 1995, although one of the performances was during the soundcheck.

#### Sebastian Bach

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=The_Frogs_(band)&action=edit&section=8 "Edit section: Sebastian Bach")\]

In 1997, Jimmy Flemion joined [Sebastian Bach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sebastian_Bach "Sebastian Bach")'s touring band. Rather than stripping Flemion of his trademark bat wings, the whole band adopted costumes as well.\[ _[citation needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed "Wikipedia:Citation needed")_\] Flemion also recorded a side project called [The Last Hard Men](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Last_Hard_Men_(band) "The Last Hard Men (band)") with Bach, [Kelley Deal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kelley_Deal "Kelley Deal") and [Jimmy Chamberlin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jimmy_Chamberlin "Jimmy Chamberlin"). Bach also performed "Lord Grunge" with the Frogs on an infamous [MTV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MTV "MTV") appearance on _Oddville._ The Frogs destroyed their set and broke several rules set forth by the show's producers, so the producers retaliated by only broadcasting the first verse of the song.

### 2001-2012

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=The_Frogs_(band)&action=edit&section=9 "Edit section: 2001-2012")\]

[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/8/89/Frogs_Austin_2011.jpg/250px-Frogs_Austin_2011.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Frogs_Austin_2011.jpg) The Frogs performing in Austin, Texas, in 2011

The Frogs' studio effort _[Hopscotch Lollipop Sunday Surprise](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hopscotch_Lollipop_Sunday_Surprise "Hopscotch Lollipop Sunday Surprise")_ was released by [Scratchie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scratchie_Records "Scratchie Records") in 2001. However, Scratchie wasn't happy with the finished album, and they put it out of print after only a few months, just like they had with the previous _[Starjob](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Starjob "Starjob")_ [EP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Extended_play "Extended play"). Fans were still enthusiastic about the band's Scratchie releases, regardless \[ _[citation needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed "Wikipedia:Citation needed")_\].

After Damian Strigens exited the band in 1998, past bassists such as Brian Hill and Jay Tiller returned for some appearances, while some new bassists began performing with the band, including [Josh Silverman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Josh_Silverman "Josh Silverman"), David Geschke, and John W. Busher. Australian [Ben Lee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ben_Lee "Ben Lee") made a special guest appearance on bass in New York City on September 28, 2001.

In 2004, [R.T. Rybak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/R.T._Rybak "R.T. Rybak"), mayor of [Minneapolis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minneapolis "Minneapolis"), crowd-surfed at a Frogs concert at [First Avenue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Avenue_(nightclub) "First Avenue (nightclub)"), a popular [nightclub](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nightclub "Nightclub") in the [Twin Cities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twin_Cities "Twin Cities"), fulfilling a promise made earlier in the summer when the club temporarily closed due to financial difficulties.[\[15\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Frogs_(band)#cite_note-15)

The group played a 25th anniversary concert at the Cactus Club in Milwaukee in 2005. Flemion opened the show by singing "Now You Know You're Black" from _[Racially Yours](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Racially_Yours "Racially Yours")_. One attendee noted, "\[I\]t was a successful fiasco once again... Jimmy is really cute when he wears the bear hat as part of his costume."[\[16\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Frogs_(band)#cite_note-16)

On November 13, 2007, Flemion performed with the Smashing Pumpkins at The Backyard in [Austin, Texas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austin,_Texas "Austin, Texas").[\[17\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Frogs_(band)#cite_note-17)[\[18\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Frogs_(band)#cite_note-18) The Frogs would later open for the Pumpkins on December 7, 2008, in Chicago.[\[19\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Frogs_(band)#cite_note-19)[\[20\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Frogs_(band)#cite_note-20)

In 2009, the Frogs were part of the lineup for UK 2009 Weekend Two, curated by [The Breeders](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Breeders "The Breeders"), of the [All Tomorrow's Parties](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/All_Tomorrow's_Parties_(music_festival) "All Tomorrow's Parties (music festival)") music festival. The band was chosen by [Animal Collective](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Animal_Collective "Animal Collective") to perform the album _It's Only Right & Natural_ in its entirety at the [All Tomorrow's Parties](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/All_Tomorrow's_Parties_(music_festival) "All Tomorrow's Parties (music festival)") festival in May 2011.[\[21\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Frogs_(band)#cite_note-21)

In December 2011, two new studio albums were announced via Flemion's Twitter account, entitled _Count Yer Blessingz_ and _Squirrel Bunny Juniper Deluxe_.[\[22\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Frogs_(band)#cite_note-22) The albums were released on iTunes and Spotify on July 5, 2012.[\[23\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Frogs_(band)#cite_note-23)

On July 7, 2012, Dennis went missing at [Wind Lake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wind_Lake "Wind Lake") in Racine, Wisconsin.[\[24\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Frogs_(band)#cite_note-24)[\[25\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Frogs_(band)#cite_note-25) His body was recovered from the water on the evening of July 10. His death was ruled an accidental drowning.

### 2013-present

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=The_Frogs_(band)&action=edit&section=10 "Edit section: 2013-present")\]

In the wake of Dennis Flemion's death, Eddie Vedder wrote "Future Days", which was released in 2013 on the Pearl Jam album, _[Lightning Bolt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lightning_Bolt_(Pearl_Jam_album) "Lightning Bolt (Pearl Jam album)")_.[\[26\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Frogs_(band)#cite_note-26) Jimmy announced on the band's Facebook page that the band would continue despite Dennis' passing. On July 7, 2017, Jimmy played a tribute show to Dennis at the Barracuda in Austin, Texas. A final album was announced plus talks of a potential documentary.

For its 30th anniversary in 2019, _It’s Only Right and Natural_ was reissued on End Of All Music.[\[27\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Frogs_(band)#cite_note-27) The vinyl reissue features remixed and remastered audio of the original album (the original album was accidentally pressed in mono when released in 1989) and comes housed in a [gatefold](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gatefold "Gatefold") sleeve with newly added artwork (plus track-by-track [liner notes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liner_note "Liner note") and intro by Flemion).

In November 2019, the Frogs announced a short tour consisting of Flemion on guitar and [Evan Dando](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Evan_Dando "Evan Dando") (of the Lemonheads) on drums.\[ _[citation needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed "Wikipedia:Citation needed")_\]

As a follow-up to 2019's reissue of _It's Only Right and Natural_, the Frogs released _1st_ on April 3, 2020.\[ _[citation needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed "Wikipedia:Citation needed")_\] Recorded during January 1986, the album, also known as _Pat's Session_, was intended to be the initial release for the band prior to _The Frogs_ 1988 self-titled debut.[\[28\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Frogs_(band)#cite_note-28) Minneapolis based Twin/Tone Records approached The Frogs and expressed fawning interest, extending an invitation to record the band after seeing them live in 1985. Dennis opted not to release due to the overabundance of reverb. Throughout the years, the discussion would persist from other sources on pursuing to fix the reverb on _1st_. Thirty-two years later, Flemion would take the recordings to a Portland, Oregon recording studio to remaster in 2018. The studio engineer successfully cleaned-up and removed the excess reverb.

On February 14, 2020, Flemion confirmed on the Frogs' Facebook page that a documentary on the band is in the works.

In 2021, a "final" album, _Damaged Goods / Sinned in Reverse_ was released digitally.\[ _[citation needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed "Wikipedia:Citation needed")_\] 2022 saw an expanded version of their debut album and an alternate version of _Hopscotch Lollipop Sunday Surprise_. The following year, a reissue of an early demo release, _Death Songs_, was released onto streaming services for its 40th anniversary. It was followed by _1980_, a collection of demos recorded in 1980. Expanded and alternate versions of the _Starjob_ EP, entitled _Starjob +_ and _Starjob?_, rounded out the year's releases. In 2024, Flemion announced he would be releasing a series of live versions of the band's material, beginning with _Rare_, focusing on live shows between 1980 and 1984, through _Well Done_, which focused between 2000 and 2006. Later that year, a deluxe version of _Racially Yours_, entitled _Racially Mine_, was released. 2025 saw the release of _Out of the Missed_, a recording of their final show before Denis Flemion's death, at [Emo's](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emo's "Emo's") in 2011.
