**Sebadoh** ([/ˈsɛbədoʊ/](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help:IPA/English "Help:IPA/English")) is an American [indie rock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indie_rock "Indie rock") band formed in 1986 in [Northampton, Massachusetts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northampton,_Massachusetts "Northampton, Massachusetts"), by [Eric Gaffney](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eric_Gaffney "Eric Gaffney") and [Lou Barlow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lou_Barlow "Lou Barlow"), with multi-instrumentalist [Jason Loewenstein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jason_Loewenstein "Jason Loewenstein") completing the line-up in 1989. Barlow co-created Sebadoh as an outlet for his songwriting when [J. Mascis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/J._Mascis "J. Mascis") gradually took over creative control of [Dinosaur Jr.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dinosaur_Jr. "Dinosaur Jr."), in which Barlow plays bass guitar.[\[1\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sebadoh#cite_note-1)

Along with such bands as [Pavement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pavement_(band) "Pavement (band)"), [Beat Happening](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beat_Happening "Beat Happening") and [Guided by Voices](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guided_by_Voices "Guided by Voices"), Sebadoh helped pioneer a [lo-fi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lo-fi_music "Lo-fi music") style of [indie rock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indie_rock "Indie rock") characterized by low-fidelity recording techniques that employed [four-track](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multitrack_recording "Multitrack recording") [cassette tape](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cassette_tape "Cassette tape") machines.[\[2\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sebadoh#cite_note-:0-2) The band's early output, such as _[The Freed Man](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Freed_Man "The Freed Man")_ and _[Weed Forestin'](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weed_Forestin' "Weed Forestin'")_ (both released 1990), as well as _[Sebadoh III](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sebadoh_III "Sebadoh III")_ (1991), was typical of this style. Following the release of _[Bubble & Scrape](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bubble_&_Scrape "Bubble & Scrape")_ in 1993, Gaffney left the band. His replacement and erstwhile stand-in, Bob Fay, appeared on _[Bakesale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bakesale "Bakesale")_ (1994) and _[Harmacy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harmacy "Harmacy")_ (1996), but was fired before the sessions for the band's major label release _[The Sebadoh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Sebadoh "The Sebadoh")_ (1999), featuring drummer [Russ Pollard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russ_Pollard "Russ Pollard").[\[3\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sebadoh#cite_note-3)

The band then paused recording for 14 years. The group, fronted by Barlow and now featuring drummer [Bob D'Amico](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bob_D'Amico "Bob D'Amico"), returned in 2012 with _[Secret EP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Secret_(EP) "Secret (EP)")_ and, in 2013, a full-length album titled _[Defend Yourself](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Defend_Yourself "Defend Yourself")_, which were both self-recorded. The album _[Act Surprised](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Act_Surprised "Act Surprised")_ followed in 2019.[\[2\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sebadoh#cite_note-:0-2)

## History

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Sebadoh&action=edit&section=1 "Edit section: History")\]

Lou Barlow was the bass player for alternative rock band Dinosaur Jr. (originally Dinosaur) in the 1980s. While both Barlow and leader [J Mascis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/J_Mascis "J Mascis") wrote songs, Mascis' material dominated the group's output because Barlow was intimidated by the guitarist's songwriting efforts.[\[4\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sebadoh#cite_note-4) Barlow spent progressively more time recording his own songs at home. Barlow and [Eric Gaffney](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eric_Gaffney "Eric Gaffney") released the _[Weed Forestin'](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weed_Forestin' "Weed Forestin'")_ cassette in 1987 on [Homestead Records](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homestead_Records "Homestead Records") under the name Sebadoh, which was a [nonsense word](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nonsense_word "Nonsense word") Barlow often muttered in his recordings.[\[5\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sebadoh#cite_note-5) Both Barlow and Gaffney contributed songs to _[The Freed Man](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Freed_Man "The Freed Man")_ (1988) cassette. Homestead Records head [Gerard Cosloy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gerard_Cosloy "Gerard Cosloy") heard the cassette release of _The Freed Man_ and released it as a full-length album on Homestead in 1989. Soon after the cassette's release Barlow was kicked out of Dinosaur Jr. Over time Sebadoh's releases became a way for Barlow to address the issues of control that manifested as the tension in and his ejection from Dinosaur Jr; Barlow said "I got a lot of hatred out just by writing those songs."[\[6\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sebadoh#cite_note-6) [Jason Loewenstein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jason_Loewenstein "Jason Loewenstein") joined in summer 1989, the first release that he played on being the "Gimme Indie Rock" single in 1991.[\[7\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sebadoh#cite_note-Larkin-7) Only ten 'band' shows were performed throughout Western Massachusetts, Boston, and New York over the period 1989–1990 before the third album, _[Sebadoh III](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sebadoh_III "Sebadoh III")_, was released.[\[7\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sebadoh#cite_note-Larkin-7)

After touring with [Firehose](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Firehose_(band) "Firehose (band)") in 1991, they signed to [Sub Pop](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sub_Pop "Sub Pop") ( [Domino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Domino_Recording_Company "Domino Recording Company") in the UK and [City Slang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City_Slang_Records "City Slang Records") in Germany) in 1992, and released the two EPs _[Rocking the Forest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rocking_the_Forest "Rocking the Forest")_ and _[Sebadoh vs. Helmet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sebadoh_vs._Helmet "Sebadoh vs. Helmet")_ just two months apart. These EPs had their track listings truncated and shuffled around and made into the American full-length release _[Smash Your Head on the Punk Rock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smash_Your_Head_on_the_Punk_Rock "Smash Your Head on the Punk Rock")_.[\[7\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sebadoh#cite_note-Larkin-7) Their fourth album _[Bubble & Scrape](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bubble_&_Scrape "Bubble & Scrape")_ was released in April 1993. The same year, the band's "Soul and Fire" EP humorously described the band's musical goal as "driving dozens of college-age lemmings off the cliff of limited imagination."[\[8\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sebadoh#cite_note-8)

Following _[Bubble & Scrape](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bubble_&_Scrape "Bubble & Scrape")_ (1993) Gaffney left the band. Gaffney has stated that he left Sebadoh "because I felt like it" and went on to explain that: "I wanted to start a band where I was the songwriter/guitar player and so I did. I also had the garage practice space and booked all our early shows. This was summer 1989, when The Freed Man LP was released on Homestead was when I called those guys on the phone to ask if they wanted to start a band, met with dead silence. Me on the other end saying, “Are you still there?” I only started playing drums when the other guys wanted to play their songs because I didn’t want to learn bass lines, and drums are my first instrument. Four years later I let the whole thing walk away from me. It was like The Blob—you want to just run out of the theater! (lest you be consumed by “The Blob.”)."[\[9\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sebadoh#cite_note-9)

Barlow has stated that the mood in the band upon Gaffney's departure was "euphoric," explaining that: "Eric was a difficult guy. He was really struggling with the band becoming more popular; he was really struggling with sharing the spotlight with me. He’s an absolute leader, and deserves a spotlight of his own, but I had kind of forced him into this band where he and I were going to be trading off, thinking that was a really great idea. Eric Gaffney did not want to be doing that. Eric Gaffney wanted to be front and center—which is great, I mean, I believed in him. I’m an Eric Gaffney fan, that’s why I was playing in a band with him, but Eric Gaffney was not a Lou Barlow fan. So him leaving the band was awesome. All of this resistance just disappeared for a while, and it was really nice."[\[10\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sebadoh#cite_note-10)

Gaffney's replacement, Bob Fay, appeared on _[Bakesale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bakesale "Bakesale")_ (1994) and the follow-up _[Harmacy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harmacy "Harmacy")_ in 1996.[\[11\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sebadoh#cite_note-The_Great_Rock_Discography-11)
Gaffney had quit the band three times between 1990 and 1993, being replaced each time by Fay.[\[12\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sebadoh#cite_note-12) Fay recorded and toured with the band during those years, performing on half the tracks of _[Rocking the Forest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rocking_the_Forest "Rocking the Forest")_, including the Fay composition "Mind Meld," and two tracks on _[Sebadoh vs. Helmet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sebadoh_vs._Helmet "Sebadoh vs. Helmet")_, whose cover image is a photograph of Fay.
Fay was fired before the sessions for _[The Sebadoh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Sebadoh "The Sebadoh")_ (1999) and replaced by [Russ Pollard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russell_Pollard "Russell Pollard"), a friend of Loewenstein's from Louisville. The band went on a tour to promote this album, including a stop in [Toronto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toronto "Toronto") in March.[\[13\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sebadoh#cite_note-13) The band then went on hiatus, with Barlow concentrating on his other project, the [Folk Implosion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Folk_Implosion "Folk Implosion"),[\[7\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sebadoh#cite_note-Larkin-7) and Loewenstein working on material for his debut solo album _At Sixes and Sevens_, released in 2002. The two reunited to play concerts in late 2003 and early 2004.

In March 2007, the "Sebadoh Classic" lineup of Barlow, Gaffney and Loewenstein went on tour together for the first time in fourteen years.[\[14\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sebadoh#cite_note-14) This coincided with a new series of reissues which repackaged some of the early albums with extra discs of unreleased tracks. First came a reissue of _[Sebadoh III](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sebadoh_III "Sebadoh III")_, then _[The Freed Man](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Freed_Man "The Freed Man")_, and _[Bubble & Scrape](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bubble_&_Scrape "Bubble & Scrape")_.[\[15\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sebadoh#cite_note-15)

The reunion tour continued into 2008, and in May included a live performance of _[Bubble & Scrape](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bubble_&_Scrape "Bubble & Scrape")_ (1993) in its entirety as part of the [All Tomorrow's Parties](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/All_Tomorrow's_Parties_(music_festival) "All Tomorrow's Parties (music festival)") curated [Don't Look Back](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Don't_Look_Back_(concert_series) "Don't Look Back (concert series)") series at London's Koko venue.

In 2011, Sebadoh toured in support of reissues for their _Bakesale_ and _Harmacy_ albums. Though _Bakesale_ was reissued on time, as of June 2015, _Harmacy_ has still not been reissued.[\[16\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sebadoh#cite_note-16) Taking the place of Gaffney on drums was [Bob D'Amico](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bob_D'Amico "Bob D'Amico"), who plays with Loewenstein in both Circle of Buzzards and [the Fiery Furnaces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Fiery_Furnaces "The Fiery Furnaces").[\[17\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sebadoh#cite_note-17)

In March 2012, Lou Barlow reissued the early recordings of _Weed Forrestin'_ on the Sebadoh bandcamp, available in a digital download, double cd, vinyl, and deluxe edition with the _Child of the Apocalypse_ sessions as a cassette tape. Fifty copies of "Weed Forrestin" came as a surprise bonus with purchases of the limited edition re-release of Dinosaur Jr.'s first three albums in a cassette boxed set released by Joyful Noise in 2012.[\[18\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sebadoh#cite_note-18)

They were chosen by [Jeff Mangum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jeff_Mangum "Jeff Mangum") of [Neutral Milk Hotel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neutral_Milk_Hotel "Neutral Milk Hotel") to perform at the [All Tomorrow's Parties](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/All_Tomorrow's_Parties_(music_festival) "All Tomorrow's Parties (music festival)") festival that he curated in March 2012 in Minehead, England.[\[19\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sebadoh#cite_note-19)

In July 2012, Sebadoh released _[Secret EP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Secret_(EP) "Secret (EP)")_ on the Sebadoh bandcamp, available in a digital download. CD copies were only available at the shows of their 2012 tour. In June 2013, [Joyful Noise](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joyful_Noise_Recordings "Joyful Noise Recordings") released it on vinyl.

Their eighth studio album, _[Defend Yourself](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Defend_Yourself "Defend Yourself")_, came out on Joyful Noise Recordings on September 17, 2013. It debuted at No. 1 on the "New Alternative Artists" Billboard chart.[\[20\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sebadoh#cite_note-20)

On March 1, 2019, the band announced that their ninth studio album _Act Surprised_ will be released on May 24, 2019, via [Dangerbird Records](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dangerbird_Records "Dangerbird Records").
The same day the first single off the album, "Celebrate the Void" was released.[\[21\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sebadoh#cite_note-acts-21)

## Style

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Sebadoh&action=edit&section=2 "Edit section: Style")\]

According to Stephen Thomas Erlewine of _[AllMusic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AllMusic "AllMusic")_, "Sebadoh embraced intentionally raw and unsophisticated home recording as the element that tied together their self-conscious sentimental pop with their noisy experimentalism." Lou Barlow's vocals have been described as "gloomy but tuneful."[\[22\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sebadoh#cite_note-22)
