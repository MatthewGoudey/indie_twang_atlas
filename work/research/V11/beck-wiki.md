**Beck David Hansen** (born **Bek  David Campbell**; July 8, 1970), known [mononymously](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mononym "Mononym") as **Beck**, is an American singer, songwriter, and record producer. He rose to fame in the early 1990s with his [experimental](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Experimental_music "Experimental music") and [lo-fi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lo-fi_music "Lo-fi music") style, and became known for creating musical collages of wide-ranging genres. He has musically encompassed [folk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Folk_music "Folk music"), [funk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Funk "Funk"), [soul](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soul_music "Soul music"), [hip-hop](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hip-hop "Hip-hop"), [electronica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electronic_music "Electronic music"), [alternative rock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alternative_rock "Alternative rock"), [country](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Country_music "Country music"), and [psychedelia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Psychedelic_music "Psychedelic music"). He has released 15 studio albums, as well as several non-album singles and a book of sheet music.

Born and raised in [Los Angeles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Los_Angeles "Los Angeles"), Beck gravitated towards hip-hop and folk in his teens and began to perform locally at coffeehouses and clubs. He moved to [New York City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_City "New York City") in 1989 and became involved in the city's [anti-folk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anti-folk "Anti-folk") movement. Returning to Los Angeles in the early 1990s, he saw his commercial breakthrough with his 1993 single " [Loser](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Loser_(Beck_song) "Loser (Beck song)")". After signing with [DGC Records](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DGC_Records "DGC Records"), the song peaked at number ten on the [_Billboard_ Hot 100](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Billboard_Hot_100 "Billboard Hot 100") and served as lead single for his third album and [major label](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Major_label "Major label") debut, _[Mellow Gold](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mellow_Gold "Mellow Gold")_ (1994). Its follow-up, _[Odelay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Odelay "Odelay")_ (1996), topped critic polls and won several awards. Before his 1996 release, the same year, he played the role of a taxi driver in the US video of [Alisha's Attic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alisha's_Attic "Alisha's Attic")'s single, " [I Am, I Feel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/I_Am,_I_Feel "I Am, I Feel")".

Beck released the country-influenced _[Mutations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mutations_(Beck_album) "Mutations (Beck album)")_ in 1998, and the funk-infused _[Midnite Vultures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Midnite_Vultures "Midnite Vultures")_ in 1999. The soft-acoustic _[Sea Change](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sea_Change_(album) "Sea Change (album)")_ in 2002 showcased a more serious Beck, and 2005's _[Guero](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guero "Guero")_ returned to _Odelay_'s [sample](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sample_(music) "Sample (music)")-based production. _[The Information](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Information_(Beck_album) "The Information (Beck album)")_ in 2006 was inspired by electro-funk, hip-hop, and psychedelia; 2008's _[Modern Guilt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Modern_Guilt "Modern Guilt")_ was inspired by 1960s pop; and 2014's folk-infused _[Morning Phase](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Morning_Phase "Morning Phase")_, a stylistic and thematic follow-up to Sea Change, won [Album of the Year](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grammy_Award_for_Album_of_the_Year "Grammy Award for Album of the Year") at the [57th Grammy Awards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/57th_Grammy_Awards "57th Grammy Awards"). His 2017 album, _[Colors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colors_(Beck_album) "Colors (Beck album)")_, won awards for Best Alternative Music Album and Best Engineered Album, Non-classical at the [61st Annual Grammy Awards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/61st_Annual_Grammy_Awards "61st Annual Grammy Awards"). His fourteenth studio album, _[Hyperspace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hyperspace_(album) "Hyperspace (album)")_, was released on November 22, 2019. In 2022, Beck was nominated for the [Rock & Roll Hall of Fame](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rock_&_Roll_Hall_of_Fame "Rock & Roll Hall of Fame").

With a [pop art](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pop_art "Pop art") collage of musical styles, oblique and [ironic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irony "Irony") lyrics, and [postmodern](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Postmodern "Postmodern") arrangements incorporating [samples](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sample_(music) "Sample (music)"), [drum machines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drum_machine "Drum machine"), live instrumentation and sound effects, Beck has been hailed by critics and the public throughout his musical career as being among the most idiosyncratically creative musicians of 1990s and 2000s [alternative rock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alternative_rock "Alternative rock"). Two of Beck's most popular and acclaimed recordings are _Odelay_ and _Sea Change_, both of which were ranked on _[Rolling Stone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rolling_Stone_(magazine) "Rolling Stone (magazine)")_'s list of [the 500 greatest albums of all time](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rolling_Stone's_500_Greatest_Albums_of_All_Time "Rolling Stone's 500 Greatest Albums of All Time"). Beck is a four-time [platinum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RIAA_certification "RIAA certification") artist; he has collaborated with several artists and has made several contributions to soundtracks.

## Early life

Bek David Campbell was born in [Los Angeles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Los_Angeles "Los Angeles") on July 8, 1970,[\[1\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beck#cite_note-1)[\[2\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beck#cite_note-2) the son of American visual artist [Bibbe Hansen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bibbe_Hansen "Bibbe Hansen") and Canadian arranger, composer, and conductor [David Campbell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Campbell_(composer) "David Campbell (composer)").[\[3\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beck#cite_note-rsencyclopedia-3) Bibbe Hansen grew up amid [Andy Warhol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andy_Warhol "Andy Warhol")'s [The Factory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Factory "The Factory") art scene of the 1960s in [New York City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_City "New York City") and was a [Warhol superstar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warhol_superstars "Warhol superstars").[\[3\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beck#cite_note-rsencyclopedia-3) She moved to [California](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/California "California") at 17 and met David Campbell.[\[4\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beck#cite_note-q97-4) Beck's maternal grandfather, artist [Al Hansen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al_Hansen "Al Hansen"), was of Norwegian descent[\[5\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beck#cite_note-5)[\[6\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beck#cite_note-6) and was a pioneer in the [avant-garde](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Avant-garde "Avant-garde") [Fluxus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fluxus "Fluxus") movement.[\[3\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beck#cite_note-rsencyclopedia-3) Beck's maternal grandmother was [Jewish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jews "Jews"); he has said that he considers himself Jewish because he was "raised celebrating Jewish holidays."[\[7\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beck#cite_note-Spin_2008-09-7)

Beck was born in a rooming house near downtown Los Angeles. As a child he lived in a declining neighborhood near [Hollywood Boulevard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hollywood_Boulevard "Hollywood Boulevard").[\[8\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beck#cite_note-rs97-8) He remembers "By the time we left there, they were ripping out miles of houses en masse and building low-rent, giant apartment blocks."[\[4\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beck#cite_note-q97-4) The working-class family struggled financially, moving to Hoover and Ninth Street, a neighborhood populated primarily by Koreans and Salvadorian refugees.[\[4\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beck#cite_note-q97-4) He was sent for a time to live with his paternal grandparents in [Kansas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kansas "Kansas"); he later remarked that he thought "they were kind of concerned" about his "weird" home life.[\[9\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beck#cite_note-rs94-9) Because his paternal grandfather was a [Presbyterian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Presbyterianism "Presbyterianism") minister, Beck grew up influenced by church music and hymns.[\[9\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beck#cite_note-rs94-9) He also spent time in [Europe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Europe "Europe") with his maternal grandfather.[\[3\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beck#cite_note-rsencyclopedia-3)

After his parents separated when he was ten,[\[4\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beck#cite_note-q97-4) Beck stayed with his mother and brother Channing in Los Angeles, where he was influenced by the city's diverse musical offerings—everything from [hip-hop](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hip-hop "Hip-hop") to [Latin music](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latin_American_music "Latin American music") and his mother's art scene—all of which would later reappear in his work.[\[10\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beck#cite_note-:%22ny%22-10) Beck obtained his first guitar at 16 and became a street musician, often playing [Lead Belly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lead_Belly "Lead Belly") covers at [Lafayette Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lafayette_Park,_Los_Angeles "Lafayette Park, Los Angeles").[\[11\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beck#cite_note-rs96-11) During his teens, Beck discovered the music of [Sonic Youth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sonic_Youth "Sonic Youth"), [Pussy Galore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pussy_Galore_(band) "Pussy Galore (band)"), and [X](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/X_(American_band) "X (American band)"), but remained uninterested in most music outside the folk genre until many years into his career.[\[3\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beck#cite_note-rsencyclopedia-3)[\[4\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beck#cite_note-q97-4) The first contemporary music that made a direct connection with Beck was hip-hop, which he first heard on [Grandmaster Flash](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grandmaster_Flash "Grandmaster Flash") records in the early 1980s.[\[4\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beck#cite_note-q97-4) Growing up in a predominantly Latin district, he found himself the only white child at his school, and quickly learned how to [breakdance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Breakdance "Breakdance").[\[4\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beck#cite_note-q97-4) When he was 17, Beck grew fascinated after hearing a [Mississippi John Hurt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mississippi_John_Hurt "Mississippi John Hurt") record at a friend's house,[\[12\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beck#cite_note-lat94-12) and spent hours in his room trying to emulate Hurt's [finger-picking](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fingerstyle_guitar "Fingerstyle guitar") techniques.[\[9\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beck#cite_note-rs94-9) Shortly thereafter Beck explored blues and folk music further, discovering [Woody Guthrie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Woody_Guthrie "Woody Guthrie") and [Blind Willie Johnson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blind_Willie_Johnson "Blind Willie Johnson").[\[12\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beck#cite_note-lat94-12)

Feeling like "a total outcast", Beck dropped out of school after junior high.[\[12\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beck#cite_note-lat94-12) He later said that although he felt school was important, he felt unsafe there.[\[8\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beck#cite_note-rs97-8) When he applied to the new [performing arts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Performing_arts "Performing arts") high school downtown, he was rejected.[\[13\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beck#cite_note-nym12-13) His brother took him to post- [Beat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beat_Generation "Beat Generation") jazz places in [Echo Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Echo_Park,_Los_Angeles "Echo Park, Los Angeles") and [Silver Lake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silver_Lake,_Los_Angeles "Silver Lake, Los Angeles"). He hung out at [Los Angeles City College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Los_Angeles_City_College "Los Angeles City College") perusing records, books, and old [sheet music](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sheet_music "Sheet music") in the college's library. He used a [fake ID](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Identity_fraud "Identity fraud") to sit in on classes there, and he also befriended a literature instructor and his poet wife.[\[13\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beck#cite_note-nym12-13) He worked at a string of odd jobs, including loading trucks and operating a [leaf blower](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leaf_blower "Leaf blower").[\[12\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beck#cite_note-lat94-12)

## Career

### Early performances and first releases (1988–1993)

Beck began as a [folk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Folk_music "Folk music") musician, switching between [country blues](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Country_blues "Country blues"), [Delta blues](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Delta_blues "Delta blues"), and more traditional rural folk music in his teenage years.[\[4\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beck#cite_note-q97-4) He began performing on city buses, often covering Mississippi John Hurt alongside original, sometimes improvisational compositions.[\[9\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beck#cite_note-rs94-9) "I'd get on the bus and start playing Mississippi John Hurt with totally improvised lyrics. Some drunk would start yelling at me, calling me [Axl Rose](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Axl_Rose "Axl Rose"). So I'd start singing about Axl Rose and the levee and bus passes and strychnine, mixing the whole thing up", he later recalled.[\[9\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beck#cite_note-rs94-9) He was also in a band called Youthless that hosted [Dadaist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dada "Dada")-inspired freeform events at city coffee shops.[\[4\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beck#cite_note-q97-4) "We had [Radio Shack](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radio_Shack "Radio Shack") mics and this homemade speaker and we'd draft people in the audience to recite comic books or do a [beatbox](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beatbox "Beatbox") thing, or we'd tie the whole audience up in masking tape," Beck recalled.[\[4\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beck#cite_note-q97-4)

In 1989, Beck caught a bus to New York City with little more than $8.00 and a guitar.[\[9\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beck#cite_note-rs94-9) He spent the summer attempting to find a job and a place to live with little success.[\[9\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beck#cite_note-rs94-9) Beck eventually began to frequent [Manhattan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manhattan "Manhattan")'s [Lower East Side](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lower_East_Side "Lower East Side"), where he became involved in the anti-folk scene at the Chameleon Club organized by [Lach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lach_(musician) "Lach (musician)"), the movement's founder.[\[14\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beck#cite_note-14)[\[15\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beck#cite_note-15) "The whole mission was to destroy all the clichés and make up some new ones," said Beck of his New York years. "Everybody knew each other. You could go up onstage and say anything, and you wouldn't feel weird or feel any pressure."[\[16\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beck#cite_note-gp94-16) During this period, Lach encouraged Beck to write his own songs about whatever was happening around him, "If a weird looking dog walks by, and you're hungry for pizza, write a song about that."[\[17\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beck#cite_note-17) Beck was roommates with [Paleface](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paleface_(musician) "Paleface (musician)"), sleeping on his couch and attending open mic nights together.[\[18\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beck#cite_note-18) Daunted by the prospect of another homeless New York winter, Beck returned to his home of Los Angeles in early 1991.[\[12\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beck#cite_note-lat94-12)[\[19\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beck#cite_note-19) "I was tired of being cold, tired of getting beat up," he later remarked. "It was hard to be in New York with no money, no place ... I kinda used up all the friends I had. Everyone on the scene got sick of me."[\[9\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beck#cite_note-rs94-9)

Back in Los Angeles, Beck began to work at a [video store](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Video_rental_shop "Video rental shop") in the [Silver Lake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silver_Lake,_Los_Angeles "Silver Lake, Los Angeles") neighborhood, "doing things like alphabetizing the pornography section".[\[9\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beck#cite_note-rs94-9) He began performing in arthouse clubs and coffeehouses such as Al's Bar and [Raji's](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Raji's "Raji's").[\[3\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beck#cite_note-rsencyclopedia-3)[\[9\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beck#cite_note-rs94-9)[\[12\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beck#cite_note-lat94-12) In order to keep indifferent audiences engaged in his music, Beck would play in a spontaneous, joking manner.[\[20\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beck#cite_note-20) "I'd be banging away on a [Son House](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Son_House "Son House") tune and the whole audience would be talking. So maybe out of desperation or boredom, or the audience's boredom, I'd make up these ridiculous songs just to see if people were listening," he later remarked.[\[21\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beck#cite_note-ew97-21) Virtually an unknown to the public and an enigma to those who met him, Beck would hop onstage between acts in local clubs and play "strange folk songs", accompanied by "what could best be described as [performance art](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Performance_art "Performance art")" while sometimes wearing a _[Star Wars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Star_Wars "Star Wars")_ [stormtrooper](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stormtrooper_(Star_Wars) "Stormtrooper (Star Wars)") mask.[\[12\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beck#cite_note-lat94-12) Beck met someone who offered to help record [demos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Demo_(music) "Demo (music)") in his living room, and he began to pass [cassette tapes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Compact_Cassette "Compact Cassette") around.[\[12\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beck#cite_note-lat94-12)

Eventually, Beck gained key boosters in Margaret Mittleman, the West Coast's director of talent acquisitions for [BMG Music Publishing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BMG_Music_Publishing "BMG Music Publishing"), and the partners behind [independent record label](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Independent_record_label "Independent record label") [Bong Load Custom Records](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bong_Load_Custom_Records "Bong Load Custom Records"): [Tom Rothrock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tom_Rothrock "Tom Rothrock"), [Rob Schnapf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rob_Schnapf "Rob Schnapf"), and Brad Lambert.[\[3\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beck#cite_note-rsencyclopedia-3) Schnapf saw Beck perform at Jabberjaw and felt he would suit their small venture.[\[12\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beck#cite_note-lat94-12) Beck expressed a loose interest in [hip-hop](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hip-hop "Hip-hop") and Rothrock introduced him to Carl Stephenson, a record producer for [Rap-A-Lot Records](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rap-A-Lot_Records "Rap-A-Lot Records").[\[12\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beck#cite_note-lat94-12)[\[22\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beck#cite_note-22) In 1992, Beck visited Stephenson's home to collaborate with him. The result—the [slide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slide_guitar "Slide guitar")- [sampling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sampling_(music) "Sampling (music)") hip-hop track " [Loser](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Loser_(Beck_song) "Loser (Beck song)")"—was a one-off experiment that Beck set aside, going back to his folk songs, making his home tapes such as _[Golden Feelings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golden_Feelings "Golden Feelings")_, and releasing several independent singles.[\[12\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beck#cite_note-lat94-12)

### _Mellow Gold_, and independent albums (1993–1994)

By 1993, Beck was living in a rat-infested shed near a Los Angeles alleyway with little money.[\[9\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beck#cite_note-rs94-9) Bong Load issued "Loser" as a single in March 1993 on 12" vinyl with only 500 copies pressed.[\[23\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beck#cite_note-Palacios_77-23) Beck felt that "Loser" was mediocre, and only agreed to its release at Rothrock's insistence.[\[24\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beck#cite_note-24) "Loser" unexpectedly received radio airplay, starting in Los Angeles, where [college radio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Campus_radio "Campus radio") station [KXLU](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KXLU "KXLU") was the first to play it,[\[25\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beck#cite_note-25)[\[23\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beck#cite_note-Palacios_77-23) and later on [Santa Monica College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santa_Monica_College "Santa Monica College") radio station [KCRW](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KCRW "KCRW"), where radio host [Chris Douridas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chris_Douridas "Chris Douridas") played the song on _[Morning Becomes Eclectic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Morning_Becomes_Eclectic "Morning Becomes Eclectic")_, the station's flagship music program. "I called the record label that day and asked to have Beck play live on the air", Douridas said. "He came in that Friday, rapped to a tape of 'Loser' and did his song 'MTV Makes Me Want to Smoke Crack.'"[\[12\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beck#cite_note-lat94-12) That night, Beck performed at the Los Angeles club Cafe Troy to a packed audience and talent scouts from major labels.[\[12\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beck#cite_note-lat94-12) The song then spread to Seattle through [KNDD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KNDD "KNDD") The End, and [KROQ-FM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KROQ-FM "KROQ-FM") began playing the song on an almost hourly basis.[\[23\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beck#cite_note-Palacios_77-23) As Bong Load struggled to press more copies of "Loser", Beck was beset with offers to sign with major labels.[\[26\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beck#cite_note-Palacios_80-26) During the bidding war in November, Beck spent several days in [Olympia, Washington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olympia,_Washington "Olympia, Washington"), recording material with [Calvin Johnson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Calvin_Johnson_(musician) "Calvin Johnson (musician)") of [Beat Happening](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beat_Happening "Beat Happening"), which would later see release the following year on Johnson's [K Records](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K_Records "K Records") as _[One Foot in the Grave](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/One_Foot_in_the_Grave_(Beck_album) "One Foot in the Grave (Beck album)")_.[\[27\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beck#cite_note-bb93-27)

A fierce bidding war ensued, with [Geffen Records](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geffen_Records "Geffen Records") A&R director Mark Kates signing Beck in December 1993 amid intense competition from [Warner Bros.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warner_Bros._Records "Warner Bros. Records") and [Capitol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Capitol_Records "Capitol Records").[\[12\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beck#cite_note-lat94-12)[\[27\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beck#cite_note-bb93-27) Beck's non-exclusive contract with Geffen allowed him an unusual amount of creative freedom, with Beck remaining free to release material through such small, independent labels as [Flipside](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flipside_Records "Flipside Records"), which issued the sprawling, 25-track collection of pre-"Loser" recordings titled _[Stereopathetic Soulmanure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stereopathetic_Soulmanure "Stereopathetic Soulmanure")_ on February 22 the following year.[\[12\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beck#cite_note-lat94-12)[\[27\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beck#cite_note-bb93-27) By the time Beck released his first album for Geffen, the low-budget, genre-blending _[Mellow Gold](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mellow_Gold "Mellow Gold")_ on March 1,[\[12\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beck#cite_note-lat94-12) "Loser" was already in the top 40 and its video in MTV's [Buzz Bin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buzz_Bin "Buzz Bin").[\[8\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beck#cite_note-rs97-8) "Loser" quickly ascended the charts in the U.S., reaching a peak of number ten on the [_Billboard_ Hot 100](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Billboard_Hot_100 "Billboard Hot 100") singles chart and topping the [Modern Rock Tracks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hot_Modern_Rock_Tracks "Hot Modern Rock Tracks") chart.[\[28\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beck#cite_note-US_charts-28) The song also charted in the UK, Australia, New Zealand, and throughout Europe. Beck's newfound position of attention led to his characterization as the "King of Slackers", as the media dubbed him the center of the new so-called "slacker" movement.[\[29\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beck#cite_note-29) Critics, feeling it the essential follow-up to [Radiohead](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radiohead "Radiohead")'s " [Creep](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Creep_(Radiohead_song) "Creep (Radiohead song)")",[\[27\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beck#cite_note-bb93-27) found vacantness in the lyrics of "Loser" strongly associated with [Generation X](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Generation_X "Generation X"), although Beck himself strongly contested his position as the face of the "slacker" generation: "Slacker my _ass_. I mean, I _never_ had any slack. I was working a $4-an-hour job trying to stay alive. That slacker stuff is for people who have the time to be depressed about everything."[\[9\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beck#cite_note-rs94-9)

### Backlash and _Odelay_ (1994–1997)

Feeling as though he was "constantly trying to prove myself",[\[8\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beck#cite_note-rs97-8) Beck suffered a backlash, with skeptics denouncing him as a self-indulgent fake and the latest marketing opportunity.[\[30\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beck#cite_note-ew94-30) In the summer of 1994, Beck was struggling and many of his fellow musicians thought he had lost his way.[\[4\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beck#cite_note-q97-4) Combined with "Loser"'s wildly popular music video and the world tour, Beck reacted believing the attention could not last, resulting in a status as a " [one-hit wonder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/One-hit_wonder "One-hit wonder")". At other concerts, crowds were treated to twenty minutes of [reggae](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reggae "Reggae") or [Miles Davis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miles_Davis "Miles Davis") or [jazz-punk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Punk_jazz "Punk jazz") iterations of "Loser".[\[13\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beck#cite_note-nym12-13) At one-day festivals in California, he surrounded himself with an artnoise combo. The drummer set fire to his cymbals; the lead guitarist "played" his guitar with the strings faced towards his body; and Beck changed the words to "Loser" so that nobody could sing along.[\[4\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beck#cite_note-q97-4) "I can't tell you how many times I was looking at faces that were looking back at me with complete bewilderment—or just pointing and shaking their heads and laughing—while performing during that period," he later recalled.[\[31\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beck#cite_note-p4k11-31) Despite this, Beck gained the respect of his peers, such as [Tom Petty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tom_Petty "Tom Petty") and [Johnny Cash](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johnny_Cash "Johnny Cash"), and created an entire wave of bands determined to recapture the _Mellow Gold_ sound.[\[32\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beck#cite_note-ocr96-32) Feeling his previous releases were just collections of demos recorded over the course of several years, Beck desired to enter the studio and record an album in a continuous linear fashion, which became _[Odelay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Odelay "Odelay")_.[\[31\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beck#cite_note-p4k11-31)

Beck blends country, blues, rap, jazz and rock on _Odelay_, the result of a year and half of feverish "cutting, pasting, layering, dubbing, and, of course, sampling".[\[11\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beck#cite_note-rs96-11) Each day, the musicians started from scratch,[\[33\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beck#cite_note-rs99-33) often working on songs for 16 hours straight.[\[11\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beck#cite_note-rs96-11) _Odelay_'s conception lies in an unfinished studio album Beck first embarked on following the success of "Loser", chronicling the difficult time he experienced: "There was a cycle of everyone dying around me," he recalled later.[\[32\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beck#cite_note-ocr96-32) He was constantly recording, and eventually put together an album of somber, orchestrated folk tunes; one that, perhaps, "could have been a commercial blockbuster along with similarly themed work by [Smashing Pumpkins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smashing_Pumpkins "Smashing Pumpkins"), [Nine Inch Nails](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nine_Inch_Nails "Nine Inch Nails") and [Nirvana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nirvana_(band) "Nirvana (band)")".[\[32\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beck#cite_note-ocr96-32) Instead, Beck plucked one song from it—the _Odelay_ album closer "Ramshackle"—and shelved the rest ("Brother" and "Feather In Your Cap" were, however, later released as B-sides).[\[4\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beck#cite_note-q97-4)[\[32\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beck#cite_note-ocr96-32) Beck was introduced to the [Dust Brothers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dust_Brothers "Dust Brothers"), producers of the Beastie Boys' album _[Paul's Boutique](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul's_Boutique "Paul's Boutique")_, whose cut-and-paste, sample-heavy production suited Beck's vision of a more fun, accessible album.\[ _[citation needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed "Wikipedia:Citation needed")_\] After a record executive explained that _Odelay_ would be a "huge mistake", he spent many months thinking "that I'd blown it forever".[\[13\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beck#cite_note-nym12-13)

_Odelay_ was released on June 18, 1996, to commercial success and critical acclaim. The record produced several hit [singles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Single_(music) "Single (music)") including " [Where It's At](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Where_It's_At_(Beck_song) "Where It's At (Beck song)")", " [Devils Haircut](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Devils_Haircut_(song) "Devils Haircut (song)")", and " [The New Pollution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_New_Pollution "The New Pollution")",[\[34\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beck#cite_note-34) and was nominated for the [Grammy Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grammy_Award "Grammy Award") for [Album of the Year](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grammy_Award_for_Album_of_the_Year "Grammy Award for Album of the Year") in 1997, winning a [Grammy Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grammy_Award "Grammy Award") for [Best Alternative Music Album](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grammy_Award_for_Best_Alternative_Music_Album "Grammy Award for Best Alternative Music Album") as well as a [Grammy Award for Best Male Rock Vocal Performance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grammy_Award_for_Best_Male_Rock_Vocal_Performance "Grammy Award for Best Male Rock Vocal Performance") for "Where It's At". During one busy week in January 1997, he landed his Grammy nominations, appeared on _[Saturday Night Live](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saturday_Night_Live "Saturday Night Live")_ and _[Howard Stern](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Howard_Stern_(E!_show) "Howard Stern (E! show)")_, and did a last-minute trot on _[The Rosie O'Donnell Show](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Rosie_O'Donnell_Show "The Rosie O'Donnell Show")_. The combined buzz gave _Odelay_ a second wind, leading to an expanded fan base and additional exposure[\[21\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beck#cite_note-ew97-21) Beck enjoyed but, like several executives at Geffen, was bewildered by the success of _Odelay_. He would often get recognized in public, which made him feel strange. "It's just weird. It doesn't feel right. It doesn't feel natural to me. I don't think I was made for that. I was never good at that," he later told _[Pitchfork](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pitchfork_(website) "Pitchfork (website)")_.[\[31\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beck#cite_note-p4k11-31) _Odelay_ sold two million copies and put "one-hit wonder" criticisms to rest. During this time, he contributed the song " [Deadweight](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deadweight_(song) "Deadweight (song)")" to the soundtrack of the film _[A Life Less Ordinary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A_Life_Less_Ordinary "A Life Less Ordinary")_ (1997).[\[35\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beck#cite_note-rs98-35)

### _Mutations_ and _Midnite Vultures_ (1998–2001)

[![Close-up portrait of musician Beck having his eyes closed against an abstract blue background.](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/0/05/Oliver_Mark_-_Beck%2C_Hamburg_2000.jpg/500px-Oliver_Mark_-_Beck%2C_Hamburg_2000.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Oliver_Mark_-_Beck,_Hamburg_2000.jpg) Beck in [Hamburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hamburg "Hamburg"), 2000

Having not been in a proper studio since "Deadweight", Beck felt anxious to "go in and just do some stuff real quick", and compiled several songs he had had for years.[\[35\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beck#cite_note-rs98-35) Beck and his bandmates wrote songs in fourteen days, although just twelve made it onto the album, 1998's _[Mutations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mutations_(Beck_album) "Mutations (Beck album)")_.[\[35\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beck#cite_note-rs98-35) Beck hired the producer [Nigel Godrich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nigel_Godrich "Nigel Godrich"), who had produced [Radiohead](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radiohead "Radiohead")'s 1997 album _[OK Computer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/OK_Computer "OK Computer")_.[\[35\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beck#cite_note-rs98-35) Godrich was leaving the United States for England in a short time, which led to the album's quick production schedule—"No looking back, no doctoring anything."[\[35\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beck#cite_note-rs98-35) The whole point of the record was to capture the performance of the musicians live, an uncharacteristic far-cry from the cut-and-paste aesthetic of _Odelay_.[\[35\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beck#cite_note-rs98-35) Though the album was originally slated for release by Bong Load Records, Geffen intervened and issued the record against Beck's wishes.[\[36\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beck#cite_note-36)[\[37\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beck#cite_note-37) The artist then sought to void his contracts with both record labels, and in turn the labels sued him for breach of contract. The litigation went on for years and it remains unclear to this day if it has ever been completely resolved.[\[38\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beck#cite_note-38) Beck was later awarded Best Alternative Music Performance for _Mutations_ at the [42nd Grammy Awards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/42nd_Grammy_Awards "42nd Grammy Awards").[\[39\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beck#cite_note-39)

_[Midnite Vultures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Midnite_Vultures "Midnite Vultures")_, Beck's next studio effort, was originally recorded as a double album, and more than 25 nearly completed songs were left behind.[\[31\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beck#cite_note-p4k11-31) In the studio, Beck and producers studied contemporary hip-hop and R&B, specifically [R. Kelly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/R._Kelly "R. Kelly"), in order to embrace and incorporate those influences in the way [Al Green](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al_Green "Al Green") and [Stax records](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stax_Records "Stax Records") had done in previous decades.[\[31\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beck#cite_note-p4k11-31) In July 1998, a core group began to assemble at Beck's [Pasadena](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pasadena,_California "Pasadena, California") home: bassist [Justin Meldal-Johnsen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Justin_Meldal-Johnsen "Justin Meldal-Johnsen"), keyboardist [Roger Joseph Manning Jr.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roger_Joseph_Manning_Jr. "Roger Joseph Manning Jr."), and producer-engineers [Mickey Petralia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mickey_Petralia "Mickey Petralia") and [Tony Hoffer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tony_Hoffer "Tony Hoffer").[\[33\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beck#cite_note-rs99-33) Dozens of session players passed through including Beck's father, David Campbell, who played viola and arranged some of the strings. The musicians held communal meals and mountain-bike rides on dusty trails nearby, but remained focused on Beck's instructions: to make an up-tempo album that would be fun to play on tour night after night.[\[33\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beck#cite_note-rs99-33) "I had so many things going on", said Beck of the recording process. "I had a couple of rooms of computers hooked up, I was doing B sides for Japan, I was programming beats in one room and someone would be cooking dinner in the other room."[\[33\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beck#cite_note-rs99-33) In November 1999, Geffen released the much-anticipated _Midnite Vultures_,[\[40\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beck#cite_note-40) which attracted confusion: "fans and critics misguidedly worried whether it was serious or a goof," and as a result, _[The New York Times](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_New_York_Times "The New York Times")_ wrote that the album "never won the audience it deserved".[\[41\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beck#cite_note-nyt05-41) The record was supported by an extensive world tour. For Beck, it was a return to the high-energy performances that had been his trademark as far back as Lollapalooza. The live stage set included a red bed that descended from the ceiling for the song " [Debra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Debra_(song) "Debra (song)")", and the touring band was complemented by a brass section.[\[42\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beck#cite_note-42) _Midnite Vultures_ was nominated for Best Album at the [43rd Annual Grammy Awards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/43rd_Annual_Grammy_Awards "43rd Annual Grammy Awards").[\[43\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beck#cite_note-43)

### _Sea Change_ (2002–2003)

In 2000, Beck and his fiancée, stylist Leigh Limon, ended their nine-year relationship.[\[44\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beck#cite_note-age-44) Beck lapsed into a period of melancholy and introspection, during which he wrote the bleak, acoustic-based tracks later found on _Sea Change_.[\[45\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beck#cite_note-heartbreak-45) Beck sat on the songs, not wanting to talk about his personal life; he later said that he wanted to focus on music and "not really strew my baggage across the public lobby". Eventually, however, he decided the songs spoke to a common experience, and that it would not seem self-indulgent to record them.[\[46\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beck#cite_note-beckchecksin-46) In 2001, Beck drifted back to the songs and called Godrich.[\[47\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beck#cite_note-time-47)

Retailers initially predicted that the album would not receive much radio support, but they also believed that Beck's maverick reputation and critical acclaim, in addition to the possibility of multiple Grammy nominations, might offset _Sea Change_'s noncommercial sound.[\[46\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beck#cite_note-beckchecksin-46) _Sea Change_, issued by Geffen in September 2002, was regardless a commercial hit and critical darling,[\[41\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beck#cite_note-nyt05-41) with _[Rolling Stone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rolling_Stone "Rolling Stone")_ revering it as "the best album Beck has ever made... an impeccable album of truth and light from the end of love. This is his _[Blood on the Tracks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blood_on_the_Tracks "Blood on the Tracks")_."[\[48\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beck#cite_note-rs02review-48) The album was later listed by the magazine as one of the best records of the decade and of all time, and it also placed second on the year's [Pazz & Jop](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pazz_&_Jop "Pazz & Jop") Critics Poll. _Sea Change_ yielded a low-key, theater-based acoustic tour, as well as a larger tour with [the Flaming Lips](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Flaming_Lips "The Flaming Lips") as Beck's opening and backing band.[\[49\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beck#cite_note-becktour-49)[\[50\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beck#cite_note-flaminglips-50) Beck was playful and energetic, sometimes throwing in covers of [the Rolling Stones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Rolling_Stones "The Rolling Stones"), [Big Star](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Big_Star "Big Star"), [the Zombies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Zombies "The Zombies") and [the Velvet Underground](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Velvet_Underground "The Velvet Underground").[\[48\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beck#cite_note-rs02review-48)[\[51\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beck#cite_note-jackwhite-51)

Following the release of _Sea Change_, Beck felt newer compositions were sketches for something more evolved in the same direction, and wrote nearly 35 more songs in the coming months, keeping demos of them on tapes in a suitcase.[\[31\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beck#cite_note-p4k11-31) During his solo tour, the tapes were left backstage during a stop in Washington, D.C., and Beck was never able to recover them. It was disheartening to the musician, who felt the two years of songwriting represented something more technically complex. As a result, Beck took a break and wrote no original compositions in 2003.[\[31\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beck#cite_note-p4k11-31) Feeling as though it might take him a while to "get back to that \[songwriting\] territory", he entered the studio with Dust Brothers to complete a project that dated back to _Odelay_. Nearly half of the songs had existed since the 1990s.[\[31\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beck#cite_note-p4k11-31)

### _Guero_ and _The Information_ (2004–2007)

[![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/3/31/Beck_Hansen.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail_unscaled)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Beck_Hansen.jpg) Beck in 2005

_[Guero](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guero "Guero")_, Beck's ninth studio album, was recorded over the span of nine months during which several significant events occurred in his life: his girlfriend, Marissa Ribisi, became pregnant; they were married; their son, Cosimo, was born; and they moved out of Silver Lake.[\[41\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beck#cite_note-nyt05-41)[\[52\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beck#cite_note-guerolito-52) The collaboration with the Dust Brothers, his second, was notable for their use of high-tech measures to achieve a lo-fi sound.[\[41\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beck#cite_note-nyt05-41) For example, after recording a "sonically perfect" version of a song at one of the nicest recording studios in Hollywood, the Dust Brothers processed it in an [Echoplex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Echoplex "Echoplex") to create a gritty, reverb-heavy sound: "We did this high-tech recording and ran it through a [transistor radio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transistor_radio "Transistor radio"). It sounded too good, that was the problem."[\[41\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beck#cite_note-nyt05-41) Initially due to be released in October 2004, _Guero_ faced delays and did not come out until March 2005, though unmastered copies of the tracks surfaced online in January.[\[53\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beck#cite_note-finished-53)

_Guero_ debuted at number two on the [_Billboard_ 200](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Billboard_200 "Billboard 200"), selling 162,000 copies, an all-time sales high.[\[54\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beck#cite_note-guerobb-54) Lead single " [E-Pro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/E-Pro "E-Pro")" peaked at number one at Modern Rock radio, making it his first chart-topper since "Loser".[\[55\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beck#cite_note-charts-55) Beck, inspired by the [Nintendocore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nintendocore "Nintendocore") remix scene and feeling a connection with its lo-fi, home-recording method, collaborated with artists 8-Bit and Paza on _[Hell Yes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hell_Yes_(EP) "Hell Yes (EP)")_, an [EP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Extended_play "Extended play") issued in February 2005.[\[52\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beck#cite_note-guerolito-52) In December 2005, Geffen also issued _[Guerolito](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guerolito "Guerolito")_, a fully reworked version of _Guero_ featuring remixes by the [Beastie Boys](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beastie_Boys "Beastie Boys")' [Ad-Rock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ad-Rock "Ad-Rock"), the Dust Brothers' [John King](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_King_(producer) "John King (producer)") and Scottish electronic duo [Boards of Canada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boards_of_Canada "Boards of Canada").[\[52\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beck#cite_note-guerolito-52) _Guerolito_ combines remixes previously heard as B-sides and new versions of album tracks to make a track-by-track reconfiguration of the album.[\[52\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beck#cite_note-guerolito-52) Also released in 2005 was _A Brief Overview_, a 12-track promotional-only "History of Beck" compilation CD sampler that featured a combination of older and newer Beck tracks.[\[56\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beck#cite_note-56)

[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/7/75/Beck_2006-01.jpg/500px-Beck_2006-01.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Beck_2006-01.jpg) Beck in 2006

_[The Information](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Information_(Beck_album) "The Information (Beck album)")_, Beck's tenth studio album, began production around the same time as _Guero_, in 2003. Working again with Godrich, Beck built a studio in his garden, where they wrote many of the tracks.[\[57\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beck#cite_note-rs06-57) "The idea was to get people in a room together recording live, hitting bad notes and screaming," said Beck, adding that the album is best described as "introspective hip hop".[\[58\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beck#cite_note-rs06.1-58) Beck described the recording process as "painful", noting that he edited down songs constantly and he perhaps recorded the album three times.[\[59\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beck#cite_note-bbc-59) For the release, Beck was allowed for the first time to fulfill a long-running wish for an unconventional rollout: he made low-budget videos to accompany each song, packaged the CD with sheets of stickers so buyers could customize the cover, and leaked tracks and videos on his website months ahead of the album's release.[\[57\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beck#cite_note-rs06-57)[\[60\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beck#cite_note-60) Digital download releases automatically downloaded the song's additional video for each single sale, and physical copies came bundled with an additional DVD featuring fifteen videos.[\[57\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beck#cite_note-rs06-57)

### _Modern Guilt_, production work, Record Club and _Song Reader_ (2008–2013)

In 2007, Beck released the single " [Timebomb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Timebomb_(Beck_song) "Timebomb (Beck song)")", which was nominated for a [Grammy Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grammy_Award "Grammy Award") for Best Solo Rock Vocal Performance.[\[61\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beck#cite_note-nyt08-61) For his next studio effort, his eleventh, Beck tapped [Danger Mouse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Danger_Mouse_(musician) "Danger Mouse (musician)") to produce, and the two first met in December 2007 to record. The duo knocked out two tracks in two days, but the notion that the album would be finished in a timely fashion soon evaporated.[\[61\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beck#cite_note-nyt08-61) Beck had known Danger Mouse casually before, as many of his former musicians ended up working with Danger Mouse's side project, [Gnarls Barkley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gnarls_Barkley "Gnarls Barkley"). Still, the musicians were surprised at how well they got along.[\[62\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beck#cite_note-rs08-62) Following the grueling recording schedule, Beck was exhausted, calling it "the most intense work I've ever done on anything", relating that he "did at least 10 weeks with no days off, until four or five in the morning every night."[\[62\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beck#cite_note-rs08-62) Beck's original vision was a short 10-track burst with two-minute songs, but the songs gradually grew as he fit 'two years of songwriting into two and a half months."[\[62\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beck#cite_note-rs08-62) _[Modern Guilt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Modern_Guilt "Modern Guilt")_ (2008) was "full of off-kilter rhythms and left-field breakdowns, with an overall 1960s vibe."[\[62\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beck#cite_note-rs08-62)

_Modern Guilt_ was the final release in Beck's contract with Geffen Records. Beck, then 38, had held the contract since his early 20s.[\[61\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beck#cite_note-nyt08-61)[\[62\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beck#cite_note-rs08-62) Released from his label contract and going independent, Beck began working more heavily on his own seven-year-old label, which went through a variety of _[Song Reader](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Song_Reader "Song Reader")_, a project Beck released in December 2012, is 20 songs presented only as sheet music, in the hopes that enterprising musicians will record their own versions.[\[71\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beck#cite_note-srnpr-71) The idea of _Song Reader_ came about nearly fifteen years prior, shortly after the release of _Odelay_.[\[13\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beck#cite_note-nym12-13) When sent a book of transcribed sheet music for that album, Beck decided to play through it and grew interested in the world before recorded sound. He aimed to keep the arrangements as open as possible, to re-create the simplicity of the standards, and became preoccupied with creating only pieces that could fit within the [Great American Songbook](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_American_Songbook "Great American Songbook").[\[13\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beck#cite_note-nym12-13) In 2013 Beck began playing special _Song Reader_ concerts with a variety of guests and announced he was working on a record of _Song Reader_ material with other musicians as well as possibly a compilation of fan versions.[\[72\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beck#cite_note-Future_Heart-72)

In the summer of 2013, Beck was reported to be working on two new studio albums: one a more self-contained acoustic disc in the vein of _One Foot in the Grave_ and another described as a "proper follow-up" to _Modern Guilt_.[\[73\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beck#cite_note-rs13-73) Beck expected to release both albums independently, and released three standalone singles over the course of the year: the [electro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electro_(music) "Electro (music)") ballad " [Defriended](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Defriended "Defriended")", the chorus-heavy "I Won't Be Long", and finally "Gimme".[\[73\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beck#cite_note-rs13-73)[\[74\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beck#cite_note-rs13.1-74)[\[75\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beck#cite_note-75) In October 2013, Beck signed to [Capitol Records](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Capitol_Records "Capitol Records").[\[76\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beck#cite_note-Columbia-76)

### _Morning Phase_, _Colors_, _Hyperspace_ (2014–2022)

In January, Beck released the lead single " [Blue Moon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blue_Moon_(Beck_song) "Blue Moon (Beck song)")" from his twelfth studio album, _[Morning Phase](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Morning_Phase "Morning Phase")_.[\[77\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beck#cite_note-Blue_Moon_Amazon-77) A second single " [Waking Light](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Waking_Light "Waking Light")" was also released a week prior to the official release of _Morning Phase_ on February 21, 2014.[\[78\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beck#cite_note-Waking_Light_Amazon-78)[\[79\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beck#cite_note-79)[\[80\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beck#cite_note-Morning_Phase_Amazon-80) For the recording of the album, Beck reunited with many of the same musicians with whom he had worked on the critically acclaimed 2002 album _[Sea Change](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sea_Change_(album) "Sea Change (album)")_, and likely because of this, it has been noted that the two albums share a similar mood and genre.[\[81\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beck#cite_note-81)

On February 8, 2015, at the 57th Annual Grammy Awards, _Morning Phase_ won three Grammys: [Best Engineered Album, Non-Classical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grammy_Award_for_Best_Engineered_Album,_Non-Classical "Grammy Award for Best Engineered Album, Non-Classical"); [Best Rock Album](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grammy_Award_for_Best_Rock_Album "Grammy Award for Best Rock Album"); and [Album of the Year](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grammy_Award_for_Album_of_the_Year "Grammy Award for Album of the Year").[\[82\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beck#cite_note-82) Upon receiving the Album of the Year award, the album beat out Pharrell Williams's _G I R L_, Beyoncé's [self-titled album](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beyonc%C3%A9_(album) "Beyoncé (album)"), Sam Smith's _[In the Lonely Hour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/In_the_Lonely_Hour "In the Lonely Hour")_, and Ed Sheeran's _[x](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/X_(Ed_Sheeran_album) "X (Ed Sheeran album)")_.[\[83\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beck#cite_note-83)

[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/1/1f/OhanaSep18-187_%2845223106461%29.jpg/500px-OhanaSep18-187_%2845223106461%29.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:OhanaSep18-187_(45223106461).jpg) Beck in October 2018

In June 2015, Beck released " [Dreams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dreams_(Beck_song) "Dreams (Beck song)")", the first single for his next album.[\[84\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beck#cite_note-84) The next June, he released the next single, " [Wow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wow_(Beck_song) "Wow (Beck song)")".[\[85\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beck#cite_note-85) On September 8, 2017, Beck released the single "Dear Life", which was followed up with the official release of "Up All Night" on September 18.[\[86\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beck#cite_note-86)[\[87\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beck#cite_note-Up_All_Night-87) The album, _[Colors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colors_(Beck_album) "Colors (Beck album)")_, was released on October 13, 2017. It was recorded at co-executive producer [Greg Kurstin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greg_Kurstin "Greg Kurstin")'s Los Angeles studio, with Beck and Kurstin playing nearly every instrument themselves.[\[88\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beck#cite_note-Rolling_Stone_Colors-88) The experimental pop-fused record received generally positive reviews from critics.[\[89\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beck#cite_note-89) On July 18, 2018, Beck performed the title track _Colors_, and the first single "Wow" on _[The Late Show with Stephen Colbert](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Late_Show_with_Stephen_Colbert "The Late Show with Stephen Colbert")_.[\[90\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beck#cite_note-90)

On April 15, 2019, Beck released a single co-produced with [Pharrell Williams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pharrell_Williams "Pharrell Williams") titled " [Saw Lightning](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saw_Lightning "Saw Lightning")" from his fourteenth studio album, titled _[Hyperspace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hyperspace_(album) "Hyperspace (album)")_.[\[91\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beck#cite_note-NPR_Hyperspace-91) The song " [Dark Places](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dark_Places_(song) "Dark Places (song)")" was released on November 6, with the album being released on November 22.[\[92\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beck#cite_note-92)

### _Ride Lonesome_ (2022–present)

On September 25, 2022, Beck released a cover of [Neil Young](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neil_Young "Neil Young")'s track " [Old Man](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_Man_(song) "Old Man (song)")" to promote a _[Sunday Night Football](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NBC_Sunday_Night_Football "NBC Sunday Night Football")_ game. The track was nominated for the [Grammy Award for Best Rock Performance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grammy_Award_for_Best_Rock_Performance "Grammy Award for Best Rock Performance") for the 65th [Grammy Awards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grammy_Awards "Grammy Awards") later in 2022.[\[93\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beck#cite_note-93)[\[94\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beck#cite_note-94) A music video for the track would be released on December 8, 2022.[\[95\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beck#cite_note-95) Following the commercial featuring the cover, Neil Young posted a still image from his 1988 music video for "This Note's For You", an anti-commercialization song in protest of the cover and commercial.[\[96\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beck#cite_note-96)

Later on February 10, 2023, he released the song "Thinking About You", his first original solo-track since 2019's [_Hyperspace_](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hyperspace_(album) "Hyperspace (album)"). A music video for the track would be released on February 20, 2023. It is currently unknown if this means a 15th studio album will be released in the upcoming future or not.[\[97\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beck#cite_note-:1-97) Many music critics have noted that it sounds like a return to the folk of _[Morning Phase](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Morning_Phase "Morning Phase")_ and [_Sea Change_](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sea_Change_(album) "Sea Change (album)").[\[98\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beck#cite_note-98)[\[99\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beck#cite_note-99)[\[97\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beck#cite_note-:1-97)

On June 21, 2023, Beck released the track " [Odyssey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Odyssey_(song) "Odyssey (song)")" with French [pop-rock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pop_rock "Pop rock") band [Phoenix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phoenix_(French_band) "Phoenix (French band)"), who did a co-headlining summer tour with Beck, which started in August and ended in September.[\[100\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beck#cite_note-100)[\[101\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beck#cite_note-101)[\[102\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beck#cite_note-102)[\[103\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beck#cite_note-103) On 29 January, 2026, Beck announced the compilation album _[Everybody's Gotta Learn Sometime](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Everybody's_Gotta_Learn_Sometime_(album) "Everybody's Gotta Learn Sometime (album)")_, curating a collection of rarities, deep cuts and cover versions, including previously unreleased recordings of "Your Cheatin' Heart" and "True Love Will Find You in the End"[\[104\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beck#cite_note-104). Initially, the eight-track album released digitally with a physical release on red vinyl set for 13 February, 2026.

On April 20, 2026, Beck released the single " [Ride Lonesome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ride_Lonesome_(song) "Ride Lonesome (song)")" to mostly positive reviews.[\[105\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beck#cite_note-105) When asked if this meant an album would follow it, Beck confirmed he was writing more music.[\[106\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beck#cite_note-106) On July 15, Beck announced his fifteenth studio album _[Ride Lonesome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ride_Lonesome_(album) "Ride Lonesome (album)")_, set to be released on September 18, 2026.[\[107\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beck#cite_note-107)

## Collaborations and contributions

[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/b/bf/BeckMSG190718-78_%2846341252074%29.jpg/500px-BeckMSG190718-78_%2846341252074%29.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:BeckMSG190718-78_(46341252074).jpg) Beck performs at [Madison Square Garden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Madison_Square_Garden "Madison Square Garden") with [Jenny Lewis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jenny_Lewis "Jenny Lewis") in 2018

Beck co-wrote and performed on the song "Flavor", from the 1994 [Jon Spencer Blues Explosion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jon_Spencer_Blues_Explosion "Jon Spencer Blues Explosion") album _[Orange](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orange_(Jon_Spencer_Blues_Explosion_album) "Orange (Jon Spencer Blues Explosion album)")_.

In 2019, Beck contributed to a tribute album for [Bruce Haack](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bruce_Haack "Bruce Haack") and Esther Nelson and their label Dimension 5 Records. The album, [Dimension Mix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dimension_Mix "Dimension Mix"), released in 2005, was a benefit for [Cure Autism Now](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cure_Autism_Now "Cure Autism Now") that was produced by [Ross Harris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ross_Harris_(actor) "Ross Harris (actor)"), an early collaborator who designed the artwork for _Mellow Gold_.

In 2004, Beck contributed a cover of the Korgi's 1980 New Wave song, "Everybody's Gotta Learn Sometime" to the soundtrack for Eternal Sunshine of the Spotless Mind.

On June 20, 2009, Beck announced that he was starting an experiment called [Record Club](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Record_Club "Record Club"), in which he and other musicians would record cover versions of entire albums in one day. The first album covered by Beck's Record Club was _[The Velvet Underground & Nico](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Velvet_Underground_&_Nico "The Velvet Underground & Nico")_. Starting on June 18, the club began posting covers of songs from the album on Thursday evenings, each with its own video.[\[108\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beck#cite_note-108) On September 4, 2009, Beck announced the second Record Club album, _[Songs of Leonard Cohen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Songs_of_Leonard_Cohen "Songs of Leonard Cohen")_. Contributors included [MGMT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MGMT "MGMT"), [Devendra Banhart](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Devendra_Banhart "Devendra Banhart"), Andrew Stockdale of [Wolfmother](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wolfmother "Wolfmother") and Binki Shapiro of [Little Joy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Little_Joy "Little Joy").[\[109\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beck#cite_note-109) In the third Record Club venture, [Wilco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wilco "Wilco"), [Feist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Feist_(singer) "Feist (singer)"), [Jamie Lidell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jamie_Lidell "Jamie Lidell") and [James Gadson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Gadson "James Gadson") joined Beck to cover [Skip Spence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Skip_Spence "Skip Spence")'s [_Oar_](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oar_(Skip_Spence_album) "Oar (Skip Spence album)"). The first song, "Little Hands", was posted on Beck's website on November 12, 2009.[\[110\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beck#cite_note-110) The Record Club has since covered albums by [INXS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/INXS "INXS") and [Yanni](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yanni "Yanni").

On June 19, 2009, Beck announced Planned Obsolescence, a weekly DJ set put together by Beck or guest DJs. Soon after, on July 7, Beck announced that his website would be featuring "extended informal conversations with musicians, artists, filmmakers, and other various persons" in a section called Irrelevant Topics. Then, on July 12, he added a section called Videotheque, which he said would contain "promotional videos from each album, as well as live clips, TV show appearances and other rarities". Also in 2009, Beck collaborated with [Charlotte Gainsbourg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charlotte_Gainsbourg "Charlotte Gainsbourg") on her album _[IRM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IRM_(album) "IRM (album)")_, which was released in January 2010. Beck wrote the music, co-wrote the lyrics, and produced and mixed the album. The lead single, " [Heaven Can Wait](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heaven_Can_Wait_(Charlotte_Gainsbourg_song) "Heaven Can Wait (Charlotte Gainsbourg song)")", is a duet by Beck and Gainsbourg.[\[111\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beck#cite_note-111)

In late February 2010, it was announced that electronic artist [Tobacco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tobacco_(musician) "Tobacco (musician)") of [Black Moth Super Rainbow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_Moth_Super_Rainbow "Black Moth Super Rainbow") had collaborated with Beck on two songs, "Fresh Hex" and "Grape Aerosmith", on his upcoming album _Maniac Meat_. Tobacco revealed that in making the album, Beck sent the vocal parts to him, and that they had never actually met.\[ _[citation needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed "Wikipedia:Citation needed")_\] In March 2010, Beck revealed that he had produced songs for the new Jamie Lidell album, _[Compass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Compass_(Jamie_Lidell_album) "Compass (Jamie Lidell album)")_.[\[112\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beck#cite_note-112) In the summer of 2010, Beck contributed songs to both _[The Twilight Saga: Eclipse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Twilight_Saga:_Eclipse_(soundtrack) "The Twilight Saga: Eclipse (soundtrack)")_ soundtrack, with "Let's Get Lost" (a duet with [Bat for Lashes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bat_for_Lashes "Bat for Lashes")),[\[113\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beck#cite_note-113) and _[True Blood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/True_Blood "True Blood") (HBO Original Series Soundtrack, Vol. 2)_, with "Bad Blood".[\[114\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beck#cite_note-114) He also contributed songs to the soundtrack of [Edgar Wright](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edgar_Wright "Edgar Wright")'s film _[Scott Pilgrim vs. the World](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scott_Pilgrim_vs._the_World "Scott Pilgrim vs. the World")_, which was released in August 2010.[\[115\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beck#cite_note-115)

In 2011, he collaborated with [Seu Jorge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seu_Jorge "Seu Jorge") on a track titled "Tropicália (Mario C. 2011 Remix)" for the [Red Hot Organization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Red_Hot_Organization "Red Hot Organization")'s charitable album _Red Hot+Rio 2_, a follow-up to the 1996 album _[Red Hot + Rio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Red_Hot_+_Rio "Red Hot + Rio")_. ## Musical style

Beck's musical style has been considered [alternative](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alternative_rock "Alternative rock")[\[137\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beck#cite_note-137) and [indie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Independent_music "Independent music").[\[138\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beck#cite_note-138) He has played many of the instruments in his music himself.[\[139\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beck#cite_note-the_information-139) Beck has also done some remixes for fellow artists, notably [David Bowie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Bowie "David Bowie") and [Björk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bj%C3%B6rk "Björk"). He has been known to synthesize several musical elements together in his music, including [folk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Folk_music "Folk music"), [psychedelia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Psychedelia "Psychedelia"), [electronic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electronic_music "Electronic music"), [country](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Country_music "Country music"), [Latin music](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latin_American_music "Latin American music"), [hip-hop](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hip-hop "Hip-hop"), [funk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Funk "Funk"), [soul](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soul_music "Soul music"), [blues](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blues "Blues"), [noise music](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Noise_music "Noise music"), [jazz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jazz "Jazz"), [art pop](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Art_pop "Art pop") and many types of rock.[\[140\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beck#cite_note-guero-140)[\[141\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beck#cite_note-141) Because of this unconventional approach, Beck has been described as a [postmodern](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Postmodernism "Postmodernism") musician.[\[142\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beck#cite_note-142) He has also taken music from Los Angeles as a reference point in his songs.[\[140\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beck#cite_note-guero-140)

Pitchfork Media applauded _Midnite Vultures_, saying, "Beck wonderfully blends [Prince](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prince_(musician) "Prince (musician)"), [Talking Heads](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Talking_Heads "Talking Heads"), _[Paul's Boutique](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul's_Boutique "Paul's Boutique")_, ' [Shake Your Bon-Bon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shake_Your_Bon-Bon "Shake Your Bon-Bon")', and Mathlete on _Midnite Vultures_, his most consistent and playful album yet." The review commented that his mix of "goofy piety and ambiguous intent" helped the album.[\[143\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beck#cite_note-143) A Beck song called "Harry Partch", a tribute to [the composer of the same name](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harry_Partch "Harry Partch") and his "corporeal" music, employs [Partch's 43-tone scale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harry_Partch's_43-tone_scale "Harry Partch's 43-tone scale").[\[144\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beck#cite_note-144)

## Art career

During 1998, Beck's art collaborations with his grandfather Al Hansen were featured in an exhibition titled "Beck & Al Hansen: Playing With Matches", which showcased solo and collaborative [collage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Collage "Collage"), assemblage, drawing and poetry works.[\[145\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beck#cite_note-145) The show toured from the [Santa Monica Museum of Art](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santa_Monica_Museum_of_Art "Santa Monica Museum of Art") to galleries in New York City and [Winnipeg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Winnipeg "Winnipeg"), Manitoba. Beck chose Winnipeg due to a family connection, as his grandfather gave their family stability through his work as a street car conductor in Winnipeg.[\[146\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beck#cite_note-146) A catalog of the show was published by Plug in Editions/Smart Art Press.[\[147\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beck#cite_note-147)

## Personal life

Beck's nine-year relationship with designer Leigh Limon and their breakup is said to have inspired his 2002 album _Sea Change_.[\[148\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beck#cite_note-Salon-148) He wrote most of the songs for the album one week after the breakup.[\[149\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beck#cite_note-Time-149) In April 2004, Beck married actress [Marissa Ribisi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marissa_Ribisi "Marissa Ribisi"), the twin sister of actor [Giovanni Ribisi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Giovanni_Ribisi "Giovanni Ribisi"), shortly before the birth of their son, Cosimo Henri.[\[150\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beck#cite_note-150)[\[151\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beck#cite_note-Spin-151) Their daughter, Tuesday, was born in 2007.[\[61\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beck#cite_note-nyt08-61) Beck filed for divorce from Ribisi on February 15, 2019.[\[152\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beck#cite_note-152) Their divorce was finalized on September 3, 2021.[\[153\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beck#cite_note-153)

Beck has described himself as both [Jewish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jews "Jews")[\[7\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beck#cite_note-Spin_2008-09-7) and a [Scientologist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scientologist "Scientologist"), but no longer identifies as the latter.[\[154\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beck#cite_note-Sunday_Tribune-154)[\[155\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beck#cite_note-:0-155) Through his parents, he has been involved in Scientology for most of his life, and his ex-wife, Marissa, is also a second-generation Scientologist.[\[156\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beck#cite_note-Celebrific-156) He publicly acknowledged his affiliation for the first time in a _[New York Times Magazine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_New_York_Times_Magazine "The New York Times Magazine")_ interview on March 6, 2005.[\[41\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beck#cite_note-nyt05-41)[\[10\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beck#cite_note-:%22ny%22-10) In a 2008 interview with [_Spin_](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spin_(magazine) "Spin (magazine)"), when asked about press interest in his spiritual life, Beck responded, "I don't pay attention to any of it, really. I'm not that aware of what the perception is. My father was doing Scientology in the '60s, so it's something that has been around for most of my life," adding, "I was raised celebrating Jewish holidays, and I consider myself Jewish. But I've read books on Scientology and drawn insights from that."[\[157\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beck#cite_note-157)[\[158\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beck#cite_note-usat-158) In a November 2019 interview with _[The Sydney Morning Herald](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Sydney_Morning_Herald "The Sydney Morning Herald")_, Beck disavowed previous reports of his being a Scientologist and clarified, "I think there's a misconception that I'm a Scientologist. I'm not a Scientologist. I don't have any connection or affiliation with it."[\[155\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beck#cite_note-:0-155)[\[158\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beck#cite_note-usat-158)

Beck's mother is artist/writer/performer [Bibbe Hansen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bibbe_Hansen "Bibbe Hansen"), a former [Andy Warhol's](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andy_Warhol "Andy Warhol") [Factory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Factory "The Factory") collaborator.[\[159\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beck#cite_note-Bibbe_Hansen,_bio-159)[\[160\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beck#cite_note-160) Since the early 1980s, she has been married to Chicano artist and media maker Sean Carrillo, a member of the performance art group ASCO, who became Beck's stepfather at age 22 when the musician was twelve years old.[\[161\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beck#cite_note-ModernDadhood2023-161) Along with Hansen, Carrillo operated the Troy Cafe, an artist hub in Los Angeles.[\[161\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beck#cite_note-ModernDadhood2023-161)

Beck has one full sibling, [fiber artist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fiber_artist "Fiber artist") Channing Hansen, born in 1972 in Los Angeles.[\[162\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beck#cite_note-162) They have a half-sister by their father and his current wife, singer and composer Pauline Frechette (known by the stage name Raven Kane), born in Los Angeles in 1985 - musician, actress, and former competitive figure skater, Alyssa Suede Lake. Poet Rain Whittaker, a friend of Channing's, lived with Hansen and Carrillo from the ages of 13 through 18.[\[163\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beck#cite_note-163)[\[159\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beck#cite_note-Bibbe_Hansen,_bio-159)[\[161\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beck#cite_note-ModernDadhood2023-161)

Beck sustained a spinal injury while filming the music video for 2005's " [E-Pro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/E-Pro "E-Pro")". The incident was severe enough to curtail his touring schedule for a few years, but he has since recovered.[\[72\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beck#cite_note-Future_Heart-72)[\[164\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beck#cite_note-164)

Beck primarily lived in [Los Feliz, Los Angeles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Los_Feliz,_Los_Angeles "Los Feliz, Los Angeles") until 2022.[\[165\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beck#cite_note-165) He owns properties in California, Tennessee, and Arkansas.[\[166\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beck#cite_note-166)
