**Half Japanese** is an American [art punk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Art_punk "Art punk") band formed by brothers [Jad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jad_Fair "Jad Fair") and David Fair in 1974,[\[1\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Half_Japanese#cite_note-1) after their family's relocation to [Uniontown, Maryland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uniontown,_Maryland "Uniontown, Maryland").

Half Japanese's original instrumentation included a small drum set, which they took turns playing; vocals; and an out-of-tune, distorted guitar. Both Fair brothers sang, although over time Jad held the frontman role. As of the band's last several releases since the 1990s, according to the album and CD credits, Half Japanese composes and plays the entirety of the music while Jad, eschewing his role as guitarist from earlier albums, plays almost no guitar but is responsible for the vocals and lyrics, which typically divide into either "love songs or monster songs."[\[2\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Half_Japanese#cite_note-2)

## Band history

Their lyrics often deal with monsters and the [supernatural](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supernatural "Supernatural") (especially as influenced by ["creature feature"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Horror_films "Horror films") and [sci-fi movies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Science_fiction_films "Science fiction films")), in addition to more conventional themes, such as young love.

The band was formed in Jad and David Fair's bedroom in the mid-1970s, with sources varying on the exact date.[\[3\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Half_Japanese#cite_note-allmusic-3) The band released their first single, "Calling All Girls", in August 1977, followed by a triple album, _[1/2 Gentlemen/Not Beasts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1/2_Gentlemen/Not_Beasts "1/2 Gentlemen/Not Beasts")_ in 1980.[\[4\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Half_Japanese#cite_note-Larkin80-4)

The band played and recorded as a duo until the early 1980s, when they began incorporating additional members into the group: Mark Jickling (guitar and vocals) and brothers Ricky and John Dreyfuss (drums and [saxophone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saxophone "Saxophone")). Since that time, dozens of musicians have come and gone under the Half Japanese banner, including Howard Wuelfing, [Don Fleming](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Don_Fleming_(musician) "Don Fleming (musician)") and Jay Spiegel both from the bands [B.A.L.L.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B.A.L.L. "B.A.L.L.") and [Velvet Monkeys](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Velvet_Monkeys "Velvet Monkeys"), and [Shockabilly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shockabilly "Shockabilly") bass player and [Shimmy Disc](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shimmy_Disc "Shimmy Disc") impresario [Mark Kramer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kramer_(musician) "Kramer (musician)") among others. Jad is the only member who has been with Half Japanese from the beginning.[\[4\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Half_Japanese#cite_note-Larkin80-4) David temporarily left the band in 1987 in order to take care of his family, and since then has been involved with the band sporadically.[\[3\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Half_Japanese#cite_note-allmusic-3)

The next line-up of Half Japanese came together in the late 1980s, proving to be a long-lasting and stable unit recording several albums and touring frequently throughout the United States, Europe, and Japan. This incarnation featured guitarist/multi-instrumentalist John Sluggett (also a longtime member of [Moe Tucker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moe_Tucker "Moe Tucker")'s band), multi-instrumentalist Jason Willett, Mick Hobbs, and drummer Gilles Reider. Since then, the group has worked with Moe Tucker from [The Velvet Underground](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Velvet_Underground "The Velvet Underground"), who produced and performed on _[Fire in the Sky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fire_in_the_Sky_(album) "Fire in the Sky (album)")_ (1992), as well as _[The Band That Would Be King](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Band_That_Would_Be_King "The Band That Would Be King")_ (1989), and with [Fred Frith](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fred_Frith "Fred Frith"), and [John Zorn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Zorn "John Zorn"), among others.[\[4\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Half_Japanese#cite_note-Larkin80-4)

In 1993, the band was brought to greater exposure when they opened for [Nirvana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nirvana_(band) "Nirvana (band)")'s 1993 _[In Utero](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/In_Utero_(album) "In Utero (album)")_ tour on dates in the US East Coast.[\[3\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Half_Japanese#cite_note-allmusic-3) [Kurt Cobain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kurt_Cobain "Kurt Cobain") would later wear a Half Japanese T-shirt when he died.[\[5\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Half_Japanese#cite_note-SPDFR-5) The band's history and influence are chronicled in the 1993 documentary _Half Japanese: The Band That Would Be King_ by [Jeff Feuerzeig](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jeff_Feuerzeig "Jeff Feuerzeig").[\[4\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Half_Japanese#cite_note-Larkin80-4) In 1997, the band signed with the [Alternative Tentacles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alternative_Tentacles "Alternative Tentacles") label and released the album _[Bone Head](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bone_Head "Bone Head")_. The same year, they self-released the album _[Heaven Sent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heaven_Sent_(Half_Japanese_album) "Heaven Sent (Half Japanese album)")_ on the label of drummer Gilles-Vincent Reider.[\[4\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Half_Japanese#cite_note-Larkin80-4)[\[3\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Half_Japanese#cite_note-allmusic-3) In 2001, they released their second album on Alternative Tentacles, _[Hello](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hello_(Half_Japanese_album) "Hello (Half Japanese album)")_.[\[3\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Half_Japanese#cite_note-allmusic-3)

The band was chosen by [Jeff Mangum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jeff_Mangum "Jeff Mangum") of [Neutral Milk Hotel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neutral_Milk_Hotel "Neutral Milk Hotel") to perform at the [All Tomorrow's Parties](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/All_Tomorrow's_Parties_(music_festival) "All Tomorrow's Parties (music festival)") festival that he curated in March 2012 in [Minehead](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minehead "Minehead").[\[6\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Half_Japanese#cite_note-6) In September 2014, the band released the album _[Overjoyed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Overjoyed_(Half_Japanese_album) "Overjoyed (Half Japanese album)")_ on [Joyful Noise Recordings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joyful_Noise_Recordings "Joyful Noise Recordings"), their first in 13 years,[\[7\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Half_Japanese#cite_note-7) which was followed by the limited edition _Bingo Ringo_ EP the following year.[\[8\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Half_Japanese#cite_note-8) The band subsequently moved to [Fire Records](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fire_Records_(UK) "Fire Records (UK)") released the albums _Hear the Lions Roar_ in 2017, _Why Not?_ in 2018, _Invincible_ in 2019 and _Crazy Hearts_ in 2020.[\[9\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Half_Japanese#cite_note-9)[\[3\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Half_Japanese#cite_note-allmusic-3) In 2021, _I Guess I'm Living: The Charmed Life Tapes_, an alternate recording of their 1988 album _Charmed Life_ containing previously unreleased material was released exclusively on [Record Store Day](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Record_Store_Day "Record Store Day").[\[10\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Half_Japanese#cite_note-10)[\[11\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Half_Japanese#cite_note-11) July 2023 saw their twentieth studio album, _Jump into Love_.[\[12\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Half_Japanese#cite_note-12)

Mick Hobbs died on January 3, 2025, at the age of 69.[\[13\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Half_Japanese#cite_note-13) David Fair died on August 29, 2026 at the age of 74, after a protracted battle with cancer.[\[14\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Half_Japanese#cite_note-14)
