**Bright Eyes** is an American [indie rock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indie_rock "Indie rock") band founded in [Omaha, Nebraska](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Omaha,_Nebraska "Omaha, Nebraska"), by singer-songwriter and guitarist [Conor Oberst](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conor_Oberst "Conor Oberst"). It consists of Oberst; multi-instrumentalist and [producer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Record_producer "Record producer") [Mike Mogis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mike_Mogis "Mike Mogis"); arranger, composer, trumpet and piano player [Nate Walcott](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nate_Walcott "Nate Walcott"); and a rotating line-up of collaborators drawn primarily from [Omaha's indie music scene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Music_of_Omaha#Indie_rock_music "Music of Omaha").

Between 1998 and 2011, the band's albums were released through [Saddle Creek Records](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saddle_Creek_Records "Saddle Creek Records"), a Nebraska-based label founded by Justin Oberst (Conor's brother) and Mogis. In January 2020, the band announced their return, having signed with [Dead Oceans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dead_Oceans "Dead Oceans"). Their most recent album— _[Five Dice, All Threes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Five_Dice,_All_Threes "Five Dice, All Threes")—_ was released in September 2024.

## History

### 1995–1998: _A Collection of Songs Written and Recorded 1995–1997_

After being a founding member of [Commander Venus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commander_Venus "Commander Venus")—which disbanded in 1997—guitarist/vocalist Conor Oberst turned to focus on his new project, Bright Eyes. In 1998, he released 20 of the songs he had been stockpiling as the first official Bright Eyes album, _[A Collection of Songs Written and Recorded 1995–1997](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A_Collection_of_Songs_Written_and_Recorded_1995%E2%80%931997 "A Collection of Songs Written and Recorded 1995–1997")._[\[7\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bright_Eyes_(band)#cite_note-7) The album saw Conor Oberst beginning to experiment with [drum machines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drum_machines "Drum machines"), keyboards and other instruments. The sound of the album ranges from bleating vocals to acoustic guitar songs and [techno](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Techno "Techno")-style synthesizer instrumentals. Critical reaction was negative, with [AllMusic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AllMusic "AllMusic") saying that many of "the songs disintegrate as his vocals are reduced to the unintelligible babbling of a child. Any balance the music maintained up to that point, however fragile, is lost and so, more than likely, is the listener."[\[8\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bright_Eyes_(band)#cite_note-8)

### 1998–1999: _Letting Off the Happiness_

On November 2, 1998, Saddle Creek released _[Letting Off the Happiness](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Letting_Off_the_Happiness "Letting Off the Happiness")_, a ten-track album that displayed a more focused and clearer sound than the previous album. According to the Saddle Creek press release, it features members of [Lullaby for the Working Class](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lullaby_for_the_Working_Class "Lullaby for the Working Class"), [Neutral Milk Hotel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neutral_Milk_Hotel "Neutral Milk Hotel"), and [of Montreal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Of_Montreal "Of Montreal"). [Park Ave.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Park_Ave._(band) "Park Ave. (band)") bandmate [Neely Jenkins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neely_Jenkins "Neely Jenkins") also contributed vocals. It was predominantly recorded in the Oberst family basement in Omaha on an analog eight track [reel to reel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reel_to_reel "Reel to reel"); with some work also done at keyboardist [Andy Lemaster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andy_Lemaster "Andy Lemaster")'s [Athens, Georgia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Athens,_Georgia "Athens, Georgia") studio.[\[9\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bright_Eyes_(band)#cite_note-9) Although almost all of the tracks feature a full band, "June on the West Coast" is performed with only acoustic guitar and vocals. "Padraic My Prince" gives a dramatic fictional account of the death of his baby brother, a story with a multitude of symbolic meanings. Oberst has referenced the song "Padraic My Prince" more than once in his music.

The song "An Attempt To Tip the Scales" on the album _[Fevers and Mirrors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fevers_and_Mirrors "Fevers and Mirrors")_ has a faux-interview near the end of the track. (Oberst is voiced by [Todd Fink](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Todd_Fink "Todd Fink"), who was a label-mate and had played in other bands with Oberst.) The interviewer is [Matt Silcock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matt_Silcock "Matt Silcock"), another labelmate on [Saddle Creek Records](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saddle_Creek_Records "Saddle Creek Records"). The interview was meant to be somewhat sarcastic and most of what the Oberst impersonator said was not true. At one point the interviewer asks the question: "So some of these references like babies in bathtubs are not biographical?" The Oberst impersonator replies: "Well I did have a brother who died in a bathtub... he drowned. Well actually I had five brothers that drowned." "No, I'm serious. My mother drowned one every year for five consecutive years. They were all named Padraic, and that's why they only got one song. It's kind of like walking out a door and discovering that it's a window."[\[10\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bright_Eyes_(band)#cite_note-10) Oberst also references the song in "Cartoon Blues" on the _[Four Winds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Four_Winds_(EP) "Four Winds (EP)")_ EP.

In November 1999, Bright Eyes released the five-song _[Every Day and Every Night](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Every_Day_and_Every_Night "Every Day and Every Night")_ EP, which included "Neely O'Hara" and "A Perfect Sonnet."

### 2000–2003: _Fevers and Mirrors_, _Lifted_, and _A Christmas Album_

In 2000, Bright Eyes released _[Fevers and Mirrors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fevers_and_Mirrors "Fevers and Mirrors"),_ with new instruments such as the flute, piano, and [accordion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Accordion "Accordion") introduced into the song arrangements. After "An Attempt to Tip the Scales", there is a mock radio interview that features [Todd Fink](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Todd_Fink "Todd Fink") of [The Faint](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Faint "The Faint") doing an impression of Oberst while reading a script that Oberst wrote. In this interview, the fake Oberst presents a strange, contradictory explanation of his attitude towards his music. It acknowledges criticisms of his [lyrics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lyrics "Lyrics") as overblown and insincere, which had begun to appear as the popularity of the band increased, but responds by stating that the lyrics are meant for personal interpretation. Oberst later commented that "It was a way to make fun of ourselves because the record is such a downer. I mean, that's one part of who I am but I also like laughing."[\[11\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bright_Eyes_(band)#cite_note-kittymagik-11)
The album placed 170 on Pitchfork Media's best 200 albums of the decade.[\[12\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bright_Eyes_(band)#cite_note-12)

With _[Lifted or The Story Is in the Soil, Keep Your Ear to the Ground](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lifted_or_The_Story_Is_in_the_Soil,_Keep_Your_Ear_to_the_Ground "Lifted or The Story Is in the Soil, Keep Your Ear to the Ground")_ in 2002, Bright Eyes became one of the year's most celebrated "new" artists, despite having been recording under that [moniker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moniker "Moniker") for a few years. They received national attention, including in several notable pieces in _[The New York Times](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_New_York_Times "The New York Times")_, the _[Los Angeles Times](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Los_Angeles_Times "Los Angeles Times")_, _[Time](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Time_(magazine) "Time (magazine)")_ magazine, _[Rolling Stone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rolling_Stone "Rolling Stone")_, _[Blender](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blender_(magazine) "Blender (magazine)")_, and _[Spin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spin_(magazine) "Spin (magazine)")_, many of which proclaimed Conor Oberst to be a significant new artist. The album was a commercial success and has sold over 250,000 copies,[\[13\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bright_Eyes_(band)#cite_note-13) a breakthrough for the label and for all of the band's peers at that time. Oberst stated that, before making this record, both he and Mike Mogis had an idea for a "sort of grandiose sound" that neither could really put into words. This was also the first album made after Oberst's break to play with [Desaparecidos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Desaparecidos_(band) "Desaparecidos (band)"). On December 1, 2002, Bright Eyes released their fifth studio album, _[A Christmas Album](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A_Christmas_Album_(Bright_Eyes_album) "A Christmas Album (Bright Eyes album)")_.

### 2004–2006: _I'm Wide Awake, It's Morning_; _Digital Ash in a Digital Urn_; and _Noise Floor_

[![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/8/8a/Bright_Eyes.jpg/500px-Bright_Eyes.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Bright_Eyes.jpg) Bright Eyes in 2005

During the [2004 election season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._presidential_election,_2004 "U.S. presidential election, 2004"), Bright Eyes toured with [Bruce Springsteen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bruce_Springsteen "Bruce Springsteen") and [R.E.M.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/R.E.M. "R.E.M.") on the [Vote for Change](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vote_for_Change "Vote for Change") tour,[\[14\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bright_Eyes_(band)#cite_note-14) further pushing Bright Eyes into the public eye. Oberst sang numerous duets with the likes of Springsteen and [Neil Young](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neil_Young "Neil Young").

January 25, 2005 saw the release of two distinctly different Bright Eyes albums: the folk-influenced _[I'm Wide Awake, It's Morning](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/I'm_Wide_Awake,_It's_Morning "I'm Wide Awake, It's Morning")_ and the electronic-pop inflected _[Digital Ash in a Digital Urn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Digital_Ash_in_a_Digital_Urn "Digital Ash in a Digital Urn")_. An extensive world tour followed the release of those albums. Part one of the tour was in support of _I'm Wide Awake_ and the second part was in support of _Digital Ash_. The first part was more similar to past tours with an intimate band setting. The decision to split the tour this way was practical as it would have been a "logistical nightmare" in terms of equipment and staff to perform songs from both albums simultaneously.[\[15\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bright_Eyes_(band)#cite_note-Faint-15) By the end of January 2005, _I'm Wide Awake_ was No.10 on the _Billboard_ charts while _Digital Ash_ was No.15.

In early 2005, Bright Eyes supported R.E.M. in their tour of Australia and New Zealand, as well as headlined shows themselves.[\[15\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bright_Eyes_(band)#cite_note-Faint-15) On May 2, 2005 Bright Eyes appeared on _[The Tonight Show with Jay Leno](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Tonight_Show_with_Jay_Leno "The Tonight Show with Jay Leno")_ and performed the [protest song](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protest_song "Protest song") " [When the President Talks to God](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/When_the_President_Talks_to_God "When the President Talks to God")" directed at President George W. Bush. A 7" vinyl single of the song was sold at concerts soon after and was also released as a free track on iTunes. In November 2005, Bright Eyes performed "True Blue" on the children's television show _[Pancake Mountain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pancake_Mountain "Pancake Mountain")._

Bright Eyes has actively protested against the [Clear Channel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clear_Channel_Communications "Clear Channel Communications") media company. Oberst has vocally advocated the boycotting of all [Clear Channel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clear_Channel_Communications "Clear Channel Communications") events, venues, and radio stations, perhaps most publicly at the Shortlist Awards show at the [Wiltern Theatre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wiltern_Theatre "Wiltern Theatre") in Los Angeles on October 5, 2003.[\[16\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bright_Eyes_(band)#cite_note-16) On November 9, 2005, Bright Eyes canceled their November 12 show in [St. Louis, Missouri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Louis,_Missouri "St. Louis, Missouri"), upon discovering that the venue was associated with [Clear Channel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clear_Channel_Communications "Clear Channel Communications").[\[17\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bright_Eyes_(band)#cite_note-17)

Bright Eyes won Artist of the Year and Song of the Year for "When the President Talks to God" at the 2006 [PLUG Independent Music Awards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PLUG_Independent_Music_Awards "PLUG Independent Music Awards")[\[18\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bright_Eyes_(band)#cite_note-18) and a special recognition award for the video for " [First Day of My Life](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Day_of_My_Life_(Bright_Eyes_song) "First Day of My Life (Bright Eyes song)")"
at the [17th GLAAD Media Awards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/17th_GLAAD_Media_Awards "17th GLAAD Media Awards").[\[19\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bright_Eyes_(band)#cite_note-19) Additionally, _[Time](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Time_(magazine) "Time (magazine)")_ listed _[I'm Wide Awake, It's Morning](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/I'm_Wide_Awake,_It's_Morning "I'm Wide Awake, It's Morning")_ as one of the top ten albums of 2005.[\[20\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bright_Eyes_(band)#cite_note-20) Later in the year, the live album _[Motion Sickness](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Motion_Sickness_(album) "Motion Sickness (album)")_ was released, documenting the _I'm Wide Awake, It's Morning_ tour.

In a skit on the May 20, 2006 episode of _[Saturday Night Live](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saturday_Night_Live "Saturday Night Live")_, [Neil Young](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neil_Young "Neil Young") (played by [Kevin Spacey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kevin_Spacey "Kevin Spacey")) was joined by [Dixie Chicks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dixie_Chicks "Dixie Chicks") and "indie sensation Bright Eyes" (played by [Andy Samberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andy_Samberg "Andy Samberg")), all of whom have been public in their criticism of [George W. Bush](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_W._Bush "George W. Bush").

After releasing three albums in 2005, Oberst stated that he did not plan to release an album in 2006.[\[21\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bright_Eyes_(band)#cite_note-21)

On October 24, 2006, a compilation of rare tracks entitled _[Noise Floor (Rarities: 1998–2005)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Noise_Floor_(Rarities:_1998%E2%80%932005) "Noise Floor (Rarities: 1998–2005)")_ was released.

### 2007–2008: _Cassadaga_

On March 8, 2007, Oberst appeared on [National Public Radio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Public_Radio "National Public Radio")'s _[All Songs Considered](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/All_Songs_Considered "All Songs Considered")_, playing a selection of tracks in his collection, as well as several from _Four Winds_.

Bright Eyes released the _[Four Winds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Four_Winds_EP "Four Winds EP")_ EP in March 2007, featuring the first single from their seventh studio album _[Cassadaga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cassadaga_(album) "Cassadaga (album)")_, released in April 2007. The track "Endless Entertainment" circulated over the internet from the new official site, ThisIsBrightEyes.com. In a 2007 issue, _Rolling Stone_ labeled "Four Winds" as a top 100 song of the year.

_Cassadaga_ reached number four in the United States, making it the band's biggest commercial success. In support of this album, Bright Eyes toured North America from February to May, and Europe and Japan from June to July.[\[22\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bright_Eyes_(band)#cite_note-22) The twelve musicians included two drummers, and they donned white uniforms in front of a video backdrop.[\[23\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bright_Eyes_(band)#cite_note-23)

During an encore on May 19, 2007 at the [Ryman Auditorium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ryman_Auditorium "Ryman Auditorium") in [Nashville, Tennessee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nashville,_Tennessee "Nashville, Tennessee"), Oberst performed a new song with [Gillian Welch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gillian_Welch "Gillian Welch") and [David Rawlings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Rawlings "David Rawlings") entitled "Man Named Truth".[\[24\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bright_Eyes_(band)#cite_note-24) The song was officially released on [Monsters of Folk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monsters_of_Folk "Monsters of Folk")'s 2009 self-titled album, on which Oberst teams up with Jim James (of My Morning Jacket), M. Ward (of She & Him), and Mike Mogis (of Bright Eyes).

[![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/0/0c/Conor_and_m.ward.jpg/500px-Conor_and_m.ward.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Conor_and_m.ward.jpg) [Conor Oberst](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conor_Oberst "Conor Oberst") and [M. Ward](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M._Ward "M. Ward") at [The Town Hall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Town_Hall_(New_York_City) "The Town Hall (New York City)").

During a 7-night stint at [The Town Hall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Town_Hall_(New_York_City) "The Town Hall (New York City)") in New York City, Bright Eyes welcomed the following guests on stage for special performances: [Lou Reed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lou_Reed "Lou Reed") on May 25; [Ben Kweller](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ben_Kweller "Ben Kweller") on May 26; [Jenny Lewis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jenny_Lewis "Jenny Lewis") and [Johnathan Rice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johnathan_Rice "Johnathan Rice") on May 28; [Norah Jones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norah_Jones "Norah Jones"), Little Willie and Derrick E on May 29; [Nick Zinner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nick_Zinner "Nick Zinner"), [Maria Taylor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maria_Taylor_(musician) "Maria Taylor (musician)") and [Ben Gibbard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ben_Gibbard "Ben Gibbard") on May 30, [Steve Earle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steve_Earle "Steve Earle") on May 31, and finally [Ron Sexsmith](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ron_Sexsmith "Ron Sexsmith") and [Britt Daniel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Britt_Daniel "Britt Daniel") on June 1.[\[25\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bright_Eyes_(band)#cite_note-Town_Hall-25)

On June 4, 2007, they performed "Hot Knives" on the _[Late Show with David Letterman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Late_Show_with_David_Letterman "Late Show with David Letterman")_.

A double-single for "Hot Knives" and "If the Brakeman Turns My Way" was released on July 9, 2007.

In August 2007, Bright Eyes postponed 3 UK shows due to illness. A U.S. tour was announced, and in September, the UK dates were canceled and not rescheduled.

On September 29, 2007, they performed with the [Los Angeles Philharmonic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Los_Angeles_Philharmonic "Los Angeles Philharmonic") at the historic [Hollywood Bowl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hollywood_Bowl "Hollywood Bowl").

Bright Eyes performed at a [Barack Obama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barack_Obama "Barack Obama") rally in [Omaha, Nebraska](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Omaha,_Nebraska "Omaha, Nebraska") on February 7, 2008.[\[26\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bright_Eyes_(band)#cite_note-26)

Zachary Nipper, the album's art director, was awarded the 2008 [Grammy Award for Best Recording Package](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grammy_Award_for_Best_Recording_Package "Grammy Award for Best Recording Package").[\[27\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bright_Eyes_(band)#cite_note-27)

### 2008–2011: _The People's Key_

Bright Eyes went on an unofficial hiatus after the release of _Cassadaga_ in 2007. During 2008 and 2009, Oberst recorded music and toured in support of his other music projects, [Conor Oberst and the Mystic Valley Band](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conor_Oberst_and_the_Mystic_Valley_Band "Conor Oberst and the Mystic Valley Band") and supergroup [Monsters of Folk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monsters_of_Folk "Monsters of Folk").[\[28\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bright_Eyes_(band)#cite_note-28)[\[29\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bright_Eyes_(band)#cite_note-29) In a June 2009 issue of _Rolling Stone_, Oberst announced that he wanted to "retire" the Bright Eyes moniker, and would be making one final album with the band: "It does feel like it needs to stop at some point. I'd like to clean it up, lock the door, say goodbye."[\[30\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bright_Eyes_(band)#cite_note-30) Saddle Creek Records reissued their [Neva Dinova](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neva_Dinova "Neva Dinova") split _[One Jug of Wine, Two Vessels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/One_Jug_of_Wine,_Two_Vessels "One Jug of Wine, Two Vessels")_ on March 23, 2010 with four brand-new songs recorded in late 2009.[\[31\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bright_Eyes_(band)#cite_note-31)

On July 31, 2010, Bright Eyes teamed up with the [American Civil Liberties Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Civil_Liberties_Union "American Civil Liberties Union") (ACLU) of Nebraska to put on a concert for equality in [Omaha, Nebraska](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Omaha,_Nebraska "Omaha, Nebraska"). The concert raised money for a federal lawsuit the ACLU filed against the city of [Fremont, Nebraska](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fremont,_Nebraska "Fremont, Nebraska") for an [ordinance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Local_ordinance "Local ordinance") the city passed on June 21, 2010 [banning](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ban_(law) "Ban (law)") the hiring of or the rental of properties to [illegal immigrants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Illegal_immigrants "Illegal immigrants").[\[32\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bright_Eyes_(band)#cite_note-32)[\[33\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bright_Eyes_(band)#cite_note-33) At this show, Bright Eyes debuted a new song entitled "Coyote Song" about two lovers separated by the [Mexico–United States border](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mexico%E2%80%93United_States_border "Mexico–United States border").[\[34\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bright_Eyes_(band)#cite_note-34)

_[The People's Key](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_People's_Key "The People's Key")_ was released on February 15, 2011, Conor Oberst's birthday.[\[35\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bright_Eyes_(band)#cite_note-35) Conor Oberst has stated that the sound of _The People's Key_ moves away from the folk sound that the band had accomplished on previous records. "We're over the [Americana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Americana_music "Americana music"), rootsy, whatever that sound is. People say country but I never thought we were very country at all. But whatever that element is or that [aesthetic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aesthetic "Aesthetic") is, I guess it's worn a little thin for me these days. So we very much wanted it to be rocking and, for lack of a better term, contemporary, or modern."[\[36\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bright_Eyes_(band)#cite_note-36)

The video for the song "Shell Games" was released via Saddle Creek Records on both the band's YouTube channel[\[37\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bright_Eyes_(band)#cite_note-37) and that of Saddle Creek,[\[38\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bright_Eyes_(band)#cite_note-38) and features the band playing against various projections. On February 24, the band performed "Jejune Stars" on the [Late Show with David Letterman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Late_Show_with_David_Letterman "Late Show with David Letterman").[\[39\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bright_Eyes_(band)#cite_note-39) They performed "Beginner's Mind" on [The Tonight Show](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Tonight_Show_with_Jay_Leno "The Tonight Show with Jay Leno") on April 14, 2011.[\[40\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bright_Eyes_(band)#cite_note-40) On June 10, Bright Eyes released the music video for "Jejune Stars". The video features the band playing in a desert with a firework rig behind them, spelling out selected lyrics as Oberst sings them.[\[41\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bright_Eyes_(band)#cite_note-Jejune_Stars_release_on_official_website,_conoroberst.com-41) In June, July, and August 2011, the band performed shows on tour with [The Mountain Goats](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Mountain_Goats "The Mountain Goats").[\[42\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bright_Eyes_(band)#cite_note-42) The band finished the year with a tour of Australia, performing at Harvest Festival as well as select headlining shows.

Bright Eyes performed a final show in [Honolulu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Honolulu "Honolulu"), [Hawaii](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hawaii "Hawaii"), on November 21, 2011.[\[43\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bright_Eyes_(band)#cite_note-43) Bright Eyes went on an unofficial hiatus as members Oberst, Mogis, and Walcott each pursued different projects.[\[44\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bright_Eyes_(band)#cite_note-44)

### 2012–2019: Hiatus and reissues

In July 2016, it was announced that the band's six last studio albums (excluding the Christmas release) were to be reissued in a box set titled _The Studio Albums 2000–2011_. _[Fevers and Mirrors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fevers_and_Mirrors "Fevers and Mirrors"), [Lifted or The Story Is in the Soil, Keep Your Ear to the Ground](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lifted_or_The_Story_Is_in_the_Soil,_Keep_Your_Ear_to_the_Ground "Lifted or The Story Is in the Soil, Keep Your Ear to the Ground"), [I'm Wide Awake, It's Morning](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/I'm_Wide_Awake,_It's_Morning "I'm Wide Awake, It's Morning"), [Digital Ash in a Digital Urn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Digital_Ash_in_a_Digital_Urn "Digital Ash in a Digital Urn"), and [Cassadaga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cassadaga_(album) "Cassadaga (album)")_ were remastered by Bob Ludwig. The box set was released on September 16, 2016. Individual releases were also issued later in the year.[\[45\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bright_Eyes_(band)#cite_note-45)

### 2020–2023: _Down in the Weeds, Where the World Once Was_ and _Companion_ series

In January 2020, Bright Eyes launched an [Instagram](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Instagram "Instagram") page and teased a 2020 return.[\[46\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bright_Eyes_(band)#cite_note-46) On March 24 they released "Persona Non Grata", their first new song in 9 years.[\[47\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bright_Eyes_(band)#cite_note-47) _[NME](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NME "NME")_'s Luke Morgan Britton wrote of the track: "\[A\] dual sense of societal dystopia and inner turmoil collide and intertwine on the quietly poignant 'Persona Non Grata'... \[It\] sees the band maintain the sage-like, cryptic feel of their latter-day records. Yet it also returns to the intimacy and immediacy of their early material."[\[48\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bright_Eyes_(band)#cite_note-48)

Bright Eyes released the single "Forced Convalescence" on April 21, 2020. The song features [Flea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flea_(musician) "Flea (musician)") from [Red Hot Chili Peppers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Red_Hot_Chili_Peppers "Red Hot Chili Peppers") on bass along with a gospel choir.[\[49\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bright_Eyes_(band)#cite_note-49) On May 27, 2020, the single "One & Done" was released along with a lyric video.[\[50\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bright_Eyes_(band)#cite_note-50)

The band announced their tenth album, _[Down in the Weeds, Where the World Once Was](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Down_in_the_Weeds,_Where_the_World_Once_Was "Down in the Weeds, Where the World Once Was")_, on June 22, 2020. On the same day, they released a new single, "Mariana Trench".[\[51\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bright_Eyes_(band)#cite_note-51) The album was released on August 21, 2020.[\[52\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bright_Eyes_(band)#cite_note-52)

Bright Eyes released the song "Miracle of Life" featuring [Phoebe Bridgers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phoebe_Bridgers "Phoebe Bridgers") on October 28, 2020. The song was made to benefit Planned Parenthood.[\[53\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bright_Eyes_(band)#cite_note-53)

On February 1, 2022 the band announced plans to reissue their first nine studio albums under [Dead Oceans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dead_Oceans "Dead Oceans"). Each reissue would be accompanied by a six-track companion EP featuring re-recorded songs from their respective albums and covers. The first three companion EPs released on May 27, 2022. The same day, "Falling Out of Love at This Volume", "Contrast and Compare" (featuring [Waxahatchee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Waxahatchee "Waxahatchee")), and "Haligh, Haligh, A Lie, Haligh (featuring [Phoebe Bridgers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phoebe_Bridgers "Phoebe Bridgers"))" were released as singles from the _A Collection of Songs Written and Recorded 1995–1997_, _Letting Off the Happiness_, and _Fevers and Mirrors_ companion EPs, respectively.[\[54\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bright_Eyes_(band)#cite_note-54) A cover of [Elliott Smith](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elliott_Smith "Elliott Smith")'s "St. Ides Heaven" with vocals from Bridgers was released as the second single from _Letting Off the Happiness: A Companion_ on March 22, 2022.[\[55\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bright_Eyes_(band)#cite_note-55)

### 2024–present: _Five Dice, All Threes_ and _Kids Table_

On September 20, 2024, the band released its eleventh studio album, _[Five Dice, All Threes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Five_Dice,_All_Threes "Five Dice, All Threes")_. The album is the second of a two-album deal that the band signed with [Dead Oceans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dead_Oceans "Dead Oceans") back in 2018.[\[56\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bright_Eyes_(band)#cite_note-Uncut-56) Regarding the album's dice-themed [framing device](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Framing_device "Framing device"), Oberst stated: "Life is a game of chance and a metaphorical street brawl, so that is what we are putting out there."[\[56\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bright_Eyes_(band)#cite_note-Uncut-56) The day before the album's release, the band cancelled its supporting tour dates stating: "We’re heartbroken to announce that our upcoming record release shows have been cancelled. The warm-up shows we played earlier this week resulted in Conor losing his voice and, on the advice of doctors, we’ve made the difficult but sensible decision to prioritise rest and recuperation for the remainder of the month."[\[57\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bright_Eyes_(band)#cite_note-57) The band later toured extensively across 2025, following Oberst's recovery.

In September 2025, the band released _Kids Table_, an eight-track EP consisting of additional songs recorded during the sessions for _Five Dice, All Threes_. On the day of the EP's release, the band stated: "During the simpler times when we were putting out records on our own label, [Saddle Creek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saddle_Creek "Saddle Creek"), we’d release EPs as precursors to albums. They’d usually feature one song from the upcoming record, with up to five additional [b-sides](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B-sides "B-sides") that didn’t make the album. _Kids Table_ is essentially the same concept, but a year after _Five Dice, All Threes_ came out, instead of beforehand like we used to do. \[...\] The songs are pieces that we all loved, but didn’t fit into the puzzle of _Five Dice_. So come eat off an ironing board with us."[\[58\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bright_Eyes_(band)#cite_note-58)

## Musical style

According to Leonie Cooper of _[NME](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NME "NME")_, Bright Eyes "was a noughties update on [Bob Dylan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bob_Dylan "Bob Dylan") and [Gram Parsons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gram_Parsons "Gram Parsons")’ 1960s sound."[\[59\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bright_Eyes_(band)#cite_note-59)
