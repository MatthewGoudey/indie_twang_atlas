_**Cherry Peel**_ is the debut album of the [Elephant 6](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elephant_6 "Elephant 6") band [of Montreal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Of_Montreal "Of Montreal"). It was released on [Bar/None Records](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bar/None_Records "Bar/None Records") in 1997. In 1999 it was reissued with additional musical contributions and the songs remixed. All subsequent reissues contain this mix.[\[2\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cherry_Peel#cite_note-2)

## Background

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Cherry_Peel&action=edit&section=1 "Edit section: Background")\]

Prior to _Cherry Peel_, Barnes had made cassettes of 4-track recordings to share with friends and family. They had sent some tapes to independent record labels as well, and they ended up being signed by [Bar/None Records](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bar/None_Records "Bar/None Records").

Barnes was then given a budget of $3,000 to record an album. Bar/None partially gave the advance with the expectation that [Jeff Mangum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jeff_Mangum "Jeff Mangum"), the frontman of [Neutral Milk Hotel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neutral_Milk_Hotel "Neutral Milk Hotel") who had just released the critical hit _[On Avery Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/On_Avery_Island "On Avery Island")_, would be co-producing. Mangum told Barnes that he would allow them to use his name to more easily get the deal but couldn't co-produce as he was busy.

Barnes had also befriended the other members of Neutral Milk Hotel, including [Julian Koster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Julian_Koster "Julian Koster"), who helped Barnes record the album. During this time, of Montreal would join the [Elephant 6 Recording Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elephant_6 "Elephant 6").

Bar/None encouraged Barnes to record the album in a studio, but Kevin was used to the intimacy home recording and did not like the sound of '90s studio polish. They vyed for a contemporary 1960s [folk-pop](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Folk-pop "Folk-pop") [analog](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Analog_recording "Analog recording") feel, akin to influences _[The Kinks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Kinks "The Kinks")_ and early [Paul McCartney](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_McCartney "Paul McCartney"). They acquired an eight track quarter-inch [reel-to-reel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reel-to-reel "Reel-to-reel") [TASCAM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TASCAM "TASCAM") to record the songs at home as they were used to doing. They also purchased multiple contact PCM mics and a tape machine from [RadioShack](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RadioShack "RadioShack"), often using the internal microphone to give vocals a signature more distorted sound.

The live lineup of the time consisted of Kevin Barnes on vocals and guitar, [Derek Almstead](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Derek_Almstead "Derek Almstead") on drums and [The Late B.P. Helium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Late_B.P._Helium "The Late B.P. Helium") on bass. Songs recorded with this lineup consisted of "I Can't Stop Your Memory", "Sleeping in the Beetle Bug", "Montreal", and "You've Got a Gift". When the album was overdubbed and remixed in 1999, "Don't Ask Me to Explain" was also re-recorded by this band, the original version being a solo four track recording. The rest of the songs were recorded entirely by Kevin.

The majority of the songs were written prior to the recording of the album, some of them being the songs presented to the label as four track recordings before Kevin was signed. Tracks that weren't re-recorded ended up being released on _[The Early Four Track Recordings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Early_Four_Track_Recordings "The Early Four Track Recordings")_ compilation. "Everything Disappears When You Come Around" and "Baby" were written during the recording process of the album.

"When You're Loved Like You Are" was written about Barnes' uncle, who was dying at the time of cancer. He left Barnes $7,000 which they would later use to buy another tape machine, on which _[The Gay Parade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Gay_Parade "The Gay Parade")_ and _[Coquelicot Asleep in the Poppies: A Variety of Whimsical Verse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coquelicot_Asleep_in_the_Poppies:_A_Variety_of_Whimsical_Verse "Coquelicot Asleep in the Poppies: A Variety of Whimsical Verse")_ were recorded.
Many of the songs, including "Don't Ask Me to Explain" and "Montreal", are about Barnes' pen pal from Montreal, Julie, after whom the band is named.[\[3\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cherry_Peel#cite_note-beepscalifornian-3) Barnes drove to Montreal to see her in person, but it ended poorly. The experience devastated Kevin and became the lyrical inspiration behind the bulk of early of Montreal material.

"Tim I Wish You Were Born a Girl" was the first song written for the album, about Barnes' friend Tim Root\[ _[citation needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed "Wikipedia:Citation needed")_\]. "In Dreams I Dance With You", "I Was Watching Your Eyes" and "At Night Trees Aren't Sleeping" were written about another friend, Orenda.\[ _[citation needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed "Wikipedia:Citation needed")_\] "You've Got a Gift" was written for Julian as a thank you for helping them throughout the making of the album.

Due to the personal lyrics and complicated chord progressions, Barnes saw _Cherry Peel_ as a more serious, introspective work than some critics and listeners did, denouncing the [twee pop](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twee_pop "Twee pop") moniker it tends to be associated with. This, as well as the toll the failed Montreal relationship took, led to less personal, more abstract lyrical content and frantic musical styles from _The Gay Parade_ until _[Satanic Panic in the Attic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Satanic_Panic_in_the_Attic "Satanic Panic in the Attic")_.[\[4\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cherry_Peel#cite_note-4)
