Musical artist

**Kathleen Margaret Edwards**[\[1\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kathleen_Edwards#cite_note-1) (born July 11, 1978)[\[2\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kathleen_Edwards#cite_note-bio-2) is a Canadian singer-songwriter and musician. Her 2002 debut album, _[Failer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Failer "Failer")_, contained the singles "Six O'Clock News" and "Hockey Skates".[\[3\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kathleen_Edwards#cite_note-3) Her next two albums – _[Back to Me](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Back_to_Me_(Kathleen_Edwards_album) "Back to Me (Kathleen Edwards album)")_ and _[Asking for Flowers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asking_for_Flowers "Asking for Flowers")_ – both made the [_Billboard_ 200](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Billboard_200 "Billboard 200") list and reached the top 10 of Billboard's [Top Heatseekers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Top_Heatseekers "Top Heatseekers") chart. In 2012, Edwards' fourth studio album, _[Voyageur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Voyageur_(Kathleen_Edwards_album) "Voyageur (Kathleen Edwards album)")_, became Edwards' first album to crack the top 100 and top 40 in the U.S., peaking at No. 39 on the U.S. [_Billboard_ 200](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Billboard_200 "Billboard 200") and No. 2 in Canada. In 2012, Edwards' song "A Soft Place To Land" won the [SOCAN Songwriting Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SOCAN_Songwriting_Prize "SOCAN Songwriting Prize"), an annual competition that honours the best song written and released by 'emerging' songwriters over the past year, as voted by the public.[\[4\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kathleen_Edwards#cite_note-4) Her musical sound has been compared to [Suzanne Vega](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suzanne_Vega "Suzanne Vega") meets [Neil Young](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neil_Young "Neil Young").[\[5\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kathleen_Edwards#cite_note-5)

## Early life

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Kathleen_Edwards&action=edit&section=1 "Edit section: Early life")\]

Edwards, the daughter of a diplomat,[\[6\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kathleen_Edwards#cite_note-6) spent portions of her youth in Korea[\[7\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kathleen_Edwards#cite_note-7) and Switzerland. Her father is Leonard Edwards, former Deputy Minister of Foreign Affairs. At age 5, Edwards began classical violin studies that continued for the next 12 years.[\[8\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kathleen_Edwards#cite_note-8) As a teenager she lived overseas, where she spent much of her time listening to her brother's [Neil Young](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neil_Young "Neil Young") and [Bob Dylan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bob_Dylan "Bob Dylan") records.[\[9\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kathleen_Edwards#cite_note-9) Her brother also bought her her first record, a [Tom Petty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tom_Petty "Tom Petty") album. After high school she decided not to attend post-secondary education, instead opting to play local clubs to pay the bills.

## Music career

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Kathleen_Edwards&action=edit&section=2 "Edit section: Music career")\]

In 1999, Edwards recorded a six-song EP entitled _Building 55_ and pressed 500 copies. By the fall of 2000, she was on tour across Canada managing her own gigs. In 2001, she wrote seven of the ten songs for her 2002 debut release _Failer_.

Edwards played at [SXSW](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SXSW "SXSW") in 2002 and was signed to Rounder Records and MapleMusic shortly after. _Failer_ was released in Canada in the fall of 2002 on [MapleMusic Recordings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MapleMusic_Recordings "MapleMusic Recordings").[\[10\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kathleen_Edwards#cite_note-10) In January 2003 _Failer_ was released by [Rounder Records](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rounder_Records "Rounder Records") in the US and internationally. _[Rolling Stone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rolling_Stone "Rolling Stone")_ declared her one of year's most promising new acts and _[Blender](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blender_(magazine) "Blender (magazine)")_ said that _Failer_'s songs possessed "an indefinable pull that makes you love the characters they describe, no matter how fucked up they are." _[The New York Times](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_New_York_Times "The New York Times")_ praised Edwards as a writer whose songs can "pare situations down to a few dozen words while they push country-rock towards its primal impulses of thump and twang." She made her television network debut on the _[Late Show with David Letterman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Late_Show_with_David_Letterman "Late Show with David Letterman")_ where she performed "Six O'Clock News."

In 2005, Edwards released _[Back to Me](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Back_to_Me_(Kathleen_Edwards_album) "Back to Me (Kathleen Edwards album)")_, which also garnered considerable critical acclaim, and led to the release of the singles "Back to Me" and "In State". The track "Summerlong" was also featured on the soundtrack of the movie _[Elizabethtown](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elizabethtown_(film) "Elizabethtown (film)")_ starring [Orlando Bloom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orlando_Bloom "Orlando Bloom") and [Kirsten Dunst](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kirsten_Dunst "Kirsten Dunst").

[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/a/ab/Kathleen_Edwards_at_NXNE_2007.JPG/250px-Kathleen_Edwards_at_NXNE_2007.JPG?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Kathleen_Edwards_at_NXNE_2007.JPG) Edwards performing at the 2007 NXNE festival

In 2008, Edwards released her third studio album, _[Asking for Flowers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asking_for_Flowers "Asking for Flowers")_. It was described by the _[San Francisco Bay Guardian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Francisco_Bay_Guardian "San Francisco Bay Guardian")_ as "her finest album to date",[\[11\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kathleen_Edwards#cite_note-11) and was a shortlisted nominee for the [2008 Polaris Music Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2008_Polaris_Music_Prize "2008 Polaris Music Prize").[\[12\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kathleen_Edwards#cite_note-12) In contrast with 2005's _Back to Me_, on which Edwards relied on her working band, _Asking for Flowers_ predominantly features session musicians.

In fall 2010, Edwards began working on her fourth studio album in Wisconsin. _[Voyageur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Voyageur_(Kathleen_Edwards_album) "Voyageur (Kathleen Edwards album)")_ was released in January 2012. It includes the single "Change the Sheets," and was co-produced by [Justin Vernon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Justin_Vernon "Justin Vernon") of [Bon Iver](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bon_Iver "Bon Iver").[\[13\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kathleen_Edwards#cite_note-13)

She appeared on the _[Late Show with David Letterman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Late_Show_with_David_Letterman "Late Show with David Letterman")_ on January 17, 2012, singing "Change the Sheets" from her new album _Voyageur_.

In November 2019, Edwards released "It's Christmastime (Let's Just Survive)", her first new single since _Voyageur_, on the [Dualtone Records](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dualtone_Records "Dualtone Records") compilation album _A Dualtone Christmas_.[\[14\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kathleen_Edwards#cite_note-14)

In May 2020, Edwards released the single "Options Open" and announced _Total Freedom_, her first new album in eight years.[\[15\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kathleen_Edwards#cite_note-Bernstein-15) The album was released on August 14, 2020.[\[16\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kathleen_Edwards#cite_note-16)

Edwards announced a new album titled _[Billionaire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Billionaire_(album) "Billionaire (album)")_ on June 3, 2025, which was released on August 22.[\[17\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kathleen_Edwards#cite_note-17) The album was produced by [Jason Isbell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jason_Isbell "Jason Isbell") and [Gena Johnson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gena_Johnson "Gena Johnson"), and Isbell's 400 Unit band backs up Edwards.[\[18\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kathleen_Edwards#cite_note-18) The first single from the album, "Save Your Soul", reached number 6 on the Americana Music Association's singles chart.[\[19\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kathleen_Edwards#cite_note-19)

## Musical collaborations

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Kathleen_Edwards&action=edit&section=3 "Edit section: Musical collaborations")\]

In 2005, Edwards lent her vocals to the duet "The Plan", recorded with [Matt Mays and El Torpedo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matt_Mays_and_El_Torpedo "Matt Mays and El Torpedo") for their self-titled album.[\[20\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kathleen_Edwards#cite_note-20) In 2006, she was nominated for [Juno Awards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Juno_Awards "Juno Awards") for Songwriter of the Year and Adult Alternative Album of the Year for _Back to Me_.[\[21\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kathleen_Edwards#cite_note-21) In 2007, Edwards worked with [John Doe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Doe_(musician) "John Doe (musician)"), of the punk rock band [X](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/X_(American_band) "X (American band)"), on his solo album _A Year in the Wilderness_. She sings on three tracks.[\[22\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kathleen_Edwards#cite_note-22)

In 2008 Edwards sang backup vocals on [Bryan Adams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bryan_Adams "Bryan Adams")' album _[11](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/11_(Bryan_Adams_album) "11 (Bryan Adams album)")_, and supported one of his tours. In 2009, she joined Adams on stage to sing one of Adams' songs, "Walk on By".[\[23\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kathleen_Edwards#cite_note-cbcradio3-23)
In 2011 Edwards contributed vocals on [Arkells](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arkells "Arkells")' song "Agent Zero", off their second album _[Michigan Left](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michigan_Left_(album) "Michigan Left (album)")_. "Soft Place to Land", one of two songs on Edwards' _Voyageur_ album co-written with [The Long Winters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Long_Winters "The Long Winters") frontman [John Roderick](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Roderick_(musician) "John Roderick (musician)"), won the 2012 [SOCAN](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Society_of_Composers,_Authors_and_Music_Publishers_of_Canada "Society of Composers, Authors and Music Publishers of Canada") [Echo Songwriting Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SOCAN_Songwriting_Prize "SOCAN Songwriting Prize").[\[24\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kathleen_Edwards#cite_note-24)

She co-wrote the song "Good Woman", from [Maren Morris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maren_Morris "Maren Morris")'s 2019 album _[Girl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Girl_(Maren_Morris_album) "Girl (Maren Morris album)")_.[\[15\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kathleen_Edwards#cite_note-Bernstein-15)

## Personal life

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Kathleen_Edwards&action=edit&section=4 "Edit section: Personal life")\]

In 2004, Edwards married guitarist and collaborator [Colin Cripps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colin_Cripps "Colin Cripps"). They divorced in 2011. Edwards was later in a relationship with [Justin Vernon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Justin_Vernon "Justin Vernon"), Wisconsin-born singer/songwriter and front-man of the band [Bon Iver](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bon_Iver "Bon Iver").[\[25\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kathleen_Edwards#cite_note-25) Edwards and Vernon separated in 2012.[\[26\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kathleen_Edwards#cite_note-26)[\[27\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kathleen_Edwards#cite_note-27) In 2020, Edwards married property developer Sean McAdam.[\[28\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kathleen_Edwards#cite_note-28) The couple now make their home in St. Petersburg, Florida.[\[29\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kathleen_Edwards#cite_note-29)

Edwards stepped back from the music scene in 2014, launching a coffee house in [Stittsville](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stittsville "Stittsville") called Quitters along with Rick Tremblay (who was her manager when she worked in a downtown Starbucks in the 1990s).[\[30\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kathleen_Edwards#cite_note-30) She insisted that she was not leaving music but just taking a break, and that the name Quitters is "kind of tongue-in-cheek". Quitters closed on March 6, 2022 after Edwards sold the business, so that she could return to her music career.[\[31\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kathleen_Edwards#cite_note-Saxberg-31)

As of 2018, Edwards continued to perform sporadically, including a number of new songs.[\[32\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kathleen_Edwards#cite_note-32) In August 2019, following the suicide of American singer-songwriter [Neal Casal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neal_Casal "Neal Casal"), Edwards opened up on Twitter about the struggle with depression that led her to take time away from her music career.[\[33\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kathleen_Edwards#cite_note-33)
