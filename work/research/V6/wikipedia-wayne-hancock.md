Musical artist

**Thomas Wayne Hancock III** (born May 1, 1965, in Dallas, Texas) better known as Wayne "The Train" Hancock, is an American singer-songwriter. Known as "The King of Juke Joint Swing,"[\[2\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wayne_Hancock#cite_note-2) his performances incorporate [jazz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jazz "Jazz"), [blues](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blues "Blues"), [western swing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_swing "Western swing"), [country](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Country_music "Country music") and [rockabilly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rockabilly "Rockabilly"), styles of music that he began listening to as a kid. His influences include [Jimmie Rodgers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jimmie_Rodgers_(country_singer) "Jimmie Rodgers (country singer)"), [Glenn Miller](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glenn_Miller "Glenn Miller"), [Tommy Dorsey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tommy_Dorsey "Tommy Dorsey"), [Hank Thompson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hank_Thompson_(musician) "Hank Thompson (musician)"), [Hank Williams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hank_Williams "Hank Williams") and [Hank Snow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hank_Snow "Hank Snow") because they were all in his parents' record collection.[\[3\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wayne_Hancock#cite_note-:0-3)

Throughout his childhood, Hancock moved around seven times because his father was a [Design engineer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Design_engineer "Design engineer") who worked at various engineering firms around the United States. Shortly after discovering [country music](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Country_music "Country music"), Hancock began writing songs at the age of twelve. Hancock's vocal style was initially influenced by [Hank Williams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hank_Williams "Hank Williams"), and he has been compared to him throughout his career.[\[3\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wayne_Hancock#cite_note-:0-3)

At eighteen years old, Hancock won the Wrangler [Country Showdown](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Country_Showdown "Country Showdown") contest. The son of a [WWII](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/WWII "WWII") veteran, Hancock joined the [United States Marine Corps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Marine_Corps "United States Marine Corps") immediately after winning the contest and was shipped to [recruit training](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Recruit_training "Recruit training"). Hancock served in the military for four years. During a six-month deployment at [Camp Hansen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Camp_Hansen "Camp Hansen") in [Okinawa, Japan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Okinawa,_Japan "Okinawa, Japan"), he wrote one of his most well known songs, "Cold Lonesome Wind" from _[Thunderstorms and Neon Signs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thunderstorms_and_Neon_Signs "Thunderstorms and Neon Signs")_.[\[3\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wayne_Hancock#cite_note-:0-3)

In 1990, Hancock moved to [Austin, Texas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austin,_Texas "Austin, Texas"). In 1994, he met [Lubbock, Texas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lubbock,_Texas "Lubbock, Texas") native [Joe Ely](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joe_Ely "Joe Ely"), a member of the [Flatlanders](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Flatlanders "The Flatlanders") at the [Continental Club](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Continental_Club?action=edit&redlink=1 "Continental Club (page does not exist)") in, Austin, Texas. Shortly thereafter, Hancock landed a role in [Terry Allen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Terry_Allen_(artist) "Terry Allen (artist)") and [Jo Harvey Allen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jo_Harvey_Allen "Jo Harvey Allen")'s play, [Chippy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chippy_(album) "Chippy (album)"). [Jimmie Dale Gilmore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jimmie_Dale_Gilmore "Jimmie Dale Gilmore") of the Flatlanders originally played Hancock's character Mr. Jukebox. When Gilmore was unable to do the second run of the play, Ely brought Hancock in, saying "This guy sounds just like [Hank Williams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hank_Williams "Hank Williams"), he'd be perfect." From there, Hancock joined the cast and went into the studio to record "Thunderstorms and Neon Signs," for the soundtrack. It was his first recording of the song.[\[3\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wayne_Hancock#cite_note-:0-3)

Hancock met [Grammy Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grammy_Award "Grammy Award") winning producer, [pedal steel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pedal_steel_guitar "Pedal steel guitar") player, and [Lubbock, Texas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lubbock,_Texas "Lubbock, Texas") native, [Lloyd Maines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lloyd_Maines "Lloyd Maines") when Maines was running sound for [Chippy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chippy_(album) "Chippy (album)"). [Maines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lloyd_Maines "Lloyd Maines") has produced all of Hancock's albums, dating back to his 1995 debut, _[Thunderstorms and Neon Signs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thunderstorms_and_Neon_Signs "Thunderstorms and Neon Signs")_, released on Deja Disc Records. The album sold 22,000 copies in the first year. The follow-up was Hancock's sophomore 1997 album, _[That's What Daddy Wants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/That's_What_Daddy_Wants "That's What Daddy Wants")_ released on [Ark 21](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ark_21 "Ark 21"). Hancock gained the nickname The Train because of his marathon length shows, going on for 3, 4, even 5 hours long."

On his debut 1999 album, _[Risin' Outlaw](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Risin'_Outlaw "Risin' Outlaw")_, [Hank Williams III](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hank_Williams_III "Hank Williams III"), grandson of [Hank Williams Sr.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hank_Williams "Hank Williams"), covered two songs from _[Thunderstorms and Neon Signs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thunderstorms_and_Neon_Signs "Thunderstorms and Neon Signs")_, the title track, and "Why Don't You Leave Me Alone." He also recorded "87 Southbound," from _[That's What Daddy Wants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/That's_What_Daddy_Wants "That's What Daddy Wants")_.[\[3\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wayne_Hancock#cite_note-:0-3)

In April 2014, Hancock was involved in a serious motorcycle accident, which left him with a fractured elbow and a collapsed lung. He was forced to cancel several months' worth of tour dates due to the wreck, but by the end of the year he was fully recovered and back on the road. In 2016, Hancock returned to the studio with producer [Lloyd Maines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lloyd_Maines "Lloyd Maines") to cut his eighth studio album Slingin' Rhythm.[\[4\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wayne_Hancock#cite_note-4)
