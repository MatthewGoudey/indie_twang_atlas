**The Bottle Rockets** were an American [rock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rock_music "Rock music") band formed in [Festus, Missouri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Festus,_Missouri "Festus, Missouri") in 1992, and based in [St. Louis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Louis "St. Louis"). Its founding members were [Brian Henneman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brian_Henneman "Brian Henneman") (guitar, vocals), Mark Ortmann (drums), Tom Parr (1992–2002, guitar, vocals) and [Tom Ray](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tom_V._Ray "Tom V. Ray") (1992–1997, bass guitar); the most recent lineup consisted of Henneman, Ortmann, John Horton (joined 2003, guitar)[\[1\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Bottle_Rockets#cite_note-1) and Keith Voegele[\[2\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Bottle_Rockets#cite_note-undertowmusic1-2)[\[3\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Bottle_Rockets#cite_note-3) (joined 2005, bass, vocals). Most members of the band have contributed to their catalog of original songs, as have Robert Parr (Tom's brother) and schoolteacher Scott Taylor (who writes lyrics for some of Henneman's tunes).

As noted in _[The New York Times](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_New_York_Times "The New York Times")_ by William Hogeland, the Bottle Rockets' songwriting has been likened to [Woody Guthrie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Woody_Guthrie "Woody Guthrie")'s folk style in spirit, smarts, and satire. The band's lyrics encapsulate the common experiences of the everyman, and are set to rousing and searing rock 'n' roll.[\[4\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Bottle_Rockets#cite_note-pagewanted1-4)[\[5\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Bottle_Rockets#cite_note-thebrooklynside1-5) The band has been named as one of the leaders of the '90s [alt-country](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alt-country "Alt-country")/ [roots rock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roots_rock "Roots rock") revival, alongside [Uncle Tupelo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uncle_Tupelo "Uncle Tupelo").[\[6\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Bottle_Rockets#cite_note-6)[\[7\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Bottle_Rockets#cite_note-7)[\[8\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Bottle_Rockets#cite_note-ReferenceA-8)[\[9\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Bottle_Rockets#cite_note-9)[\[10\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Bottle_Rockets#cite_note-10) The strong social commentary of their songs reflects the influences of Woody Guthrie, [Neil Young](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neil_Young "Neil Young") and [The Replacements](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Replacements_(band) "The Replacements (band)").[\[4\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Bottle_Rockets#cite_note-pagewanted1-4)[\[11\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Bottle_Rockets#cite_note-11)[\[12\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Bottle_Rockets#cite_note-eastbayexpress1-12)

## History

### The 1990s

> For much of the Nineties, Missouri's Bottle Rockets were the torchbearers for smart Southern-style rock.— [Mark Kemp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mark_Kemp "Mark Kemp"), _[Rolling Stone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rolling_Stone "Rolling Stone")_[\[13\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Bottle_Rockets#cite_note-13)

The Bottle Rockets released their self-titled first album in 1993.[\[14\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Bottle_Rockets#cite_note-14) _[The Brooklyn Side](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Brooklyn_Side "The Brooklyn Side")_ followed on [East Side Digital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Side_Digital_Records "East Side Digital Records"), in 1994, to resounding critical acclaim.[\[5\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Bottle_Rockets#cite_note-thebrooklynside1-5) In 1995, the Bottle Rockets then signed with [Atlantic Records](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atlantic_Records "Atlantic Records"), which re-released _The Brooklyn Side_. In January of that same year, the Bottle Rockets appeared on the [FX](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FX_(TV_channel) "FX (TV channel)") morning talk show _Breakfast Time_ in what was supposed to be an episode-long appearance, but after performing only one song, they left the studio because they were annoyed by the shows puppet Bob, and they would not return to the studio unless he was gone. Bob stayed and the band didn't return. The single "Radar Gun" was a hit on rock radio—reaching No.27 on _Billboard_'s rock chart—and the band toured extensively to support the album. The band appeared on the television show [Late Night with Conan O'Brien](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Late_Night_with_Conan_O'Brien "Late Night with Conan O'Brien") performing one of their original songs as well as being featured in a comedic skit. The relationship with Atlantic Records turned out to be difficult.[\[15\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Bottle_Rockets#cite_note-15) Most of the original staff who promoted _The Brooklyn Side_ had been laid off or fired from Atlantic. The release of the Bottle Rockets' next record, _[24 Hours a Day](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/24_Hours_a_Day_(album) "24 Hours a Day (album)")_, was delayed until late 1997. The band parted ways with Atlantic in 1998.[\[8\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Bottle_Rockets#cite_note-ReferenceA-8)

The Bottle Rockets are featured in the [PBS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_Broadcasting_Service "Public Broadcasting Service") [documentary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Documentary_film "Documentary film") _The Mississippi River of Song: The Grassroots of American Music_.[\[16\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Bottle_Rockets#cite_note-16) In the series, which is narrated by [Ani DiFranco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ani_DiFranco "Ani DiFranco"), Brian Henneman says that he and the band are "reporters from the heartland" writing stories about their friends.[\[17\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Bottle_Rockets#cite_note-17) The Bottle Rockets performed live at the [Smithsonian Institution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smithsonian_Institution "Smithsonian Institution") in [Washington, DC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Washington,_DC "Washington, DC") at the premiere for the film, and also appear on the Smithsonian Folkways Recordings soundtrack.[\[18\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Bottle_Rockets#cite_note-18)

Bottle Rockets then signed with Doolittle records, which later became [New West Records](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_West_Records "New West Records").[\[19\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Bottle_Rockets#cite_note-19) Doolittle released an [EP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Extended_play "Extended play") of [outtakes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Outtakes "Outtakes") from _24 Hours A Day_ called _Leftovers_ in 1998. About this time, Tom Ray was replaced on bass by [Robert Kearns](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Kearns_(musician) "Robert Kearns (musician)"). The Bottle Rockets' fourth full-length record, _[Brand New Year](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brand_New_Year_(The_Bottle_Rockets_album) "Brand New Year (The Bottle Rockets album)")_, was released on Doolittle in 1999. "Power hooks and muscular guitar fights that would make Skynyrd proud" and "'70s power rock with a dirty edge—sort of [ZZ Top](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ZZ_Top "ZZ Top") meets [Lynyrd Skynyrd](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lynyrd_Skynyrd "Lynyrd Skynyrd") meets [Bad Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bad_Company "Bad Company")" is the calling card of _Brand New Year_.[\[20\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Bottle_Rockets#cite_note-20)

### 2000–2005

The Bottle Rockets performed at the Horseshoe Tavern in early 2000.[\[21\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Bottle_Rockets#cite_note-21) The band again had problems with their record label, and did not record anything else until _Songs of Sahm_, a collection of songs by [Doug Sahm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doug_Sahm "Doug Sahm"), which came out on [Chicago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chicago "Chicago") label Bloodshot Records in early 2002.[\[22\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Bottle_Rockets#cite_note-22)[\[23\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Bottle_Rockets#cite_note-23) Shortly after finishing this record, Parr left the band. Bottle Rockets toured as a three-piece for a while, and recorded their fifth full-length record _[Blue Sky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blue_Sky_(Bottle_Rockets_album) "Blue Sky (Bottle Rockets album)")_ (which was released in 2003 on the Sanctuary label), before adding multi-instrumentalist John Horton to the band.

Kearns amicably split with the band in the spring of 2005. After a brief search the Bottle Rockets named Keith Voegele as their new bassist.[\[2\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Bottle_Rockets#cite_note-undertowmusic1-2) Voegele is from Saint Louis and has played in bands including the Phonocaptors.

After the Bottle Rockets' eponymous debut, having a radio hit ("Radar Gun") on their second album, extensive touring, and critical acclaim,[\[24\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Bottle_Rockets#cite_note-24) the band endured a decade of subsequent hard luck, including problems with record companies, a UPS strike holding up distribution of one of their new records, band personnel changes, and family emergencies.[\[25\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Bottle_Rockets#cite_note-25)[\[26\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Bottle_Rockets#cite_note-bottlerockets1-26)[\[27\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Bottle_Rockets#cite_note-27) Concurrent with the band's business difficulties, [grunge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grunge "Grunge") and [alternative rock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alternative_rock "Alternative rock") came to prominence. As a result, the music industry effectively abandoned traditional rock artists, in favor of marketing more trendy acts.[\[28\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Bottle_Rockets#cite_note-28)[\[29\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Bottle_Rockets#cite_note-29) The path Henneman and the Bottle Rockets had been on seemed to disappear.

Despite those struggles, in 2005, founders Brian Henneman and Mark Ortmann got the band back on course, along with the newest additions John Horton and Keith Voegele, the current line-up of band members. The band also re-hired their manager from the early days, Bob Andrews.[\[30\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Bottle_Rockets#cite_note-30)

### 2006–2007

The Bottle Rockets' first live album _Live in Heilbronn Germany_ was released in February 2006. The double-disc set was recorded on July 17, 2005 at the Burgerhaus, Heilbronn-Bockingen, Germany with the band's current roster. It was released in Europe on CD and vinyl by Blue Rose Records.

[Bloodshot Records](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bloodshot_Records "Bloodshot Records") released the band's next album, _Zoysia_, recorded in Ardent Studios in [Memphis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Memphis,_Tennessee "Memphis, Tennessee") with producer [Jeff Powell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jeff_Powell_(record_producer)?action=edit&redlink=1 "Jeff Powell (record producer)"), in June 2006. [Zoysia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zoysia "Zoysia") (zoy-zhuh), a metaphor for tolerance and centered values and common ground, is a hardy grass, plentiful in Festus/Crystal City and Saint Louis, Missouri, where these hardworking musicians grew up. After years of misleading portrayals of the band's music as "hillbilly",[\[31\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Bottle_Rockets#cite_note-31)[\[32\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Bottle_Rockets#cite_note-riverfronttimes.com-32) the band's catalog proves otherwise with themes of maturity, generosity of spirit, neighborliness, insightful self-reflection, personal roots and modern society, individualism, pride of place, slow-mending hearts, and post-9/11 reality through the filter of a couple's romance.[\[8\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Bottle_Rockets#cite_note-ReferenceA-8)[\[33\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Bottle_Rockets#cite_note-33) The Bottle Rockets continued with _Zoysia_:[\[26\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Bottle_Rockets#cite_note-bottlerockets1-26)

> It would be a mistake to claim that Missouri's answer to Neil Young's Crazy Horse has gone soft but their first release in more than three years shows greater range and reflection than is typical for the rock-solid quartet. The opening "Better Than Broken", the brooding "Happy Anniversary", and the acoustic wistful "Where I Come From" all evoke the aftermath of romantic upheaval.... "Middle Man" could be the band's signature tune defining a sensibility that is Middle American in more than geography. The sage wisdom of frontman Brian Henneman's "Blind" and the twang of "Feeling Down" show the band's countrier side while "I Quit" has the groove of retro soul. Yet the guitar finale of the seven-minute album-closing title song \["Zoysia"\] finds the Bottle Rockets as explosive as ever. — _Don McLeese_

_Zoysia_ received positive reviews, including a spot on novelist/ [audiophile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Audiophile "Audiophile") [Stephen King](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stephen_King "Stephen King")'s Best Records of 2006 list in _Entertainment Weekly_ magazine.[\[34\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Bottle_Rockets#cite_note-34)

### 2008: 15th anniversary year

[![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/a/a7/Bottle_Rockets_NYC.jpg/500px-Bottle_Rockets_NYC.jpg)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Bottle_Rockets_NYC.jpg) The Bottle Rockets at City Winery (New York City) January 2016

The band celebrated its 15th anniversary throughout 2008.[\[32\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Bottle_Rockets#cite_note-riverfronttimes.com-32)[\[35\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Bottle_Rockets#cite_note-35) Instead of extensive touring like in previous years, the band played only 15 shows in select cities during the entire year.

The Bottle Rockets' reputation as the underdog spokesmen translated into a collaboration with fans in 2008.[\[36\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Bottle_Rockets#cite_note-36)[\[37\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Bottle_Rockets#cite_note-37) Fans of the Bottle Rockets had a voice in the band's 15th Anniversary Tour, having been invited to design set lists for the 2008 tour from the Bottle Rockets’ catalog plus one cover song suggestion. The band hand-picked a fan's set list for each of the special anniversary shows and entered the set list winners in a year-long contest to win one of 2 grand prizes, a $1500.00 custom Golden Rocket guitar by [Creston Electric Instruments](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Creston_Electric_Instruments "Creston Electric Instruments") or the "Bottle Rockets for life" prize package.[\[38\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Bottle_Rockets#cite_note-38)

The results of the first such collaboration were detailed by Roy Kasten and Barry Gilbert in their reviews of the performance, in the _Riverfront Times_ and the _St. Louis Post-Dispatch_, respectively.[\[39\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Bottle_Rockets#cite_note-39)[\[40\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Bottle_Rockets#cite_note-40)

While other artists have covered Bottle Rockets’ songs, including [John Hiatt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Hiatt "John Hiatt"), (Hiatt recorded "Welfare Music" for [Don Imus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Don_Imus "Don Imus")’ _Ranch Record_), Peter Blackstock of _[No Depression](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/No_Depression_(periodical) "No Depression (periodical)")_ implores:

> "...somebody, **SOMEBODY** record \[the Bottle Rockets’ song\] 'Kerosene' on your next album. Big band, little band, [country](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Country_music "Country music") rock band, [rock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rock_music "Rock music") band,... [punk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Punk_rock "Punk rock") rock band,...somebody just get smart and give this song the respect, and legs, it deserves."[\[41\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Bottle_Rockets#cite_note-41)[\[42\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Bottle_Rockets#cite_note-42)

In July 2008, The Bottle Rockets reunited with producer [Eric Ambel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eric_Ambel "Eric Ambel") ( _Brooklyn Side_, _24 Hours A Day_, _Leftovers_, _Brand New Year_) at his Cowboy Technical Services Recording Studio in Williamsburg, Brooklyn, New York for their latest album, _Lean Forward_ (Bloodshot).

### 2009

On May 2, The Bottle Rockets played a special concert at the High Dive in [Champaign, IL](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Champaign,_IL "Champaign, IL") that was filmed for an upcoming live concert documentary [DVD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DVD "DVD") release.[\[43\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Bottle_Rockets#cite_note-43) The set list included "Hard Times," "Done It All," "Shame On Me," "Give Me Room," "Way It Used to Be" and "The Long Way" from the then-forthcoming _Lean Forward_ album.

_Lean Forward_ was released by Bloodshot Records.[\[12\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Bottle_Rockets#cite_note-eastbayexpress1-12)[\[44\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Bottle_Rockets#cite_note-44)[\[45\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Bottle_Rockets#cite_note-45)[\[46\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Bottle_Rockets#cite_note-46) The album charted on the Billboard Heatseekers Albums chart at No.23.

### 2017

On November 27, 2017, permission was granted to the [Live Music Archive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Live_Music_Archive "Live Music Archive") at the [Internet Archive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Internet_Archive "Internet Archive") to add a collection for live audio recordings for public download and noncommercial, royalty-free circulation.[\[47\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Bottle_Rockets#cite_note-47) As of March 2018, over 100 live recordings had been posted to that site.

### 2021

On March 2, 2021, the band announced its retirement, for reasons as posted to its public Facebook account: "It’s with a sad heart that we announce this uneasy news: Brian has decided to retire from the Bottle Rockets. Although he’s in good health, he’s been feeling the passage of time and has lost interest in anything that distracts from or takes him away from home. Unfortunately, this means the Bottle Rockets can’t continue as we know it."[\[48\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Bottle_Rockets#cite_note-48)
