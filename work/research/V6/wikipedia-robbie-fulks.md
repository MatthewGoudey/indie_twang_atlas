Musical artist

**Robert William** " **Robbie**" **Fulks** (born March 25, 1963) is an American [alternative country](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alternative_country "Alternative country") singer-songwriter, instrumentalist and long-time resident of [Chicago, Illinois](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chicago,_Illinois "Chicago, Illinois").[\[2\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robbie_Fulks#cite_note-CMHoF-13HillbillyGiants-2001-2)[\[3\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robbie_Fulks#cite_note-HuffPo-GoneAwayBackward-2013-3) He has released 15 albums over a career spanning more than 30 years.[\[4\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robbie_Fulks#cite_note-LastCall-GoneAwayBackward-2013-4) His 2016 record _[Upland Stories](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Upland_Stories "Upland Stories")_ was nominated for a [Grammy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grammy_Award "Grammy Award") for [Best Folk Album](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grammy_Award_for_Best_Folk_Album "Grammy Award for Best Folk Album") and the song "Alabama at Night" was nominated for a Grammy for [Best American Roots Song](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grammy_Award_for_Best_American_Roots_Song "Grammy Award for Best American Roots Song").[\[5\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robbie_Fulks#cite_note-LATimes-GrammyAwardNominees-2016-5)

## Early life

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Robbie_Fulks&action=edit&section=1 "Edit section: Early life")\]

Fulks was born in [York](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/York,_Pennsylvania "York, Pennsylvania"), [Pennsylvania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pennsylvania "Pennsylvania"), the son of a school teacher father. He grew up in small towns in southeastern [Pennsylvania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pennsylvania "Pennsylvania"), the [Blue Ridge Mountains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blue_Ridge_Mountains "Blue Ridge Mountains") area of [Virginia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Virginia "Virginia"), and the [Piedmont](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Piedmont_(United_States) "Piedmont (United States)") region of [North Carolina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Carolina "North Carolina"). Fulks's family moved often when he was young, eventually settling in North Carolina when he was 12, and Fulks considers North Carolina his childhood home.[\[6\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robbie_Fulks#cite_note-Outlaw-GoneAwayBackward-2012-6) He has a younger brother named Jubal.[\[2\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robbie_Fulks#cite_note-CMHoF-13HillbillyGiants-2001-2)

Fulks was exposed to music through his family in which everyone played a different musical instrument, from his Aunt Stella on [banjo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Banjo "Banjo"), his Aunt Mildred on [violin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Violin "Violin") and his mother on [autoharp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Autoharp "Autoharp"), to his father playing [guitar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guitar "Guitar").[\[2\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robbie_Fulks#cite_note-CMHoF-13HillbillyGiants-2001-2)[\[6\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robbie_Fulks#cite_note-Outlaw-GoneAwayBackward-2012-6)[\[7\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robbie_Fulks#cite_note-ChiTonight-2014-7) Fulks picked up his aunt's banjo when he was six and started playing guitar at age 11.[\[2\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robbie_Fulks#cite_note-CMHoF-13HillbillyGiants-2001-2)[\[8\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robbie_Fulks#cite_note-IndyWeek-UplandStories-2016-8)

He graduated from [Carolina Friends School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carolina_Friends_School "Carolina Friends School")[\[8\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robbie_Fulks#cite_note-IndyWeek-UplandStories-2016-8) in 1980 at the age of 17 and moved to New York City, where he attended [Columbia College, Columbia University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Columbia_College,_Columbia_University "Columbia College, Columbia University") with the class of 1984.[\[6\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robbie_Fulks#cite_note-Outlaw-GoneAwayBackward-2012-6)[\[9\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robbie_Fulks#cite_note-:0-9)

## Career

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Robbie_Fulks&action=edit&section=2 "Edit section: Career")\]

While at [Columbia University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Columbia_University "Columbia University"), Fulks often played at [Gerdes Folk City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gerdes_Folk_City "Gerdes Folk City") and other places in [Greenwich Village](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greenwich_Village "Greenwich Village").[\[6\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robbie_Fulks#cite_note-Outlaw-GoneAwayBackward-2012-6) He also performed on campus in places such as the [Postcrypt Coffeehouse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Postcrypt_Coffeehouse "Postcrypt Coffeehouse").[\[9\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robbie_Fulks#cite_note-:0-9) He eventually dropped out of college after two years to pursue music full time.[\[10\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robbie_Fulks#cite_note-NYT-2000-10)

### Chicago

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Robbie_Fulks&action=edit&section=3 "Edit section: Chicago")\]

Fulks moved to Chicago in 1983 and started teaching at Chicago's [Old Town School of Folk Music](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_Town_School_of_Folk_Music "Old Town School of Folk Music").[\[2\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robbie_Fulks#cite_note-CMHoF-13HillbillyGiants-2001-2) In 1987, he joined [The Special Consensus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Special_Consensus "The Special Consensus") Bluegrass Band, where he showcased his unique guitar flatpicking style. In the early 1990s he performed in the musical _Woody Guthrie's American Song_.[\[11\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robbie_Fulks#cite_note-allmusic-bio-11)

He also recorded his original compositions. Many of his early tracks were engineered by [Steve Albini](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steve_Albini "Steve Albini"), and the Chicago punk-country label [Bloodshot Records](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bloodshot_Records "Bloodshot Records") began releasing them in 1993.[\[6\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robbie_Fulks#cite_note-Outlaw-GoneAwayBackward-2012-6)[\[12\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robbie_Fulks#cite_note-Trib-2011-Honest-12)[\[13\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robbie_Fulks#cite_note-NoDep-LindaRay1997-13)

### Nashville

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Robbie_Fulks&action=edit&section=4 "Edit section: Nashville")\]

While living in Chicago, Fulks worked for four years as a country songwriter, mainly for Music Row publisher API.[\[2\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robbie_Fulks#cite_note-CMHoF-13HillbillyGiants-2001-2) His solo debut album, _Country Love Songs_, engineered by [Steve Albini](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steve_Albini "Steve Albini"),[\[11\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robbie_Fulks#cite_note-allmusic-bio-11) was released on Bloodshot Records in 1996 and received positive reviews. The Skeletons, members of whom later formed the band [The Morells](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Morells "The Morells"), played on most of the tracks[\[13\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robbie_Fulks#cite_note-NoDep-LindaRay1997-13) and [Tom Brumley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tom_Brumley "Tom Brumley") and [Buck Owens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buck_Owens "Buck Owens") also featured. _Country Love Songs_ was followed in 1997 by _South Mouth_, which confirmed Fulks's retro-alternative image.

Fans had grown used to his rough and sparse sound, but his third album, 1998's _[Let's Kill Saturday Night](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Let's_Kill_Saturday_Night "Let's Kill Saturday Night")_, on [Geffen Records](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geffen_Records "Geffen Records"),[\[14\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robbie_Fulks#cite_note-Soundbite-2000-14)[\[15\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robbie_Fulks#cite_note-NYT-Dresden-15) was different. Fulks recorded it during the spring of 1998 in Nashville with producer Rick Will, and the album included performances by [Lucinda Williams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lucinda_Williams "Lucinda Williams"), [Sam Bush](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sam_Bush "Sam Bush"), [Bill Lloyd](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bill_Lloyd_(country_musician) "Bill Lloyd (country musician)"), and [Al Anderson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al_Anderson_(NRBQ) "Al Anderson (NRBQ)"), as well as guitarist Rob Gjersoe ( [Jimmie Dale Gilmore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jimmie_Dale_Gilmore "Jimmie Dale Gilmore")), bassist Lorne Rall, and drummer Dan Massey.[\[2\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robbie_Fulks#cite_note-CMHoF-13HillbillyGiants-2001-2)

Geffen Records disbanded shortly after the release of the record and Fulks, finding himself without a label, started his own company, Boondoggle Records, and released an album of previously unreleased material called _The Very Best of Robbie Fulks_. He also licenses his music for distribution by Bloodshot.[\[2\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robbie_Fulks#cite_note-CMHoF-13HillbillyGiants-2001-2)

## Output

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Robbie_Fulks&action=edit&section=5 "Edit section: Output")\]

> I called it _Gone Away Backward_ because it's a phrase from the Bible… I stumbled on the phrase later in the evolution of the record when I was looking around for a title. I think it's a nice piquant phrase that has three good, strong, mellifluous words in it. As far as the backwardness of the record, I think it goes backward in terms of nostalgia for the past – bittersweet nostalgia for the past – as well as the recession having knocked everything backwards for people. In that sense, it's not an album about the past, it's an album about now.

—"Interview with Robbie Fulks about 'Gone Away Backward'"

by Kim Ruehl, _No Depression Magazine_ (September 18, 2013)

In 2001 he released _Couples in Trouble_, a dark, brooding, and decidedly non-country album, and _13 Hillbilly Giants_, a collection of covers of classic country numbers, both obscure and well known. Both records were released by Bloodshot Records. Also in 2001, Fulks was an inaugural member of the judging panel for the Independent Music Awards, which supported independent artists.[\[16\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robbie_Fulks#cite_note-IMA-judges-16)

Fulks did not release another album until 2005. _Georgia Hard_ on [Yep Roc Records](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yep_Roc_Records "Yep Roc Records") was a return to his county roots, and was notable for its use of long-time Nashville talent such as [Lloyd Green](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lloyd_Green "Lloyd Green"), Hank Singer, [Dennis Crouch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dennis_Crouch_(bassist) "Dennis Crouch (bassist)"), and [Dallas Wayne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dallas_Wayne "Dallas Wayne").[\[17\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robbie_Fulks#cite_note-Pure2005-17) He also released a novelty single called "Fountains of Wayne Hotline," in which he imagined the power pop band [Fountains of Wayne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fountains_of_Wayne "Fountains of Wayne") having a hotline that struggling songwriters could call for help with their song structure.

In April 2007, Fulks released a 2-CD album, _Revenge!_ (also on Yep Roc Records), which consisted mainly of live concert recordings of older songs and included some new material. One disc, labeled _Standing_, featured a full-band sound, while the second disc, _Sitting,_ consisted of Fulks solo with little or no musical accompaniment. _Standing_ opens with the tongue-in-cheek studio track "We're on the Road", which describes life on tour and simulates a telephone call to Fulks from Yep Roc Records President Glenn Dicker, demanding a new record and denigrating the sales performance of the "path-breaking, not chart-breaking" album _Georgia Hard._

In 2009, Fulks released an alphabetically organized collection of 50 songs via his website, a compilation called _50-Vc. Doberman._[\[18\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robbie_Fulks#cite_note-NYTimes-50-18) He noted that this method, and electronic-only release in general, was not typical of musicians who worked in his genre.[\[19\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robbie_Fulks#cite_note-ChiReader-AlbiniFulks-2014-19) In 2010, Fulks released his next album, _Happy: Robbie Fulks Plays the Music of Michael Jackson,_ on Yep Roc Records, in which he covered some of Jackson's songs as a tribute. He had been working on the album since 2005.[\[20\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robbie_Fulks#cite_note-ChicagoMag-Happy-20)[\[21\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robbie_Fulks#cite_note-NoDep-Happy-21)

In 2013, Fulks released _Gone Away Backward,_ an acoustic album recorded and mixed by [Steve Albini](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steve_Albini "Steve Albini") and released on Bloodshot Records. It was his first release on that label since 2001's _13 Hillbilly Giants._ The album featured banjo, mandolin, fiddle, and upright bass.[\[22\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robbie_Fulks#cite_note-Spin-2013stream-22) He said one of the songs, "That's Where I'm From", was autobiographical.[\[23\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robbie_Fulks#cite_note-NoDep-GoneAway-23) Four songs from _50-Vc Doberman_ were the genesis of _Gone Away Backward:_[\[24\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robbie_Fulks#cite_note-NoDep-Dec2013-int-24) the title came from the [Book of Isaiah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Book_of_Isaiah "Book of Isaiah").[\[25\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robbie_Fulks#cite_note-Bible-Isaiah-GAB-25) Fulks said that _Gone Away Backward_ went back to his roots as a musician.[\[7\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robbie_Fulks#cite_note-ChiTonight-2014-7) The stylistic choice was a good fit with the other musicians who appeared on the recording, Robbie Gjersoe, Jenny Scheinman, and Mike Bub. They recorded live without any overdubbing or elaborate production and laid down around 25 songs in three days.[\[26\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robbie_Fulks#cite_note-NPR-WorldCafe-2014-26)

In 2014, Fulks joined members of [The Mekons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Mekons "The Mekons"), dubbed "mini-Mekons", on a trip to write and record on the island of [Jura](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jura,_Scotland "Jura, Scotland") in Scotland. The resulting record, _[Jura](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jura_(album) "Jura (album)")_, was released in November 2015 and consisted of traditional songs and original songs written on the trip.[\[27\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robbie_Fulks#cite_note-RStone-Jura-2015-27) In April 2016, he released _[Upland Stories](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Upland_Stories "Upland Stories"),_[\[28\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robbie_Fulks#cite_note-FunnyOrDie-Odenkirk-2016-28) and the album was nominated for a 2017 Grammy Award for best folk album. The opening track, "Alabama at Night", was nominated for best American roots song.[\[29\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robbie_Fulks#cite_note-29)

## Musical style

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Robbie_Fulks&action=edit&section=6 "Edit section: Musical style")\]

[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/c/c2/Robbie_Fulks_-_December_2013.jpg/250px-Robbie_Fulks_-_December_2013.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Robbie_Fulks_-_December_2013.jpg) Robbie Fulks playing a house concert on December 14, 2013

As a songwriter, Fulks is difficult to categorize.[\[30\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robbie_Fulks#cite_note-NPR-FA-2013-30) Nathan Rabin of A.V. Club says he "has a genius for twisting and subverting country tropes," and "there's more to Fulks than tomfoolery and glibly satirical lyrics."[\[31\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robbie_Fulks#cite_note-AV-2010genius-31) Many of his compositions are silly, funny or spoof songs, such as "Godfrey" ("the sickly, unemployed, amateur children's magician") on the compilation children's album _The Bottle Let Me Down_, while others are serious country songs.[\[32\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robbie_Fulks#cite_note-MemeFirst-Godfrey-32)

His music is widely described as either alternative country or folk. Fulks's songwriting often includes humor.[\[33\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robbie_Fulks#cite_note-AltC-funny-RegGuard2013-33) Peter Applebome of _The New York Times_ describes his work as "one part artful country, one part artful sendup of country, and one part a little of everything else."[\[10\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robbie_Fulks#cite_note-NYT-2000-10)

Fulks has an encyclopedic knowledge of country and pop music[\[19\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robbie_Fulks#cite_note-ChiReader-AlbiniFulks-2014-19) and has produced a critically lauded tribute to [Johnny Paycheck](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johnny_Paycheck "Johnny Paycheck") called _Touch My Heart: A Tribute to Johnny Paycheck,_ which was released by [Sugar Hill Records](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sugar_Hill_Records_(bluegrass_label) "Sugar Hill Records (bluegrass label)") in 2004.[\[34\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robbie_Fulks#cite_note-Billboard-Paycheck-34) He considers himself adventurous, and is always willing to try new things and experiment. "Why not push the envelope and see what you are capable of doing rather than recycle the same old ideas over and over again," he said.[\[7\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robbie_Fulks#cite_note-ChiTonight-2014-7) His musicianship has been called "impeccable".[\[35\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robbie_Fulks#cite_note-NYT-impeccable-35) Jim Fusilli of the _[Wall Street Journal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Wall_Street_Journal "The Wall Street Journal")_ suggested that "a world in which Fulks isn't a household name is somehow upside down."[\[36\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robbie_Fulks#cite_note-WSJ-Artistry-36)

Fulks often plays at [The Hideout](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Hideout_Inn "The Hideout Inn"), a bar and club in Chicago, and has worked long-term residencies there,[\[12\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robbie_Fulks#cite_note-Trib-2011-Honest-12)[\[37\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robbie_Fulks#cite_note-UBee-2010-Hideout-37)[\[38\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robbie_Fulks#cite_note-Reader-Margasak2013-38) performing anything from current popular hits to jazz to obscure country masterpieces. He says he rarely performs his own compositions at these shows, preferring to explore other contemporary music that has caught his attention.[\[19\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robbie_Fulks#cite_note-ChiReader-AlbiniFulks-2014-19) His live performances feature improvised rearrangements of his own songs, off-the-cuff musical humor, and covers of songs by [Michael Jackson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_Jackson "Michael Jackson"), [Cher](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cher "Cher"), [Shania Twain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shania_Twain "Shania Twain"), and [The Bangles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Bangles "The Bangles"), among others.

Fulks has co-written with [Dallas Wayne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dallas_Wayne "Dallas Wayne") and [NRBQ](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NRBQ "NRBQ")'s [Al Anderson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al_Anderson_(NRBQ) "Al Anderson (NRBQ)") and others.[\[17\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robbie_Fulks#cite_note-Pure2005-17) He had a long-time association with engineer [Steve Albini](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steve_Albini "Steve Albini") ( [Big Black](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Big_Black "Big Black"), [Shellac](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shellac_(band) "Shellac (band)")).[\[19\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robbie_Fulks#cite_note-ChiReader-AlbiniFulks-2014-19)

## Personal life

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Robbie_Fulks&action=edit&section=7 "Edit section: Personal life")\]

Fulks has three children, sons Nicolas, Preston, and Tennessee; and two grandsons, Logan and Miles.[\[7\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robbie_Fulks#cite_note-ChiTonight-2014-7)[\[17\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robbie_Fulks#cite_note-Pure2005-17) His wife is actress [Donna Jay Fulks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Donna_Jay_Fulks "Donna Jay Fulks"). Fulks's oldest son, [Nicolas Fulks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_The_Amazing_Race_(American_TV_series)_contestants "List of The Amazing Race (American TV series) contestants"), and father-in-law, [Donald Jerousek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_The_Amazing_Race_(American_TV_series)_contestants "List of The Amazing Race (American TV series) contestants"), were contestants on [season 12](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Amazing_Race_12 "The Amazing Race 12") of _[The Amazing Race](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Amazing_Race_(American_TV_series) "The Amazing Race (American TV series)")_ and were placed third.[\[39\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robbie_Fulks#cite_note-Trib-AmazingRace2008-39)
