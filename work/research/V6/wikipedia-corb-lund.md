**Corby Clark Marinus Lund** (born January 29, 1969) is a Canadian [country and western](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Country_and_western "Country and western") singer-songwriter from [Taber, Alberta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taber,_Alberta "Taber, Alberta"), Canada. He has released twelve albums, three of which are certified gold. Lund tours regularly in Canada, the United States and Australia, and has received several awards in Canada and abroad.

## Biography

Corb Lund grew up in Southern Alberta living on his family's farm and ranches near [Taber](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taber,_Alberta "Taber, Alberta"), [Cardston](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cardston,_Alberta "Cardston, Alberta") and [Rosemary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rosemary,_Alberta "Rosemary, Alberta").[\[3\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corb_Lund#cite_note-3) Lund left his hometown of Taber and moved to [Edmonton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edmonton,_Alberta "Edmonton, Alberta"), where he enrolled in the [Grant MacEwan College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grant_MacEwan_College "Grant MacEwan College") to study jazz guitar and bass.

Lund was a founding member of [The Smalls](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Smalls "The Smalls"). The band retired in the fall of 2001 but reunited in 2014 for a string of shows, the so-called "Slight Return" tour.[\[4\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corb_Lund#cite_note-4)

Lund formed his country trio, the Corb Lund Band, in 1995.[\[5\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corb_Lund#cite_note-mont-5) He turned his attention to his own band exclusively when the Smalls broke up in 2001.[\[5\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corb_Lund#cite_note-mont-5) The band changed its name to "Corb Lund and the Hurtin' Albertans" in 2005[\[5\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corb_Lund#cite_note-mont-5) shortly after guitarist Grant Siemens joined the group, and has been touring and recording under that title ever since.

## Other media

Lund starred as the 50-year-old oilfield contractor Ray Mitchell in the 2022 Canadian film _Guitar Lessons._[\[6\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corb_Lund#cite_note-6)

## Personal life

Lund currently lives in Lethbridge, Alberta, and spends much of his downtime at his family ranch east of [Mountain View, Alberta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mountain_View,_Alberta "Mountain View, Alberta").[\[7\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corb_Lund#cite_note-7)

## The Hurtin' Albertans

[![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/f/fb/Corb_Lund.jpg/500px-Corb_Lund.jpg)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Corb_Lund.jpg) Corb Lund and the Hurtin' Albertans at the 2007 Glastonbury Festival

**Corb Lund and the Hurtin' Albertans** are a [Canadian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canadians "Canadians") [country music](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Country_music "Country music") band, formerly known as the **Corb Lund Band**. The Hurtin' Albertans is Lund's touring band. They have released nine albums to critical acclaim.[\[8\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corb_Lund#cite_note-8)[\[9\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corb_Lund#cite_note-9) The band tours regularly in Canada, the United States and Australia. Much of their time is spent in the [Canadian Prairies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canadian_Prairies "Canadian Prairies") and the American southwest.

The band's members are:

- Sean Burns, bass
- Grant "Demon" Siemens, guitar and other strings
- Lyle Molzan/Brady Valgardson, drums

Former Members:

- Ryan "Vik" Vikedal, drums
- Kurt Ciesla, bass
- Karie Brown, bass

The band has toured Europe, where they played the UK Glastonbury Festival, and Australia several times. The group was featured in the movie "Slither" (2006) and were part of the soundtrack to the 2008 documentary, "Holler Back: (Not) Voting in an American Town." They have also provided accompaniment for an NBC special in 2006, on which former world figure skating champion and fellow Albertan, [Kurt Browning](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kurt_Browning "Kurt Browning"), performed a routine to "Expectation and the Blues". Their music can also be heard in the ski film "Nine Winters Old."

Lund signed a three-album deal with [New West Records](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_West_Records "New West Records") (home of [Dwight Yoakam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dwight_Yoakam "Dwight Yoakam"), [Steve Earle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steve_Earle "Steve Earle"), [Kris Kristofferson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kris_Kristofferson "Kris Kristofferson") and other major artists) in 2009. His first record on New West, _[Losin' Lately Gambler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Losin'_Lately_Gambler "Losin' Lately Gambler")_, was released in September 2009.

Corb Lund and the Hurtin' Albertans played their 2009 single "Long Gone to Saskatchewan" in Ottawa for the 2011 [Canada Day](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canada_Day "Canada Day") ceremonies in the presence of the newlywed Duke and Duchess of Cambridge on their Royal visit to Canada.[\[10\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corb_Lund#cite_note-royal-10)

Corb Lund and the Hurtin' Albertans released their seventh studio album, _[Cabin Fever](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cabin_Fever_(Corb_Lund_album) "Cabin Fever (Corb Lund album)")_, on August 14, 2012. It debuted at number 1 on the _[Billboard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Billboard_(magazine) "Billboard (magazine)")_ [Canadian Albums Chart](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canadian_Albums_Chart "Canadian Albums Chart"). In June 2013, the album was longlisted for the [2013 Polaris Music Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2013_Polaris_Music_Prize "2013 Polaris Music Prize").

Corb Lund released _Things That Can't Be Undone_ in 2015. Lund worked with producer [Dave Cobb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dave_Cobb "Dave Cobb") (Sturgill Simpson, Chris Stapleton, Jason Isbell) to explore new styles and sounds on the album.[\[11\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corb_Lund#cite_note-11) The album appeared on the !earshot National Top 50 Chart in December that year.[\[12\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corb_Lund#cite_note-12)

## Charity work

In March 2008, the United Nations Children's Fund announced that the band had donated the use of the song "Horse Soldier, Horse Soldier" as the theme song of UNICEF Team Canada, the Canadian national [equestrian skill-at-arms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tent_pegging "Tent pegging") team, to support the team's work with UNICEF to provide food and medical care to [AIDS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AIDS "AIDS") orphans and infants infected with [HIV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HIV "HIV") in the global south.

In March 2010, Lund appeared as part of [Young Artists for Haiti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Young_Artists_for_Haiti "Young Artists for Haiti") to record a benefit version of [K'naan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K'naan "K'naan")'s song " [Wavin' Flag](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wavin'_Flag "Wavin' Flag")".

On July 11, 2010, Lund headlined the Medicine Hat Flood Relief Show, which raised $68,000 for Canadian Red Cross 2010 Flood Relief campaign, which benefits those affected by the disastrous flooding in Southern Alberta.

Lund has supported the [Centre for Family Literacy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Centre_for_Family_Literacy "Centre for Family Literacy") in Edmonton, Alberta since 2010.

Lund participated in the 2013 Alberta Flood Relief Concert at the Calgary McMahon Stadium. The concert raised at least $1.5 million for flood relief.

Lund co-hosted and headlined the "Fire Aid" benefit concert supporting victims of the Fort McMurray wildfire at Edmonton's Commonwealth Stadium in 2016.[\[39\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corb_Lund#cite_note-39)

In 2021, Lund publicly expressed opposition to proposed [coal mines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coal_in_Alberta "Coal in Alberta") in the foothills of the Rocky Mountains, citing concerns of fellow southern Alberta ranchers about possible water pollution.[\[40\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corb_Lund#cite_note-40) In June 2021, Lund held a small benefit concert in support of landowners.[\[41\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corb_Lund#cite_note-41) In 2025 Lund submitted a Citizen Initiative proposal to ban coal mining in the eastern slopes of the Rocky Mountains.[\[42\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corb_Lund#cite_note-42)[\[43\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corb_Lund#cite_note-43)
