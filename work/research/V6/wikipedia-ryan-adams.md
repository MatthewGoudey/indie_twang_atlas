Musical artist

**David Ryan Adams** (born November 5, 1974) is an American [rock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rock_music "Rock music") and [country](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Country_music "Country music") singer-songwriter. He has released 30 studio albums and 3 as a member of [Whiskeytown](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Whiskeytown "Whiskeytown").

In 2000, Adams left Whiskeytown and released his debut solo album, _[Heartbreaker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heartbreaker_(Ryan_Adams_album) "Heartbreaker (Ryan Adams album)")_, to critical acclaim. The album was nominated for the [Shortlist Music Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shortlist_Music_Prize "Shortlist Music Prize"). The following year, his profile increased with the release of the UK certified-gold _[Gold](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gold_(Ryan_Adams_album) "Gold (Ryan Adams album)")_, which included the single " [New York, New York](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York,_New_York_(Ryan_Adams_song) "New York, New York (Ryan Adams song)")". During this time, Adams worked on several unreleased albums, which were consolidated into a third solo release, _[Demolition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Demolition_(Ryan_Adams_album) "Demolition (Ryan Adams album)")_ (2002). Working at a prolific rate, Adams released the [classic rock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rock_(album) "Rock (album)")-influenced _[Rock N Roll](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rock_n_Roll_(Ryan_Adams_album) "Rock n Roll (Ryan Adams album)")_ (2003), after a planned album, _[Love Is Hell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Love_Is_Hell_(Ryan_Adams_album) "Love Is Hell (Ryan Adams album)")_, was rejected by his [Lost Highway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Universal_Music_Group_Nashville#Lost_Highway_Records "Universal Music Group Nashville") label. As a compromise, _Love Is Hell_ was released as two EPs and eventually released in its full-length state in 2004.

After breaking his wrist during a live performance, Adams took a hiatus and formed a backing band called [the Cardinals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ryan_Adams_&_the_Cardinals "Ryan Adams & the Cardinals"), who supported him on his next four studio albums. In 2009, due to complications from [Ménière's disease](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M%C3%A9ni%C3%A8re's_disease "Ménière's disease"), Adams disbanded the Cardinals and took a break from music.[\[1\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ryan_Adams#cite_note-1) However, he returned to the music scene in 2010, releasing his 13th studio album, _[Ashes & Fire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ashes_&_Fire "Ashes & Fire")_. Adams released his 14th album, _[Ryan Adams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ryan_Adams_(album) "Ryan Adams (album)")_ in 2014.

In 2015, Adams gained attention for his cover album _[1989](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1989_(Ryan_Adams_album) "1989 (Ryan Adams album)")_, a song-for-song cover of [Taylor Swift](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taylor_Swift "Taylor Swift")'s album of the same name. In 2019, several women accused Adams of sexual harassment, leading to a delay in the release of three planned albums. Adams later issued an apology and eventually released six more albums between 2020 and 2022. In addition to his own material, Adams has also produced albums for [Willie Nelson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Willie_Nelson "Willie Nelson"), [Jesse Malin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jesse_Malin "Jesse Malin"), [Jenny Lewis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jenny_Lewis "Jenny Lewis"), and [Fall Out Boy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fall_Out_Boy "Fall Out Boy"), and has collaborated with [Counting Crows](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Counting_Crows "Counting Crows"), [Weezer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weezer "Weezer"), [Norah Jones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norah_Jones "Norah Jones"), [America](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/America_(band) "America (band)"), [Minnie Driver](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minnie_Driver "Minnie Driver"), [Cowboy Junkies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cowboy_Junkies "Cowboy Junkies"), [Leona Naess](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leona_Naess "Leona Naess"), [Toots and the Maytals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toots_and_the_Maytals "Toots and the Maytals"), [Beth Orton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beth_Orton "Beth Orton"), and [Krista Polvere](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Krista_Polvere "Krista Polvere"). He has written _[Infinity Blues](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Infinity_Blues "Infinity Blues")_, a book of poems, and _[Hello Sunshine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hello_Sunshine_(book) "Hello Sunshine (book)")_, collection of poems and short stories.

## Early life

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Ryan_Adams&action=edit&section=1 "Edit section: Early life")\]

David Ryan Adams was born on November 5, 1974, in [Jacksonville, North Carolina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jacksonville,_North_Carolina "Jacksonville, North Carolina").[\[2\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ryan_Adams#cite_note-allmusicbio-2) He is the middle child of three, with an older brother and younger sister.[\[3\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ryan_Adams#cite_note-3) His childhood has been described as "dysfunctional". His father left when he was five at which time he, his mother and his siblings had to move in with his grandparents since they became homeless as a result of the divorce. He has said in an interview, "I became who I am now because of my grandparents"[\[4\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ryan_Adams#cite_note-guardian_2011-09-22-4) and of his grandmother "...she was like a mother to me."[\[5\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ryan_Adams#cite_note-5) His mother remarried when he was 13.

At the age of eight, Adams began writing short stories and limericks on his grandmother's typewriter. In his own words, "I started writing short stories when I was really into [Edgar Allan Poe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edgar_Allan_Poe "Edgar Allan Poe"). Then later, when I was a teenager, I got really hard into cult fiction: [Hubert Selby, Jr.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hubert_Selby,_Jr. "Hubert Selby, Jr."), [Henry Miller](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_Miller "Henry Miller"), [Jack Kerouac](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jack_Kerouac "Jack Kerouac")." At 14, Adams began learning to play an electric guitar his mother and stepfather had bought him and soon joined a local band named Blank Label. While they quickly disbanded, they did record a short three-track 7-inch record, in 1991.[\[6\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ryan_Adams#cite_note-6)

Adams dropped out of [Jacksonville High School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jacksonville_High_School_(North_Carolina) "Jacksonville High School (North Carolina)") in 10th grade, at the age of 16, subsequently moving into bandmate Jere McIlwean's rental house, just outside Jacksonville.[\[7\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ryan_Adams#cite_note-7) Around this time, he played and performed with a number of local bands, most notably his and McIlwean's The Patty Duke Syndrome. After obtaining his [GED](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/General_Educational_Development "General Educational Development"), Adams left Jacksonville for [Raleigh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Raleigh,_North_Carolina "Raleigh, North Carolina"), shortly followed by McIlwean. The Patty Duke Syndrome broke up in 1994[\[8\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ryan_Adams#cite_note-8) after releasing a [split 7-inch single](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Split_album "Split album") with GlamourPuss.

## Career

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Ryan_Adams&action=edit&section=2 "Edit section: Career")\]

### Whiskeytown

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Ryan_Adams&action=edit&section=3 "Edit section: Whiskeytown")\]

Main article: [Whiskeytown](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Whiskeytown "Whiskeytown")

Following the breakup of his high school band, The Patty Duke Syndrome, Adams helped found [Whiskeytown](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Whiskeytown "Whiskeytown") with [Caitlin Cary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caitlin_Cary "Caitlin Cary"), Eric "Skillet" Gilmore, Steve Grothmann and [Phil Wandscher](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phil_Wandscher "Phil Wandscher"). Whiskeytown saw Adams move to [alt-country](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alternative_country "Alternative country"), describing [punk rock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Punk_rock "Punk rock") as "too hard to sing" in the title track of Whiskeytown's debut album _[Faithless Street](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Faithless_Street "Faithless Street")_. Whiskeytown was heavily influenced by a number of country-rock pioneers, most notably [Gram Parsons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gram_Parsons "Gram Parsons").[\[9\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ryan_Adams#cite_note-9) The band quickly gained critical acclaim with the release of their second full-length album, _[Strangers Almanac](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strangers_Almanac "Strangers Almanac")_, their first [major label](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Record_label "Record label") release. A third album, _[Pneumonia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pneumonia_(album) "Pneumonia (album)"),_ was completed in 1999, but record label problems delayed its release. It was eventually released by [Lost Highway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lost_Highway_Records "Lost Highway Records") in 2001, at which time the band was effectively done.

### Solo career (2000–2004)

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Ryan_Adams&action=edit&section=4 "Edit section: Solo career (2000–2004)")\]

Adams made his solo debut in 2000, with _[Heartbreaker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heartbreaker_(Ryan_Adams_album) "Heartbreaker (Ryan Adams album)")_ (produced by [Ethan Johns](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethan_Johns "Ethan Johns")).[\[10\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ryan_Adams#cite_note-10) [Emmylou Harris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emmylou_Harris "Emmylou Harris") sang backup on "Oh My Sweet Carolina". Other backing vocals and instruments were provided by [Gillian Welch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gillian_Welch "Gillian Welch"), [David Rawlings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Rawlings "David Rawlings"), [Pat Sansone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pat_Sansone "Pat Sansone"), and Kim Richey as Adams embraced a style more reminiscent of folk music. It was met with considerable critical success, but sales were slow.[\[11\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ryan_Adams#cite_note-11)

Adams released _[Gold](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gold_(Ryan_Adams_album) "Gold (Ryan Adams album)")_, the follow-up to _Heartbreaker_, in 2001. It was well received. Adams, however, initially refused to promote the record through radio station meet-and-greets and other music-industry conventions, instead opting for more recording and some live dates.\[ _[citation needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed "Wikipedia:Citation needed")_\] A video was eventually made for the album's first single, " [New York, New York](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York,_New_York_(Ryan_Adams_song) "New York, New York (Ryan Adams song)")". The music video featured Adams performing in front of the city's skyline four days before the [September 11 attacks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/September_11_attacks "September 11 attacks"). The video was played often on [MTV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MTV "MTV") and [VH1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/VH1 "VH1") after the attacks and became Adams's breakthrough to mainstream music consumers.

Following the success of _Gold_, in 2002 Adams was blocked by his label from releasing his choice for a follow-up album. This would be the second time, the first being with _Gold;_ Adams had recorded "the Suicide Handbook" which was rejected on the grounds that it was "too sad". The label opted this time around to cherry pick (without Adams' involvement) from four of Adams' recorded albums it had already dismissed as unreleasable (48 Hours, The Suicide Handbook, The Pinkhearts and The Swedish Sessions) to create _[Demolition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Demolition_(Ryan_Adams_album) "Demolition (Ryan Adams album)"),_ released in September 2002. Although the album garnered him more critical attention, it failed to sell as well as _Gold_. The same year, Adams produced [Jesse Malin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jesse_Malin "Jesse Malin")'s first album, _[The Fine Art of Self Destruction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Fine_Art_of_Self_Destruction "The Fine Art of Self Destruction")_, and later worked with Malin to form the punk-rock group [The Finger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Finger_(band) "The Finger (band)") (under the pseudonyms, "Warren Peace" and "Irving Plaza" respectively), who released two E.P.s which were collected together to form _[We Are Fuck You](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/We_Are_Fuck_You "We Are Fuck You")_, released on [One Little Indian Records](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/One_Little_Indian_Records "One Little Indian Records") in 2003. He also starred in a [Gap](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gap_(clothing_retailer) "Gap (clothing retailer)") advertisement with Willie Nelson, performing a cover of Hank Williams's " [Move It on Over](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Move_It_On_Over_(song) "Move It On Over (song)")".

In May 2002, Adams joined Elton John on _[CMT Crossroads](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CMT_Crossroads "CMT Crossroads")_,[\[12\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ryan_Adams#cite_note-12) which brings together country artists with musicians from other genres. During the show, John referred to Adams as "fabulous one" and spoke of how _Heartbreaker_ inspired him to record _[Songs from the West Coast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Songs_from_the_West_Coast "Songs from the West Coast")_, which at the time was his most successful album in several years. Also in 2002, Adams reportedly recorded a cover of [The Strokes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Strokes "The Strokes")' debut album _[Is This It](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Is_This_It "Is This It")_, though it has never been publicly released.[\[13\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ryan_Adams#cite_note-13)

In 2002 and 2003, Adams worked on recording _[Love Is Hell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Love_Is_Hell_(Ryan_Adams_album) "Love Is Hell (Ryan Adams album)")_, intending to release it in 2003. [Lost Highway Records](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lost_Highway_Records "Lost Highway Records") deemed that it was not commercially viable and was reluctant to release it, leading Adams to go back to the studio. Two weeks later he returned to Lost Highway with _[Rock n Roll](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rock_n_Roll_(Ryan_Adams_album) "Rock n Roll (Ryan Adams album)")_, which featured guest musicians including [Melissa Auf der Maur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Melissa_Auf_der_Maur "Melissa Auf der Maur"), [Green Day](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Green_Day "Green Day")'s [Billie Joe Armstrong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Billie_Joe_Armstrong "Billie Joe Armstrong"), and Adams's girlfriend at the time, [Parker Posey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parker_Posey "Parker Posey").[\[14\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ryan_Adams#cite_note-14) Adams' songwriting received additional exposure when [Joan Baez](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joan_Baez "Joan Baez") included his song "In My Time of Need", from his debut release, on her 2003 album _[Dark Chords on a Big Guitar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dark_Chords_on_a_Big_Guitar "Dark Chords on a Big Guitar")._

Also released in 2003, Adams formed a punk band called The Finger with [Jesse Malin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jesse_Malin "Jesse Malin"), Colin Burns, and Johnny T. Yerington.[\[1\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Finger_(band)#cite_note-1 "The Finger (band)") The name derived from notorious early/mid-1990s [Raleigh, North Carolina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Raleigh,_North_Carolina "Raleigh, North Carolina") rock band Finger, of which Adams was a big fan. This light-hearted project allowed both artists to return to their punk backgrounds (Adams began his music career as singer for The Patty Duke Syndrome and Malin began his career in the hardcore punk band [Heart Attack](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heart_Attack_(band) "Heart Attack (band)") and more famously as the lead singer of [D Generation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/D_Generation "D Generation")). They began by releasing two EPs: _We Are Fuck You_ and _Punk's Dead Let's Fuck_ which were later collected to form the album _[We Are Fuck You](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/We_Are_Fuck_You "We Are Fuck You")_ that was released in 2003.

Adams and Lost Highway Records eventually agreed that the label would release _[Rock N Roll](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rock_n_Roll_(Ryan_Adams_album) "Rock n Roll (Ryan Adams album)")_ as well as _Love Is Hell_, on the condition that _Love Is Hell_ be split into two EP installments. _Rock N Roll_ and _Love Is Hell, Pt. 1_ were released in November 2003, followed by _Love Is Hell, Pt. 2_ in December. Both albums were well received by critics, and in May 2004 _Love Is Hell_ was re-released as a full-length album.

_Love Is Hell_ included a cover of [Oasis'](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oasis_(band) "Oasis (band)") " [Wonderwall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wonderwall_(song) "Wonderwall (song)")", which Adams had previously performed live, and about which [Noel Gallagher](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Noel_Gallagher "Noel Gallagher") once said, "I never got my head round this song until I went to \[see\] Ryan Adams play and he did an amazing cover of it."[\[15\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ryan_Adams#cite_note-15) The song earned Adams a [Grammy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grammy_Award "Grammy Award") nomination for " [Best Solo Rock Vocal Performance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grammy_Award_for_Best_Solo_Rock_Vocal_Performance "Grammy Award for Best Solo Rock Vocal Performance")".[\[16\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ryan_Adams#cite_note-16)

While on tour to support _Love Is Hell_ in January 2004, Adams fractured his wrist during a performance at the [Royal Court Theatre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Court_Theatre,_Liverpool "Royal Court Theatre, Liverpool") in Liverpool. Adams fell off the end of the stage into the lowered orchestra pit six feet below, while performing "The Shadowlands". Dates from Adams's European and American tours had to be canceled as a result of his injury.[\[17\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ryan_Adams#cite_note-17)

Adams was featured on the album [_True Love_](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/True_Love_(Toots_&_the_Maytals_album) "True Love (Toots & the Maytals album)") by [Toots and the Maytals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toots_and_the_Maytals "Toots and the Maytals"), which won the [Grammy Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grammy_Award "Grammy Award") in 2004 for Best Reggae Album, and showcased many notable musicians including [Willie Nelson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Willie_Nelson "Willie Nelson"), [Eric Clapton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eric_Clapton "Eric Clapton"), [Jeff Beck](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jeff_Beck "Jeff Beck"), [Trey Anastasio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trey_Anastasio "Trey Anastasio"), [Gwen Stefani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gwen_Stefani "Gwen Stefani") / [No Doubt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/No_Doubt "No Doubt"), [Ben Harper](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ben_Harper "Ben Harper"), [Bonnie Raitt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bonnie_Raitt "Bonnie Raitt"), [Manu Chao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manu_Chao "Manu Chao"), [The Roots](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Roots "The Roots"), [Keith Richards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Keith_Richards "Keith Richards"), [Toots Hibbert](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toots_Hibbert "Toots Hibbert"), [Paul Douglas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_Douglas_(musician) "Paul Douglas (musician)"), Jackie Jackson, [Ken Boothe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ken_Boothe "Ken Boothe"), and [The Skatalites](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Skatalites "The Skatalites").[\[18\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ryan_Adams#cite_note-18)

Main article: [Ryan Adams and the Cardinals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ryan_Adams_and_the_Cardinals "Ryan Adams and the Cardinals")

The year 2005 saw Adams join with backing band [the Cardinals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ryan_Adams_and_the_Cardinals "Ryan Adams and the Cardinals") to produce two albums, _[Cold Roses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cold_Roses "Cold Roses")_ and _[Jacksonville City Nights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jacksonville_City_Nights "Jacksonville City Nights")_. _Cold Roses_, a double album, included backing vocals from [Rachael Yamagata](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rachael_Yamagata "Rachael Yamagata") on three songs; "Let It Ride", "Cold Roses" and "Friends". His second album of the year, _Jacksonville City Nights_, featured a duet with [Norah Jones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norah_Jones "Norah Jones") on "Dear John". As well as releasing two albums with The Cardinals, Adams released the solo album _[29](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/29_(album) "29 (album)")_ late in the year.

[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/4/4a/The_Cardinals08.jpg/250px-The_Cardinals08.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:The_Cardinals08.jpg) Adams live with The Cardinals in November 2008

Adams befriended [Phil Lesh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phil_Lesh "Phil Lesh") of the [Grateful Dead](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grateful_Dead "Grateful Dead"), after first meeting him at the [Jammys](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jammys "Jammys") awards in New York in 2005. The two performed [Jerry Garcia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jerry_Garcia "Jerry Garcia") and [Robert Hunter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Hunter_(lyricist) "Robert Hunter (lyricist)")'s Grateful Dead classic, "Wharf Rat". Adams performed at subsequent outings of [Phil Lesh and Friends](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phil_Lesh_and_Friends "Phil Lesh and Friends"), including a two-night stand at [Red Rocks Amphitheatre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Red_Rocks_Amphitheatre "Red Rocks Amphitheatre") outside of [Denver](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Denver "Denver"), Colorado and on New Year's Eve 2005 at the Bill Graham Event Center in San Francisco. Throughout 2006, Lesh's live performances included compositions by Adams, including several from _Cold Roses_ ("Cold Roses", "Let It Ride", and "Magnolia Mountain").

Adams produced [Willie Nelson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Willie_Nelson "Willie Nelson")'s album _[Songbird](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Songbird_(Willie_Nelson_album) "Songbird (Willie Nelson album)")_, while he and The Cardinals performed as Nelson's backing band. The album was released in October 2006. He also opened for Nelson at the [Hollywood Bowl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hollywood_Bowl "Hollywood Bowl") later that fall, a show that featured [Phil Lesh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phil_Lesh "Phil Lesh") on bass and multiple [Grateful Dead](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grateful_Dead "Grateful Dead") songs. Late in 2006, Adams experimented with [hip-hop](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hip-hop "Hip-hop"), adding to his web site 18 albums worth of new recordings under various pseudonyms, featuring humorous and nonsensical lyrics, as well as covers of two [Bob Dylan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bob_Dylan "Bob Dylan") songs (" [Isis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isis_(song) "Isis (song)")" and " [You're a Big Girl Now](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/You're_a_Big_Girl_Now "You're a Big Girl Now")").

Adams released his ninth album on June 26, 2007, titled _[Easy Tiger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Easy_Tiger "Easy Tiger")_.[\[19\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ryan_Adams#cite_note-19) The album includes many tracks which were debuted during 2006's tours, as well as other older tracks which were previously unreleased.

On October 23, 2007, Adams released _[Follow the Lights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Follow_the_Lights "Follow the Lights")_, an EP featuring three new songs: "Follow the Lights","Blue Hotel" and "My Love for You Is Real", along with live studio versions of other previously released songs and a cover of Alice in Chains' "Down In A Hole".[\[20\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ryan_Adams#cite_note-20) Adams also appeared as a guest musician on Cowboy Junkies' 2007 album and DVD _[Trinity Revisited](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trinity_Revisited "Trinity Revisited")_, a 20th-anniversary re-recording of their classic album _[The Trinity Session](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Trinity_Session "The Trinity Session")_. In 2007 Adams co-wrote a song with Australian singer-songwriter [Krista Polvere](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Krista_Polvere "Krista Polvere") for her debut record _Here Be Dragons_; he also played guitar and piano on the album, which was recorded in New York.[\[21\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ryan_Adams#cite_note-21)

A new album with The Cardinals, _[Cardinology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cardinology "Cardinology")_ was released on October 28, 2008.[\[22\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ryan_Adams#cite_note-22)[\[23\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ryan_Adams#cite_note-Planet_Sound_News-23) Adams has also announced plans to release a book, entitled _[Infinity Blues](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Infinity_Blues "Infinity Blues")_.[\[24\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ryan_Adams#cite_note-24)[\[25\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ryan_Adams#cite_note-25) According to [Lost Highway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lost_Highway_Records "Lost Highway Records") chairman, Luke Lewis, there will be an "anthology" release in 2009, featuring several new songs.[\[26\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ryan_Adams#cite_note-26)

On January 14, 2009, Adams announced that he was quitting the Cardinals after their final show on March 20, 2009, at the Fox Theatre in [Atlanta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atlanta "Atlanta"). Adams cited hearing loss due to [Ménière's disease](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M%C3%A9ni%C3%A8re's_disease "Ménière's disease") as well as disillusionment with the music industry, the media and audience behavior as reasons for his decision.[\[27\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ryan_Adams#cite_note-27)[\[28\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ryan_Adams#cite_note-28)[\[29\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ryan_Adams#cite_note-29)[\[30\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ryan_Adams#cite_note-30) He also stated that he has been working on two new books, in addition to _Infinity Blues_.[\[31\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ryan_Adams#cite_note-31) The second book, released in the fall of 2009, is entitled _Hello Sunshine_. Preorders of _Hello Sunshine_ were shipped on August 18 by publisher Akashic Books.

### Post-Cardinals (2009)

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Ryan_Adams&action=edit&section=6 "Edit section: Post-Cardinals (2009)")\]

In May 2009, drummer [Brad Pemberton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brad_Pemberton "Brad Pemberton") stated that: "everyone was a bit fried, so it was the right time to step back for a minute. I encouraged Ryan to go and get married, and have a life and find some peace; the guy hasn't really slowed down in ten years, and he needed it as much as we did. Ryan and I have shared too much and are too good of friends to not ever do anything again, but I think we all need to do our own thing for a minute."[\[32\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ryan_Adams#cite_note-32)
The Cardinals, without Adams, have recorded together alongside [Gin Wigmore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gin_Wigmore "Gin Wigmore") for her debut album.[\[33\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ryan_Adams#cite_note-33)
Adams has dismissed any possibility of a reunion in the near future, saying it wouldn't be right after the death of Cardinals bassist Chris 'Spacewolf' Feinstein.

In August 2009, Adams began posting on the fan-site, Ryan Adams Archive, discussing the possibility of a [Whiskeytown](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Whiskeytown "Whiskeytown") reunion, new songs and releasing his many 'unreleased' albums.[\[34\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ryan_Adams#cite_note-34) In August, Adams also began blogging for The Awl.[\[35\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ryan_Adams#cite_note-35) In September 2009, Adams debuted a new song online, entitled "Happy Birthday",[\[36\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ryan_Adams#cite_note-36) and began releasing singles, featuring previously unreleased material, from his new record label, [PAX AM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PAX_AM "PAX AM").[\[37\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ryan_Adams#cite_note-37)

Casal released a [photo-book](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Photo-book "Photo-book"), _[Ryan Adams & the Cardinals: A View of Other Windows](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ryan_Adams_&_the_Cardinals:_A_View_of_Other_Windows "Ryan Adams & the Cardinals: A View of Other Windows")_, in February 2010, documenting his time within the band.

### PAX AM and return to music (2010–2013)

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Ryan_Adams&action=edit&section=7 "Edit section: PAX AM and return to music (2010–2013)")\]

In March 2010, Adams announced a new [metal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metal_music "Metal music")-influenced album, entitled _[Orion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orion_(Ryan_Adams_album) "Orion (Ryan Adams album)")_, to be released on his [PAX AM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PAX_AM "PAX AM") label. _Orion_ was released on vinyl only on May 18, 2010, and was sold through the PAX AM online store.[\[38\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ryan_Adams#cite_note-stereokill.net-38) According to Adams's Facebook page, he had completed two unreleased albums, _Blackhole_ and _[Cardinals III/IV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cardinals_III/IV "Cardinals III/IV")_, and was recording new albums in both New York City and Los Angeles.

On October 29, 2010, Adams played his first live show since stepping down from the Cardinals in March 2009, at a benefit hosted by [Judd Apatow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Judd_Apatow "Judd Apatow"). He was backed by Marshall Vore, [Sebastian Steinberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sebastian_Steinberg "Sebastian Steinberg") and former Cardinal Jamie Candiloro, who were billed as "The Ryan Adams Band". They debuted three new songs and were joined onstage by Mandy Moore for "Oh My Sweet Carolina".

In November 2010, Adams announced the release of _[Cardinals III/IV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cardinals_III/IV "Cardinals III/IV")_, his twelfth studio album, which was recorded in 2006. This album is his fifth with [The Cardinals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Cardinals_(rock_band) "The Cardinals (rock band)") and was released on December 14, 2010, through [PAX AM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PAX_AM "PAX AM").

In 2011, Adams announced a European tour, and noted that he was working on a new studio album with producer [Glyn Johns](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glyn_Johns "Glyn Johns").

On April 21, 2011, Adams was the surprise opener for an [Emmylou Harris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emmylou_Harris "Emmylou Harris") show at the El Rey Theatre in Los Angeles. He performed several new songs, as well as "Oh My Sweet Carolina" with Emmy, a track they recorded for 2000's _[Heartbreaker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heartbreaker_(Ryan_Adams_album) "Heartbreaker (Ryan Adams album)")_.

On June 16, 2011, through his Twitter account, Ryan suggested that his album _Blackhole_ could be released around Christmas.

In the September 2011 issue of _[Q Magazine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Q_Magazine "Q Magazine")_, Ryan revealed details of his first solo release since leaving [The Cardinals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Cardinals_(rock_band) "The Cardinals (rock band)"), entitled _[Ashes & Fire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ashes_&_Fire "Ashes & Fire")_. He also discussed the possibility of releasing a third book, _Phoenix_, which he says concerns "a loveable rat."[\[39\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ryan_Adams#cite_note-39)

In early 2012, Ryan was nominated for a [Brit Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brit_Award "Brit Award") for Best International Male. On June 18, 2012, Adams released a live box set chronicling his return to live performances, entitled _[Live After Deaf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Live_After_Deaf "Live After Deaf")_. His album _Ashes & Fire_ was nominated for the 55th Annual Grammy Awards.[\[40\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ryan_Adams#cite_note-40)

On April 21, 2013, Adams released an EP, _7 Minutes in Heaven_, with his newly formed [punk rock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Punk_rock "Punk rock") band [Pornography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pornography_(band) "Pornography (band)"), featuring [Make Out](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Make_Out_(band) "Make Out (band)") vocalist Leah Hennessey and frequent collaborator Johnny T. Yerington. In July 2013, Adams collaborated as a producer with rock band [Fall Out Boy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fall_Out_Boy "Fall Out Boy") at his PAX AM Studio, which resulted in Fall Out Boy's _[PAX AM Days](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PAX_AM_Days "PAX AM Days")_ EP. On March 19, 2013, Adams played a live show with a full band for the first time since the Cardinals disbanded. The show was for Noel Gallagher's Teenage Cancer Trust benefit. The venue's website stated that Ryan was playing new songs from a forthcoming album.

### Self-titled studio album, PAX AM Single Series, _1989_ and _Prisoner_ (2014–2018)

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Ryan_Adams&action=edit&section=8 "Edit section: Self-titled studio album, PAX AM Single Series, 1989 and Prisoner (2014–2018)")\]

On July 1, 2014, stories emerged that a self-titled album, _[Ryan Adams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ryan_Adams_(album) "Ryan Adams (album)")_, would be released on September 9, 2014.[\[41\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ryan_Adams#cite_note-41) Since this announcement, Adams has released a 7-inch vinyl EP, entitled _[1984](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1984_(EP) "1984 (EP)")_ and announced tour dates through mid October 2014. He debuted several new songs from the self-titled album in a series of festival concerts in late July 2014. On September 9, 2014, the self-titled album _[Ryan Adams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ryan_Adams_(album) "Ryan Adams (album)")_ was released on PAX-AM records.

In addition to the self-titled album, Adams announced a project he calls the "PAX AM Single Series", with a new seven-inch release each month. Adams stated, "I'm going to release a single every month until I can't do it any more."[\[42\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ryan_Adams#cite_note-wau-42) The first of these releases, the _[1984](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1984_(EP) "1984 (EP)")_ EP, was released in August 2014 and is described by Adams as, "pay\[ing\] homage to the halcyon days of the earliest releases from \[record labels\] [Dischord](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dischord_Records "Dischord Records"), [SST](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SST_Records "SST Records"), [Touch & Go](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Touch_and_Go_Records "Touch and Go Records") and their ilk." In 2014 and 2015, Adams continued to release digital singles, with each single being accompanied by at least 2 B-sides. Singles in 2014 included "Jacksonville", "Vampires", and "Do You Laugh When You Lie?", while singles in 2015 included "No Shadow" (featuring actor [Johnny Depp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johnny_Depp "Johnny Depp"), who had previously appeared on his self-titled album), "Blue Light", "I Do Not Feel Like Being Good", "Willow Lane", and "Burn in the Night". Often selling out within minutes of being released through PAX AM's website, the singles remain available for digital download.

On December 5, 2014, "Gimme Something Good", a song from the _Ryan Adams_ album, was nominated for the Grammy Award for Best Rock Song.[\[43\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ryan_Adams#cite_note-43)

On August 5, 2015, Adams announced via [Instagram](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Instagram "Instagram") that he was going to cover and release his version of [Taylor Swift](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taylor_Swift "Taylor Swift")'s 2014 album _[_1989_](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1989_(Taylor_Swift_album) "1989 (Taylor Swift album)")_. He released multiple clips via Twitter and Instagram showcasing his takes on Swift's songs.[\[44\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ryan_Adams#cite_note-44) Adams' [_1989_](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1989_(Ryan_Adams_album) "1989 (Ryan Adams album)") was released on September 21, 2015. It debuted at No. 7 on the US _Billboard_ 200 chart, one position ahead of Swift's _1989_, which was in its 48th week on the chart.[\[45\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ryan_Adams#cite_note-45) Adams said in October 2016 that he would never cover another full album again following the recording of _1989_.[\[46\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ryan_Adams#cite_note-46)

On December 6, 2016, Adams announced a new album, _[Prisoner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prisoner_(Ryan_Adams_album) "Prisoner (Ryan Adams album)")_. It was released on PAX AM/ [Blue Note](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blue_Note_Records "Blue Note Records") on February 17, 2017. The album's first single "Do You Still Love Me?" was released on December 7, 2016[\[47\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ryan_Adams#cite_note-47) and the second single, "To Be Without You" was released on December 23, 2016.[\[48\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ryan_Adams#cite_note-48)

In 2018, Adams released a stand-alone single, "Baby I Love You", and contributed one song, "20th Call of the Day", to the _[Juliet, Naked](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Juliet,_Naked_(film) "Juliet, Naked (film)")_ soundtrack.

### _Wednesdays_, _Big Colors_, _Chris_, and further albums (2019–present)

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Ryan_Adams&action=edit&section=9 "Edit section: Wednesdays, Big Colors, Chris, and further albums (2019–present)")\]

In January 2019, Adams announced his plans to release three albums that calendar year.[\[49\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ryan_Adams#cite_note-49) The first, _[Big Colors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Big_Colors "Big Colors")_, was due to be released April 19 and feature 15 tracks. The second, _[Wednesdays](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wednesdays_(album) "Wednesdays (album)")_, was to feature 17 tracks and did not yet have a confirmed release date. The release of all three albums was put on hold after the _New York Times_ broke allegations of sexual misconduct.[\[50\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ryan_Adams#cite_note-pulled-50) After a five-month silence, Adams returned in July 2019, posting a soundboard recording of a song titled "I'm Sorry and I Love You".[\[51\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ryan_Adams#cite_note-51) On December 11, 2020, Adams eventually [surprise-released](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Surprise_album "Surprise album") _Wednesdays_, which features "I'm Sorry and I Love You"[\[52\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ryan_Adams#cite_note-52) as well as several tracks originally announced for inclusion on _Big Colors_.[\[53\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ryan_Adams#cite_note-AllMusic-53) A music video for "I'm Sorry and I Love You" was uploaded to YouTube on December 15, 2020. Its description mentions that _Wednesdays_ is the first in a trilogy of albums, including _Big Colors_ and _Chris_.[\[54\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ryan_Adams#cite_note-Rolling_Stone_Germany_Wednesdays-54)

On April 23, 2021, Adams surprise-released "Do Not Disturb" as the lead single from the revised edition of _Big Colors_. The album was released on June 11, 2021, with a different track listing from the one announced in 2019.[\[55\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ryan_Adams#cite_note-Rolling_Stone_Germany_Big_Colors-55)

On March 2, 2022, Adams announced his first headlining show since the _New York Times_ article.[\[56\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ryan_Adams#cite_note-56) It sold out within hours and took place at [Carnegie Hall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carnegie_Hall "Carnegie Hall") on May 14.[\[57\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ryan_Adams#cite_note-57) Additional concerts were announced later in March,[\[58\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ryan_Adams#cite_note-ChrisRockNYC-58) with another batch announced in June.[\[59\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ryan_Adams#cite_note-59) On March 25, Adams released _Chris_ as the final installment of the trilogy.[\[58\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ryan_Adams#cite_note-ChrisRockNYC-58) It was followed by four other albums: _Romeo & Juliet_ on April 25,[\[60\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ryan_Adams#cite_note-R&JRockNYC-60) _FM_ on July 22,[\[61\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ryan_Adams#cite_note-61) _Devolver_ on September 23,[\[62\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ryan_Adams#cite_note-DevolverSobriety-62) and _Nebraska_ on December 7, a cover of the [Bruce Springsteen album](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nebraska_(album) "Nebraska (album)") of the same name. _Devolver_ was released as a free download along with a statement from Adams thanking his fans for their support.[\[63\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ryan_Adams#cite_note-63) He announced further shows in fall and winter of 2022,[\[64\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ryan_Adams#cite_note-64) followed by a return to the UK in April 2023.[\[65\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ryan_Adams#cite_note-65) On December 26, 2022, Adams released a cover of the [Bob Dylan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bob_Dylan "Bob Dylan") album _[Blood on the Tracks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blood_on_the_Tracks "Blood on the Tracks")_, and on April 14, 2023, he released a cover of the [Oasis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oasis_(band) "Oasis (band)") album _[(What's the Story) Morning Glory?](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/(What's_the_Story)_Morning_Glory%3F "(What's the Story) Morning Glory?")_, which also included covers of b-sides from that album's singles.[\[66\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ryan_Adams#cite_note-66)

On January 1, 2024, Adams surprise-released four more studio albums: _Sword & Stone_, _Star Sign_, _Heatwave_ and _1985_, as well as a live version of _Prisoner_.[\[67\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ryan_Adams#cite_note-67)

## Personal life

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Ryan_Adams&action=edit&section=10 "Edit section: Personal life")\]

### Relationships and health

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Ryan_Adams&action=edit&section=11 "Edit section: Relationships and health")\]

Adams was engaged to singer-songwriter [Leona Naess](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leona_Naess "Leona Naess"), but the engagement ended in 2003.[\[68\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ryan_Adams#cite_note-68) From 2003 to 2005 Adams dated actress [Parker Posey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parker_Posey "Parker Posey"). In 2008, Adams began dating singer-songwriter and actress [Mandy Moore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mandy_Moore "Mandy Moore"). They became engaged in February 2009 and they were married on March 10, 2009, in Savannah, Georgia.[\[69\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ryan_Adams#cite_note-69) On January 23, 2015, Adams and Moore announced in a joint statement that they were separating.[\[70\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ryan_Adams#cite_note-70) The divorce was finalized in June 2016.[\[71\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ryan_Adams#cite_note-71)

In 2007, Adams stated that he does not drink or take drugs.[\[72\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ryan_Adams#cite_note-72) He revealed that he had endured "an extended period of substance abuse" that ended in 2006. He indicated that he routinely snorted [heroin mixed with cocaine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Speedball_(drug) "Speedball (drug)"), and abused alcohol and [pills](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tablet_(pharmacy) "Tablet (pharmacy)"). He beat his addiction with the assistance of his girlfriend at the time, Jessica Joffe, using [Valium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diazepam "Diazepam") therapy and occasionally attending [twelve-step](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twelve-step_program "Twelve-step program") meetings.[\[73\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ryan_Adams#cite_note-73) In the following years, he made several statements indicating he was "newly sober"[\[74\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ryan_Adams#cite_note-74) and he told [TMZ](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TMZ "TMZ") in June 2022 that he had reached nine months of sobriety.[\[75\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ryan_Adams#cite_note-75) He celebrated one year of sobriety with the free release of his album _Devolver_ on September 23, 2022.[\[62\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ryan_Adams#cite_note-DevolverSobriety-62)

Adams has [Ménière's disease](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M%C3%A9ni%C3%A8re's_disease "Ménière's disease") and [tinnitus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tinnitus "Tinnitus"). A flyer advising of Adams' condition is affixed to the first several rows of seats at his concerts, with the admonition for audience members to refrain from taking flash photos or using [autofocus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Autofocus "Autofocus") assist beams when photographing at his shows.[\[76\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ryan_Adams#cite_note-76) Adams has also openly spoken about experiencing [depression](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Depression_(mood) "Depression (mood)") and [anxiety](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anxiety "Anxiety").[\[77\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ryan_Adams#cite_note-LAMAG-77)

### Disputes with fans and other artists

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Ryan_Adams&action=edit&section=12 "Edit section: Disputes with fans and other artists")\]

At a concert in October 2002, at Nashville's [Ryman Auditorium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ryman_Auditorium "Ryman Auditorium"), someone in the audience yelled out a request for " [Summer of '69](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Summer_of_'69 "Summer of '69")", a song by the similarly named [Bryan Adams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bryan_Adams "Bryan Adams"). Adams reacted with a stream of expletives, and ordered the house lights turned on, _[The Tennessean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Tennessean "The Tennessean")_ newspaper reported. He eventually found the fan who made the joke-request, paid him $30 cash as a refund for the show, ordered him to leave, and said he would not play another note until he had left.[\[78\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ryan_Adams#cite_note-78) In a 2014 interview, Ryan Adams denied that the audience-member was asked to leave "for screaming a Bryan Adams song", but rather because the man was drunk: "The reason why the guy was asked to leave by me was because I was doing an a-capella three-piece with [Gillian Welch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gillian_Welch "Gillian Welch"), [David Rawlings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Rawlings "David Rawlings"), and myself of ' [Bartering Lines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heartbreaker_(Ryan_Adams_album) "Heartbreaker (Ryan Adams album)")', and in between the quiet parts the guy was screaming."[\[79\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ryan_Adams#cite_note-79)[\[80\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ryan_Adams#cite_note-80) In April 2015, Ryan Adams, who had since become friends with Bryan Adams, played "Summer of '69" at the end of another performance at the Ryman in Nashville, an act described by _[Rolling Stone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rolling_Stone "Rolling Stone")_ as "an olive branch to the city that was once his home".[\[81\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ryan_Adams#cite_note-81)

Adams left an angry message on critic [Jim DeRogatis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jim_DeRogatis "Jim DeRogatis")'s answering machine, in response to a scathing review DeRogatis gave of an Adams show in 2003.[\[4\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ryan_Adams#cite_note-guardian_2011-09-22-4)

During filming of the BBC's long-running show _Songwriter's Circle_, where Adams was joined by American folk-singer [Janis Ian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Janis_Ian "Janis Ian") and New Zealand's [Neil Finn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neil_Finn "Neil Finn"), it was reported that he refused to participate in a number of the songs performed on the night and was generally dismissive of collaborating with the others. The fallout from the show resulted in an online back-and-forth argument between Adams, Ian, and members of the public, who cited Adams's rude behavior and eccentric demeanor toward his colleagues.[\[82\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ryan_Adams#cite_note-82) In August 2017, Adams singled out [Father John Misty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Father_John_Misty "Father John Misty") for criticism,[\[83\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ryan_Adams#cite_note-83)[\[84\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ryan_Adams#cite_note-84) while a month earlier he made derogatory remarks about [The Strokes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Strokes "The Strokes") through social media.[\[85\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ryan_Adams#cite_note-85)[\[86\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ryan_Adams#cite_note-86)[\[87\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ryan_Adams#cite_note-87)

In October 2025, Adams ended a show in [Melbourne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Melbourne "Melbourne") early, later writing on social media that [Australia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Australia "Australia") is the "worst country ever" to play in and [Australians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Australians "Australians") "the worst people".[\[88\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ryan_Adams#cite_note-88)[\[89\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ryan_Adams#cite_note-:1-89) Days later, he apologized, saying that he overreacted to a heckler and was also bothered by the [flash photography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flash_(photography) "Flash (photography)") because of his [Ménière's disease](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M%C3%A9ni%C3%A8re's_disease "Ménière's disease").[\[89\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ryan_Adams#cite_note-:1-89)

### Harassment allegations

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Ryan_Adams&action=edit&section=13 "Edit section: Harassment allegations")\]

On February 13, 2019, _[The New York Times](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_New_York_Times "The New York Times")_ reported that seven women (including [Phoebe Bridgers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phoebe_Bridgers "Phoebe Bridgers") and ex-wife [Mandy Moore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mandy_Moore "Mandy Moore")) said Adams offered to assist them with their music careers, then pursued the women romantically. They also claimed that Adams retaliated when they spurned his advances, hindering their careers and harassing them in text messages and on social media.[\[90\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ryan_Adams#cite_note-90)[\[91\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ryan_Adams#cite_note-91) The accusations surfaced in the context of the [#MeToo movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MeToo_movement "MeToo movement") against [sexual harassment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sexual_harassment "Sexual harassment"). Adams initially denied the accusations but in July 2020, he issued a statement in the _[Daily Mail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daily_Mail "Daily Mail")_ apologizing for his actions.[\[92\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ryan_Adams#cite_note-92)[\[93\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ryan_Adams#cite_note-93)[\[94\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ryan_Adams#cite_note-94)

## Recognition

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Ryan_Adams&action=edit&section=14 "Edit section: Recognition")\]

Adams is recognized for his highly prolific songwriting and a singing style that resembles that of a country rocker, even though he played [punk rock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Punk_rock "Punk rock") in the early part of his career. Adams has frequently experimented with different genres, although for the major part of his career his musical style has been [alternative rock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alternative_rock "Alternative rock"). He is also one of the few artists to emerge from the alt-country scene into mainstream commercial success.[\[95\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ryan_Adams#cite_note-All_cobain-95)

His musical style and dynamism has been praised by various artists like [Frank Turner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frank_Turner "Frank Turner"),[\[96\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ryan_Adams#cite_note-96) [Elton John](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elton_John "Elton John"),[\[97\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ryan_Adams#cite_note-97) [Willie Nelson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Willie_Nelson "Willie Nelson"),[\[98\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ryan_Adams#cite_note-Willie_Nelson-98) [Taylor Swift](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taylor_Swift "Taylor Swift"),[\[99\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ryan_Adams#cite_note-99) [Norah Jones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norah_Jones "Norah Jones"),[\[100\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ryan_Adams#cite_note-100) [Wesley Schultz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wesley_Schultz "Wesley Schultz"),[\[101\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ryan_Adams#cite_note-101) [Jared Followill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jared_Followill "Jared Followill")[\[102\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ryan_Adams#cite_note-102) and [Noel Gallagher](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Noel_Gallagher "Noel Gallagher").[\[103\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ryan_Adams#cite_note-103) American author [Stephen King](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stephen_King "Stephen King") is historically a fan of Adams, stating in 2007, "I won't say Adams is the best North American singer songwriter since [Neil Young](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neil_Young "Neil Young")... but I won't say he isn't either".[\[104\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ryan_Adams#cite_note-Stephen_Queen-104)
