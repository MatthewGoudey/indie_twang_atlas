Musical artist

**Neko Richelle Case** ([/ˈniːkoʊˈkeɪs/](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help:IPA/English "Help:IPA/English") [_NEE-koh KAYSS_](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help:Pronunciation_respelling_key "Help:Pronunciation respelling key");[\[2\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neko_Case#cite_note-Time-Blows-Empire-2) born September 8, 1970)[\[3\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neko_Case#cite_note-ExclaimTimeline-3) is an American singer-songwriter and member of the Canadian [indie rock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indie_rock "Indie rock") group [the New Pornographers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_New_Pornographers "The New Pornographers"). Case's singing voice has been described by contemporaries and critics as a "flamethrower",[\[4\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neko_Case#cite_note-4) her "powerhouse \[voice\] seems like it might level buildings,"[\[5\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neko_Case#cite_note-5) "a 120-mph fastball,"[\[6\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neko_Case#cite_note-6) and a "vocal tornado".[\[7\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neko_Case#cite_note-Menaker-7) Critics also note her idiosyncratic, "cryptic,"[\[8\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neko_Case#cite_note-8) "imagistic"[\[7\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neko_Case#cite_note-Menaker-7) lyrics, and credit her as a significant figure in the early 21st-century American revival of the [tenor guitar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tenor_guitar "Tenor guitar").[\[9\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neko_Case#cite_note-9)[\[10\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neko_Case#cite_note-10) Case's body of work has spanned and drawn on a range of traditions including country, folk, art rock, indie rock, and pop and is frequently described as resisting classification.[\[11\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neko_Case#cite_note-11)[\[12\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neko_Case#cite_note-12)[\[13\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neko_Case#cite_note-13)[\[14\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neko_Case#cite_note-14)

## Early life

Born in [Alexandria, Virginia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexandria,_Virginia "Alexandria, Virginia"), Case is the only child of James Bamford Case.[\[15\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neko_Case#cite_note-15) Case's maternal family surname was originally Shevchenko; her great-aunt was the professional wrestler [Ella Waldek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ella_Waldek "Ella Waldek").[\[16\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neko_Case#cite_note-EW-Amazing-16) Her father, an airman serving in the [United States Air Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Air_Force "United States Air Force"),[\[17\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neko_Case#cite_note-Furious-Thrice-17) was based in Virginia at the time of her birth. Case's parents, who were teenagers when they had her, are of Ukrainian ancestry.[\[3\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neko_Case#cite_note-ExclaimTimeline-3) Her parents divorced when Case began school.[\[3\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neko_Case#cite_note-ExclaimTimeline-3) In her memoir, Case indicated that she was told that her mother died of cancer when she was in the second grade, but only two years later, she was told that this was not correct. After that, her mother flitted in and out of her life, and eventually Case cut ties with her mother for good. As she writes in the book, she had a revelation: "Perhaps her mother had never been sick at all."[\[18\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neko_Case#cite_note-18)

Case's family relocated several times during her childhood due to her stepfather's work as an [archaeologist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Archaeologist "Archaeologist"). She lived in Western Massachusetts, Vermont, Oregon and Washington.[\[3\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neko_Case#cite_note-ExclaimTimeline-3) She considers [Tacoma, Washington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tacoma,_Washington "Tacoma, Washington") to be her hometown.[\[19\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neko_Case#cite_note-19)[\[20\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neko_Case#cite_note-20)

By the age of 18 she was performing as a drummer for the Del Logs and the Propanes, playing in venues including a punk club called the Community World Theater.[\[3\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neko_Case#cite_note-ExclaimTimeline-3)

## Music career

### Vancouver

In 1994, Case moved to [Vancouver](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vancouver "Vancouver"), British Columbia, to attend the [Emily Carr Institute of Art and Design](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emily_Carr_Institute_of_Art_and_Design "Emily Carr Institute of Art and Design"), leaving without a degree in 1998.[\[21\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neko_Case#cite_note-21)[\[22\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neko_Case#cite_note-22) While in Vancouver, she played drums in several local bands, including the Del Logs, the Propanes, the Weasels, [Cub](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cub_(band) "Cub (band)"), and [Maow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maow "Maow").[\[23\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neko_Case#cite_note-23) These bands were, for the most part, local [punk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Punk_rock "Punk rock") groups. Case said of the vibrant Vancouver punk rock scene at that time, "A lot of women wanted to play music because they were inspired, because it was an incredibly good time for music in the Northwest. There was a lot of clubs, a lot of bands, a lot of people coming through, a lot of all-ages stuff—it was a very exciting time to live there."[\[17\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neko_Case#cite_note-Furious-Thrice-17)

In 1998, she left Canada for [Seattle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seattle "Seattle"), Washington. Before going, Case recorded vocals for a few songs that ended up on _[Mass Romantic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mass_Romantic "Mass Romantic")_, [the New Pornographers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_New_Pornographers "The New Pornographers")' first album. Her lead vocals on songs like "Letter from an Occupant" are straightforward, full-volume [power-pop](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Power-pop "Power-pop") performances, shedding any country elements. Released on November 28, 2000, _Mass Romantic_ became a surprise success.[\[24\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neko_Case#cite_note-WMC-24) Although the band was originally conceived as a side project for its members, the New Pornographers remain a prominent presence in the indie rock world, having released their ninth album in 2023.

In addition to recording with the New Pornographers, Case collaborates with other Canadian musicians, including [the Sadies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Sadies "The Sadies") and [Carolyn Mark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carolyn_Mark "Carolyn Mark"), and has recorded material by several noted Canadian songwriters, in particular on her 2001 [EP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Extended_play "Extended play") _[Canadian Amp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canadian_Amp "Canadian Amp")_. As a result, she is also considered a significant figure in Canadian music—both [CBC Radio 3](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CBC_Radio_3 "CBC Radio 3") and the [Society of Composers, Authors and Music Publishers of Canada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Society_of_Composers,_Authors_and_Music_Publishers_of_Canada "Society of Composers, Authors and Music Publishers of Canada") have referred to Case as an "honourary \[sic\] Canadian".[\[25\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neko_Case#cite_note-25) In 2018 Case performed at the Vancouver Folk Music Festival.[\[26\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neko_Case#cite_note-26)

### Seattle

Case embraced country music on her 1997 album, _[The Virginian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Virginian_(album) "The Virginian (album)")_. The album contained original compositions as well as covers of songs by [Ernest Tubb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ernest_Tubb "Ernest Tubb"), [Loretta Lynn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Loretta_Lynn "Loretta Lynn") and the 1974 [Queen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Queen_(band) "Queen (band)") song " [Misfire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Misfire_(song) "Misfire (song)")".[\[27\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neko_Case#cite_note-Peppiatt2004-27) When the album was released, critics compared Case to [honky-tonk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Honky_tonk "Honky tonk") singers like Lynn and [Patsy Cline](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Patsy_Cline "Patsy Cline"), and to [rockabilly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rockabilly "Rockabilly") pioneer [Wanda Jackson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wanda_Jackson "Wanda Jackson"), particularly in her vocal timbre.[\[28\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neko_Case#cite_note-28)

On February 22, 2000, Case released her second solo album, _[Furnace Room Lullaby](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Furnace_Room_Lullaby "Furnace Room Lullaby")_. The album introduced the [country noir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Country_noir "Country noir") elements that have defined Case's subsequent solo career. That tone was evident even from the cover photo, featuring Case sprawled out corpse-like on a concrete floor.

Case sometimes tours with Canadian singer and songwriter Carolyn Mark as [the Corn Sisters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Corn_Sisters "The Corn Sisters").[\[29\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neko_Case#cite_note-holliston-29) One of their performances, at Seattle's Hattie's Hat restaurant in [Ballard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ballard,_Seattle,_Washington "Ballard, Seattle, Washington"), was recorded and released as an album, _[The Other Women](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Other_Women_(The_Corn_Sisters_album) "The Other Women (The Corn Sisters album)")_, on November 28, 2000.[\[29\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neko_Case#cite_note-holliston-29)

### Chicago

In October 1999, around the time _Furnace Room Lullaby_ was released, Case left Seattle[\[17\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neko_Case#cite_note-Furious-Thrice-17) for Chicago because she felt that Seattle was no longer hospitable to its local artists.[\[30\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neko_Case#cite_note-30)

Case's first work in Chicago was an eight-song [EP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Extended_play "Extended play") that she recorded in her kitchen. _[Canadian Amp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canadian_Amp "Canadian Amp")_, her first recording without Her Boyfriends, was released on her own Lady Pilot label in 2001. She wrote two of the tracks, with the remaining six being covers, including [Neil Young](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neil_Young "Neil Young")'s "Dreaming Man" and Hank Williams' "Alone and Forsaken". Four of the covers were written by Canadian artists. The EP was initially available only at Case's live shows and directly from Mint Records' website, but it eventually saw wider release.[\[31\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neko_Case#cite_note-31)

Case also recorded her third full-length album, _[Blacklisted](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blacklisted_(Neko_Case_album) "Blacklisted (Neko Case album)")_, while living in Chicago.[\[32\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neko_Case#cite_note-32)

In April 2003, Case was voted the "Sexiest Babe of Indie Rock" in a Playboy.com internet poll, receiving 32% of the vote. _[Playboy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Playboy "Playboy")_ asked her to pose nude for the magazine, but she declined their offer. She told _[Entertainment Weekly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Entertainment_Weekly "Entertainment Weekly")_ that

> I didn't want to be the girl who posed in _Playboy_ and then—by the way—made some music. I would be really fucking irritated if after a show somebody came up to me and handed me some naked picture of myself and wanted me to sign it instead of my CD.[\[33\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neko_Case#cite_note-33)

### Neko Case & Her Boyfriends

Case recorded and toured for several years as Neko Case & Her Boyfriends before performing solely under her name. Albums released as Neko Case & Her Boyfriends include _[The Virginian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Virginian_(album) "The Virginian (album)")_ (1997) and _[Furnace Room Lullaby](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Furnace_Room_Lullaby "Furnace Room Lullaby")_ (2000). She primarily performed her own material, but also performed and recorded [cover versions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cover_version "Cover version") of songs by artists such as [My Morning Jacket](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/My_Morning_Jacket "My Morning Jacket"), [Harry Nilsson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harry_Nilsson "Harry Nilsson"), [Loretta Lynn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Loretta_Lynn "Loretta Lynn"), [Tom Waits](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tom_Waits "Tom Waits"), [Nick Lowe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nick_Lowe "Nick Lowe"), [Buffy Sainte-Marie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buffy_Sainte-Marie "Buffy Sainte-Marie"), [Scott Walker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scott_Walker_(singer) "Scott Walker (singer)"), [Randy Newman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Randy_Newman "Randy Newman"), [Queen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Queen_(band) "Queen (band)"), [Bob Dylan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bob_Dylan "Bob Dylan"), [Neil Young](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neil_Young "Neil Young"), [Sparks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sparks_(band) "Sparks (band)") and [Hank Williams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hank_Williams "Hank Williams").[\[34\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neko_Case#cite_note-34)

### New Pornographers

The 2010 New Pornographers album _[Together](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Together_(New_Pornographers_album) "Together (New Pornographers album)")_ features Case as lead vocalist on "Crash Years" and "My Shepherd."[\[35\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neko_Case#cite_note-35) The 2014 album _[Brill Bruisers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brill_Bruisers "Brill Bruisers")_ features Case as lead vocalist on "Champions of Red Wine" and "Marching Orders."[\[36\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neko_Case#cite_note-36) The 2017 album _[Whiteout Conditions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Whiteout_Conditions "Whiteout Conditions")_ features Case as lead vocalist on "Play Money" and "This is the World of the Theater."[\[37\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neko_Case#cite_note-37)

### Solo

#### _Blacklisted_

Case recorded her third full-length album, _[Blacklisted](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blacklisted_(Neko_Case_album) "Blacklisted (Neko Case album)")_, in [Tucson, Arizona](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tucson,_Arizona "Tucson, Arizona"). It was the first full-length album credited to Case alone, without Her Boyfriends, and was released on [Bloodshot Records](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bloodshot_Records "Bloodshot Records") on August 20, 2002. Some believe the title _Blacklisted_ alludes to Case being banned for life from the _[Grand Ole Opry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grand_Ole_Opry "Grand Ole Opry")_ because she took her shirt off during a performance on August 4, 2001, at one of their outdoors "Opry Plaza" concerts,[\[39\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neko_Case#cite_note-39)[\[40\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neko_Case#cite_note-guardian13Mar2009-40) though Case herself has denied this.[\[41\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neko_Case#cite_note-41) Asked about the incident in 2004, Case said "I had [heatstroke](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heatstroke "Heatstroke"). People would love it to be a 'fuck you' punk thing. But it was actually a physical ailment thing."[\[42\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neko_Case#cite_note-42)

[![Case's face](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/6/61/Neko_Case_at_All_Points_West_%28edit%29.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail_unscaled)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Neko_Case_at_All_Points_West_(edit).jpg) Case in 2009

Most of the album's fourteen songs are originals; the exceptions being covers of "Running Out of Fools", previously a hit for [Aretha Franklin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aretha_Franklin "Aretha Franklin"), and "Look for Me (I'll Be Around)" previously performed by [Sarah Vaughan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sarah_Vaughan "Sarah Vaughan"). _Blacklisted_ finds Case even deeper in a "country noir" mood, and was described by critics as lush, bleak, and atmospheric. Case cited filmmaker [David Lynch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Lynch "David Lynch"), composer [Angelo Badalamenti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Angelo_Badalamenti "Angelo Badalamenti"), and Neil Young's soundtrack to the film _[Dead Man](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dead_Man "Dead Man")_ as influences.[\[17\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neko_Case#cite_note-Furious-Thrice-17)

> I hope I can comfort people a bit—maybe show people that making music is fun and accessible to them as well. I'm not out to become [Faith Hill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Faith_Hill "Faith Hill"), I never want to play an arena, and I never want to be on the [MTV Video Music Awards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MTV_Video_Music_Awards "MTV Video Music Awards"), much less make a video with me in it. I would like to reach a larger audience and see the state of music change in favor of musicians and music fans in my lifetime. I care very much about that.[\[43\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neko_Case#cite_note-43)

#### _The Tigers Have Spoken_

In April 2004, Case played several shows with longtime collaborators [the Sadies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Sadies "The Sadies") in Chicago and [Toronto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toronto "Toronto"). These shows were recorded and released as a live album, _[The Tigers Have Spoken](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Tigers_Have_Spoken "The Tigers Have Spoken")_, by Anti Records in October 2004.[\[44\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neko_Case#cite_note-44)

#### _Fox Confessor Brings the Flood_

_[Fox Confessor Brings the Flood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fox_Confessor_Brings_the_Flood "Fox Confessor Brings the Flood")_ was released on March 7, 2006. The album was recorded primarily in Tucson, over the course of two years as Case worked on the live _The Tigers Have Spoken_ and continued to play with the New Pornographers. Critics hailed the record not only for Case's trademark vocals but also her use of stark imagery and non-standard song structures. _Fox Confessor Brings the Flood_ wound up on many "Best of 2006" lists, such as No.1 on the Amazon.com music editors' picks and No.2 on [NPR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NPR "NPR")'s _[All Songs Considered](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/All_Songs_Considered "All Songs Considered")_. The album debuted at No.54 on the [_Billboard_ 200](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Billboard_200 "Billboard 200") albums chart. It contains Case's most autobiographical song, "Hold On, Hold On". Case said: "the song is actually about me. It's not metaphorical about other people. It's not little pieces of my life made into a story about someone else or someone fictitious."[\[45\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neko_Case#cite_note-45)

"Hold On, Hold On" has since been covered by [Marianne Faithfull](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marianne_Faithfull "Marianne Faithfull") on her 2009 album _[Easy Come, Easy Go](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Easy_Come,_Easy_Go_(Marianne_Faithfull_album) "Easy Come, Easy Go (Marianne Faithfull album)")_.[\[46\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neko_Case#cite_note-46) It was used over an episode of [_The Killing_](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Killing_(U.S._TV_series) "The Killing (U.S. TV series)") (Season 1 Episode 6) before the final credits, and in the 2015 film _[One More Time](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/One_More_Time_(2015_film) "One More Time (2015 film)")_. "John Saw That Number" was used in the snowboarding movie "City. Park City".

#### _Middle Cyclone_

Case's next album, _[Middle Cyclone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Middle_Cyclone "Middle Cyclone")_, was released on March 3, 2009. In advance of a U.S. and European tour, Case appeared as a musical guest on _[The Tonight Show with Jay Leno](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Tonight_Show_with_Jay_Leno "The Tonight Show with Jay Leno")_. Later in 2009 she also appeared on _[Late Show with David Letterman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Late_Show_with_David_Letterman "Late Show with David Letterman")_, _[The Tonight Show with Conan O'Brien](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Tonight_Show_with_Conan_O'Brien "The Tonight Show with Conan O'Brien")_ and _[Late Night with Jimmy Fallon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Late_Night_with_Jimmy_Fallon "Late Night with Jimmy Fallon")_. Amazon.com rated _[Middle Cyclone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Middle_Cyclone "Middle Cyclone")_ the number one album of 2009.[\[47\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neko_Case#cite_note-47) _Middle Cyclone_ debuted at No.3 on the _[Billboard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Billboard_(magazine) "Billboard (magazine)")_ charts in its first week of release, making it Case's first album ever to reach the top ten in the United States.

At the time of its release, no other record from an independent record company had debuted at a higher position in 2009.[\[48\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neko_Case#cite_note-48) She toured extensively to promote _[Middle Cyclone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Middle_Cyclone "Middle Cyclone")_ with dates in North America, Europe, and Australia, as well as a performance at [Lollapalooza](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lollapalooza "Lollapalooza") 2009 in [Grant Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grant_Park_(Chicago) "Grant Park (Chicago)"), Chicago.

#### _The Worse Things Get, the Harder I Fight, the Harder I Fight, the More I Love You_

In June 2013, Case announced a new album, _[The Worse Things Get, the Harder I Fight, the Harder I Fight, the More I Love You](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Worse_Things_Get,_the_Harder_I_Fight,_the_Harder_I_Fight,_the_More_I_Love_You "The Worse Things Get, the Harder I Fight, the Harder I Fight, the More I Love You")_, which was released on September 3.[\[49\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neko_Case#cite_note-13album-49)

#### _Hell-On_

In early March, 2018, Case released a teaser for an album titled _[Hell-On](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hell-On "Hell-On")_, her first solo work in almost five years. The teaser featured Case lying down singing a song of the same name while snakes move around her. The album was released on June 1, 2018.[\[50\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neko_Case#cite_note-50)

#### _Truckdriver, Gladiator, Mule_

On November 13, 2015, Case released a compilation vinyl box set containing eight of her solo albums. The set contains her first six studio albums, including the first vinyl pressing of _[The Virginian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Virginian_(album) "The Virginian (album)"),_ as well as a live album.[\[51\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neko_Case#cite_note-51)

#### _Wild Creatures_

On April 19, 2022, Case released _[Wild Creatures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wild_Creatures "Wild Creatures")_, described as "digital-only, career retrospective".[\[52\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neko_Case#cite_note-52) The album was released on CD, double vinyl, and MP3. It contains 22 tracks from Case's discography, plus one new song, "Oh, Shadowless".

#### _Neon Grey Midnight Green_

## Personal life

In a 2013 [NPR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NPR "NPR") interview, while discussing her single "Man", Case described having complicated feelings of [gender](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gender_identity "Gender identity") and [femininity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Femininity "Femininity"): "I don't really think of myself specifically as a woman, you know? I'm kind of a critter... I'm probably a little imbalanced in that if you were to look at a human creature as kind of a vase or something, my glass is a little bit more full of the man stuff than the woman stuff".[\[72\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neko_Case#cite_note-72) As of November 2025, her [Twitter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twitter "Twitter") bio listed her pronouns as "She/Sir".[\[73\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neko_Case#cite_note-73) Speaking with _[PBS News Hour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PBS_News_Hour "PBS News Hour")_ in 2025 to promote her memoir, Case mentioned that she had "never felt like a girl" and described herself as "a [genderfluid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Genderfluid "Genderfluid") person".[\[74\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neko_Case#cite_note-74)

In her 2025 memoir, _The Harder I Fight the More I Love You_, Neko writes "As a female-identifying person, I know my own tenacity and desire. I know the desires and passions of other women and we are unstoppable."
