**Stewart Ransom** " **Rhett**" **Miller II** (born September 6, 1970)[\[1\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rhett_Miller#cite_note-DallasObserver-FightSongs-1999-1)[\[2\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rhett_Miller#cite_note-2) is the lead singer of the [alternative country rock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alternative_country_rock "Alternative country rock") band [Old 97's](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_97's "Old 97's"). He also records and performs as a solo musician, and has been published as a writer of both fiction and non-fiction.[\[3\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rhett_Miller#cite_note-NoDepression-Instigator-2002-3)[\[4\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rhett_Miller#cite_note-Salon-Old97s-MessedUp-2014-4)

## Early life

Miller, a seventh-generation Texan, was born in [Austin, Texas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austin,_Texas "Austin, Texas").[\[1\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rhett_Miller#cite_note-DallasObserver-FightSongs-1999-1) Miller's parents divorced when he was 17 years old. Miller, the oldest of three children, has a younger brother and sister.[\[5\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rhett_Miller#cite_note-AmericanSongwriter-Old97-BarrierReef-2012-5)[\[6\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rhett_Miller#cite_note-WTFMaron-Episode554-2014-6) Rhett's paternal grandfather, Giles E. Miller, was a young millionaire scion of a successful textile family who, in 1952 owned the first NFL football team in the south, the [Dallas Texans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dallas_Texans_(NFL) "Dallas Texans (NFL)"). The Texans folded after seven games, marking the last time an NFL franchise would go bankrupt.[\[7\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rhett_Miller#cite_note-SportsIllustrated-DallasTexans-2015-7)[\[8\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rhett_Miller#cite_note-Billboard-TooFarToCare-1997-8)

Miller's family lived in [Highland Park, Texas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Highland_Park,_Texas "Highland Park, Texas"), where he went to [Armstrong Elementary School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armstrong_Elementary_School_(Highland_Park,_Texas) "Armstrong Elementary School (Highland Park, Texas)"). In 4th grade, Miller was hospitalized for several months due to a severe inner-ear problem. In 6th grade, he began attending [St. Mark's School of Texas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Mark's_School_of_Texas "St. Mark's School of Texas"), a private boys' school in [North Dallas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Dallas "North Dallas"). He started taking guitar lessons when he was 12 years old and writing songs when he was 13. Miller has said that his time at St. Mark's was very difficult, and that he was ostracized and bullied, leading to depression which culminated in a suicide attempt at the age of 14.[\[1\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rhett_Miller#cite_note-DallasObserver-FightSongs-1999-1) The following year in April 1985 Miller played his first gig at 500 Cafe in downtown Dallas.[\[6\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rhett_Miller#cite_note-WTFMaron-Episode554-2014-6) Through high school Miller played in bands, becoming a local folk performer and headlining small venues and opening for such nationally touring artists as [Rosanne Cash](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rosanne_Cash "Rosanne Cash"), [Chris Isaak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chris_Isaak "Chris Isaak"), and [The Lords of the New Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Lords_of_the_New_Church "The Lords of the New Church"). Also in high school, Miller edited St. Mark's literary magazine and helped start an alternative literary magazine called _The Rag,_ for which he wrote poetry.

In 1989, Miller graduated from [St. Mark's School of Texas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Mark's_School_of_Texas "St. Mark's School of Texas").[\[1\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rhett_Miller#cite_note-DallasObserver-FightSongs-1999-1) He briefly attended [Sarah Lawrence College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sarah_Lawrence_College "Sarah Lawrence College") on a creative writing scholarship before deciding to move back to Texas to pursue a music career.[\[9\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rhett_Miller#cite_note-MensJournal-Feature-2014-9)

## Career

In 1989, while still in high school, Miller released an album called _[Mythologies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mythologies_(Rhett_Miller_album) "Mythologies (Rhett Miller album)")._ The album title was taken from a book of essays by the French media philosopher [Roland Barthes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roland_Barthes "Roland Barthes"). Only 1,000 copies of the CD exist. Miller signed and numbered each one.[\[1\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rhett_Miller#cite_note-DallasObserver-FightSongs-1999-1)

In 1990, when Miller returned to Dallas after his semester at college, Miller formed a band called Sleepy Heroes with childhood friend and future Old 97’s bassist Murry Hammond. Sleepy Heroes was a power-pop three piece. They released one album, _Under a Radio Sun,_ before they broke up. The Old 97's song, "Victoria", was written during the last few months of Sleepy Heroes.[\[1\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rhett_Miller#cite_note-DallasObserver-FightSongs-1999-1)

Miller was the lead singer of various bands in Dallas between 1990 and 1993: Rhett Miller's Third Eye, Buzz, Rhett's Exploding, and Retablo, for which Miller self-recorded an unreleased record on cassette which included some early Old 97's songs.[\[1\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rhett_Miller#cite_note-DallasObserver-FightSongs-1999-1)

In 1993, Miller and Hammond formed [Old 97's](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_97's "Old 97's") as a three piece acoustic act along with their neighbor at Dallas' Marquita Court Apartments, guitarist Ken Bethea. They played as a three piece for six months before adding Darin Lin Wood on drums. He played with the band for a few weeks in the summer of 1993 before being replaced by Philip Peeples who has remained the band's drummer ever since.

Old 97’s first album, _[Hitchhike To Rhome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hitchhike_To_Rhome "Hitchhike To Rhome"),_ came out on local Dallas label Big Iron Records in 1993. During the first year of Old 97’s, Miller also performed as a touring member of the band [Killbilly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Killbilly "Killbilly"). It was during a Killbilly tour that Miller met [Nan Warshaw](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nan_Warshaw "Nan Warshaw"), owner of Chicago's [Bloodshot Records](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bloodshot_Records "Bloodshot Records"), the label which released Old 97’s second album, _[Wreck Your Life](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wreck_Your_Life "Wreck Your Life")._

After a sold-out SXSW showcase in 1995, Old 97’s found themselves the subject of a major label bidding war. 15 record labels fought to sign the band, with [Elektra Records](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elektra_Records "Elektra Records") A&R rep [Tom DeSavia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tom_DeSavia?action=edit&redlink=1 "Tom DeSavia") finally inking the band to a multi-album deal. Their first Elektra release, _[Too Far To Care](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Too_Far_To_Care "Too Far To Care"),_ came out in 1997, followed by _[Fight Songs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fight_Songs_(Old_97's_album) "Fight Songs (Old 97's album)")_ and _[Satellite Rides](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Satellite_Rides "Satellite Rides")._[\[10\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rhett_Miller#cite_note-Vanyaland-Traveler-2016-10)

In 2002, Miller released _[The Instigator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Instigator "The Instigator")_ on [Elektra Records](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elektra_Records "Elektra Records"). The record was produced and recorded with [Jon Brion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jon_Brion "Jon Brion"), received critical acclaim and substantial airplay on alternative-oriented radio stations.[\[3\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rhett_Miller#cite_note-NoDepression-Instigator-2002-3)

In 2006, Miller released _[The Believer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Believer_(Rhett_Miller_album) "The Believer (Rhett Miller album)")_ on the [Verve Forecast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Verve_Records "Verve Records") label.[\[11\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rhett_Miller#cite_note-NoDepression-Old97s-BlameGravity-2008-11) It includes a cover of Brion's "I Believe She's Lying" and "Fireflies", a duet with [Rachael Yamagata](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rachael_Yamagata "Rachael Yamagata").

In 2009, Miller released his fourth record, the self-titled _[Rhett Miller](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rhett_Miller_(album) "Rhett Miller (album)")_, on [Shout! Factory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shout!_Factory "Shout! Factory"). The record includes Jon Brion on guitar and bass, [The Apples in Stereo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Apples_in_Stereo "The Apples in Stereo")'s [John Dufilho](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Dufilho "John Dufilho") on drums and Billy Harvey on guitar.[\[12\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rhett_Miller#cite_note-AVClub-SelfTitled-2009-12) In 2011, Miller self-released a live recording of _[The Interpreter: Live at Largo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Interpreter_(album) "The Interpreter (album)")._

[![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/0/06/Rhett_Miller_2013.jpg/500px-Rhett_Miller_2013.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Rhett_Miller_2013.jpg) Miller performing in 2013

In 2012, Miller released _[The Dreamer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Dreamer_(Rhett_Miller_album) "The Dreamer (Rhett Miller album)")._[\[13\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rhett_Miller#cite_note-AmericanSongwriter-WriterOfTheWeek-2012-13) The record, a Maximum Sunshine release which Miller self-produced,[\[14\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rhett_Miller#cite_note-Magnet-Dreamer-2012-14) included collaborations with [Rosanne Cash](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rosanne_Cash "Rosanne Cash") and [Ben Kweller](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ben_Kweller "Ben Kweller").[\[15\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rhett_Miller#cite_note-PopMatters-Dreamer-2012-15)

In 2015, Miller released his sixth solo record, called _[The Traveler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Traveler_(Rhett_Miller_album) "The Traveler (Rhett Miller album)"),_ on [ATO Records](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ATO_Records "ATO Records").[\[16\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rhett_Miller#cite_note-Billboard-Traveler-2015-16) The album was a notable shift for Miller, as he recorded it with a [Portland, Oregon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portland,_Oregon "Portland, Oregon")-based bluegrass band called [Black Prairie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_Prairie "Black Prairie"), which includes members of [The Decemberists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Decemberists "The Decemberists"). The album also features contributions from [Peter Buck](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_Buck "Peter Buck") and [Scott McCaughey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scott_McCaughey "Scott McCaughey") of [R.E.M.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/R.E.M. "R.E.M.")[\[17\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rhett_Miller#cite_note-RollingStone-Traveler-2015-17)

In 2018, Miller released _The Messenger_ on [ATO Records](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ATO_Records "ATO Records").[\[18\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rhett_Miller#cite_note-RollingStone-Messenger-2018-18)

In 2022, Miller released _The Misfit_ on [ATO Records](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ATO_Records "ATO Records").[\[19\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rhett_Miller#cite_note-NPR-Misfit-2022-19)

In 2025, Miller released _A Lifetime of Riding by Night_ on [ATO Records](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ATO_Records "ATO Records").[\[20\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rhett_Miller#cite_note-RollingStone-Lifetime-2025-20)

### Writing

Miller has written short stories, essays and articles that have appeared in _[Rolling Stone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rolling_Stone "Rolling Stone"),_ _[The Baffler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Baffler "The Baffler"),_ _[Bookforum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bookforum "Bookforum"),_ _[Sports Illustrated](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sports_Illustrated "Sports Illustrated"),_ _[McSweeney's](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/McSweeney's "McSweeney's"),_[\[21\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rhett_Miller#cite_note-SanDiegoCityBeat-Instigator-2002-21) _[The Atlantic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Atlantic "The Atlantic"),_ and _[Salon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salon_(website) "Salon (website)")._[\[4\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rhett_Miller#cite_note-Salon-Old97s-MessedUp-2014-4)

### Other projects

In addition to his solo work, Miller has worked on various collaborations, including co-writing with other musicians.[\[22\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rhett_Miller#cite_note-BrooklynVegan-Traveler-2015-22)

- 2004: Recorded a version of [Simon and Garfunkel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simon_and_Garfunkel "Simon and Garfunkel")'s "Homeward Bound" for one of [MasterCard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MasterCard "MasterCard")'s "Priceless" advertisements[\[23\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rhett_Miller#cite_note-Mastercard-HomewardBound-2003-23)
- 2008: Co-produced the first EP, _No One Will Know_, of New York band [The Spring Standards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Spring_Standards "The Spring Standards")
- 2009: Appeared as a member of the musical ensemble in the _[30 Rock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/30_Rock "30 Rock")_ episode _[Kidney Now!](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kidney_Now! "Kidney Now!")_[\[24\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rhett_Miller#cite_note-NYTimes-30Rock-2009-24)
- 2019: On 1/23/19 Miller announced the 1/24 start of his podcast, _Wheels Off_, subtitled A Show About the Messy Reality Behind the Creative Life. In partnership with Revoice Media, it's an 11-episode series; each segment features host Miller conversing with musicians, writers, artists, actors, comedians and other creative people about the pivotal moments that shaped their work, what it means to create in a digital age and grappling with the challenges and joys of living a creative life.[\[25\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rhett_Miller#cite_note-25)

### Awards

In 2025, Miller's band the Old 97's received a Lifetime Achievement award from the [Americana Music Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Americana_Music_Association "Americana Music Association").[\[26\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rhett_Miller#cite_note-26)[\[27\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rhett_Miller#cite_note-27)

## Philanthropy

In 2006, Miller and his brother Ross Miller launched the Breathe Easy Concert Series, an annual event in Dallas that raises money for the [Cystic Fibrosis Foundation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cystic_Fibrosis_Foundation "Cystic Fibrosis Foundation") and awareness about [cystic fibrosis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cystic_fibrosis "Cystic fibrosis").[\[28\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rhett_Miller#cite_note-DallasObserver-BreateEasy-2008-28)

In 2016, Miller appeared as part of the Okay to Say initiative sponsored by the Meadows Mental Health Policy Institute in Dallas, which encourages the use of therapy to prevent suicide and address mental health issues.[\[29\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rhett_Miller#cite_note-OkayToSay-Miller-2016-29) In the campaign, Miller discusses his own suicide attempt when he was 14 years old,[\[6\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rhett_Miller#cite_note-WTFMaron-Episode554-2014-6) and how therapy has helped him over the years.[\[30\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rhett_Miller#cite_note-DallasNews-OkayToSay-2016-30) Miller said that [Jason Isbell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jason_Isbell "Jason Isbell") encouraged him to talk publicly about his sobriety.[\[18\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rhett_Miller#cite_note-RollingStone-Messenger-2018-18)

## Personal life

In 1997, Miller moved from Dallas to Los Angeles. In 2000, he moved to New York City. Miller and his then-fiancée, Erica Iahn, lived three blocks south of the [World Trade Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Trade_Center_(1973%E2%80%932001) "World Trade Center (1973–2001)") and were at home on [9/11](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/9/11 "9/11"). He shared journal writing about their experience, which was published in _[The Atlantic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Atlantic "The Atlantic")_ in September 2011.[\[31\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rhett_Miller#cite_note-Atlantic-AboutThatDay911-2011-31) Miller now lives in New York's [Hudson Valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hudson_Valley "Hudson Valley").[\[6\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rhett_Miller#cite_note-WTFMaron-Episode554-2014-6)

In 2002, Miller married former model Erica Iahn a week after he completed production of _The Instigator._ Iahn found out she was pregnant with their first child, Max, while Miller was on tour with [Tori Amos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tori_Amos "Tori Amos") to promote the album. Their daughter, Soleil, was born in the spring of 2006.[\[32\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rhett_Miller#cite_note-DailyNorthwestern-Believer-2006-32)[\[33\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rhett_Miller#cite_note-RollingStone-Believer-2005-33)

Miller said he got the nickname "Rhett" because his mother liked [Rhett Butler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rhett_Butler "Rhett Butler") from the movie _[Gone with the Wind](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gone_with_the_Wind_(film) "Gone with the Wind (film)")._[\[8\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rhett_Miller#cite_note-Billboard-TooFarToCare-1997-8)
