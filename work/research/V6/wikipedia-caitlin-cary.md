Musical artist

**Caitlin Cary** (born October 28, 1968) is an [alternative country](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alternative_country "Alternative country") musician and visual artist from [Seville, Ohio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seville,_Ohio "Seville, Ohio").

## Early life

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Caitlin_Cary&action=edit&section=1 "Edit section: Early life")\]

Caitlin Cary is the youngest of seven siblings (all older brothers). Her entire family was involved in music to some degree, with her parents' love for singing and her father's interest in building instruments.[\[1\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caitlin_Cary#cite_note-yeproc-1) She began to play the violin at age five, but put it aside as a teenager.[\[2\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caitlin_Cary#cite_note-popmattersbio-2) In addition to the violin, she also played her father's harpsichords, where she wrote some of her own songs.[\[1\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caitlin_Cary#cite_note-yeproc-1)

Cary went to college at the [College of Wooster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/College_of_Wooster "College of Wooster") in [Ohio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ohio "Ohio").[\[1\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caitlin_Cary#cite_note-yeproc-1) She began working on a degree in English. During her college time, she picked up playing the violin again, and she formed a small 'jokey' band called Garden Weasels. After graduating from the College of Wooster, she enrolled in the graduate program in creative writing  at [North Carolina State](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Carolina_State "North Carolina State").[\[2\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caitlin_Cary#cite_note-popmattersbio-2)

## Career

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Caitlin_Cary&action=edit&section=2 "Edit section: Career")\]

### Whiskeytown

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Caitlin_Cary&action=edit&section=3 "Edit section: Whiskeytown")\]

In 1993, musician [Ryan Adams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ryan_Adams "Ryan Adams") contacted Cary and asked her if she would play violin in a band that he was starting. Cary agreed, and they formed [Whiskeytown](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Whiskeytown "Whiskeytown").[\[2\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caitlin_Cary#cite_note-popmattersbio-2)

### Solo career

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Caitlin_Cary&action=edit&section=4 "Edit section: Solo career")\]

In 2000, Cary released her first solo EP _Waltzie_, produced by [Chris Stamey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chris_Stamey "Chris Stamey").[\[3\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caitlin_Cary#cite_note-CC-nodep-2000-3)

Cary's debut album _While You Weren't Looking_ was released in 2002 and featured Whiskeytown's [Mike Daly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mike_Daly "Mike Daly") (guitar), who co-wrote and played on most of the songs.[\[4\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caitlin_Cary#cite_note-CC-billboard-2002-4) Personnel also included Mike Santoro (bass), Skillet Gilmore (drums), and Jen Gunderman (keyboards). [Thad Cockrell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thad_Cockrell "Thad Cockrell"), Tonya Lamm (Hazeldine), and Lynn Blakey provided harmonies.[\[2\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caitlin_Cary#cite_note-popmattersbio-2)

2003's _I'm Staying Out_ featured guest appearances from [Mary Chapin Carpenter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mary_Chapin_Carpenter "Mary Chapin Carpenter"), [Mitch Easter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mitch_Easter "Mitch Easter"), [Don Dixon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Don_Dixon_(musician) "Don Dixon (musician)"), Greg Humphreys, [Audley Freed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Audley_Freed "Audley Freed"), and [Jane Scarpantoni](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jane_Scarpantoni "Jane Scarpantoni").[\[5\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caitlin_Cary#cite_note-CC-nodep-2003-5)

### Other projects

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Caitlin_Cary&action=edit&section=5 "Edit section: Other projects")\]

In 2005, Cary released an album of duets, _Begonias_, with [Thad Cockrell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thad_Cockrell "Thad Cockrell") with songs composed by the duo.[\[6\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caitlin_Cary#cite_note-6)

In 2013, Cary co-founded the [North Carolina Music Love Army](http://ncmusiclovearmy.org/) with [Jon Lindsay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jon_Lindsay_(musician) "Jon Lindsay (musician)"). The collective of North Carolina–based musicians created the _We Are Not For Sale: Songs of Protest_ LP to oppose the regressive actions of the [North Carolina General Assembly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Carolina_General_Assembly "North Carolina General Assembly"). The album was released worldwide via [Redeye](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Redeye_Distribution "Redeye Distribution") on November 26, 2013.[\[7\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caitlin_Cary#cite_note-CC-blueindian-2014-7)

In 2010, Caitlin performed with Matt Douglas (lead singer and songster for The Proclivities) in Raleigh's annual Love Hangover show, in which male/female duos sing love song covers.[\[8\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caitlin_Cary#cite_note-CC-slowcoustic-2011-8) They then formed the group Small Ponds, who released an EP on Last Chance Records in September 2010.[\[9\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caitlin_Cary#cite_note-CC-indyweek-2010-9)

### Visual arts

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Caitlin_Cary&action=edit&section=6 "Edit section: Visual arts")\]

Cary is an accomplished visual artist, creating fabric collages she calls "Needle Print." Examples of her work are prominently featured on her website.[\[10\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caitlin_Cary#cite_note-CC-artspace-2017-10)

## Personal life

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Caitlin_Cary&action=edit&section=7 "Edit section: Personal life")\]

Cary is married to drummer/artist Skillet Gilmore, and they live in South [Raleigh, North Carolina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Raleigh,_North_Carolina "Raleigh, North Carolina").[\[11\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caitlin_Cary#cite_note-CC-waltermag-2017-11)
