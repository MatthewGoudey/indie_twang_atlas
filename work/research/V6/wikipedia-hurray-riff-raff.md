**Hurray for the Riff Raff** is an American band formed in New Orleans in 2007 by Alynda Mariposa Segarra, a singer-songwriter from [the Bronx](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Bronx "The Bronx"), [New York](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_(state) "New York (state)").

As Segarra's project, the group originally performed different styles of [folk music](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Folk_music "Folk music") while releasing several albums [independently](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Independent_music "Independent music").

## Background

Alynda Mariposa Segarra (formerly known as Alynda Lee) was raised by their aunt and uncle in [the Bronx](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Bronx "The Bronx") where they developed an early appreciation for [doo-wop](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doo-wop "Doo-wop") and [Motown](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Motown "Motown"). They are of [Puerto Rican](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Puerto_Rican_people "Puerto Rican people") heritage.[\[1\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hurray_for_the_Riff_Raff#cite_note-OffBeat-2014-1)

Segarra became a regular attendee of [hardcore punk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hardcore_punk "Hardcore punk") shows at [ABC No Rio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ABC_No_Rio "ABC No Rio") when they were young.[\[2\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hurray_for_the_Riff_Raff#cite_note-eMusic-2010-2) They left their home in the Bronx at age 17, spending time crossing [North America](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_America "North America"), hopping [freight trains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Freight_trains "Freight trains").[\[2\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hurray_for_the_Riff_Raff#cite_note-eMusic-2010-2)

Around 2007, Segarra became a part of the [Dead Man Street Orchestra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dead_Man_Street_Orchestra "Dead Man Street Orchestra"), a hobo band _[Time](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Time_(magazine) "Time (magazine)")_ magazine documented in a 2007 photo essay.[\[3\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hurray_for_the_Riff_Raff#cite_note-Time-2007-3) Segarra traveled with the group for two years, releasing two independent albums and embarking on freight train tours across the country.

## Musical career

[![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/1/17/Hurray_for_the_Riff_Raff_-_Haldern_Pop_Festival_2017-12.jpg/960px-Hurray_for_the_Riff_Raff_-_Haldern_Pop_Festival_2017-12.jpg)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Hurray_for_the_Riff_Raff_-_Haldern_Pop_Festival_2017-12.jpg) Hurray for the Riff Raff at [Haldern Pop](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haldern_Pop "Haldern Pop") Festival 2017.

In 2008, Segarra self-released Hurray for the Riff Raff's first studio album, _It Don't Mean I Don't Love You_, followed by another self-released album, _Young Blood Blues_, in 2010, which was dedicated to the memory of [Marcella Grace Eiler.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marcella_Grace_Eiler "Marcella Grace Eiler")[\[4\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hurray_for_the_Riff_Raff#cite_note-4) In February 2011, Hurray for the Riff Raff were featured in an article in UK newspaper _[The Times](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Times "The Times")_ based around the [HBO](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HBO "HBO") TV series _[Treme](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treme_(TV_series) "Treme (TV series)")_, with their track "Daniella" having been included in the show's selection of New Orleans' essential songs.[\[5\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hurray_for_the_Riff_Raff#cite_note-LooseMusic-2011-5)

On March 21, 2011, Hurray for the Riff Raff released their self-titled CD on [Loose Music](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Loose_Music "Loose Music") in Europe, composed of Segarra's favorite songs from the band's two previous records. Tracks from this indie-label debut release by the band received airplay on [BBC Radio 2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BBC_Radio_2 "BBC Radio 2") and [BBC Radio 6 Music](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BBC_Radio_6_Music "BBC Radio 6 Music").

In May 2012, Hurray for the Riff Raff released _Look Out Mama_ on their own label, Born to Win Records, as well as on [Loose Music](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Loose_Music "Loose Music") in Europe. _[No Depression](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/No_Depression_(magazine) "No Depression (magazine)")_ said it "sounds like something The Band would’ve had playing on a Victorola while making _Music From Big Pink_ in Woodstock."[\[6\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hurray_for_the_Riff_Raff#cite_note-NoDep-2012-6) The album was recorded in [Nashville, Tennessee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nashville,_Tennessee "Nashville, Tennessee") at The Bomb Shelter Studios and produced by Andrija Tokic ( [Alabama Shakes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alabama_Shakes "Alabama Shakes")).[\[7\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hurray_for_the_Riff_Raff#cite_note-AmericanSongwriter-2012-7) The record features the single "End of the Line".

_My Dearest Darkest Neighbor_ was then released on July 1, 2013, through [Mod Mobilian Records](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mod_Mobilian "Mod Mobilian") and [This Is American Music](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/This_Is_American_Music "This Is American Music"). The album was previously available only as a [Kickstarter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kickstarter "Kickstarter") reward and in limited local release and featured handmade covers.[\[8\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hurray_for_the_Riff_Raff#cite_note-Kickstarter-LookOutMama-2012-8)[\[9\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hurray_for_the_Riff_Raff#cite_note-TimesPicayune-2013-9) Tracks on the record include songs by [Townes Van Zandt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Townes_Van_Zandt "Townes Van Zandt"), [Billie Holiday](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Billie_Holiday "Billie Holiday"), [Leadbelly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leadbelly "Leadbelly"), [John Lennon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Lennon "John Lennon"), [Lucinda Williams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lucinda_Williams "Lucinda Williams"), [Joni Mitchell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joni_Mitchell "Joni Mitchell"), [Hank Williams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hank_Williams "Hank Williams"), and [George Harrison](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Harrison "George Harrison"), and features Segarra's interpretation of [Gillian Welch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gillian_Welch "Gillian Welch")'s "Ruination Day".

_[The Wall Street Journal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Wall_Street_Journal "The Wall Street Journal")_ describes Segarra's singing thus: "She has a subtle, expressive voice that she wraps around songs that draw on the sounds and styles of the American South, and her lyrics often take unconventional tack on traditional subjects".[\[10\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hurray_for_the_Riff_Raff#cite_note-WSJ-2013-10)

_[Spin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spin_(magazine) "Spin (magazine)")_ previewed a video of the band's song "St. Roch Blues".[\[11\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hurray_for_the_Riff_Raff#cite_note-Spin-StRoch-2014-11)

In February 2014, Hurray for the Riff Raff had their [ATO Records](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ATO_Records "ATO Records") debut with _Small Town Heroes_.[\[12\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hurray_for_the_Riff_Raff#cite_note-ATO-signs-2013-12) The record featured “The Body Electric,” a song that NPR's Ann Powers called “The Political Song of the Year” in 2014 and says the album "sweeps across eras and genres with grace and grit."

Describing Segarra's music, [NPR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NPR "NPR") says "Segarra's morning-after alto might be the least showy great voice to hit the national scene this year."[\[13\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hurray_for_the_Riff_Raff#cite_note-NPR-NewPolFolk-2014-13)

In early July 2016, the band played the Danish festival of [Roskilde](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roskilde_Festival "Roskilde Festival") to an enthusiastic audience.[\[14\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hurray_for_the_Riff_Raff#cite_note-14) In December 2016, they announced a new record, _[The Navigator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Navigator_(Hurray_for_the_Riff_Raff_album) "The Navigator (Hurray for the Riff Raff album)")_, on [_Pitchfork_](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pitchfork_Media "Pitchfork Media") with the first single from the album, "Rican Beach." The album was released on March 10, 2017, on [ATO Records](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ATO_Records "ATO Records").[\[15\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hurray_for_the_Riff_Raff#cite_note-15)

In late 2018, Segarra joined organizers of the [Newport Folk Festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Newport_Folk_Festival "Newport Folk Festival") to travel to [Puerto Rico](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Puerto_Rico "Puerto Rico") and deliver instruments to various public schools.[\[16\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hurray_for_the_Riff_Raff#cite_note-16) During that visit, Segarra played their first concert at La Respuesta in [Santurce](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santurce,_San_Juan,_Puerto_Rico "Santurce, San Juan, Puerto Rico").[\[17\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hurray_for_the_Riff_Raff#cite_note-17)

In 2019, Hurray for the Riff Raff's "Pa'lante" won Best Music Video at the [SXSW Film Festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_by_Southwest "South by Southwest") Jury and Specials Awards.[\[18\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hurray_for_the_Riff_Raff#cite_note-18)

In 2021, [Nonesuch Records](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nonesuch_Records "Nonesuch Records") announced Hurray for the Riff Raff's debut album on the label. _[Life on Earth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Life_on_Earth_(Hurray_for_the_Riff_Raff_album) "Life on Earth (Hurray for the Riff Raff album)")_ was released on February 18, 2022. Its eleven new “nature punk” tracks on the theme of survival are "music for a world in flux—songs about thriving, not just surviving, while disaster is happening."[\[19\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hurray_for_the_Riff_Raff#cite_note-19) Leading up to its release, _[Stereogum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stereogum "Stereogum")_ made it their album of the week, noting that the album's "sonic backdrop is fluid yet unmistakable no matter what form it takes. Even at their most synthetic, these tracks come across as earthy and alive—an extension of their environment. Even at their smallest and quietest, they feel huge."[\[20\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hurray_for_the_Riff_Raff#cite_note-20) The album was met with critical acclaim upon its release.[\[21\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hurray_for_the_Riff_Raff#cite_note-21)

Hurray for the Riff Raff released their eighth studio album _[The Past Is Still Alive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Past_Is_Still_Alive "The Past Is Still Alive")_ on February 23, 2024.[\[22\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hurray_for_the_Riff_Raff#cite_note-22) The _[Associated Press](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Associated_Press "Associated Press")_ named the album to its list of the best work from 2024, calling it a "beautiful portrayal of the U.S." and noting that Segarra pulled from their background as a [hitchhiker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hitchhiking "Hitchhiking"), train hopper and outsider artist to document adventures "that can only happen far from home".[\[23\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hurray_for_the_Riff_Raff#cite_note-23)

## Personal life

Segarra lives in Chicago,[\[24\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hurray_for_the_Riff_Raff#cite_note-24) is [non-binary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Non-binary "Non-binary"),[\[25\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hurray_for_the_Riff_Raff#cite_note-25) and uses they/them pronouns.[\[26\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hurray_for_the_Riff_Raff#cite_note-26)
