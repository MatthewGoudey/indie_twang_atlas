Musical artist

**Catherine Tift Merritt** (born January 8, 1975) is an American singer-songwriter and musician. She has released seven studio albums and was nominated for a Grammy Award for Best Country Album for _[Tambourine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tambourine_(album) "Tambourine (album)")_. Merritt is a board member of the Artist Rights Alliance and is a Practitioner-in-Residence at the [Franklin Humanities Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Franklin_Humanities_Institute "Franklin Humanities Institute") at [Duke University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duke_University "Duke University").[\[3\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tift_Merritt#cite_note-3)[\[4\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tift_Merritt#cite_note-4)

## Early life and education

Merritt was born in Houston and grew up in [Raleigh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Raleigh,_North_Carolina "Raleigh, North Carolina").[\[2\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tift_Merritt#cite_note-DemingAllMusic-2) She credits her father's eclectic taste in music as a major influence.[\[5\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tift_Merritt#cite_note-5) At the age of 20, Merritt enrolled at the [University of North Carolina at Chapel Hill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_North_Carolina_at_Chapel_Hill "University of North Carolina at Chapel Hill") to study creative writing, where she studied under [Doris Betts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doris_Betts "Doris Betts").[\[6\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tift_Merritt#cite_note-6)[\[7\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tift_Merritt#cite_note-IndyWeek2025-7)

## Career

Merritt joined a band called the Carbines and played small clubs in the cities of Chapel Hill and Raleigh.[\[8\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tift_Merritt#cite_note-8) In 1998, the band released a 7-inch single, "Jukejoint Girl," and in 1999 the album _[The Two Dollar Pistols with Tift Merritt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Two_Dollar_Pistols_with_Tift_Merritt "The Two Dollar Pistols with Tift Merritt")_ on [Yep Roc Records](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yep_Roc_Records "Yep Roc Records").[\[9\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tift_Merritt#cite_note-9)

In 2000, Merritt won the [MerleFest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MerleFest "MerleFest")'s Chris Austin Songwriting Contest[\[10\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tift_Merritt#cite_note-10) and in 2002 released her debut album, _[Bramble Rose](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bramble_Rose "Bramble Rose")_. The record landed on the top ten lists for both _[Time](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Time_(magazine) "Time (magazine)")_ and _[The New Yorker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_New_Yorker "The New Yorker")_, and was called the best debut of the year by the _[Associated Press](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Associated_Press "Associated Press")_.[\[11\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tift_Merritt#cite_note-11) While touring to promote _Bramble Rose,_ Merritt opened for fellow North Carolinian [Ryan Adams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ryan_Adams "Ryan Adams"), who had helped her secure her first management and record contracts.[\[12\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tift_Merritt#cite_note-12)

Her follow-up release, 2004's _[Tambourine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tambourine_(album) "Tambourine (album)")_, was produced by [George Drakoulias](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Drakoulias "George Drakoulias") and featured backing by [Benmont Tench](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Benmont_Tench "Benmont Tench"), [Mike Campbell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mike_Campbell_(musician) "Mike Campbell (musician)"), [Neal Casal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neal_Casal "Neal Casal") and [Don Heffington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Don_Heffington "Don Heffington").[\[13\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tift_Merritt#cite_note-13) The album was nominated for the Best Country Album [Grammy Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grammy_Award "Grammy Award") in 2004.[\[14\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tift_Merritt#cite_note-14) She was nominated for [Americana Music Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Americana_Music_Association "Americana Music Association"): Album of the Year, Artist of the Year, and Song of the Year in 2005[\[15\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tift_Merritt#cite_note-15) Merritt's performance on _[Austin City Limits](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austin_City_Limits "Austin City Limits")_ was released as a DVD on [New West Records](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_West_Records "New West Records").[\[16\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tift_Merritt#cite_note-16) A sold-out concert at the North Carolina Museum of Art in Raleigh, North Carolina, was released under the title _Home Is Loud_ that same year.[\[17\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tift_Merritt#cite_note-17)

Her next album, _[Another Country](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Another_Country_(Tift_Merritt_album) "Another Country (Tift Merritt album)")_ was released on [Fantasy Records](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fantasy_Records "Fantasy Records") in 2008. The record was produced by [George Drakoulias](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Drakoulias "George Drakoulias") and featured guitarists [Charlie Sexton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charlie_Sexton "Charlie Sexton") and [Doug Pettibone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doug_Pettibone "Doug Pettibone").[\[18\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tift_Merritt#cite_note-18) Despite her Grammy nomination, Lost Highway Records dropped Merritt after _[Tambourine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tambourine_(album) "Tambourine (album)")_ and Merritt felt "clueless by the decision and tapped of her energy."[\[19\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tift_Merritt#cite_note-19) Merritt wrote the new album in a Paris apartment, "a city where she knew all of two people and didn't speak the language at all."[\[20\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tift_Merritt#cite_note-20)[\[21\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tift_Merritt#cite_note-21)[\[22\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tift_Merritt#cite_note-22) _[Paste](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paste_(magazine) "Paste (magazine)")_ magazine gave the album a four-star review.[\[23\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tift_Merritt#cite_note-23) The song "Broken" was nominated for an Americana Music Award for Song of the Year.[\[24\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tift_Merritt#cite_note-24)[\[25\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tift_Merritt#cite_note-Merritt_EFMF_2009-25)[\[26\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tift_Merritt#cite_note-26)

Her EP, _Please Break the Silence of the Middle of the Night,_ was released on January 1, 2008, by the Concord Music Group as an iTunes exclusive.[\[27\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tift_Merritt#cite_note-27)
While touring England, Merritt played a piano at the University of Buckingham's Radcliffe Centre for music and lectures, a building that had formerly been a church. "She thought the amazing acoustics and inspiring ambiance would make an ideal setting for a live acoustic-solo record."[\[28\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tift_Merritt#cite_note-ConcordBuck-28)[\[29\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tift_Merritt#cite_note-29) The record _Buckingham Solo_ was recorded live on November 29, 2008 and released on Fantasy Records on April 18, 2009.[\[28\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tift_Merritt#cite_note-ConcordBuck-28)[\[30\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tift_Merritt#cite_note-30)

[![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/3/35/Tift_Merritt_cs.jpg/500px-Tift_Merritt_cs.jpg)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Tift_Merritt_cs.jpg) Merritt on stage in Seattle in 2010

Merritt's album, _See You on the Moon_, was released in June 2010 on [Fantasy Records](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fantasy_Records "Fantasy Records").[\[31\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tift_Merritt#cite_note-31)[\[32\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tift_Merritt#cite_note-32) Her next album, _Traveling Alone_, was released on [Yep Roc Records](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yep_Roc_Records "Yep Roc Records") in October 2012.[\[33\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tift_Merritt#cite_note-33) The album was produced by [Tucker Martine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tucker_Martine "Tucker Martine") and featured [Marc Ribot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marc_Ribot "Marc Ribot") on guitar and [Calexico's](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Calexico_(band) "Calexico (band)") John Convertino on percussion.[\[34\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tift_Merritt#cite_note-YepRocRecords2022-34) Merritt announced that _Traveling Alone_ would be re-released in a 10th Anniversary Edition on November 4, 2022.[\[34\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tift_Merritt#cite_note-YepRocRecords2022-34)

In 2013, Merritt collaborated with [Simone Dinnerstein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simone_Dinnerstein "Simone Dinnerstein") on an album that bridged both of their musical interests called _Night_. The track listing includes traditional American songs like _[The Wayfaring Stranger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Wayfaring_Stranger_(song) "The Wayfaring Stranger (song)")_, a cover of [Johnny Nash](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johnny_Nash "Johnny Nash"), as well as classical music like "Dido's Lament" from [Henry Purcell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_Purcell "Henry Purcell")'s opera _[Dido and Aeneas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dido_and_Aeneas "Dido and Aeneas")_.[\[35\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tift_Merritt#cite_note-35)

The Grammy Museum hosted Merritt as part of their Spotlight series in 2013. Tift participated in a Q&A with the audience as well as performing for the audience.[\[36\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tift_Merritt#cite_note-36)[\[37\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tift_Merritt#cite_note-37)

In 2013, Merritt released _Traveling Companion_ on Yep Roc. Her album _Stitch of the World_ came out in 2017.

Meritt created a new site in 2023 where she has shared interviews with other musicians on The Spark—originally an interview program she hosted for [Marfa Public Radio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marfa_Public_Radio "Marfa Public Radio")—including [Rosanne Cash](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rosanne_Cash "Rosanne Cash") and [Rhiannon Giddens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rhiannon_Giddens "Rhiannon Giddens").[\[38\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tift_Merritt#cite_note-38)[\[39\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tift_Merritt#cite_note-39) In her Nightcap series, she has posted some "project threads" including covers of _Black Boys on Mopeds_ by [Sinead O'Connor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sin%C3%A9ad_O'Connor "Sinéad O'Connor") and _[9 to 5](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/9_to_5_(Dolly_Parton_song) "9 to 5 (Dolly Parton song)")_ by [Dolly Parton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dolly_Parton "Dolly Parton"): "Long before a project turns forward facing, beta tests, questions, failed drafts, surprise pleasures, writing, music and laughter line the walls."[\[40\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tift_Merritt#cite_note-40)[\[41\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tift_Merritt#cite_note-SubstackAbout-41)

In 2025, Merritt marked the 20th anniversary of her Grammy-nominated album _[Tambourine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tambourine_(album) "Tambourine (album)")_ with the reissue of the original record as well as a companion collection of home demos and unreleased material, _Time and Patience: Tambourine Kitchen Recordings_ on One Riot Records.[\[42\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tift_Merritt#cite_note-EntertainmentFocus2025-42)[\[43\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tift_Merritt#cite_note-Grammys-43)
Merritt describes the "kitchen recordings" this way: "These songs weren’t hits. I was a good writer, not a great one. My band wasn’t good enough. At least that’s what I was told.... But I believed in these demos back then—and it is a tender moment to bring them to you now, proudly, so much time and patience later."[\[42\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tift_Merritt#cite_note-EntertainmentFocus2025-42) The reissue is dedicated to [Neal Casal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neal_Casal "Neal Casal") and [Don Heffington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Don_Heffington "Don Heffington"), "cherished members of Merritt's original studio band."[\[42\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tift_Merritt#cite_note-EntertainmentFocus2025-42)

Although Merritt has indicated that "I rarely tour these days and certainly not for long stretches of time", she appeared at the 2025 [Glastonbury Festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glastonbury_Festival "Glastonbury Festival") and is on the schedule for the 2025 AmericanaFest.[\[41\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tift_Merritt#cite_note-SubstackAbout-41)[\[44\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tift_Merritt#cite_note-44)[\[45\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tift_Merritt#cite_note-45)

Merritt has been the opening act for [Joan Baez](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joan_Baez "Joan Baez"),[\[46\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tift_Merritt#cite_note-46) [Kris Kristofferson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kris_Kristofferson "Kris Kristofferson"),[\[47\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tift_Merritt#cite_note-47) and sang " [The Star-Spangled Banner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Star-Spangled_Banner "The Star-Spangled Banner")" for then-Senator [Barack Obama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barack_Obama "Barack Obama") at his last campaign rally.[\[48\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tift_Merritt#cite_note-48)

## Reception

Merritt's sound has been described as "sonic short stories and poignant performances."[\[49\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tift_Merritt#cite_note-49) She has been compared to artists like [Joni Mitchell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joni_Mitchell "Joni Mitchell")[\[50\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tift_Merritt#cite_note-Jurgensen-50) and [Emmylou Harris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emmylou_Harris "Emmylou Harris").[\[51\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tift_Merritt#cite_note-newyorker.com-51)

A review in _The New Yorker_ praised her[\[51\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tift_Merritt#cite_note-newyorker.com-51) and _[The Wall Street Journal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Wall_Street_Journal "The Wall Street Journal")_ included her in a weekend feature on singer-songwriters, describing her as "in the tradition of [Joni Mitchell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joni_Mitchell "Joni Mitchell"), [James Taylor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Taylor "James Taylor") and [Leonard Cohen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leonard_Cohen "Leonard Cohen")".[\[50\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tift_Merritt#cite_note-Jurgensen-50)

## Personal life

In 2009, Merritt married Zeke Hutchins.[\[52\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tift_Merritt#cite_note-52) They separated in late 2013.[\[53\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tift_Merritt#cite_note-53) In 2016, she and Eric Heywood had a daughter named Jean.[\[54\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tift_Merritt#cite_note-54)
