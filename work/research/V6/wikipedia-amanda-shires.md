Musical artist

**Amanda Rose Shires** (born March 5, 1982) is an American [singer-songwriter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Singer-songwriter "Singer-songwriter") and [fiddle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fiddle "Fiddle") player. Shires has released nine solo albums starting in 2005, her most recent being _[Nobody's Girl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobody's_Girl_(album) "Nobody's Girl (album)")_ in 2025. In 2019, she founded a country music supergroup called [The Highwomen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Highwomen "The Highwomen") alongside [Brandi Carlile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brandi_Carlile "Brandi Carlile"), [Maren Morris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maren_Morris "Maren Morris") and [Natalie Hemby](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natalie_Hemby "Natalie Hemby") and has also performed as a member of the [Texas Playboys](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bob_Wills "Bob Wills"), [Thrift Store Cowboys](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thrift_Store_Cowboys "Thrift Store Cowboys"), and [Jason Isbell & the 400 Unit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jason_Isbell_&_the_400_Unit "Jason Isbell & the 400 Unit"), as well as in a duo with [Rod Picott](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rod_Picott "Rod Picott"). Along with Jason Isbell & the 400 Unit, Shires won the [Grammy Award for Best Americana Album](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grammy_for_Best_Americana_Album "Grammy for Best Americana Album") for their 2017 album _[The Nashville Sound](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Nashville_Sound "The Nashville Sound")_.[\[3\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amanda_Shires#cite_note-GRAMMY-2020-3)

## Early life

Following her parents' divorce, Shires' childhood was divided between the Texas cities of [Lubbock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lubbock,_Texas "Lubbock, Texas") and [Mineral Wells](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mineral_Wells,_Texas "Mineral Wells, Texas").[\[4\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amanda_Shires#cite_note-Chron-4) Her mother is a retired nurse. She also used to be a [barrel racer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barrel_racing "Barrel racing") at [rodeos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rodeo "Rodeo").[\[5\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amanda_Shires#cite_note-prodigy-5) Her father owns a [wholesale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wholesaling "Wholesaling") [nursery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plant_nursery "Plant nursery") in Mineral Wells and has a hobby of [gold prospecting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gold_prospecting "Gold prospecting") in [Alaska](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alaska "Alaska").[\[5\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amanda_Shires#cite_note-prodigy-5) She is distantly related to noted photographer Erica Shires, who produced the video for the song "Swimmer".[\[6\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amanda_Shires#cite_note-6)

At the age of 10, she happened to be with her father while he was shopping for hunting gear at a [pawn shop](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pawn_shop "Pawn shop") in Mineral Wells.[\[5\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amanda_Shires#cite_note-prodigy-5)[\[7\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amanda_Shires#cite_note-wilson-7) She saw an inexpensive [fiddle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fiddle "Fiddle") for sale and her father agreed to buy it for her on the condition that she learn to play it.[\[5\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amanda_Shires#cite_note-prodigy-5)[\[7\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amanda_Shires#cite_note-wilson-7)[\[8\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amanda_Shires#cite_note-CST-8) It took a while for her to play it well; she usually practiced outside because her dog had a habit of howling while she played.[\[9\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amanda_Shires#cite_note-9) When she was 12, she started taking fiddle lessons in Lubbock from Lanny Fiel, who knew Frankie McWhorter from the [Texas Playboys](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Texas_Playboys "Texas Playboys").[\[10\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amanda_Shires#cite_note-half-10) She then started learning fiddle from McWhorter and was later invited to play at the band's shows.[\[10\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amanda_Shires#cite_note-half-10)[\[11\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amanda_Shires#cite_note-sunday_cbs-11) At the age of 15, she joined the [Texas Playboys](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Texas_Playboys "Texas Playboys"), the former backing band for [Western swing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_swing "Western swing") legend [Bob Wills](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bob_Wills "Bob Wills").[\[12\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amanda_Shires#cite_note-Tex-12)[\[13\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amanda_Shires#cite_note-npr_gross-13)

## Musical career

[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/9/99/Amanda_Shires_2016.jpg/500px-Amanda_Shires_2016.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Amanda_Shires_2016.jpg) Shires performing in 2016

Shires continued to pursue full time work as a touring side musician in the early 2000s, gaining experience backing veterans of the Texas [outlaw country](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Outlaw_country "Outlaw country") circuit. She played fiddle on tour with [Billy Joe Shaver](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Billy_Joe_Shaver "Billy Joe Shaver") and later [Todd Snider](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Todd_Snider "Todd Snider"), both highly respected singer-songwriters. Shaver encouraged Shires to record her fiddle playing and singing in order to provide a CD sample for sale.[\[14\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amanda_Shires#cite_note-tm_weiner-14)[\[15\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amanda_Shires#cite_note-lonestar-15)

In 2005 Shires released a public [demo album](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Demo_(music) "Demo (music)"), the mostly instrumental _[Being Brave](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Being_Brave "Being Brave")_.[\[14\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amanda_Shires#cite_note-tm_weiner-14)[\[15\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amanda_Shires#cite_note-lonestar-15)[\[16\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amanda_Shires#cite_note-oxford_alford-16) She described the album as a type of résumé offered to show attendees: "When we toured it was expected that the fiddle players would have their own stuff to sell; which is why I recorded _Being Brave_, which isn’t really a ‘debut’ album, but more of a showcase for my playing and occasional singing."[\[17\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amanda_Shires#cite_note-magpie_2014-17)

Encouraged by positive support from the songwriting veterans she was playing fiddle for on Texas tours, Shires moved to Nashville to pursue a solo career in 2008.[\[18\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amanda_Shires#cite_note-lsm_skanse-18) The next year, she released _[West Cross Timbers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Cross_Timbers "West Cross Timbers")_ as her debut album as a solo singer-songwriter.

Shires had toured had extensively with Rod Picott from 2006.[\[19\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amanda_Shires#cite_note-Tenn-19) In 2009 she and Picott released the collaborative album _Sew Your Heart with Wires_. Since that time, Shires has also performed and recorded with [Jason Isbell & the 400 Unit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jason_Isbell_&_the_400_Unit "Jason Isbell & the 400 Unit"), [Devotchka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Devotchka "Devotchka"), [Chris Isaak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chris_Isaak "Chris Isaak"), [Thrift Store Cowboys](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thrift_Store_Cowboys "Thrift Store Cowboys"), [Blackberry Smoke](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blackberry_Smoke "Blackberry Smoke"), and [Justin Townes Earle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Justin_Townes_Earle "Justin Townes Earle").[\[20\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amanda_Shires#cite_note-NJ-20)

In 2011, she appeared in the movie _[Country Strong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Country_Strong "Country Strong")_ as one of the musicians backing singer Kelly Canter, played by [Gwyneth Paltrow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gwyneth_Paltrow "Gwyneth Paltrow").[\[21\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amanda_Shires#cite_note-spin-21) During 2011, Shires began attending [Sewanee: The University of the South](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sewanee:_The_University_of_the_South "Sewanee: The University of the South"), a small liberal arts university in [Sewanee, Tennessee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sewanee,_Tennessee "Sewanee, Tennessee").[\[22\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amanda_Shires#cite_note-22) She graduated with a [Master of Fine Arts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Master_of_Fine_Arts "Master of Fine Arts") degree in [poetry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poetry "Poetry") in 2017.[\[23\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amanda_Shires#cite_note-23)[\[24\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amanda_Shires#cite_note-24)[\[25\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amanda_Shires#cite_note-25)

During a break from classes at Sewanee, in July 2011 following the release of her third album _[Carrying Lightning](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carrying_Lightning "Carrying Lightning")_, Shires suffered a severe injury to her left ring finger while using a rope swing jumping into Saint Andrews Lake near the campus.[\[26\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amanda_Shires#cite_note-nd_bialas-26) The injury caused loss of feeling and muscle strength that nearly ended Shires’ fiddle playing career. Saying she drew inspiration from Billy Joe Shaver, who was missing two fingers, Shires eventually relearned her violin fingering through two years of rehabilitation.[\[26\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amanda_Shires#cite_note-nd_bialas-26)[\[27\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amanda_Shires#cite_note-sbt_conway-27)[\[28\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amanda_Shires#cite_note-dailytimes_sw-28)

On the strength of _Carrying Lightning_, Shires was named Artist of the Year 2011 by _[Texas Music](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Texas_Music "Texas Music")_ magazine,[\[18\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amanda_Shires#cite_note-lsm_skanse-18) appearing on the publication's Winter 2012 cover.[\[29\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amanda_Shires#cite_note-txmusic2012-29) _[Down Fell the Doves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Down_Fell_the_Doves "Down Fell the Doves")_ followed _Carrying Lightning_ in August 2013, and _[My Piece of Land](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/My_Piece_of_Land "My Piece of Land")_ was released in September 2016. The latter proved to be Shires' breakthrough, gaining her a nomination from the [Americana Music Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Americana_Music_Association "Americana Music Association").

Shires won the Emerging Artist of the Year Award at the 2017 [Americana Music Honors & Awards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Americana_Music_Honors_&_Awards "Americana Music Honors & Awards"). That year, she supported [John Prine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Prine "John Prine") on his worldwide tour alongside Isbell for select dates. Shires announced that she was working on a new album, _[To The Sunset](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/To_The_Sunset "To The Sunset")_, which was due for release in August 2018[\[30\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amanda_Shires#cite_note-30) and which, like _My Piece of Land_, would be produced by [Dave Cobb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dave_Cobb "Dave Cobb"). Isbell revealed on Twitter that [Gillian Welch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gillian_Welch "Gillian Welch") and [David Rawlings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Rawlings "David Rawlings") will make a guest appearance.[\[31\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amanda_Shires#cite_note-31) As a member of the 400 Unit, Shires won the [Grammy Award for Best Americana Album](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grammy_Award_for_Best_Americana_Album "Grammy Award for Best Americana Album") for _[The Nashville Sound](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Nashville_Sound "The Nashville Sound")_. Shires contributed to _[The Tree of Forgiveness](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Tree_of_Forgiveness "The Tree of Forgiveness")_, the 2018 album by [John Prine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Prine "John Prine").[\[32\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amanda_Shires#cite_note-32) _To The Sunset_ was released on August 3, 2018.

After researching the lack of airplay women artists received on American country radio stations, which an academic paper found had dropped to less than twenty percent of songs played by 2018,[\[33\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amanda_Shires#cite_note-songdata_pdf-33)[\[34\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amanda_Shires#cite_note-tandf_watson-34) Shires formed the country supergroup [The Highwomen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Highwomen "The Highwomen"), along with [Brandi Carlile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brandi_Carlile "Brandi Carlile"), [Natalie Hemby](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natalie_Hemby "Natalie Hemby"), and [Maren Morris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maren_Morris "Maren Morris").[\[35\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amanda_Shires#cite_note-enews_morris-35)[\[36\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amanda_Shires#cite_note-NYTimes-Feature-2019-36) "Redesigning Women" was the first single from their [self-titled debut album](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Highwomen_(album) "The Highwomen (album)") released on September 6, 2019.[\[37\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amanda_Shires#cite_note-37) Following her 2021 holiday album _[For Christmas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/For_Christmas "For Christmas")_, Shires released her eighth solo album _[Take It Like a Man](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Take_It_Like_a_Man_(Amanda_Shires_album) "Take It Like a Man (Amanda Shires album)")_ on July 26, 2022.

Shires announced her ninth album, _[Nobody's Girl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobody's_Girl_(album) "Nobody's Girl (album)")_ on July 18, 2025 alongside the release of its lead single "A Way It Goes". It was recorded at [Sound Emporium Studios](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sound_Emporium_Studios "Sound Emporium Studios"), produced by [Lawrence Rothman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lawrence_Rothman "Lawrence Rothman"), and released on September 26, 2025.[\[38\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amanda_Shires#cite_note-38)

In the summer of 2026, Shires opened the Sweet Judy Blue Eyes: Farewell Tour show at [Tanglewood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tanglewood "Tanglewood"). Appearing along with Shires to sing for [Judy Collins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Judy_Collins "Judy Collins") during her farewell performance at the venue were [Madeleine Peyroux](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Madeleine_Peyroux "Madeleine Peyroux"), [Mary Chapin Carpenter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mary_Chapin_Carpenter "Mary Chapin Carpenter") and [Rosanne Cash](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rosanne_Cash "Rosanne Cash").[\[39\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amanda_Shires#cite_note-bso_2026-39)[\[40\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amanda_Shires#cite_note-collins_fb-40)

## Musical style

David Menconi of the magazine _[Spin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spin_(magazine) "Spin (magazine)")_ said that Shires "sings \[...\] like an earthbound [Emmylou Harris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emmylou_Harris "Emmylou Harris")",[\[21\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amanda_Shires#cite_note-spin-21) and her vocals have been likened to those of [Dolly Parton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dolly_Parton "Dolly Parton").[\[41\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amanda_Shires#cite_note-41)[\[42\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amanda_Shires#cite_note-WSJ-42) Reviewers have variously described a "catch" or "quaver",[\[43\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amanda_Shires#cite_note-kilkenny_2012-43)[\[44\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amanda_Shires#cite_note-paste_danton-44) musically called [vibrato](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vibrato "Vibrato"), in Shire's voice that allows her to express vulnerability or apprehension, or as Jewly Hight writing for [NPR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NPR "NPR") explained, acts "as a subtle emotive tool or punctuation in her storytelling, (which) feels like an electrical charge shuddering through the live wire of her voice".[\[45\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amanda_Shires#cite_note-npr_hight-45) The imagery in her songwriting has been compared to that of [Tom Waits](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tom_Waits "Tom Waits").[\[4\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amanda_Shires#cite_note-Chron-4)[\[42\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amanda_Shires#cite_note-WSJ-42) Although her principal instrument is the fiddle, she also plays the [ukulele](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukulele "Ukulele").[\[42\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amanda_Shires#cite_note-WSJ-42)

## Personal life

Shires began dating fellow musician [Jason Isbell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jason_Isbell "Jason Isbell") in 2011. The couple married on February 23, 2013[\[46\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amanda_Shires#cite_note-46) and had their first child[\[47\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amanda_Shires#cite_note-47) on September 1, 2015.[\[48\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amanda_Shires#cite_note-DemChronicle-TourPreview-2015-48)[\[49\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amanda_Shires#cite_note-Instagram-Baby-2015-49) She was previously in a relationship with singer [Rod Picott](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rod_Picott "Rod Picott").[\[50\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amanda_Shires#cite_note-50) In December 2023, Isbell filed for divorce from Shires.[\[51\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amanda_Shires#cite_note-51) Their divorce was finalized in March 2025.[\[52\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amanda_Shires#cite_note-52)
