**Old 97's** is an American [rock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rock_music "Rock music") band from [Dallas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dallas,_Texas "Dallas, Texas"). Formed in 1992,[\[6\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_97%27s#cite_note-bio-6) they have released thirteen studio albums, two full [extended plays](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Extended_play "Extended play"), shared split duty on another EP, and they have a live album. Their most recent release is titled _American Primitive_.

Old 97's were pioneers of the [alt-country](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alt-country "Alt-country") movement during the mid-to-late 1990s, then they developed more of a [power pop](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Power_pop "Power pop") style in the 2000s.[\[4\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_97%27s#cite_note-Keyes-4) Lead vocalist and primary songwriter [Rhett Miller](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rhett_Miller "Rhett Miller") has said the band's style is "loud [folk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Folk_music "Folk music")".[\[7\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_97%27s#cite_note-7) The band's name is in reference to the [Wreck of the Old 97](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wreck_of_the_Old_97 "Wreck of the Old 97").[\[8\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_97%27s#cite_note-8)

## Career

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Old_97%27s&action=edit&section=1 "Edit section: Career")\]

Initially a popular Dallas bar band, Old 97's toured the country after releasing their first album, 1994's _[Hitchhike to Rhome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hitchhike_to_Rhome "Hitchhike to Rhome")_ and 1995's split EP release _Stoned / Garage Sale_ with fellow Dallas band Funland on [Idol Records](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Idol_Records "Idol Records"). In [Chicago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chicago "Chicago"), they caught the attention of [Bloodshot Records](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bloodshot_Records "Bloodshot Records") and were signed to record their next album. 1995's _[Wreck Your Life](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wreck_Your_Life "Wreck Your Life")_ brought them to the attention of [Elektra Records](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elektra_Records "Elektra Records"),[\[6\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_97%27s#cite_note-bio-6) who hoped that alt-country could be a new post- [grunge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grunge "Grunge") trend. The band's lead singer [Rhett Miller](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rhett_Miller "Rhett Miller"), was born in Austin and has lived in [New York City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_City "New York City") and [Los Angeles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Los_Angeles "Los Angeles"). Many of the band's songs include local [Texas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Texas "Texas") references, with references to New York City, Los Angeles, and Chicago appearing in the lyrics as well.

Most songs are written and sung by Miller, with bassist Murry Hammond performing the vocals on one or two tracks per album. Hammond also handled the singing on a number of country covers, especially in the band's earlier years. Hammond also performs gospel at his local church. The band's 2004 album _[Drag It Up](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drag_It_Up "Drag It Up")_ includes a song written and sung by guitarist Ken Bethea, "Coahuila." In 2005, _[Blender](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blender_(magazine) "Blender (magazine)")_ magazine ranked the band's then most successful single, 1999's "Murder (Or a Heart Attack)", as the 176th greatest song "since you were born."[\[9\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_97%27s#cite_note-9)

Their music has been featured in a number of films and TV series including _[The Break-Up](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Break-Up "The Break-Up")_ ("Salome", "Melt Show", "Timebomb"), _[Clay Pigeons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clay_Pigeons "Clay Pigeons")_ ("Timebomb"), _[Slither](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slither_(2006_film) "Slither (2006 film)")_ ("The New Kid"), _[Ned's Declassified School Survival Guide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ned's_Declassified_School_Survival_Guide "Ned's Declassified School Survival Guide")_ ("Big Brown Eyes"), _[Ed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ed_(TV_series) "Ed (TV series)")_ ("Question", "King of All the World"), _[Scrubs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scrubs_(2001_TV_series) "Scrubs (2001 TV series)")_ ("Question"), _[Veronica Mars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Veronica_Mars "Veronica Mars")_ ("Adelaide", "Four-Leaf Clover", "The New Kid") and _[Scorpion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scorpion_(TV_series) "Scorpion (TV series)")_ ("Question").[\[10\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_97%27s#cite_note-10) With respect to _The Break-Up_, a key scene toward the end of the film takes place at an Old 97's concert. In the season 2 episode "Tango and Smash", of the series _[FUBAR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FUBAR_(TV_series) "FUBAR (TV series)")_, the Old 97's appear as a random unnamed house band at a fancy restaurant in Tallinn who are asked if they might know the oldie "Dance With Me"–though they are only able to closely replicate the Old 97's (effectively playing a remix of their own song) for a lengthy dance scene between two of the series leads.[\[11\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_97%27s#cite_note-11)

Rhett Miller has recorded seven solo studio albums and a live album, with 2002's _[The Instigator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Instigator "The Instigator")_ receiving substantial airplay on [alternative](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alternative_rock "Alternative rock")-oriented radio stations. He toured with [Neil Finn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neil_Finn "Neil Finn") in 2004. Miller's second major commercial album, _[The Believer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Believer_(Rhett_Miller_album) "The Believer (Rhett Miller album)")_, was released in February 2006.[\[12\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_97%27s#cite_note-12) Murry Hammond's solo album, _I Don't Know Where I'm Going But I'm on My Way_, was released April 21, 2008. Old 97's have been heralded as a great live band, although they claim never to rehearse their act.[\[13\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_97%27s#cite_note-13) They included five live songs as a bonus disc on _Satellite Rides_, and released a live double CD set recorded at historic [Gruene Hall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gruene_Hall "Gruene Hall") in 2005, _[Alive & Wired](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alive_&_Wired "Alive & Wired")_.

[Rhino Entertainment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rhino_Entertainment "Rhino Entertainment") released _[Hit by a Train: The Best of Old 97's](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hit_by_a_Train:_The_Best_of_Old_97's "Hit by a Train: The Best of Old 97's")_, an 18-track compilation of songs from the band's beginnings through 2001, featuring liner notes and an essay by rock critic [Robert Christgau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Christgau "Robert Christgau"). Satellite Riders is a faux "tribute band"—it is actually an alias the band performs under when contractual obligations prevent them from using the Old 97's name.[\[14\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_97%27s#cite_note-14) The Ranchero Brothers is the name used when only Rhett and Murry Hammond perform. The recording of "Timebomb" from the live album _[Alive & Wired](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alive_&_Wired "Alive & Wired")_ was made available for download along with other alt-country songs for the music video games _[Rock Band](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rock_Band "Rock Band")_ and _[Rock Band 2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rock_Band_2 "Rock Band 2")_.[\[15\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_97%27s#cite_note-15)

Their next studio project, _The Grand Theatre_, was released in two volumes. The first, _[The Grand Theatre, Volume One](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Grand_Theatre,_Volume_One "The Grand Theatre, Volume One")_, was released on October 12, 2010.[\[16\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_97%27s#cite_note-16) The second, _[The Grand Theatre, Volume Two](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Grand_Theatre,_Volume_Two "The Grand Theatre, Volume Two")_, was released on July 5, 2011.[\[17\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_97%27s#cite_note-17) In 2013, the band released an EP containing two tracks with vocals by [Waylon Jennings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Waylon_Jennings "Waylon Jennings") entitled _[Old 97's & Waylon Jennings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_97's_&_Waylon_Jennings "Old 97's & Waylon Jennings")_. Rhett Miller called the previously unreleased tunes with Jennings "the band's 'holy grail.'"[\[18\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_97%27s#cite_note-18) The band's tenth studio album, _[Most Messed Up](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Most_Messed_Up "Most Messed Up")_, was released April 29, 2014, by [ATO Records](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ATO_Records "ATO Records") and was their best-selling album to date, reaching No. 30 on the Billboard 200.[\[19\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_97%27s#cite_note-19)

Their next two releases on ATO Records were _Graveyard Whistling_, their 11th studio album, released February 24, 2017,[\[20\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_97%27s#cite_note-20) and _Love the Holidays_, an album of mostly original Christmas songs, released November 16, 2018. ATO Records released the band's next studio album, aptly named _Twelfth_, on August 21, 2020. The twelve tracks were produced by Vance Powell who produced _Graveyard Whistling_. Old 97's noted that the recording sessions in [Nashville](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nashville "Nashville") started on the night of a deadly [tornado outbreak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tornado_outbreak_of_March_2%E2%80%933,_2020 "Tornado outbreak of March 2–3, 2020") and right before the [COVID-19 pandemic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/COVID-19_pandemic "COVID-19 pandemic") hit.[\[21\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_97%27s#cite_note-21)[\[22\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_97%27s#cite_note-22)

The band members portrayed the alien rock band Bzermikitokolok and the Knowheremen in _[The Guardians of the Galaxy Holiday Special](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Guardians_of_the_Galaxy_Holiday_Special "The Guardians of the Galaxy Holiday Special")_, singing the original song "I Don't Know What Christmas Is (But Christmastime Is Here)" and their 2018 song "Here It Is Christmastime," the latter with [Kevin Bacon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kevin_Bacon "Kevin Bacon") singing lead.[\[23\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_97%27s#cite_note-Guardians-23)[\[24\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_97%27s#cite_note-24) They were honored with a Lifetime Achievement Award at the [2025 Americana Music Honors & Awards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_Americana_Music_Honors_&_Awards "2025 Americana Music Honors & Awards") on September 10, 2025.[\[25\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_97%27s#cite_note-25)[\[26\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_97%27s#cite_note-26)
