Musical artist

**Justin Townes Earle** (January 4, 1982–August 20, 2020) was an American singer-songwriter and musician. After his debut EP, _Yuma_ (2007), he released eight full-length albums. He was recognized with an [Americana Music Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Americana_Music_Award "Americana Music Award") for Emerging Artist of the Year in 2009 and for Song of the Year in 2011 for " [Harlem River Blues](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harlem_River_Blues "Harlem River Blues")". His father is [alternative country](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alternative_country "Alternative country") artist [Steve Earle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steve_Earle "Steve Earle").

## Early life

Earle grew up in [South Nashville](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Nashville "South Nashville"), [Tennessee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tennessee "Tennessee"), with his mother, Carol Ann Hunter Earle. His father, [Steve Earle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steve_Earle "Steve Earle"), named him in honor of his own mentor, singer and songwriter [Townes Van Zandt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Townes_Van_Zandt "Townes Van Zandt").[\[2\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Justin_Townes_Earle#cite_note-wsj24Nov2010-2) When Justin was two, his father left the family, but after Steve Earle got sober in 1994 he returned.[\[3\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Justin_Townes_Earle#cite_note-3) The younger Earle dropped out of school, occasionally touring with and working for his father, eventually moving to eastern [Tennessee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tennessee "Tennessee") with other songwriters.[\[4\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Justin_Townes_Earle#cite_note-4) Like his father, he battled addiction beginning in his early teens.[\[5\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Justin_Townes_Earle#cite_note-5)

## Career

Earle played in two [Nashville](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nashville "Nashville") bands: the rock band the Distributors and the [ragtime](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ragtime "Ragtime") and [bluegrass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bluegrass_music "Bluegrass music") combo the Swindlers.[\[6\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Justin_Townes_Earle#cite_note-CMT-6) He spent some time as guitarist and keyboardist for his father's touring band the Dukes.[\[6\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Justin_Townes_Earle#cite_note-CMT-6) He developed a hybrid style of music mixing folk, blues, and country.[\[6\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Justin_Townes_Earle#cite_note-CMT-6)

### Albums

In 2007, Earle released a six-song EP called _Yuma_.[\[6\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Justin_Townes_Earle#cite_note-CMT-6) He signed a contract with Chicago's [Bloodshot Records](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bloodshot_Records "Bloodshot Records") and released an album called _The Good Life_ in 2008.[\[6\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Justin_Townes_Earle#cite_note-CMT-6) In 2009, he released the album _[Midnight at the Movies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Midnight_at_the_Movies "Midnight at the Movies")_.[\[6\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Justin_Townes_Earle#cite_note-CMT-6) In 2010, he released the album _[Harlem River Blues](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harlem_River_Blues "Harlem River Blues")_, followed by the album _[Nothing's Gonna Change the Way You Feel About Me Now](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nothing's_Gonna_Change_the_Way_You_Feel_About_Me_Now "Nothing's Gonna Change the Way You Feel About Me Now")_ in 2012.[\[6\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Justin_Townes_Earle#cite_note-CMT-6) From 2014 to 2017, Earle released a "family trilogy" of albums, comprising _[Single Mothers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Single_Mothers_(album) "Single Mothers (album)")_ (2014), _[Absent Fathers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Absent_Fathers "Absent Fathers")_ (2015), and _[Kids in the Street](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kids_in_the_Street_(Justin_Townes_Earle_album) "Kids in the Street (Justin Townes Earle album)")_ (2017). His final album, _[The Saint of Lost Causes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Saint_of_Lost_Causes "The Saint of Lost Causes")_, was released in May 2019 on New West Records, produced by [Adam Bednarik](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adam_Bednarik "Adam Bednarik") and Earle. Earle also produced [Wanda Jackson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wanda_Jackson "Wanda Jackson")'s album _[Unfinished Business](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unfinished_Business_(Wanda_Jackson_album) "Unfinished Business (Wanda Jackson album)")_ in 2012 recorded and mixed by Adam Bednarik.[\[7\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Justin_Townes_Earle#cite_note-7)

In August 2024, _All In: Unreleased & Rarities (The New West Years)_, a compilation of unheard demos and rare material was released on New West Records. It was compiled and produced by Adam Bednarik and Kim Buie.[\[8\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Justin_Townes_Earle#cite_note-8)

## Appearances

[![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/a/a7/Justin_Townes_Earle_%287428606756%29_%28cropped%29.jpg/500px-Justin_Townes_Earle_%287428606756%29_%28cropped%29.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Justin_Townes_Earle_(7428606756)_(cropped).jpg) Earle on stage in Houston, Texas, 2012

Earle played the [Grand Ole Opry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grand_Ole_Opry "Grand Ole Opry") in 2008, [WSM Radio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/WSM_(AM) "WSM (AM)"), [South By Southwest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_By_Southwest "South By Southwest") (2008–2010, 2012), the [Beacon Theatre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beacon_Theatre_(New_York_City) "Beacon Theatre (New York City)") (May 2009), the [Bristol Rhythm and Roots Reunion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bristol_Rhythm_and_Roots_Reunion "Bristol Rhythm and Roots Reunion") (September 2009), [Bonnaroo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bonnaroo "Bonnaroo") (2009) [Bumbershoot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bumbershoot "Bumbershoot") (2010), the [East Coast Blues & Roots Music Festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Coast_Blues_&_Roots_Music_Festival "East Coast Blues & Roots Music Festival") (Australia, 2012), the [Bowery Ballroom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bowery_Ballroom "Bowery Ballroom") (March 2010), the [Winnipeg Folk Festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Winnipeg_Folk_Festival "Winnipeg Folk Festival") (July 2008), and the [Nelsonville Music Festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nelsonville_Music_Festival "Nelsonville Music Festival") (2008 and 2011).[\[9\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Justin_Townes_Earle#cite_note-stuarts-9)[\[10\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Justin_Townes_Earle#cite_note-nelsonville-10)

In 2009, Earle co-billed The Big Surprise Tour with [Gillian Welch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gillian_Welch "Gillian Welch") and [David Rawlings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Rawlings "David Rawlings"), [Old Crow Medicine Show](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_Crow_Medicine_Show "Old Crow Medicine Show"), and [The Felice Brothers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Felice_Brothers "The Felice Brothers").[\[11\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Justin_Townes_Earle#cite_note-11)

In 2012, he appeared in an episode of the [HBO](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HBO "HBO") television series _[Treme](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treme_(TV_series) "Treme (TV series)")_ with his father.[\[12\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Justin_Townes_Earle#cite_note-Treme-12)

## Honors and distinctions

In September 2009, Earle received an [Americana Music Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Americana_Music_Award "Americana Music Award") for New and Emerging Artist of the Year.[\[13\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Justin_Townes_Earle#cite_note-Paste-13)

In 2011, Earle received the [Americana Music Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Americana_Music_Award "Americana Music Award") in the Song of the Year category for "Harlem River Blues".[\[14\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Justin_Townes_Earle#cite_note-Seattle_2012-14) His album of the same name has been described as having a "gently flowing, urban Americana sound, with horns, organ and tangy electric guitar".[\[14\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Justin_Townes_Earle#cite_note-Seattle_2012-14) That year he also contributed a cover of " [Maybe Baby](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maybe_Baby_(song) "Maybe Baby (song)")" on the 2011 tribute album _[Rave on Buddy Holly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rave_on_Buddy_Holly "Rave on Buddy Holly")_,[\[15\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Justin_Townes_Earle#cite_note-15) and played [Newport Folk Festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Newport_Folk_Festival "Newport Folk Festival")[\[16\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Justin_Townes_Earle#cite_note-16) and the [Hardly Strictly Bluegrass Festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hardly_Strictly_Bluegrass_Festival "Hardly Strictly Bluegrass Festival").[\[17\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Justin_Townes_Earle#cite_note-17)

_Nothing's Gonna Change the Way You Feel About Me Now_ was listed at album number 37 on _[Rolling Stone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rolling_Stone_(magazine) "Rolling Stone (magazine)")'s_ list of the top 50 albums of 2012, with the annotation as follows: "The son of country-rock renegade Steve Earle has grown into a songwriter to rival his dad."[\[18\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Justin_Townes_Earle#cite_note-18)

On January 4, 2021, Steve Earle & the Dukes released the album _[J.T.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/J.T._(Steve_Earle_&_The_Dukes_album) "J.T. (Steve Earle & The Dukes album)")_, a tribute to Justin Townes Earle.

On January 4, 2023, a tribute concert was held at the Ryman Auditorium in memory of Justin. Performers included Steve Earle & The Dukes and special guests: Shooter Jennings, Bonnie Whitmore, Amanda Shires, Willy and Cody Braun, Buddy Miller, Ben Nichols (Lucero), Dustin Welch, Elizabeth Cook, Emmylou Harris, Jason Isbell, Jessica Lea Mayfield, Joe Pug, Jon Langford, Lilly Hiatt, Scotty Melton, Stacey Earle and Mark Stuart.[\[19\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Justin_Townes_Earle#cite_note-19)[\[20\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Justin_Townes_Earle#cite_note-20)

In a 2026 interview with [Garden & Gun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garden_&_Gun "Garden & Gun"), Earle's biographer Jonathan Bernstein noted how he helped create a musical environment in which roots-minded artists like [Tyler Childers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tyler_Childers "Tyler Childers"), [Charley Crockett](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charley_Crockett "Charley Crockett") and [Sierra Ferrell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sierra_Ferrell "Sierra Ferrell") could thrive and become major recording and touring artists. “Justin was someone who quietly has been a major influence and years ahead of his time commercially,” Bernstein said.[\[21\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Justin_Townes_Earle#cite_note-Garden_&_Gun-21)

## Personal life and death

Earle began using illicit drugs at age 12 and continued for many years. In his words, "I discovered very fast that my way of doing things was going to get me in trouble, and I kept going with it, because I believed the myth for a long time, and I believed I had to destroy myself to make great art."[\[22\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Justin_Townes_Earle#cite_note-22)

He went to rehabilitation clinics nine times followed by periods of [sobriety](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sobriety "Sobriety"). He relapsed in September 2010 and again in 2017.[\[2\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Justin_Townes_Earle#cite_note-wsj24Nov2010-2)[\[6\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Justin_Townes_Earle#cite_note-CMT-6)[\[23\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Justin_Townes_Earle#cite_note-23)[\[24\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Justin_Townes_Earle#cite_note-24)

Earle moved to New York City in 2009,[\[25\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Justin_Townes_Earle#cite_note-25)[\[26\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Justin_Townes_Earle#cite_note-26) but returned to Nashville in 2014.[\[27\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Justin_Townes_Earle#cite_note-27) He married Jenn Marie Maynard in 2013[\[28\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Justin_Townes_Earle#cite_note-28) and he and his wife lived on the West Coast.[\[2\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Justin_Townes_Earle#cite_note-wsj24Nov2010-2) Their only child, a daughter named Etta, was born in June 2017.[\[29\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Justin_Townes_Earle#cite_note-29)

Earle died on August 20, 2020, in Nashville, Tennessee, at the age of 38,[\[30\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Justin_Townes_Earle#cite_note-NYT-30) from an accidental overdose of [fentanyl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fentanyl "Fentanyl")-laced [cocaine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cocaine "Cocaine").[\[31\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Justin_Townes_Earle#cite_note-31)
