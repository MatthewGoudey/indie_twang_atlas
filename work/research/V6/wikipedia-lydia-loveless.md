Musical artist

**Lydia Loveless** ( [née](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/N%C3%A9e "Née") **Ankrom**, born September 4, 1990) is an American [alternative country](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alternative_country "Alternative country")[\[1\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lydia_Loveless#cite_note-SPIN-KickAss-2014-1) singer-songwriter from [Columbus, Ohio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Columbus,_Ohio "Columbus, Ohio").[\[2\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lydia_Loveless#cite_note-lantern_loveless-2) Their[\[a\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lydia_Loveless#cite_note-fn1-3) music combines pop music, [classic country](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Classic_country "Classic country"), [honky tonk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Honky_tonk "Honky tonk"), and [punk rock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Punk_rock "Punk rock").[\[3\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lydia_Loveless#cite_note-ChiTrib-PunkRock-2012-4)[\[4\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lydia_Loveless#cite_note-AllMusic-Bio-5)

## Early life

Loveless was born in [Coshocton, Ohio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coshocton,_Ohio "Coshocton, Ohio"), in the [Newcastle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Newcastle_Township,_Coshocton_County,_Ohio "Newcastle Township, Coshocton County, Ohio") area.[\[5\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lydia_Loveless#cite_note-OGibbs-2013-6) They are the daughter of Parker Chandler and have two older sisters: Eleanor Sinacola, and Jessica, who now performs under the stage name "Jessica Wabbit".[\[2\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lydia_Loveless#cite_note-lantern_loveless-2) Loveless grew up on a farm in a rural area outside of Coshocton and was home-schooled.[\[6\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lydia_Loveless#cite_note-KidsInterviewBands2014-7)[\[7\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lydia_Loveless#cite_note-Grantland-PopCulture-2014-8) They said they felt like an outcast in a town that emphasized religion and conformity[\[3\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lydia_Loveless#cite_note-ChiTrib-PunkRock-2012-4)[\[4\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lydia_Loveless#cite_note-AllMusic-Bio-5) until they moved to [Columbus, Ohio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Columbus,_Ohio "Columbus, Ohio"), when they were 14. They enjoyed [Hank Williams III](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hank_Williams_III "Hank Williams III") and punk-influenced country music, while also embracing popular music and rock and roll and "pretty much anything on [Kemado Records](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kemado_Records "Kemado Records")."[\[8\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lydia_Loveless#cite_note-Interview-Machine-9)[\[9\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lydia_Loveless#cite_note-NoDep-Indest-10)

Their family is musical: Loveless's father was a pastor, drummer, and later country-western bar owner for a time.[\[8\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lydia_Loveless#cite_note-Interview-Machine-9) They and their sisters played several instruments.[\[2\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lydia_Loveless#cite_note-lantern_loveless-2) Loveless took piano lessons,[\[10\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lydia_Loveless#cite_note-WattFromPedro-Real-2016-11) then began trying to play the guitar at 12.[\[11\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lydia_Loveless#cite_note-AmSong-2014-12)[\[12\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lydia_Loveless#cite_note-MusicLegends-SE-2014-13)

## Career

[![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/8/8c/Lydia_Loveless_performing_at_SXSW_2014.JPG/500px-Lydia_Loveless_performing_at_SXSW_2014.JPG)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Lydia_Loveless_performing_at_SXSW_2014.JPG) Lydia Loveless performing at SXSW 2014

In 2004, Loveless, their father, and their sisters made up part of a four-member new wave pop band called Carson Drew,[\[10\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lydia_Loveless#cite_note-WattFromPedro-Real-2016-11) named after the father in the [Nancy Drew](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nancy_Drew "Nancy Drew") books. Loveless played bass.[\[9\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lydia_Loveless#cite_note-NoDep-Indest-10) The band broke up in 2007.[\[2\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lydia_Loveless#cite_note-lantern_loveless-2)

At a show in [Cincinnati](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cincinnati "Cincinnati") where they opened for his band, Loveless met producer David Rhodes Brown ( [500 Miles to Memphis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/500_Miles_to_Memphis "500 Miles to Memphis"))[\[7\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lydia_Loveless#cite_note-Grantland-PopCulture-2014-8) who went on to produce their first album, 2010's _The Only Man_. Loveless was not happy with the slick production of the album.[\[3\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lydia_Loveless#cite_note-ChiTrib-PunkRock-2012-4) Loveless made the record when they were 15 years old.[\[13\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lydia_Loveless#cite_note-CMJ-QA-2014-14) Loveless clarified that they like the songs they wrote, but the drawn-out process (over three years) to get the album released influenced their feelings about the project.[\[7\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lydia_Loveless#cite_note-Grantland-PopCulture-2014-8)[\[14\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lydia_Loveless#cite_note-ColMnthly-breakout-2012-15)

Columbus attorney Steve McGann became their manager. Loveless and their band drove 20 hours to Austin, Texas, and ended up playing for [Bloodshot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bloodshot_Records "Bloodshot Records") owners Rob Miller and Nan Warshaw at the [2010 South by Southwest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_by_Southwest#2010 "South by Southwest") music festival.[\[14\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lydia_Loveless#cite_note-ColMnthly-breakout-2012-15)

In 2012, signed with Bloodshot and determined to make a more raw and edgier album, they released _Indestructible Machine_ with songs that feature themes of frustration with their hometown, [drinking](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drinking#Alcoholic_beverages "Drinking"), depression, and a humorous song about being stalked by a man who referred to himself as [Steve Earle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steve_Earle "Steve Earle") but was not in fact the singer of " [Copperhead Road](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Copperhead_Road_(song) "Copperhead Road (song)")".[\[3\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lydia_Loveless#cite_note-ChiTrib-PunkRock-2012-4)[\[4\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lydia_Loveless#cite_note-AllMusic-Bio-5)[\[15\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lydia_Loveless#cite_note-spinner-16)[\[16\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lydia_Loveless#cite_note-17) Loveless recorded the album with many live takes and a minimum of overdubs at [Grove City, Ohio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grove_City,_Ohio "Grove City, Ohio")'s Sonic Lounge recording studio with engineer Joe Viers.[\[3\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lydia_Loveless#cite_note-ChiTrib-PunkRock-2012-4)[\[14\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lydia_Loveless#cite_note-ColMnthly-breakout-2012-15) _[Spin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spin_(magazine) "Spin (magazine)")_ characterized the record as standing out "for its utter lack of bullshit", with "roaring vocals, in her narrators' lived-in-bars recklessness, and in her overall inability to mince words."[\[17\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lydia_Loveless#cite_note-SPIN-BlazesAhead-2014-18)

_Indestructible Machine_ received praise from [AllMusic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AllMusic "AllMusic") and in publications such as the _[Chicago Tribune](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chicago_Tribune "Chicago Tribune")_, _Spin_, and _[The Washington Post](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Washington_Post "The Washington Post")_.[\[3\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lydia_Loveless#cite_note-ChiTrib-PunkRock-2012-4)[\[4\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lydia_Loveless#cite_note-AllMusic-Bio-5)[\[18\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lydia_Loveless#cite_note-wpost_blog-19)[\[19\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lydia_Loveless#cite_note-cdispatch-20) [Greg Kot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greg_Kot "Greg Kot") wrote that Loveless's "defiant tone is matched by songs that put country and punk on equal ground, unvarnished and direct".[\[3\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lydia_Loveless#cite_note-ChiTrib-PunkRock-2012-4)[\[20\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lydia_Loveless#cite_note-PM-Indestructible-21)[\[21\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lydia_Loveless#cite_note-MusicTimes-2013-22)

In the Spring of 2013, Loveless did an extensive Canadian tour supporting the [Supersuckers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supersuckers "Supersuckers").[\[22\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lydia_Loveless#cite_note-SuoniPer-2013-23) Loveless and their band also toured Scandinavia and Spain during the fall of 2013.

[![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/f/f9/Lydia_Loveless_with_Old_97s.jpg/500px-Lydia_Loveless_with_Old_97s.jpg)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Lydia_Loveless_with_Old_97s.jpg) Lydia Loveless singing Four Leaf Clover with the [Old 97s](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_97s "Old 97s"). 9:30 Club (Washington DC) May 31, 2014

In 2013, Loveless released _Boy Crazy_, an [EP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Extended_play "Extended play"). Mark Deming of AllMusic praised the release, asserting that the album "is further proof that Loveless is a major talent, and if her next album is as good as this, she may run the risk of becoming a very big star".[\[23\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lydia_Loveless#cite_note-amg_boycrazy-24) One of the songs, "Lover's Spat", is about the serial killer [Jeffrey Dahmer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jeffrey_Dahmer "Jeffrey Dahmer").[\[24\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lydia_Loveless#cite_note-BostonGlobe-BC-25)

_[Rolling Stone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rolling_Stone "Rolling Stone")_ cited Loveless as one of its "10 New Artists You Need to Know: January 2014".[\[25\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lydia_Loveless#cite_note-RStone-10Artists-2014-26)

In February 2014, Loveless released their third full-length record, _[Somewhere Else](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Somewhere_Else_(Lydia_Loveless_album) "Somewhere Else (Lydia Loveless album)")_, on [Bloodshot Records](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bloodshot_Records "Bloodshot Records"), which has a dark, "poppy" vibe.[\[26\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lydia_Loveless#cite_note-BSHQ-SomewhereElse-27)[\[27\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lydia_Loveless#cite_note-SPIN-Cowpunk2013-28) _[Stereogum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stereogum "Stereogum")_ said Loveless is using their "unmistakable voice as a songwriter, and she's only getting better at using it to blur the line between running her mouth and pouring out her heart."[\[28\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lydia_Loveless#cite_note-Stereogum-ReallyWant-2014-29) Loveless was listed as one of "5 Best New Artist for January 14" by _Spin_ magazine.[\[29\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lydia_Loveless#cite_note-SPIN-5Best-2014-30) The record includes "Head", a single Loveless wrote with their guitar player, Todd May, a fellow songwriter.[\[30\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lydia_Loveless#cite_note-Elle-2014-31) The album had a very positive reception[\[31\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lydia_Loveless#cite_note-Metacritic-SE-32) and entered [_Billboard_](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Billboard_(magazine) "Billboard (magazine)")'s [Heatseekers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Billboard_Heatseekers "Billboard Heatseekers") chart (new entries to _Billboard_ charts, compiled by [Nielsen SoundScan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nielsen_SoundScan "Nielsen SoundScan")) the first week of its release at position number 7.[\[12\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lydia_Loveless#cite_note-MusicLegends-SE-2014-13)[\[32\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lydia_Loveless#cite_note-Billboard-Heatseekers-7-Feb2014-33)

In April 2014, Loveless released the _Mile High/Blind_ 7" record for [Record Store Day](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Record_Store_Day "Record Store Day"). The record had a non-album cut titled "Mile High" on the A side and a cover of [Kesha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kesha "Kesha")'s "Blind" on the B side. It was a limited edition release on lime green vinyl.[\[33\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lydia_Loveless#cite_note-Spin-Blind-2014-34) The tracks were released in digital album format on May 27, 2014.[\[34\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lydia_Loveless#cite_note-BklynVegan-2014-35)

In April 2015, Loveless was part of a [Record Store Day](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Record_Store_Day "Record Store Day") release with label-mate, [Cory Branan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cory_Branan "Cory Branan"). The two artists cover two [Prince](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prince_(musician) "Prince (musician)") songs: Loveless doing " [I Would Die 4 U](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/I_Would_Die_4_U "I Would Die 4 U")" and Branan doing " [Under the Cherry Moon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parade_(Prince_album) "Parade (Prince album)")". The 7" limited edition releases were also pressed onto purple vinyl.[\[35\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lydia_Loveless#cite_note-NPR-AllSongs-IWouldDie4U-2015-36)

Loveless donated their vocal talent to the end credits song from the film, _[A Dog Named Gucci](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A_Dog_Named_Gucci "A Dog Named Gucci")_, in the song "One Voice", which also features the voices of [Norah Jones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norah_Jones "Norah Jones"), [Aimee Mann](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aimee_Mann "Aimee Mann"), [Susanna Hoffs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Susanna_Hoffs "Susanna Hoffs"), [Neko Case](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neko_Case "Neko Case"), [Brian May](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brian_May "Brian May") and [Kathryn Calder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kathryn_Calder "Kathryn Calder"). It was produced by Dean Falcone, who also wrote the film's score. "One Voice" was released on [Record Store Day](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Record_Store_Day "Record Store Day"), April 16, 2016, with profits from the sale of the single going to benefit animal charities.[\[36\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lydia_Loveless#cite_note-DVDReport-DogGucci-2016-37)

In August 2016, [Bloodshot Records](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bloodshot_Records "Bloodshot Records") released their third studio album, _[Real](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Real_(Lydia_Loveless_album) "Real (Lydia Loveless album)")_.[\[37\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lydia_Loveless#cite_note-Spin-Longer-2016-38)[\[38\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lydia_Loveless#cite_note-BostonGlobe-Real-2016-39) Their first ever music video, for the album's first single "Longer" received its world premiere through _[Rolling Stone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rolling_Stone "Rolling Stone")_ in July 2016. The video was directed by filmmaker [Gorman Bechard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gorman_Bechard "Gorman Bechard"), who directed the documentary on Loveless.[\[39\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lydia_Loveless#cite_note-RollingStone-Longer-2016-40) On August 19, a second Bechard-directed music video was released this time for the song "Clumps".[\[40\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lydia_Loveless#cite_note-41) The next morning Loveless and their band made their American TV network debut, performing three songs on _[CBS Saturday Morning](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CBS_Saturday_Morning "CBS Saturday Morning")_.[\[41\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lydia_Loveless#cite_note-CBSNews-SatSessions-2016-42) A video for the song "European", also directed by Bechard, debuted in November 2016.[\[42\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lydia_Loveless#cite_note-RollingStone-Real-EuropeanDebut-2016-43)

_[The A.V. Club](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_A.V._Club "The A.V. Club")_ called _Real_ "an adventurous, brutal honest sucker punch",[\[43\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lydia_Loveless#cite_note-44) while _[The Boston Globe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Boston_Globe "The Boston Globe")_ said, "Loveless continues to manifest a remarkable combination of bruised vulnerability and desperate longing, alongside a tough, self-deprecating resilience, but there's more of the former and less of the latter this time. She's still preoccupied with the downsides of love: longing for something you don't (or can't) have, the inevitable dissolution of whatever you manage to find (and the difficulty of finding it in the first place), and the emotional pitfalls of navigating it."[\[44\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lydia_Loveless#cite_note-Globe-Real-2016-45) _Rolling Stone_ liked the shift in genre towards a more pop sound.[\[45\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lydia_Loveless#cite_note-RStone-Real-Review-2016-46)

Loveless released their fourth studio album _[Daughter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daughter_(album) "Daughter (album)")_ through their own label, Honey, You're Gonna Be Late Records on September 25, 2020.[\[46\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lydia_Loveless#cite_note-Rolling_Stone-2020-47)

## Documentary

Loveless was the subject of a documentary called _[Who Is Lydia Loveless?](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Who_Is_Lydia_Loveless%3F "Who Is Lydia Loveless?")_,[\[47\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lydia_Loveless#cite_note-ArgusLeader-LovelessDoc-2015-48) in which filmmaker [Gorman Bechard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gorman_Bechard "Gorman Bechard") ( _[Color Me Obsessed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Color_Me_Obsessed "Color Me Obsessed")_, _[Every Everything: The Music, Life & Times of Grant Hart](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Every_Everything:_The_Music,_Life_&_Times_of_Grant_Hart "Every Everything: The Music, Life & Times of Grant Hart")_) documented the making of Loveless's album _Real_,[\[47\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lydia_Loveless#cite_note-ArgusLeader-LovelessDoc-2015-48) as well as following their on the road, and looking into what life is like for a band at their level in the music industry.[\[48\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lydia_Loveless#cite_note-49) "I also wanted to look at stuff we normally don't see a lot of. What are the finances for a band like this? Where does the money go? Who gets the money? Is Spotify good? Is Spotify bad? How does piracy affect you? What about the fans? I really wanted to go into all of that for a band that can still sell out 200–250 seat venues and bars but is still all travelling in an old Ford van. A good night is when they have a couple of hotel rooms. No one is rolling in the dough so to speak. So what is it at that point when you have amazing critical success and acclaim but you're not there yet?"[\[49\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lydia_Loveless#cite_note-NoDep-WhoIs-2016-50) In October 2015, Bechard and his crew filmed a live Lydia Loveless concert at Skully's in their hometown of Columbus for the documentary.[\[50\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lydia_Loveless#cite_note-51) The film had its world premiere on April 7, 2016 at the [Columbus International Film & Video Festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Columbus_International_Film_&_Video_Festival "Columbus International Film & Video Festival").[\[51\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lydia_Loveless#cite_note-52)

## Songwriting

Loveless characterizes their songwriting as "singing about emotions".[\[8\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lydia_Loveless#cite_note-Interview-Machine-9) Loveless is an avowed fan of [Kesha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kesha "Kesha"), and has played "Blind" in their live shows, as well as in a recording featured on their _Boy Crazy and Single(s)_ release.[\[52\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lydia_Loveless#cite_note-MercNews-AE-2012-53)[\[53\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lydia_Loveless#cite_note-WSJ-2014-54)

## Personal life

Loveless was married to their bassist, Ben Lamb,[\[19\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lydia_Loveless#cite_note-cdispatch-20) who is also a graphic artist.[\[59\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lydia_Loveless#cite_note-hot17-Ben-2012-60) They resided in [Columbus, Ohio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Columbus,_Ohio "Columbus, Ohio");[\[3\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lydia_Loveless#cite_note-ChiTrib-PunkRock-2012-4)[\[4\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lydia_Loveless#cite_note-AllMusic-Bio-5) after they divorced, Loveless moved to Raleigh, North Carolina.[\[60\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lydia_Loveless#cite_note-61)

Loveless's older sisters, Jessica Wabbit and Eleanor Sinacola, also have their own bands (The Girls! and Dead Girlfriend, respectively).[\[2\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lydia_Loveless#cite_note-lantern_loveless-2) Loveless's younger brother, Nate, is the drummer of Shores of Elysium, a [death metal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Death_metal "Death metal")/ [deathcore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deathcore "Deathcore") band from [Columbus, Ohio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Columbus,_Ohio "Columbus, Ohio").[\[61\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lydia_Loveless#cite_note-NPR-FirstListen-62)

Loveless is gender-fluid and uses _they/she_ pronouns.[\[62\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lydia_Loveless#cite_note-63)[\[63\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lydia_Loveless#cite_note-64)[\[64\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lydia_Loveless#cite_note-Instagram-65)
