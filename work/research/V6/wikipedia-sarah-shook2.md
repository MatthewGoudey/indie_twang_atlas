Musical artist

**River Shook** (born September 15, 1985) is an American [country](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Country_music "Country music") singer-songwriter from [Chatham County, North Carolina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chatham_County,_North_Carolina "Chatham County, North Carolina").[\[1\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sarah_Shook#cite_note-AuntieBellum-Profile-2016-1)[\[2\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sarah_Shook#cite_note-Fader-NothinFeelsRight-2017-2) Their "high lonesome" style incorporates [country-punk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cowpunk "Cowpunk"), [twang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twang "Twang"), and [outlaw country](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Outlaw_country "Outlaw country").[\[3\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sarah_Shook#cite_note-NoDep-Sidelong-2017-3)[\[4\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sarah_Shook#cite_note-NoDep-Sidelong-May-2017-4)[\[5\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sarah_Shook#cite_note-Glide-Sidelong-2017-5)

## Early life

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Sarah_Shook&action=edit&section=1 "Edit section: Early life")\]

Shook was born in [Rochester](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rochester,_New_York "Rochester, New York"), [New York](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_(state) "New York (state)"). They were homeschooled and grew up in a [fundamentalist Christian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christian_fundamentalism "Christian fundamentalism") family where music was restricted; they were permitted only to listen to [classical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Classical_music "Classical music") and [worship music](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Contemporary_worship_music "Contemporary worship music").[\[6\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sarah_Shook#cite_note-TheBoot-Years-2018-6) When Shook was 9 years old they taught themself [piano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Piano "Piano"),[\[7\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sarah_Shook#cite_note-AmSongwriter-Years-2018-7) and in high school they taught themself [acoustic guitar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Acoustic_guitar "Acoustic guitar").[\[8\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sarah_Shook#cite_note-IndyWeek-Sidelong-2015-8)

The family moved often when Shook was young. In July 2005, when they were 19 years old, they and their family moved to [Garner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garner,_North_Carolina "Garner, North Carolina"), [North Carolina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Carolina "North Carolina").[\[1\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sarah_Shook#cite_note-AuntieBellum-Profile-2016-1)[\[8\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sarah_Shook#cite_note-IndyWeek-Sidelong-2015-8)

## Career

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Sarah_Shook&action=edit&section=2 "Edit section: Career")\]

In 2010, Shook started their first band, Sarah Shook and the Devil. The band was made up of Shook on vocals and guitar, Jon Baughman on [bass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bass_guitar "Bass guitar"), Phil Sullivan on [lap steel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lap_steel_guitar "Lap steel guitar"), and Eric Peterson on guitar. The band released a seven-song EP, 2013's _Seven_[\[9\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sarah_Shook#cite_note-NoDep-Sidelong-2015-9)[\[10\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sarah_Shook#cite_note-GrafWV-Sidelong-2017-10) but disbanded later the same year.[\[8\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sarah_Shook#cite_note-IndyWeek-Sidelong-2015-8)

In 2013, Shook formed the band Sarah Shook and the Dirty Hands, with Devil bandmate Eric Peterson. They played regionally, mostly in North Carolina, for around a year.[\[10\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sarah_Shook#cite_note-GrafWV-Sidelong-2017-10) Shook then started a new band called Sarah Shook & the Disarmers in mid to late 2014. The band started as a recording project, with Eric Peterson on guitar, and John Howie Jr. ( [Two Dollar Pistols](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tift_Merritt "Tift Merritt"), John Howie Jr. & the Rosewood Bluff) on drums, Jason Hendrick on bass fiddle, and Phil Sullivan on lap steel.[\[11\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sarah_Shook#cite_note-PopMatters-Sidelong-2017-11)[\[12\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sarah_Shook#cite_note-Paste-Years-Review-2018-12)

Sarah Shook and the Disarmers released their first full-length album _Sidelong,_ on October 16, 2015, at the [Cat's Cradle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cat's_Cradle_(venue) "Cat's Cradle (venue)") Back Room in Chapel Hill, NC, with a re-release in 2017.[\[13\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sarah_Shook#cite_note-VICE-Noisey-Sidelong-2017-13) _Sidelong_ was produced by Ian Schreier at Manifold Recording Studios, which is near the [Haw River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haw_River "Haw River") in North Carolina. The record was tracked and recorded live in April 2015.[\[14\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sarah_Shook#cite_note-WSJ-Sidelong-2017-14) The band found regional and national success with the record. Shook signed with Kathie Russell of RedKats Artist Management, which led to their signing with Chicago's [Bloodshot Records](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bloodshot_Records "Bloodshot Records") in January 2017.[\[15\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sarah_Shook#cite_note-RollingStone-10toKnow-2016-15)[\[16\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sarah_Shook#cite_note-Noisey-Years-2018-16)

The records was number 2 on _[Indy Week](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indy_Week "Indy Week")'s_ top 25 best albums of 2015.[\[17\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sarah_Shook#cite_note-IndyWeek-Top25Albums-2015-17) Sarah Shook and the Disarmers were listed as one of 10 New Country Artists to Know in July 2016.[\[15\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sarah_Shook#cite_note-RollingStone-10toKnow-2016-15) Also in 2016, [BuzzFeed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BuzzFeed "BuzzFeed") Community listed Shook as one of five women country artists who are impacting music,[\[18\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sarah_Shook#cite_note-BuzzFeed-5Women-2016-18) and the album received positive reviews.[\[19\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sarah_Shook#cite_note-Paste-Sidelong-2017-19)[\[20\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sarah_Shook#cite_note-WCQS-Sidelong-2016-20)[\[21\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sarah_Shook#cite_note-WUNC-Sidelong-2015-21)

In April 2018, Sarah Shook & the Disarmers released their second record, titled _Years,_ on [Bloodshot Records](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bloodshot_Records "Bloodshot Records"). Shook and the band worked on the songs while touring heavily.[\[22\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sarah_Shook#cite_note-RollingStone-Years-2018-22) Shook also focused on learning vocal techniques as a way to control and release their vocals, which led to what many reviews cited as a crisper vocal sound.[\[16\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sarah_Shook#cite_note-Noisey-Years-2018-16) Music critic [Greg Kot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greg_Kot "Greg Kot") compared their vocals to those of [jazz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jazz "Jazz") vocals, noting that Shook and the band, who have toured together for years, are ferocious.[\[23\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sarah_Shook#cite_note-SoundOpinions-Years-Review-2018-23) The song "Good as Gold" was chosen as one of _[Rolling Stone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rolling_Stone "Rolling Stone")'s_ 10 best country and Americana songs of the week in March 2018.[\[24\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sarah_Shook#cite_note-RollingStone-BestSong-GoodAsGold-2018-24) _Years_ went on to receive multiple positive reviews.[\[23\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sarah_Shook#cite_note-SoundOpinions-Years-Review-2018-23)[\[25\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sarah_Shook#cite_note-PopMatters-Years-Review-2018-25)[\[26\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sarah_Shook#cite_note-WideOpenCountry-Years-Review-2018-26)[\[27\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sarah_Shook#cite_note-ChicagoTrib-Years-Review-2018-27) The Disarmers at the time was made up of Eric Peterson on guitar, Aaron Oliva on bass, Jack Foster on drums, and Adam "Ditch" Kurtz on pedal steel.[\[28\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sarah_Shook#cite_note-Bandsintown-Years-2018-28)

A documentary, _[What it Takes: film en douze tableaux](https://www.imdb.com/title/tt7423972/)_ by director [Gorman Bechard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gorman_Bechard "Gorman Bechard"), premiered at the [Independent Film Festival Boston](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Independent_Film_Festival_Boston "Independent Film Festival Boston") on 29 April 2018, and was released to DVD on November 9, 2018. The film follows Shook as they wrote and recorded _Years_.[\[29\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sarah_Shook#cite_note-29)[\[30\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sarah_Shook#cite_note-30)[\[31\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sarah_Shook#cite_note-31) The music blog _If It's Too Loud_ commented, "Film documentaries are typically put out by a record company as a way to cash in on an artist and are little more than fluff pieces, or they focus on drama within a band. _What it Takes: film en douze tableaux_ doesn't do any of that. While Bechard is an obvious fan of his subject, he shows them as is, and doesn't try to show them glossed over or hyped up."[\[32\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sarah_Shook#cite_note-32)

In December 2018, _Years_ was included in the best-albums-of-2018 year-end lists of _[PopMatters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PopMatters "PopMatters")_ (32nd)[\[33\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sarah_Shook#cite_note-33) and _[Louder Than War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louder_Than_War "Louder Than War")_ (39th).[\[34\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sarah_Shook#cite_note-34)

In November 2021, Shook announced a third album, _Nightroamer_, which was released on February 18, 2022 via [Thirty Tigers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thirty_Tigers "Thirty Tigers") (following the sale of Bloodshot Records, which had caused the album to be delayed). In addition to returning members Peterson, Oliva and original pedal steel player Phil Sullivan, [Will Rigby](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Will_Rigby "Will Rigby") joined the band on drums. There were also additional performances from [organist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Organist "Organist") Skip Edwards, with longtime [Dwight Yoakam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dwight_Yoakam "Dwight Yoakam") collaborator [Pete Anderson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pete_Anderson "Pete Anderson") producing the album.

In May 2022, Shook announced a new [indie rock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indie_rock "Indie rock") side project called Mightmare. They are joined in the band by Ash Lopez on bass, Blake Tallent on guitar, and Ethan Standard on drums. The band's debut album, _Cruel Liars_, was released on October 14, 2022, via [Kill Rock Stars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kill_Rock_Stars "Kill Rock Stars").[\[35\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sarah_Shook#cite_note-35)

## Personal life

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Sarah_Shook&action=edit&section=3 "Edit section: Personal life")\]

Shook has a son.[\[8\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sarah_Shook#cite_note-IndyWeek-Sidelong-2015-8) Shook is [non-binary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Non-binary "Non-binary") and uses _[they/them](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Singular_they "Singular they")_ pronouns.[\[36\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sarah_Shook#cite_note-CountryQueer-2020-36)[\[37\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sarah_Shook#cite_note-37)[\[38\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sarah_Shook#cite_note-38)[\[39\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sarah_Shook#cite_note-39)[\[40\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sarah_Shook#cite_note-40)[\[41\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sarah_Shook#cite_note-41)

Shook grew up in a deeply religious environment, but is now an [atheist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atheism "Atheism").[\[42\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sarah_Shook#cite_note-DailyTarHeel-Sidelong-2016-42) Shook is [bisexual](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bisexuality "Bisexuality"),[\[4\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sarah_Shook#cite_note-NoDep-Sidelong-May-2017-4) and is politically active in supporting [LGBT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LGBT "LGBT") and [civil rights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Civil_and_political_rights "Civil and political rights") causes.[\[43\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sarah_Shook#cite_note-DailyTimes-Sidelong-2017-43) Along with fellow musician and activist Erika Libero (Henbrain),[\[44\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sarah_Shook#cite_note-IndyWeek-Activism-2016-44) they won the 2016 Indy Arts Award for their work with [Chapel Hill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chapel_Hill,_North_Carolina "Chapel Hill, North Carolina")-based Safe Space initiative, which was an effort to get local businesses to put up stickers that offered [safe spaces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Safe_space "Safe space") for people needing it, and for their work putting on a two-day music festival, Manifest, which includes bands that have at least one female or [non-binary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Non-binary_gender "Non-binary gender") member.[\[45\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sarah_Shook#cite_note-SheShreds-TheNail-2017-45)[\[46\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sarah_Shook#cite_note-WideOpenCountry-Sidelong-2017-46)
