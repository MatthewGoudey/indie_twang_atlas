Musical artist

**William Elliott Whitmore** (born May 11, 1978) is an American [blues](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blues "Blues"), [country](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Country_music "Country music"), [folk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Folk_music "Folk music") singer and musician.[\[1\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Elliott_Whitmore#cite_note-NPR-TalkOfTheNation-FieldSongs-2011-1)[\[2\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Elliott_Whitmore#cite_note-People-RadiumDeath-2015-2) He plays roots-folk music that is often inspired by his life on his family farm in the hills of southeastern Iowa.[\[3\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Elliott_Whitmore#cite_note-Noisey-RadiumDeath-2015-3)[\[4\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Elliott_Whitmore#cite_note-WorldCafe-FieldSongs-2011-4)

## Early life and education

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=William_Elliott_Whitmore&action=edit&section=1 "Edit section: Early life and education")\]

Whitmore grew up on a 160-acre horse farm in [Lee County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lee_County,_Iowa "Lee County, Iowa"), Iowa, not far outside of [Keokuk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Keokuk,_Iowa "Keokuk, Iowa"), Iowa. His parents, Elyse Whitmore (née Tweedy) and Harold Whitmore,[\[5\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Elliott_Whitmore#cite_note-PamDixon-WealthyElliott-FamilyTree-5) were farmers, and the farm had been in his family since his third great-grandfather, who was an immigrant from Ireland.[\[6\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Elliott_Whitmore#cite_note-Pitch-FieldSongs-2012-6) After his parents died, he converted the farm to row crops, which he leases to a neighboring farmer so he can be on the road to perform. He has an older brother and sister.[\[7\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Elliott_Whitmore#cite_note-Westword-AnimalsInTheDark-2009-7)

While he was growing up, the family was musical, with his dad playing the guitar, his mother playing the accordion and piano, and both his paternal and maternal grandfathers playing the banjo.[\[8\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Elliott_Whitmore#cite_note-DailyIowan-2017-8) [Charley Pride](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charley_Pride "Charley Pride") and [Willie Nelson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Willie_Nelson "Willie Nelson") were huge influences growing up. Whitmore sang from his earliest years, but when he was a teenager he began playing guitar, then eventually the banjo.[\[7\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Elliott_Whitmore#cite_note-Westword-AnimalsInTheDark-2009-7)

In addition to playing locally with a cousin, Whitmore, after a short stint in San Francisco, moved to [Iowa City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iowa_City "Iowa City") and got involved in the punk rock and the [DIY scene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DIY_ethic "DIY ethic"), forming a band called Lost Cause.[\[7\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Elliott_Whitmore#cite_note-Westword-AnimalsInTheDark-2009-7)

In 1996, Whitmore graduated from [Central Lee High School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Lee_High_School "Central Lee High School") in [Donnellson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Donnellson,_Iowa "Donnellson, Iowa"), Iowa.[\[9\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Elliott_Whitmore#cite_note-DailyGateCity-PrairieYieldsPlow-2001-9)

## Career

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=William_Elliott_Whitmore&action=edit&section=2 "Edit section: Career")\]

When he was first starting out, Whitmore was a roadie for an [Iowa City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iowa_City "Iowa City") punk band called Ten Grand, but he eventually started performing as an opener for the band and toured extensively with Ten Grand.[\[10\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Elliott_Whitmore#cite_note-LittleVillage-TenGrand-2013-10) This led to him getting signed to the Chicago-based office of [Southern Records](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_Records "Southern Records").[\[7\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Elliott_Whitmore#cite_note-Westword-AnimalsInTheDark-2009-7) Whitmore said he turned to songwriting as a way to deal with the deaths of his parents, who died within a few years of each other, and the deaths of his grandparents.[\[11\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Elliott_Whitmore#cite_note-OnMilwaukee-HalwaysAlways-SongBlackbird-2007-11)

Whitmore built a long-term relationship with [Southern Records](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_Records "Southern Records"), releasing three records on the label. The three records on Southern, 2003's _Hymns for the Hopeless,_ 2005's _Ashes to Dust,_ and 2006's _Song of the Blackbird,_ were a trilogy that were focused on death and his experience processing the deaths of those close to him.[\[12\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Elliott_Whitmore#cite_note-SittingIn-AnimalsInTheDark-2009-12) He recorded the records with Mike Lust at Phantom Manor in Chicago.[\[11\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Elliott_Whitmore#cite_note-OnMilwaukee-HalwaysAlways-SongBlackbird-2007-11)

In 2009, Whitmore signed with mini-major [ANTI-](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ANTI- "ANTI-"), where he released three records.[\[7\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Elliott_Whitmore#cite_note-Westword-AnimalsInTheDark-2009-7) The first record for ANTI-, 2009's _Animals in the Dark,_ was a concept album. It reached Number 50 on the _[Billboard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Billboard_(magazine) "Billboard (magazine)")_ Heatseekers [chart](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Record_chart "Record chart").

The song "Civilizations" off his 2015 record, _Radium Death,_ is about how an [oil pipeline](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dakota_Access_Pipeline "Dakota Access Pipeline") running from South Dakota to Illinois was going to go through his grandmother's property on their farm.[\[13\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Elliott_Whitmore#cite_note-StLouisMag-RadiumDeath-2016-13)

In 2016, Whitmore worked with gold-selling recording artist [Esmé Patterson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Esm%C3%A9_Patterson "Esmé Patterson"), whom he had toured with in 2015,[\[14\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Elliott_Whitmore#cite_note-14) to create a record in which they each played a song written by the other on each side of a split 7" double-sided single, released by [Bloodshot Records](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bloodshot_Records "Bloodshot Records")[\[15\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Elliott_Whitmore#cite_note-15) in November, 2016 and acquired by [Rough Trade Records](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rough_Trade_Records "Rough Trade Records") as a limited edition printing.[\[16\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Elliott_Whitmore#cite_note-16) Entitled _Play Each Other's Songs_, the A side features Whitmore covering [Esmé Patterson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Esm%C3%A9_Patterson "Esmé Patterson")'s song "Elysium", and Patterson in turn covering Whitmore's "Not Feeling Any Pain" on the B side.[\[17\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Elliott_Whitmore#cite_note-Summary_-_Release_Code_r9413041-17) The record garnered favorable reviews by critics and a video shot in downtown [Los Angeles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Los_Angeles "Los Angeles") featuring Patterson dancing to her cover of Whitmore's song.[\[18\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Elliott_Whitmore#cite_note-18)

In April 2018, Whitmore signed with [Bloodshot Records](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bloodshot_Records "Bloodshot Records"), with a new release expected in the fall of 2018.[\[19\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Elliott_Whitmore#cite_note-Bloodshot-Signing-2018-19)[\[20\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Elliott_Whitmore#cite_note-DesMoinesRegister-BloodshotSigning-2018-20)

The 2018 record is a covers record called _Kilonova._ Covers include songs by [Bad Religion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bad_Religion "Bad Religion"), [Bill Withers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bill_Withers "Bill Withers"), [Captain Beefheart](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Captain_Beefheart "Captain Beefheart"), [Johnny Cash](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johnny_Cash "Johnny Cash"), [The Magnetic Fields](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Magnetic_Fields "The Magnetic Fields"), and [ZZ Top](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ZZ_Top "ZZ Top").[\[21\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Elliott_Whitmore#cite_note-BrooklynVegan-Kilonova-2018-21)

Whitmore has toured with [Chris Cornell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chris_Cornell "Chris Cornell"), [City and Colour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City_and_Colour "City and Colour"), [Murder by Death](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Murder_by_Death_(band) "Murder by Death (band)"), [Clutch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clutch_(band) "Clutch (band)"), [Lucero](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lucero_(band) "Lucero (band)"), [Converge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Converge_(band) "Converge (band)"), [Red Sparowes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Red_Sparowes "Red Sparowes"), [Modern Life is War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Modern_Life_is_War "Modern Life is War"), [Frontier Ruckus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frontier_Ruckus "Frontier Ruckus"), [Frank Turner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frank_Turner "Frank Turner"), [Esmé Patterson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Esm%C3%A9_Patterson "Esmé Patterson") and [The Low Anthem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Low_Anthem "The Low Anthem").\[ _[citation needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed "Wikipedia:Citation needed")_\]

His live act features Whitmore's distinctive deep [baritone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baritone "Baritone") voice,[\[2\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Elliott_Whitmore#cite_note-People-RadiumDeath-2015-2)[\[22\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Elliott_Whitmore#cite_note-Noisey-MissionCreekFestival-2014-22) and features Whitmore playing the banjo or guitar while singing, though on occasion he performs [a cappella](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A_cappella "A cappella"). He sometimes tours with a full band, but often plays roots-folk music as a solo act.[\[7\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Elliott_Whitmore#cite_note-Westword-AnimalsInTheDark-2009-7)

Two documentaries about [Richard Proenneke](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_Proenneke "Richard Proenneke"), "From the Heartland to the Great Northwest" and "The Living Wilderness" are narrated by Whitmore. For years, William's grandmother, Edith Tweedy, was a pen pal of Richard's.

Whitmore has a long-term musical relationship with his cousin Luke Tweedy, head engineer and producer at Iowa City's Flat Black Studios.[\[23\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Elliott_Whitmore#cite_note-ANTI-RadiumDeath-EPK-2015-23)

### Middle Western

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=William_Elliott_Whitmore&action=edit&section=3 "Edit section: Middle Western")\]

In 2016, Whitmore and musician and producer [David Zollo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Zollo "David Zollo") formed the folk band Middle Western.[\[24\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Elliott_Whitmore#cite_note-IowaPublicRadio-MiddleWestern-GreatIAPotluck-2016-24) An upcoming record is planned.[\[25\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Elliott_Whitmore#cite_note-QuadCityTimes-MiddleWestern-2017-25) Other members of Middle Western include Stephen Howard (guitar, bass), Stevie Doyle (guitar, bass), and Brian Cooper (drums).[\[26\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Elliott_Whitmore#cite_note-IowaPublicRadio-MiddleWestern-2016-26)

## Personal life

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=William_Elliott_Whitmore&action=edit&section=4 "Edit section: Personal life")\]

Whitmore is married and lives on the farm where he grew up. His daughter was born in March 2020.[\[27\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Elliott_Whitmore#cite_note-WEW-Bio-27)[\[28\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Elliott_Whitmore#cite_note-DowntownAtDawn-Wife-2013-28)[\[29\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Elliott_Whitmore#cite_note-29)

Whitmore has stated that he is an [atheist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atheism "Atheism").[\[13\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Elliott_Whitmore#cite_note-StLouisMag-RadiumDeath-2016-13)
