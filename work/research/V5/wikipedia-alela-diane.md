Musical artist

**Alela Diane Menig** (born April 20, 1983), known as **Alela Diane**, is an American singer-songwriter from [Nevada City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nevada_City,_California "Nevada City, California"), [California](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/California "California").

## Early life and education

Diane was born in [Nevada City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nevada_City,_California "Nevada City, California"), [California](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/California "California") on April 20, 1983. She grew up singing with her musician parents and performing in the school choir.[\[1\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alela_Diane#cite_note-Holocenemusic-1) She taught herself guitar, began writing songs and her first recordings was released in 2003 as _Forest Parade_. Diane was invited by [Joanna Newsom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joanna_Newsom "Joanna Newsom"), also from Nevada City, to make her first solo public appearances and later played with the Nevada City band Black Bear before continuing her solo career.[\[2\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alela_Diane#cite_note-Bio-2)

## Career

[![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/0/09/Alela_Diane_%281%29.jpg/500px-Alela_Diane_%281%29.jpg)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Alela_Diane_(1).jpg) Diane in Tivoli De Helling, 2009

The songs for her album _[The Pirate's Gospel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Pirate's_Gospel "The Pirate's Gospel")_ were written on a trip to [Europe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Europe "Europe"). They were recorded in her father’s studio and were initially self-released in 2004, in paper and lace sleeves with hand lettering. The album was issued in revised form by Holocene Music[\[1\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alela_Diane#cite_note-Holocenemusic-1) in October 2006, and received widespread critical acclaim.[\[3\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alela_Diane#cite_note-3)[\[4\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alela_Diane#cite_note-4)[\[5\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alela_Diane#cite_note-5)[\[6\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alela_Diane#cite_note-6)[\[7\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alela_Diane#cite_note-7)

A new song, "Dry Grass and Shadows", was issued on a compilation of Nevada City artists,[\[8\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alela_Diane#cite_note-8) and five more new songs were issued on a limited-edition 10" vinyl pressing, _Songs Whistled Through White Teeth_, released in the [United Kingdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Kingdom "United Kingdom") in October 2006. _The Pirate's Gospel_ was released in the UK on Names Records[\[9\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alela_Diane#cite_note-9) in April 2007, garnering favorable reviews in _[The Times](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Times "The Times")_[\[10\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alela_Diane#cite_note-10) and _[NME](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NME "NME")_.[\[11\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alela_Diane#cite_note-11)

She toured the U.S. both solo and with [Tom Brosseau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tom_Brosseau "Tom Brosseau"), and opened for [Iron & Wine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iron_&_Wine "Iron & Wine"), [Akron/Family](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Akron/Family "Akron/Family"), [the Decemberists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Decemberists "The Decemberists") and [Vashti Bunyan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vashti_Bunyan "Vashti Bunyan"). She also toured extensively in Europe (UK, [Ireland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ireland "Ireland"), [France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/France "France"), [Switzerland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Switzerland "Switzerland"), [Netherlands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Netherlands "Netherlands"), [Belgium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belgium "Belgium"), [Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Germany "Germany")) in the spring of 2008.[\[12\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alela_Diane#cite_note-12)

She sang on an album of cover songs, _The Silence of Love_, released in November 2008, recorded by Eddie Bezalel and Hugo Nicholson with musicians [Josh Klinghoffer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Josh_Klinghoffer "Josh Klinghoffer"), [Joey Waronker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joey_Waronker "Joey Waronker"), Gus Seyffert, [Leo Abrahams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leo_Abrahams "Leo Abrahams") and [Woody Jackson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Woody_Jackson "Woody Jackson") under the name Headless Heroes.[\[13\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alela_Diane#cite_note-13)

Her second album, _[To Be Still](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/To_Be_Still "To Be Still")_, was released in February 2009 on [Rough Trade Records](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rough_Trade_Records "Rough Trade Records").[\[14\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alela_Diane#cite_note-14)[\[15\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alela_Diane#cite_note-15)[\[16\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alela_Diane#cite_note-16) In early 2009, she toured the US opening for [Blitzen Trapper](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blitzen_Trapper "Blitzen Trapper"), and spent the better part of that year touring Europe.

Her third album, _[Alela Diane & Wild Divine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alela_Diane_&_Wild_Divine "Alela Diane & Wild Divine")_, was released in April 2011, and was recorded with a backing band, Wild Divine, which included her father, Tom Menig, and her then-husband, Tom Bevitori. She and Wild Divine toured the US and Europe to promote the album, and in July 2011, they opened for the [Fleet Foxes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fleet_Foxes "Fleet Foxes") on a string of dates. In the fall of the same year, she also accompanied Fleet Foxes as their opening act in Europe.

In 2012, her song "Take Us Back" was featured on the end credits of the "No Time Left", the fifth and final episode of the adventure game _[The Walking Dead](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Walking_Dead_(2012_video_game) "The Walking Dead (2012 video game)")_ by [Telltale Games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Telltale_Games "Telltale Games"). In 2019, the song was once again used near the end of _[The Walking Dead: The Final Season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Walking_Dead:_The_Final_Season "The Walking Dead: The Final Season")_ episode "Take us Back".

Her fourth album, _[About Farewell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/About_Farewell "About Farewell")_, was released on her own label, Rusted Blue Records, in digital format in June 2013, with the physical edition issued in July.[\[17\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alela_Diane#cite_note-17)

In 2014, the track "The Light" appeared on _[The Walking Dead](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Walking_Dead_(TV_series) "The Walking Dead (TV series)")_ fourth season soundtrack, _[Songs of Survival Vol. 2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Walking_Dead:_Songs_of_Survival_Vol._2 "The Walking Dead: Songs of Survival Vol. 2")_.

On October 16, 2015, Diane and guitarist Ryan Francesconi released the album _Cold Moon_, featuring the "thoughtful guitar picking of Francesconi \[and\] Diane’s naturalist, poetic lyrics".[\[18\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alela_Diane#cite_note-18)

Preceded by the single "Émigré", her fifth album, _Cusp_, was released on February 9, 2018.[\[19\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alela_Diane#cite_note-19)

## Personal life

Diane married Tom Bevitori in 2011.[\[20\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alela_Diane#cite_note-sfexam-20) They divorced and she married Toren Volkmann in August 2013.[\[20\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alela_Diane#cite_note-sfexam-20)[\[21\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alela_Diane#cite_note-21) They have two daughters, Vera Marie (born October 2013)[\[22\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alela_Diane#cite_note-22) and Oona (born February 2017).[\[23\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alela_Diane#cite_note-23)
