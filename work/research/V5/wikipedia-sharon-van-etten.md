Musical artist

**Sharon Katharine Van Etten** (born February 26, 1981) is an American singer-songwriter, musician and actress. Originally from [New Jersey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Jersey "New Jersey"), and currently based in [Los Angeles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Los_Angeles "Los Angeles"), Van Etten's music is noted for its [indie rock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indie_rock "Indie rock") aesthetic and personal lyrical content. In the studio and during live performances, Van Etten is currently accompanied by her backing band, **the Attachment Theory**, which consists of [Devra Hoff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Devra_Hoff "Devra Hoff") (bass guitar, backing vocals), Teeny Lieberson (keyboards, guitar, backing vocals) and Jorge Balbi (drums).

To date, Van Etten has released seven studio albums: _[Because I Was in Love](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Because_I_Was_in_Love "Because I Was in Love")_ (2009), _[Epic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Epic_(Sharon_Van_Etten_album) "Epic (Sharon Van Etten album)")_ (2010), _[Tramp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tramp_(album) "Tramp (album)")_ (2012), _[Are We There](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Are_We_There "Are We There")_ (2014), _[Remind Me Tomorrow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Remind_Me_Tomorrow "Remind Me Tomorrow")_ (2019), _[We've Been Going About This All Wrong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/We've_Been_Going_About_This_All_Wrong "We've Been Going About This All Wrong")_ (2022) and _[Sharon Van Etten & the Attachment Theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sharon_Van_Etten_&_the_Attachment_Theory "Sharon Van Etten & the Attachment Theory")_ (2025).

As an actress, Van Etten starred as Rachel in both series of the [Netflix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Netflix "Netflix") [mystery drama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mystery_film "Mystery film") _[The OA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_OA "The OA")_ (2016–2019), and has appeared in the feature films _[Never Rarely Sometimes Always](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Never_Rarely_Sometimes_Always "Never Rarely Sometimes Always")_ (2020) and _[How It Ends](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/How_It_Ends_(2021_film) "How It Ends (2021 film)")_ (2021).

## Early life

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Sharon_Van_Etten&action=edit&section=1 "Edit section: Early life")\]

Van Etten was born in [Belleville, New Jersey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belleville,_New_Jersey "Belleville, New Jersey"), the middle child of five.[\[2\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sharon_Van_Etten#cite_note-nytimes_2019-01-03-2) She lived in Nutley, New Jersey, then moved to Clinton, New Jersey as a pre-teen. Van Etten attended [North Hunterdon High School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Hunterdon_High_School "North Hunterdon High School"), at which she participated in the chorus and performed in stage musicals.[\[3\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sharon_Van_Etten#cite_note-3)[\[4\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sharon_Van_Etten#cite_note-SVE-NPR-Bucket-2011-4)[\[5\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sharon_Van_Etten#cite_note-5)[\[6\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sharon_Van_Etten#cite_note-6)

Later, Van Etten moved to [Murfreesboro, Tennessee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Murfreesboro,_Tennessee "Murfreesboro, Tennessee") to attend [Middle Tennessee State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Middle_Tennessee_State_University "Middle Tennessee State University") and studied recording, but dropped out after a year. She ended up working at the Red Rose, a coffee and record shop and music venue in Murfreesboro for about five years. She fell into an abusive relationship with a rock musician who discouraged her from writing songs. After five years, she left in the middle of the night with whatever she could carry.[\[7\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sharon_Van_Etten#cite_note-NYTimes-RoughHarm-2012-7)

Van Etten lived in [Brooklyn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brooklyn "Brooklyn"), New York for a number of years; she resided in the neighborhood of [Ditmas Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ditmas_Park,_Brooklyn "Ditmas Park, Brooklyn").[\[7\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sharon_Van_Etten#cite_note-NYTimes-RoughHarm-2012-7)

### 2007–2010: _Because I Was in Love_ and _Epic_

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Sharon_Van_Etten&action=edit&section=3 "Edit section: 2007–2010: Because I Was in Love and Epic")\]

Main articles: [Because I Was in Love](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Because_I_Was_in_Love "Because I Was in Love") and [Epic (Sharon Van Etten album)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Epic_(Sharon_Van_Etten_album) "Epic (Sharon Van Etten album)")

Van Etten self-released handmade CDs until 2009, when her debut studio recording was released.[\[8\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sharon_Van_Etten#cite_note-Pitchfork-BIWiL-2009-8)[\[9\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sharon_Van_Etten#cite_note-Quietus-2012-9) Before her studio debut, she worked at Astor Wines and as a publicist at [Ba Da Bing Records](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ba_Da_Bing_Records "Ba Da Bing Records").[\[10\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sharon_Van_Etten#cite_note-ljs31Mar2011-10)

Van Etten's debut, _[Because I Was in Love](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Because_I_Was_in_Love "Because I Was in Love")_, was released on May 26, 2009, on Language of Stone, and was manufactured and distributed by [Drag City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drag_City_(record_label) "Drag City (record label)"). _Because I Was in Love_ was produced by [Greg Weeks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greg_Weeks "Greg Weeks") at Hexham Head studio in [Philadelphia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philadelphia "Philadelphia").[\[8\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sharon_Van_Etten#cite_note-Pitchfork-BIWiL-2009-8)

On September 21, 2010, Van Etten released her second studio album, _[Epic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Epic_(Sharon_Van_Etten_album) "Epic (Sharon Van Etten album)"),_ on Ba Da Bing Records. With no set band at the time, Van Etten called on friends Jeffrey Kish, Dave Hartley, Jessica Larrabee, and Andy LaPlant of [She Keeps Bees](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/She_Keeps_Bees "She Keeps Bees"), Cat Martino, [Meg Baird](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meg_Baird "Meg Baird"), Jim Callan, and [Brian Christinzio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B.C._Camplight "B.C. Camplight"). The first song recorded for the album was "Love More", recorded in December 2009 by record producer Brian McTear for Weathervane Music's Shaking Through documentary video series. The remainder of the album was produced by Brian McTear with engineer Amy Morrissey in May 2010 at Miner Street Recordings in Philadelphia. [NPR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NPR "NPR") described it as possessing "a fuller sound compared to the super-spare arrangements on her first two self-produced albums, but _Epic_ still feels incredibly intimate, with lots of room to breathe and unfold."[\[11\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sharon_Van_Etten#cite_note-NPR-Epic-1stListen-2010-11)

### 2011–2015: _Tramp_ and _Are We There_

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Sharon_Van_Etten&action=edit&section=4 "Edit section: 2011–2015: Tramp and Are We There")\]

Main articles: [Tramp (album)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tramp_(album) "Tramp (album)") and [Are We There](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Are_We_There "Are We There")

[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/e/ee/Sharon_Van_Etten_%28January_6%2C_2013%29.jpg/250px-Sharon_Van_Etten_%28January_6%2C_2013%29.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Sharon_Van_Etten_(January_6,_2013).jpg) Van Etten performing in 2013

Van Etten's third studio album, _[Tramp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tramp_(album) "Tramp (album)")_, was released on February 7, 2012, on [Jagjaguwar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jagjaguwar "Jagjaguwar"). _Tramp_ was produced by [the National](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_National_(band) "The National (band)")'s [Aaron Dessner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aaron_Dessner "Aaron Dessner") and recorded in his home studio in [Brooklyn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brooklyn "Brooklyn"), New York.[\[12\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sharon_Van_Etten#cite_note-AmSongwriter-2012-12) Additional recording took place at Miner Street Recordings in Philadelphia, where the album was also mixed with engineers and mixers Brian McTear and Jonathan Low. The album features musicians Doug Keith, Thomas Bartlett, [Bryan Devendorf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bryan_Devendorf "Bryan Devendorf"), [Bryce Dessner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bryce_Dessner "Bryce Dessner"), [Matt Barrick](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Walkmen "The Walkmen"), [Rob Moose](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rob_Moose "Rob Moose"), [Julianna Barwick](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Julianna_Barwick "Julianna Barwick"), [Peter Silberman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Antlers_(band) "The Antlers (band)"), Logan Coale, Clarice Jensen, [Ben Lanz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beirut_(band) "Beirut (band)"), [Zach Condon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beirut_(band) "Beirut (band)"), and [Jenn Wasner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wye_Oak_(band) "Wye Oak (band)").[\[13\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sharon_Van_Etten#cite_note-AVClub-2011-13)

May 2014 brought about the release of Van Etten's fourth studio album, titled _[Are We There](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Are_We_There "Are We There")_, on Jagjaguwar.[\[14\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sharon_Van_Etten#cite_note-Tumbler-FYeah-2013-14) Van Etten produced the record with [Stewart Lerman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stewart_Lerman "Stewart Lerman"), with the guidance of bandmate and manager Zeke Hutchins. Most of the recording was done at Hobo Sound Studios in [Weehawken, New Jersey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weehawken,_New_Jersey "Weehawken, New Jersey"), with piano tracks being recorded at [Electric Lady Studios](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electric_Lady_Studios "Electric Lady Studios") in [New York City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_City "New York City").[\[15\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sharon_Van_Etten#cite_note-CoS-AWT-2014-15) The record features musicians Zeke Hutchins, Doug Keith, [Heather Woods Broderick](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heather_Woods_Broderick "Heather Woods Broderick"), Dave Hartley, [Adam Granduciel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adam_Granduciel "Adam Granduciel"), [Marisa Anderson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marisa_Anderson "Marisa Anderson"), [Stuart Bogie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stuart_Bogie "Stuart Bogie"), Mickey Free, Mary Lattimore, Little Isidor, Jacob Morris, [Torres](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Torres_(musician) "Torres (musician)")' Mackenzie Scott, [Shearwater](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shearwater_(band) "Shearwater (band)")'s [Jonathan Meiburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jonathan_Meiburg "Jonathan Meiburg"), [Lower Dens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lower_Dens "Lower Dens")' [Jana Hunter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jana_Hunter "Jana Hunter"), and [Efterklang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Efterklang "Efterklang") touring member [Peter Broderick](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_Broderick "Peter Broderick"). The [extended play](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Extended_play "Extended play") (EP), _I Don't Want to Let You Down,_ a compilation of songs that were not included on _Are We There,_ was released on Jagjaguwar in 2015.

### 2016–2022: _Remind Me Tomorrow_ and _We've Been Going About This All Wrong_

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Sharon_Van_Etten&action=edit&section=5 "Edit section: 2016–2022: Remind Me Tomorrow and We've Been Going About This All Wrong")\]

Main articles: [Remind Me Tomorrow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Remind_Me_Tomorrow "Remind Me Tomorrow") and [We've Been Going About This All Wrong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/We've_Been_Going_About_This_All_Wrong "We've Been Going About This All Wrong")

After the release of _Are We There_, Van Etten took some time away from music.[\[16\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sharon_Van_Etten#cite_note-npr_2022-05-06-16) She pursued acting, had a child, and applied to [Brooklyn College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brooklyn_College "Brooklyn College") to study psychology and mental health counseling.[\[17\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sharon_Van_Etten#cite_note-lamag_2019-02-17-17)[\[2\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sharon_Van_Etten#cite_note-nytimes_2019-01-03-2) Van Etten composed the score for the film _[Strange Weather](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strange_Weather_(film) "Strange Weather (film)")_ (2016), working on the music in a practice space she shared with [Michael Cera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_Cera "Michael Cera"). In between working on the music for the film she recorded dozens of [demos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Demo_(music) "Demo (music)") for new songs, which would form the basis for her next album.[\[2\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sharon_Van_Etten#cite_note-nytimes_2019-01-03-2)

In 2018, Van Etten released a new track, "Comeback Kid", and announced her next studio album _[Remind Me Tomorrow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Remind_Me_Tomorrow "Remind Me Tomorrow")_, released on January 18, 2019.[\[18\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sharon_Van_Etten#cite_note-18) _Remind Me Tomorrow_ was a departure from Van Etten's previous guitar-focused work, featuring more synthesizers, drums, and experimental sounds.[\[2\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sharon_Van_Etten#cite_note-nytimes_2019-01-03-2)[\[17\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sharon_Van_Etten#cite_note-lamag_2019-02-17-17)

In 2019, Van Etten moved with her son and partner to Los Angeles, California. She stated her desire with the move was to settle down and diversify her career to be less reliant on traveling tours.[\[19\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sharon_Van_Etten#cite_note-nme_2022-04-15-19) The [COVID-19 pandemic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/COVID-19_pandemic "COVID-19 pandemic") in 2020 scuttled those plans, and during the resulting [lockdowns](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/COVID-19_lockdowns "COVID-19 lockdowns") Van Etten wrote songs that focused on her new life as well as the wider political landscape and "collective trauma" people were experiencing.[\[19\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sharon_Van_Etten#cite_note-nme_2022-04-15-19)

Van Etten played bass guitar and sang harmony vocals as the three surviving members of the rock band [Fountains of Wayne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fountains_of_Wayne "Fountains of Wayne") performed in a televised benefit with various New Jersey-affiliated musicians to raise funds for COVID-19 relief in April 2020. She filled the role left vacant by the COVID-19-related death of [Adam Schlesinger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adam_Schlesinger "Adam Schlesinger") a few weeks earlier. She and the other three members of the band played simultaneously from remote locations. The band played the song "Hackensack" from their third studio album _[Welcome Interstate Managers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Welcome_Interstate_Managers "Welcome Interstate Managers")_ (2003).[\[20\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sharon_Van_Etten#cite_note-20)

On May 15, 2020, Van Etten released a cover version of [Brinsley Schwarz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brinsley_Schwarz "Brinsley Schwarz")'s 1974 song " [(What's So Funny 'Bout) Peace, Love, and Understanding](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/(What's_So_Funny_'Bout)_Peace,_Love,_and_Understanding "(What's So Funny 'Bout) Peace, Love, and Understanding")" with [Queens of the Stone Age](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Queens_of_the_Stone_Age "Queens of the Stone Age") frontman [Josh Homme](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Josh_Homme "Josh Homme").[\[21\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sharon_Van_Etten#cite_note-21)

In October 2020, Van Etten made a song titled "Let Go" for the documentary film, _[Feels Good Man](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Feels_Good_Man "Feels Good Man")_, directed by Arthur Jones about cartoonist [Matt Furie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matt_Furie "Matt Furie"), the creator of the Internet meme [Pepe the Frog](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pepe_the_Frog "Pepe the Frog").[\[22\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sharon_Van_Etten#cite_note-22)

On November 16, 2020, Van Etten released two cover versions of traditional holiday songs, " [Silent Night](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silent_Night "Silent Night")" and " [Blue Christmas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blue_Christmas_(song) "Blue Christmas (song)")".[\[23\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sharon_Van_Etten#cite_note-23)

On May 20, 2021, Van Etten released a single with [Angel Olsen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Angel_Olsen "Angel Olsen"), " [Like I Used To](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Like_I_Used_To_(Sharon_Van_Etten_and_Angel_Olsen_song) "Like I Used To (Sharon Van Etten and Angel Olsen song)")," which was produced by [John Congleton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Congleton "John Congleton"). Olsen and Van Etten appear in the music video with their hair styled in similar shag haircuts.[\[24\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sharon_Van_Etten#cite_note-24)

In July 2021, Van Etten took part in the [Newport Folk Festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Newport_Folk_Festival "Newport Folk Festival").[\[25\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sharon_Van_Etten#cite_note-25)

On May 6, 2022, Van Etten released her sixth studio album _[We've Been Going About This All Wrong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/We've_Been_Going_About_This_All_Wrong "We've Been Going About This All Wrong")_.[\[26\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sharon_Van_Etten#cite_note-26)

### 2023–present: _Sharon Van Etten & the Attachment Theory_

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Sharon_Van_Etten&action=edit&section=6 "Edit section: 2023–present: Sharon Van Etten & the Attachment Theory")\]

Main article: [Sharon Van Etten & the Attachment Theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sharon_Van_Etten_&_the_Attachment_Theory "Sharon Van Etten & the Attachment Theory")

On August 23, 2024, ten years after the initial release of her fourth studio album _Are We There_, Van Etten released the single "Every Time the Sun Comes Up" (Alternate Version) on 7 inch vinyl.[\[27\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sharon_Van_Etten#cite_note-27)

In February 2025, she released _[Sharon Van Etten & the Attachment Theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sharon_Van_Etten_&_the_Attachment_Theory "Sharon Van Etten & the Attachment Theory")_ which received "universal praise" on Metacritic.

## Influences and musical style

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Sharon_Van_Etten&action=edit&section=7 "Edit section: Influences and musical style")\]

Van Etten cites [Ani DiFranco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ani_DiFranco "Ani DiFranco") as a key influence, saying, "She was the first musician I had ever heard whose songs were super confessional. She could really play guitar... That was my first experience with non-pop female musicians. She made me want to start playing more."[\[28\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sharon_Van_Etten#cite_note-Jones2010-28)

Van Etten possesses a [contralto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Contralto "Contralto") vocal range, which Caleb Caldwell of _[Slant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slant_Magazine "Slant Magazine")_ described as "husky".[\[29\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sharon_Van_Etten#cite_note-29) NPR described her vocals as raspy, elegant and luminous,[\[30\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sharon_Van_Etten#cite_note-30) while _[Consequence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Consequence_(publication) "Consequence (publication)")_ called it "earthy".[\[31\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sharon_Van_Etten#cite_note-31) Music journalists of _[The Guardian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Guardian "The Guardian")_, _[Rolling Stone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rolling_Stone "Rolling Stone")_ and _[The Independent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Independent "The Independent")_ wrote that Van Etten's voice and singing style sometimes evoked [Siouxsie Sioux](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siouxsie_Sioux "Siouxsie Sioux")'s of [Siouxsie and the Banshees](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siouxsie_and_the_Banshees "Siouxsie and the Banshees").[\[32\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sharon_Van_Etten#cite_note-32) Van Etten's music is characterized by a heavy use of harmonies.[\[7\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sharon_Van_Etten#cite_note-NYTimes-RoughHarm-2012-7) _[Pitchfork](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pitchfork_(website) "Pitchfork (website)")_ described her songs as having "echoes of [folk tradition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Folk_music#Traditional_folk_music "Folk music")."[\[8\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sharon_Van_Etten#cite_note-Pitchfork-BIWiL-2009-8) [NPR Music](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NPR_Music "NPR Music") asserts: "Her songs are heartfelt without being overly earnest; her poetry is plainspoken but not overt, and her elegant voice is wrapped in enough rasp and sorrow to keep from sounding too pure or confident."[\[11\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sharon_Van_Etten#cite_note-NPR-Epic-1stListen-2010-11) With "Comeback Kid" and _Remind Me Tomorrow_, Van Etten introduced electronic sounds into her music.[\[33\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sharon_Van_Etten#cite_note-pitchfork-33) She has said, "I listen to a lot of [OMD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orchestral_Manoeuvres_in_the_Dark "Orchestral Manoeuvres in the Dark")... I'm into a lot of the new post-punk electronic stuff."[\[28\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sharon_Van_Etten#cite_note-Jones2010-28)

Van Etten has also cited [Scott Walker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scott_Walker_(singer) "Scott Walker (singer)"), [Cocteau Twins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cocteau_Twins "Cocteau Twins"), [Rowland S. Howard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rowland_S._Howard "Rowland S. ## Acting

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Sharon_Van_Etten&action=edit&section=8 "Edit section: Acting")\]

Since 2016, Van Etten has appeared in both seasons of the Netflix drama _[The OA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_OA "The OA")_ as Rachel, a fellow abductee along with Prairie in Dr. Percy's basement lab/terrarium.[\[2\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sharon_Van_Etten#cite_note-nytimes_2019-01-03-2) Rachel and the other captives are subjected to after-life experiments while conspiring over a period of years to possibly escape, and at one point, Rachel sings a song of remembrance. Van Etten also appeared in episode six of the 2017 _[Twin Peaks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twin_Peaks_season_3 "Twin Peaks season 3")_ series on [Showtime](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Showtime_(TV_network) "Showtime (TV network)"), in which she performed the song “Tarifa” from her album _[Are We There](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Are_We_There "Are We There")_.[\[36\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sharon_Van_Etten#cite_note-36)

Van Etten made her feature film debut with a supporting role in the 2020 film _[Never Rarely Sometimes Always](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Never_Rarely_Sometimes_Always "Never Rarely Sometimes Always")_ directed by [Eliza Hittman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eliza_Hittman "Eliza Hittman"), for which she also wrote and performed the original track "Staring at a Mountain".[\[37\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sharon_Van_Etten#cite_note-37)

Van Etten also appeared in the 2021 film _[How It Ends](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/How_It_Ends_(2021_film) "How It Ends (2021 film)")_ as Jet. The film featured two new songs by Van Etten, "How Much I Loved You" and "Till We Meet Again".[\[38\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sharon_Van_Etten#cite_note-38)

In 2026, she is a guest star from the children's television in _Yo Gabba Gabbaland_ in Season 2.

## Personal life

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Sharon_Van_Etten&action=edit&section=9 "Edit section: Personal life")\]

Van Etten had her first child, a son, in 2017 with husband Zeke Hutchins. Hutchins used to be her drummer and then became her manager. After living in New York City for 15 years, she moved with her family to Los Angeles in September 2019.[\[2\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sharon_Van_Etten#cite_note-nytimes_2019-01-03-2)[\[39\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sharon_Van_Etten#cite_note-39)
