Musical artist

**Laura Anne Gibson** (born August 9, 1979)[\[1\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laura_Gibson#cite_note-1) is an American singer-songwriter. She currently records for the U.S. independent label [Barsuk Records](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barsuk_Records "Barsuk Records"), and the Berlin-based label [City Slang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City_Slang "City Slang"). Gibson's most recent album _Goners_ was released October 26, 2018.

In addition to her solo work, Gibson has released a collaboration album with Ethan Rose, titled _Bridge Carols_, in 2009.

## Early life

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Laura_Gibson&action=edit&section=1 "Edit section: Early life")\]

Gibson was born and raised in the coastal logging town of [Coquille, Oregon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coquille,_Oregon "Coquille, Oregon"). She was classically trained on [cello](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cello "Cello") in her youth.[\[2\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laura_Gibson#cite_note-bio-2) Gibson's father died of cancer in her childhood.[\[3\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laura_Gibson#cite_note-fc-3) She attended [Linfield College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linfield_College "Linfield College") in [McMinnville, Oregon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/McMinnville,_Oregon "McMinnville, Oregon") where she completed pre-medicine requirements and competed for the Women's basketball and Track and Field teams.[\[3\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laura_Gibson#cite_note-fc-3)

## Career

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Laura_Gibson&action=edit&section=2 "Edit section: Career")\]

### 2004–2011

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Laura_Gibson&action=edit&section=3 "Edit section: 2004–2011")\]

Gibson relocated to [Portland, Oregon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portland,_Oregon "Portland, Oregon")[\[4\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laura_Gibson#cite_note-4) after graduating from college, and worked odd jobs prior to composing music.[\[3\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laura_Gibson#cite_note-fc-3) She began performing songs for patients at [hospice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hospice "Hospice") homes prior to writing her first [extended play](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Extended_play "Extended play"), _Amends_, which was released independently in 2004.[\[5\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laura_Gibson#cite_note-5) She released her debut album, _If You Come to Greet Me_ in 2006 on the Portland-based indie label [Hush Records](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hush_Records "Hush Records").[\[3\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laura_Gibson#cite_note-fc-3) Gibson collaborated with [The Decemberists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Decemberists "The Decemberists")'s drummer [Rachel Blumberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rachel_Blumberg "Rachel Blumberg") on the release, as well as on her second album, _Beasts of Seasons_.[\[2\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laura_Gibson#cite_note-bio-2)

Gibson performed at [South by Southwest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_by_Southwest "South by Southwest") in 2007 to promote _If You Come to Greet Me_, and was likened by critics to [Jolie Holland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jolie_Holland "Jolie Holland").[\[6\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laura_Gibson#cite_note-6) In 2008, she appeared on [NPR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NPR "NPR")'s inaugural installment of [Tiny Desk Concerts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tiny_Desk_Concerts "Tiny Desk Concerts").[\[7\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laura_Gibson#cite_note-7)[\[8\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laura_Gibson#cite_note-8) The concept for the Tiny Desk Concerts was conceived following one of her concerts that NPR music journalists [Stephen Thompson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stephen_Thompson_(journalist) "Stephen Thompson (journalist)") and [Bob Boilen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bob_Boilen "Bob Boilen") had attended, when they were unable to hear her performance over the noise of the crowd.[\[9\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laura_Gibson#cite_note-9) Boilen then arranged for Gibson to perform at his desk at the NPR office, and it subsequently evolved into a recurring feature.[\[10\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laura_Gibson#cite_note-:3-10) She would later reappear again at the 200th concert.[\[11\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laura_Gibson#cite_note-11)

In 2008, Gibson released _Six White Horses - Blues & Traditionals Vol. I_ via Portland-based indie label [Hush Records](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hush_Records "Hush Records"). [\[12\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laura_Gibson#cite_note-12) In 2009, she released a collaboration album with Ethan Rose, titled _Bridge Carols_, which featured experimental vocals and was recorded in various outdoor locations.[\[13\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laura_Gibson#cite_note-13)

### 2012–present

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Laura_Gibson&action=edit&section=4 "Edit section: 2012–present")\]

Her third release, _[La Grande](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/La_Grande_(album) "La Grande (album)")_ (2012), was named after and inspired by the city of [La Grande, Oregon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/La_Grande,_Oregon "La Grande, Oregon"),[\[2\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laura_Gibson#cite_note-bio-2) and received critical acclaim; _[Pitchfork](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pitchfork_Media "Pitchfork Media")_ wrote of the album: "Rather than another exercise in genre-dabbling and dilettantism, _La Grande_ succeeds as a cohesive work thanks to the persistence of Gibson's vision. As a songwriter she's preoccupied with those timeless questions of the human condition, but seldom if ever stumbles into pretension or self-satisfaction."[\[14\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laura_Gibson#cite_note-14)

Gibson has composed music and lyrics for multiple commercials, including a version of "Hey There Little Red Riding Hood" for [Volvo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volvo "Volvo"), as well as original music for [Microsoft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Microsoft "Microsoft") and the Humane Society.[\[15\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laura_Gibson#cite_note-15) In 2014 Gibson composed the song "Live Long in Oregon" for the Cover Oregon Campaign, the Oregon branch of the Affordable Health Care Act. The commercial was discussed on several national news outlets. After Oregon dropped its state run coverage, the commercial was parodied on the premiere episode of _[Last Week Tonight with John Oliver](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Last_Week_Tonight_with_John_Oliver "Last Week Tonight with John Oliver")_, featuring [Lisa Loeb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lisa_Loeb "Lisa Loeb") in Gibson's role.

In 2015, Gibson was principal composer and lyricist for Up the Fall, a musical production created for performers with developmental disabilities, for the Portland-based Non-Profit PHAME Academy. She has also collaborated with [Oregon Ballet Theatre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oregon_Ballet_Theatre "Oregon Ballet Theatre") and Portland Playhouse. On April 1, 2016, Gibson released her fourth album, _[Empire Builder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Empire_Builder_(album) "Empire Builder (album)")_.[\[16\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laura_Gibson#cite_note-16)

## Personal life

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Laura_Gibson&action=edit&section=5 "Edit section: Personal life")\]

In 2014, Gibson relocated from Portland, Oregon to [New York City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_City "New York City") to complete a [Master of Fine Arts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Master_of_Fine_Arts "Master of Fine Arts") in fiction writing at [Hunter College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hunter_College "Hunter College").[\[17\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laura_Gibson#cite_note-bell-17)

On March 26, 2015 Gibson's Manhattan apartment was destroyed in the [2015 East Village gas explosion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2015_East_Village_gas_explosion "2015 East Village gas explosion") which killed two people. Gibson was home at the time of the explosion but escaped unharmed. A recovery fund, established by friends and fans, raised several thousand dollars.[\[17\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laura_Gibson#cite_note-bell-17)
