Musical artist

**Jessica Pratt** (born April 24, 1987) is an American singer-songwriter. Her [self-titled debut album](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jessica_Pratt_(album) "Jessica Pratt (album)") was released in 2012 via Birth Records, a record label founded by White Fence songwriter [Tim Presley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tim_Presley "Tim Presley") to release Pratt's music. She is often associated with the [freak folk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Freak_folk "Freak folk") movement.[\[5\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jessica_Pratt_(musician)#cite_note-impose-5)[\[6\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jessica_Pratt_(musician)#cite_note-p4k-review-6)[\[7\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jessica_Pratt_(musician)#cite_note-cos-review-7) Her second and third albums are _[On Your Own Love Again](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/On_Your_Own_Love_Again "On Your Own Love Again")_ (2015, [Drag City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drag_City_(record_label) "Drag City (record label)")) and _[Quiet Signs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quiet_Signs "Quiet Signs")_ (2019, [Mexican Summer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mexican_Summer "Mexican Summer")/ [City Slang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City_Slang "City Slang")). She released her fourth album, _[Here in the Pitch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Here_in_the_Pitch "Here in the Pitch")_, in 2024.

## Early life

Pratt grew up in [Redding](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Redding,_California "Redding, California").[\[8\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jessica_Pratt_(musician)#cite_note-:0-8) Pratt was raised by her mother, an astrologer, who exposed her to a broad range of artists, including [Tim Buckley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tim_Buckley "Tim Buckley"), [X](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/X_(American_band) "X (American band)"), and [the Gun Club](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Gun_Club "The Gun Club"). After her older brother gave up playing his [Stratocaster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fender_Stratocaster "Fender Stratocaster"), she learned to play the guitar around the age of 15. She took his guitar and started practicing with the 1971 [T. Rex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/T._Rex_(band) "T. Rex (band)") album _[Electric Warrior](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electric_Warrior "Electric Warrior")_. She was soon able to play the guitar parts of the record.[\[9\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jessica_Pratt_(musician)#cite_note-thefader-9) She began recording songs at the age of 16, using her mother's [Fender guitar amp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fender_Amplifiers "Fender Amplifiers") and microphone.[\[5\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jessica_Pratt_(musician)#cite_note-impose-5)

## Musical career

After she moved to [San Francisco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Francisco "San Francisco"), she was introduced to Tim Presley's solo project, White Fence, through Presley's brother, who was her roommate for three years.[\[10\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jessica_Pratt_(musician)#cite_note-bpm-10) In the following years, Presley heard Pratt's demo songs through her then-boyfriend, who had posted her songs on [Facebook](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Facebook "Facebook"). He eventually contacted her to release her music.[\[11\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jessica_Pratt_(musician)#cite_note-nowave-11)

Pratt's [self-titled debut album](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jessica_Pratt_(album) "Jessica Pratt (album)") was released in 2012 through Presley's label, Birth Records. The album featured the songs that were originally recorded in 2007 over [analogue tape](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reel-to-reel_audio_tape_recording "Reel-to-reel audio tape recording").[\[10\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jessica_Pratt_(musician)#cite_note-bpm-10) The initial 500 pressings of the album sold out in less than two weeks.[\[9\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jessica_Pratt_(musician)#cite_note-thefader-9) It received attention from music websites and magazines, including _[Pitchfork](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pitchfork_(website) "Pitchfork (website)")_,[\[6\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jessica_Pratt_(musician)#cite_note-p4k-review-6) _[Consequence of Sound](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Consequence_(publication) "Consequence (publication)")_[\[7\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jessica_Pratt_(musician)#cite_note-cos-review-7) and _[PopMatters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PopMatters "PopMatters")_.[\[12\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jessica_Pratt_(musician)#cite_note-popmatters-12)

In January 2014, she revealed the studio version of a new track, "Game That I Play."[\[13\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jessica_Pratt_(musician)#cite_note-13)

In October 2014, Pratt announced her second album, _[On Your Own Love Again](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/On_Your_Own_Love_Again_(album) "On Your Own Love Again (album)")_.[\[14\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jessica_Pratt_(musician)#cite_note-14) Pratt toured extensively around this record, both as a headliner and in support of [Beach House](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beach_House "Beach House"), [Panda Bear](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Panda_Bear_(musician) "Panda Bear (musician)") and José Gonzáles.[\[15\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jessica_Pratt_(musician)#cite_note-15) _On Your Own Love Again_ was ranked at #85 of _Pitchfork_'s Best Albums of the 2010s.[\[16\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jessica_Pratt_(musician)#cite_note-16) "Back, Baby" was sampled by [Troye Sivan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Troye_Sivan "Troye Sivan") on his 2023 album _[Something to Give Each Other](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Something_to_Give_Each_Other "Something to Give Each Other")_.[\[17\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jessica_Pratt_(musician)#cite_note-17)[\[18\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jessica_Pratt_(musician)#cite_note-18)

In October 2018, she announced her third album, _[Quiet Signs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quiet_Signs "Quiet Signs")_, and released a video for "This Time Around". _Pitchfork_ reviewed the song, giving it the Best New Music designation and noting a stylistic shift, comparing it to "a [Tropicalia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tropicalia "Tropicalia") version of a Christmas song, or a '60s jukebox standard playing in a beach town diner during the off-season." The album was released in February 2019 on the independent labels [Mexican Summer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mexican_Summer "Mexican Summer") and [City Slang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City_Slang "City Slang"). It was the first one recorded in a proper studio which provided a crystalline sound. _Pitchfork_ states the album "warps the typically direct, observational role of a singer-songwriter into something altogether more mystifying".[\[19\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jessica_Pratt_(musician)#cite_note-Pitchfor19-19)

After writing throughout the pandemic, Pratt returned to Gary's Electric with _Quiet Signs_ co-producer Al Carlson, Matt McDermott and session musicians including Spencer Zahn and Mauro Refosco, this time influenced by Beach Boys' _[Pet Sounds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pet_Sounds "Pet Sounds")_ and the music of the '60s group the Walker Brothers. "I've always been very interested in that micro era of '60s pop music where the production is atmospheric like a snow globe," she told the _New York Times_ on her intentions throughout the writing and recording process, which spanned from 2020 to 2023.[\[8\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jessica_Pratt_(musician)#cite_note-:0-8)

In February 2024, she released a video for a new track titled "Life Is" which served as the lead single for album _[Here in the Pitch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Here_in_the_Pitch "Here in the Pitch")_, which was released on May 3, 2024, via Mexican Summer and City Slang, to universal acclaim.[\[20\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jessica_Pratt_(musician)#cite_note-20) "Her chords are crisper, her singing more concrete and commanding, occasionally imagining echoes of lost Bowie or Beatles ballads aside her twilight bossa nova grooves," wrote Jen Pelly for _NPR_.[\[21\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jessica_Pratt_(musician)#cite_note-21) _Pitchfork_ awarded the album a Best New Music designation and an 8.8 score, stating Pratt's "fourth album of hypnagogic folk music hones her mysterious song to its finest point."[\[22\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jessica_Pratt_(musician)#cite_note-22) The lead single from the record, "Life Is," concluded Chanel's Fall-Winter 2024/25 Haute Couture Show.[\[23\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jessica_Pratt_(musician)#cite_note-23) She performed the song with her band on _[The Late Show](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Late_Show_with_Stephen_Colbert "The Late Show with Stephen Colbert")_ in July 2024.[\[24\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jessica_Pratt_(musician)#cite_note-24)

On August 2, 2024, she featured on the single " [Highjack](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Highjack "Highjack")" by [ASAP Rocky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ASAP_Rocky "ASAP Rocky").

## Musical style

According to Philip Cosores of _Consequence of Sound_, Pratt's music "displays a lyrical and musical range without straying from a palette of picked acoustic guitar and raw, bending vocals" and nods to "60s folk, California [classic rock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rock_music#Golden_age "Rock music"), and the early 2000s freak [folk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Folk_music "Folk music")."[\[7\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jessica_Pratt_(musician)#cite_note-cos-review-7) She is compared to various folk artists, including [Joni Mitchell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joni_Mitchell "Joni Mitchell"), [Joan Baez](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joan_Baez "Joan Baez"), [Sibylle Baier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sibylle_Baier "Sibylle Baier"), [David Crosby](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Crosby "David Crosby") and [Karen Dalton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karen_Dalton_(singer) "Karen Dalton (singer)").[\[9\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jessica_Pratt_(musician)#cite_note-thefader-9)[\[12\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jessica_Pratt_(musician)#cite_note-popmatters-12)[\[25\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jessica_Pratt_(musician)#cite_note-boston1-25) She also expressed admiration for [Ariel Pink](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ariel_Pink "Ariel Pink").[\[26\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jessica_Pratt_(musician)#cite_note-26)

Pratt dislikes the Joan Baez comparisons, and is hesitant about being classified strictly as "folk" or "freak-folk."[\[5\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jessica_Pratt_(musician)#cite_note-impose-5)[\[9\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jessica_Pratt_(musician)#cite_note-thefader-9) In an interview with _Impose_ magazine, she stated on the freak folk comparisons:

> I think anybody has an opposition to being pigeonholed into semi-trendy music genres. I definitely love a lot of those artists. There have been comparisons to people like Joan Baez \[who\] plays very straight-forward folk music, almost academic folk music. I've written so much new material that I'm almost ready for a next record. I guess it's just my fear of sounding one-dimensional, or being classified as strictly a folk artist.[\[5\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jessica_Pratt_(musician)#cite_note-impose-5)
