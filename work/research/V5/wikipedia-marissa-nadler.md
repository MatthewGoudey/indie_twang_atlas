Musical artist

**Marissa Rachael Nadler** (born April 5, 1981)[\[1\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marissa_Nadler#cite_note-1) is an American singer and songwriter. She is currently signed to [Sacred Bones Records](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sacred_Bones_Records "Sacred Bones Records") and [Bella Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bella_Union "Bella Union"), and released her tenth studio album, _New Radiations_, in August 2025.[\[2\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marissa_Nadler#cite_note-2) Her music has been characterized as blending "traditional folk, Gothic Americana, and dreamy pop into an original musical framework".[\[3\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marissa_Nadler#cite_note-3) Her music "is rooted in old-school country and folk but brings in elements of experimental and black metal".[\[4\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marissa_Nadler#cite_note-4) Sometimes the term "dream folk" has been invoked to describe her work.[\[5\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marissa_Nadler#cite_note-5)

Possessing a [mezzo-soprano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mezzo-soprano "Mezzo-soprano") voice, Nadler has received acclaim for her vocals, which was described by _[Pitchfork](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pitchfork_(website) "Pitchfork (website)")_ as one "you would follow straight into Hades", and also "textured and angelic, with just a hint of pain captured within her iridescent falsetto". _[The Boston Globe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Boston_Globe "The Boston Globe")_ said, "She has a voice that, in mythological times, could have lured men to their deaths at sea, an intoxicating soprano drenched in gauzy reverb that hits bell-clear heights, lingers, and tapers off like rings of smoke".

## Early life

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Marissa_Nadler&action=edit&section=1 "Edit section: Early life")\]

Marissa Nadler was born in [Washington, D.C.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Washington,_D.C. "Washington, D.C.") and raised in [Needham, Massachusetts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Needham,_Massachusetts "Needham, Massachusetts").[\[6\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marissa_Nadler#cite_note-6) She was raised [Jewish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jews "Jews").[\[7\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marissa_Nadler#cite_note-7) As a teenager, she taught herself to play guitar in a style similar to [fingerpicking](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fingerstyle_guitar "Fingerstyle guitar"), playing a steady bass pattern with the thumb and filling out syncopated rhythms with the index finger.[\[8\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marissa_Nadler#cite_note-boston.com-8) It was described as having an "unorthodox, dusky sound".[\[9\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marissa_Nadler#cite_note-:0-9) Also while in her teens, she began to write songs as a hobby.[\[9\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marissa_Nadler#cite_note-:0-9) She studied painting at [Rhode Island School of Design](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rhode_Island_School_of_Design "Rhode Island School of Design"), where she obtained a bachelor's degree in illustration in 2003, and a master's degree in art education a year later. During her studies, she began to perform at [open mic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Open_mic "Open mic") events around [Providence.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Providence,_Rhode_Island "Providence, Rhode Island") While exploring artistic techniques such as illustration, painting, bookbinding, [wood carving](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wood_carving "Wood carving") and [encaustic painting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Encaustic_painting "Encaustic painting"), she also honed her songwriting craft.[\[8\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marissa_Nadler#cite_note-boston.com-8) She subsequently recorded an album titled _Autumn Rose_ (2002) as well as the four-track EP _Somber Ghost Recordings_(2003), neither of which have ever been released.[\[10\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marissa_Nadler#cite_note-Alamist-10) Nadler also contributed to the [Pearls Before Swine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pearls_Before_Swine_(band) "Pearls Before Swine (band)") tribute album _For the Dead in Space - Volumes II & III_ in 2003.

## Career

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Marissa_Nadler&action=edit&section=2 "Edit section: Career")\]

### 2004–2010: First releases

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Marissa_Nadler&action=edit&section=3 "Edit section: 2004–2010: First releases")\]

Main articles: [Ballads of Living and Dying](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ballads_of_Living_and_Dying "Ballads of Living and Dying"), [The Saga of Mayflower May](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Saga_of_Mayflower_May "The Saga of Mayflower May"), [Songs III: Bird on the Water](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Songs_III:_Bird_on_the_Water "Songs III: Bird on the Water"), and [Little Hells](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Little_Hells "Little Hells")

Nadler released her first official album, _[Ballads of Living and Dying](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ballads_of_Living_and_Dying "Ballads of Living and Dying")_, on Eclipse Records in January 2004.[\[11\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marissa_Nadler#cite_note-ballads-11) In a positive review, Matthew Murphy wrote on [Pitchfork](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pitchfork_(website) "Pitchfork (website)") that, "Nadler has the sort of voice that you'd follow straight to Hades. Her luxurious, resonant soprano is immediately transfixing, and throughout these songs it envelops the listener like a dense fog rolling in off the moors."[\[12\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marissa_Nadler#cite_note-12) The follow-up _[The Saga of Mayflower May](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Saga_of_Mayflower_May "The Saga of Mayflower May")_ was released in July 2005.[\[9\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marissa_Nadler#cite_note-:0-9) Both records were distributed in the United States by Eclipse, and by the UK label Beautiful Happiness in Europe.[\[13\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marissa_Nadler#cite_note-saga-13) Nadler went on some of her first tours with acts such as the late [American primitive guitarist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_primitive_guitar "American primitive guitar") [Jack Rose](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jack_Rose_(guitarist) "Jack Rose (guitarist)") and the [drone metal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drone_metal "Drone metal") band [Earth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Earth_(American_band) "Earth (American band)").[\[14\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marissa_Nadler#cite_note-14)

Nadler released her third studio album, _[Songs III: Bird on the Water](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Songs_III:_Bird_on_the_Water "Songs III: Bird on the Water")_, on [Peacefrog Records](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peacefrog_Records "Peacefrog Records") in Europe on March 12, 2007. The album was recorded and produced by [Greg Weeks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greg_Weeks "Greg Weeks") of the band [Espers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Espers_(band) "Espers (band)") in Philadelphia and was subsequently released in the US and Canada on August 12, 2007, by [Kemado Records](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kemado_Records "Kemado Records").[\[15\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marissa_Nadler#cite_note-three-15) It was nominated for two [PLUG Independent Music Awards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PLUG_Independent_Music_Awards "PLUG Independent Music Awards") in 2008: "Best Female Artist of the Year" and "Best Americana Record of the Year". Nadler also won "Outstanding Singer-Songwriter of the Year" at the 2008 [Boston Music Awards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boston_Music_Awards "Boston Music Awards"), with three nominations altogether.[\[16\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marissa_Nadler#cite_note-16)

Her fourth album, _[Little Hells](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Little_Hells "Little Hells")_, produced by [Chris Coady](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chris_Coady "Chris Coady"), was released on March 3, 2009[\[17\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marissa_Nadler#cite_note-littlehells-17) and received praise from many critics.[\[18\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marissa_Nadler#cite_note-wear-18) including 4-star reviews from magazines such as _[Mojo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mojo_(magazine) "Mojo (magazine)")_, _[Rolling Stone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rolling_Stone "Rolling Stone")_ in France and Germany, _[Uncut](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uncut_(magazine) "Uncut (magazine)")_, and _[Q](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Q_(magazine) "Q (magazine)")_. It received an 8.3 from _Pitchfork_.[\[19\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marissa_Nadler#cite_note-pitchfork-19) The album departed from the earlier folk-based template with the introduction of electronic elements.[\[9\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marissa_Nadler#cite_note-:0-9) In early 2010, Nadler contributed vocals on _[Portal of Sorrow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portal_of_Sorrow "Portal of Sorrow")_, the final album by [black metal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_metal "Black metal") project [Xasthur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xasthur "Xasthur").[\[20\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marissa_Nadler#cite_note-20)

### 2011–2012: _Marissa Nadler_ and _The Sister_

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Marissa_Nadler&action=edit&section=4 "Edit section: 2011–2012: Marissa Nadler and The Sister")\]

Main articles: [Marissa Nadler (album)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marissa_Nadler_(album) "Marissa Nadler (album)") and [The Sister (EP)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Sister_(EP) "The Sister (EP)")

On June 14, 2011, an [eponymous record](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marissa_Nadler_(album) "Marissa Nadler (album)") was released worldwide on Nadler's own label, Box of Cedar Records.[\[21\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marissa_Nadler#cite_note-deb-21) The song "Baby, I Will Leave You in the Morning" was released as a free download on March 8, followed by a subsequent music video.[\[22\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marissa_Nadler#cite_note-video-22) The album was positively received by _Pitchfork_, which gave it 8.1 out of 10,[\[23\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marissa_Nadler#cite_note-pitchforkdebut-23) and it was called "a stellar collection of sullen melodies and lovelorn anecdotes akin to those of [Joni Mitchell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joni_Mitchell "Joni Mitchell")".[\[24\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marissa_Nadler#cite_note-pastea-24)

On May 29, 2012, she released _[The Sister](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Sister_(EP) "The Sister (EP)"),_ a short eight-song "companion" album to _Marissa Nadler_.[\[25\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marissa_Nadler#cite_note-album-25) The album was dubbed by _[Paste](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paste_(magazine) "Paste (magazine)")_ as an "impressive concoction of stark minimalism".[\[24\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marissa_Nadler#cite_note-pastea-24)

In November 2012, Nadler collaborated with [Angel Olsen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Angel_Olsen "Angel Olsen") on two cover songs that were shared on Nadler's [SoundCloud](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SoundCloud "SoundCloud") page: the [Richard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_Thompson_(musician) "Richard Thompson (musician)") and [Linda Thompson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linda_Thompson_(singer) "Linda Thompson (singer)") song "My Dreams Have Withered and Died" and the [Mickey Newbury](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mickey_Newbury "Mickey Newbury") song "Frisco Depot".[\[26\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marissa_Nadler#cite_note-otichforkolsen-26)

### 2015–2016: _Strangers_

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Marissa_Nadler&action=edit&section=6 "Edit section: 2015–2016: Strangers")\]

The album _Strangers_, recorded with producer Dunn in 2015, was released on May 20, 2016, on Sacred Bones and Bella Union.[\[45\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marissa_Nadler#cite_note-45) A limited-edition version with original artwork included a pre-release cassette of _Bury Your Name_.
The album release was accompanied by an extensive tour of North America and Europe. In late 2016, Nadler joined [Ghost](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ghost_(Swedish_band) "Ghost (Swedish band)") for the US tour.

_Strangers_ holds a score of 76 out of 100 from [Metacritic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metacritic "Metacritic") based on "generally favorable reviews".[\[46\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marissa_Nadler#cite_note-46) The album was listed on several lists of 2016's best albums to date, including _Spin_,[\[47\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marissa_Nadler#cite_note-47) _[Newsweek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Newsweek "Newsweek")_,[\[48\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marissa_Nadler#cite_note-48) _Uncut_[\[49\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marissa_Nadler#cite_note-49) and [The Quietus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Quietus "The Quietus").[\[50\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marissa_Nadler#cite_note-50) _Spin_ also listed "Janie in Love" as one of the best songs of 2016 so far.[\[51\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marissa_Nadler#cite_note-51)

During this period, Nadler directed and animated music videos for _Strangers_ tracks "Janie in Love"[\[52\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marissa_Nadler#cite_note-52) and "All the Colors of the Dark",[\[53\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marissa_Nadler#cite_note-53) as well as "Blood and Tears",[\[54\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marissa_Nadler#cite_note-54) a [Danzig](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Danzig_(band) "Danzig (band)") cover she had self-released as a digital-only single for Halloween 2015.[\[55\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marissa_Nadler#cite_note-55) In late September 2016, a video for the _Strangers_ song "Dissolve", featuring both animation and live performance, was released on Pitchfork.tv.[\[56\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marissa_Nadler#cite_note-56)

### 2017–2018: _Leave the Light On: Bedroom Recordings_ and _For My Crimes_

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Marissa_Nadler&action=edit&section=7 "Edit section: 2017–2018: Leave the Light On: Bedroom Recordings and For My Crimes")\]

After touring Europe and North America promoting _Strangers_, Nadler guested on the [Lawrence Rothman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lawrence_Rothman "Lawrence Rothman") album _The Book of Law_ (2017),[\[57\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marissa_Nadler#cite_note-57) and on _Ojalá_ (2017), the debut album by Lost Horizons, a duo consisting of Simon Raymonde ( [Cocteau Twins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cocteau_Twins "Cocteau Twins")) and Richie Thomas ( [Dif Juz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dif_Juz "Dif Juz"), [the Jesus and Mary Chain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Jesus_and_Mary_Chain "The Jesus and Mary Chain")).[\[58\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marissa_Nadler#cite_note-58) On September 15, 2017, Sacred Bones released Nadler's first compilation album, _Leave the Light On: Bedroom Recordings_, a collection of home recordings and demos dating back to 2014.[\[59\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marissa_Nadler#cite_note-59)[\[60\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marissa_Nadler#cite_note-60) She also self-released two covers albums on [Bandcamp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bandcamp "Bandcamp") for free download.[\[61\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marissa_Nadler#cite_note-61)

| --- |
| "The latest in a long career of consistently stellar records, _For My Crimes_ is a shining example of how Nadler continues to impress and improve upon each release" |

Nadler's eighth studio album, _For My Crimes_, was released by Sacred Bones on September 28, 2018.[\[63\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marissa_Nadler#cite_note-63) _Rolling Stone_ wrote that, in contrast to the 2014 break-up album _July_, _For My Crimes_, "seems to touch instead on the daily realities of making a relationship work, the epilogue to a happily-ever-after love story when real life kicks in".[\[64\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marissa_Nadler#cite_note-64) It was her first album cover to feature her fine art, an untitled painting.[\[65\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marissa_Nadler#cite_note-65) The album included guest vocals by Olsen, [Sharon Van Etten](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sharon_Van_Etten "Sharon Van Etten") and [Kristin Kontrol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kristin_Kontrol "Kristin Kontrol"). The album's first video, "Blue Vapor", was directed by Thomas McMahan, who "utilizes various experimental and mixed media animation techniques, merging the mood and imagery of the song with hallucinatory visuals..."[\[66\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marissa_Nadler#cite_note-66) Nadler's wardrobe in the video for "Blue Vapor" was created and put together by New York-based [costume designer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Costume_designer "Costume designer") and [stylist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wardrobe_stylist "Wardrobe stylist") [Jenni Hensler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jenni_Hensler "Jenni Hensler"), who also styled Nadler's [Pitchfork.tv](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pitchfork_(website)#History "Pitchfork (website)") performance clip for the song "Dissolve."[\[67\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marissa_Nadler#cite_note-vapor-67)[\[68\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marissa_Nadler#cite_note-dissolve-68) _[For My Crimes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/For_My_Crimes "For My Crimes")_ was relatively well-received by critics and fans.

### 2021-2024: _Instead of Dreaming_ and _The Path of the Clouds_

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Marissa_Nadler&action=edit&section=9 "Edit section: 2021-2024: Instead of Dreaming and The Path of the Clouds")\]

In February 2021, she released the single "Sleepwalk & Lonely People", and in May 2021 the compilation album _Instead of Dreaming_.
Nadler released her ninth solo album, _The Path of the Clouds_ on October 29, 2021.[\[86\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marissa_Nadler#cite_note-:1-86) [Like others](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Category:Albums_impacted_by_the_COVID-19_pandemic "Category:Albums impacted by the COVID-19 pandemic"), the album found its origins in the Covid-19 pandemic: "Gripped by wanderlust while suddenly housebound at the start of the pandemic in 2020, Nadler escaped into writing, and came back with a stunning set of songs about metamorphosis, love, mysticism, and murder."[\[87\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marissa_Nadler#cite_note-auto1-87) A review from the magazine [UNCUT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uncut_(magazine) "Uncut (magazine)") notes that, "\[e\]scape is a central theme of the album, and several songs recall historical cases of mysterious disappearances, but infused with personal experience, blurring the line between fact and fiction."[\[88\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marissa_Nadler#cite_note-auto2-88) This self-produced album contains the most sonically elaborate work of her discography.[\[89\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marissa_Nadler#cite_note-auto-89) Various guests make an appearance on _The Path of the Clouds_ including harpist [Mary Lattimore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mary_Lattimore "Mary Lattimore") and former [Cocteau Twins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cocteau_Twins "Cocteau Twins") bassist [Simon Raymonde](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simon_Raymonde "Simon Raymonde"). [Emma Ruth Rundle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emma_Ruth_Rundle "Emma Ruth Rundle") contributes a guitar solo to "Turned into Air" and Amber Webber of [Black Mountain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_Mountain_(band) "Black Mountain (band)") provides backing vocals on "Elegy". Milky Burgess played various instruments throughout the album.[\[87\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marissa_Nadler#cite_note-auto1-87)

The Path of the Clouds has received critical acclaim. _NME_ gave the album a positive review, writing, "This is a departure for Nadler in a number of ways, with more sophisticated production, unusual storytelling, and a firm look ahead to her future as an artist (more piano, more power vocally)."[\[86\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marissa_Nadler#cite_note-:1-86) _Pitchfork_ awarded the album a score of 7.5, comparing the album favourably to the true crime genre that inspired it: "The thrills of _The Path of the Clouds_ are far richer than most true crime fiction, but like the best examples of the genre, it leaves you breathless."[\[90\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marissa_Nadler#cite_note-90) The album received four stars from  8/10 from [UNCUT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uncut_(magazine) "Uncut (magazine)"),[\[89\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marissa_Nadler#cite_note-auto-89) MusicOMH,[\[88\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marissa_Nadler#cite_note-auto2-88) Rock n Reel,[\[91\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marissa_Nadler#cite_note-91) [MOJO](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mojo_(magazine) "Mojo (magazine)"),[\[92\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marissa_Nadler#cite_note-92) and [Record Collector](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Record_Collector "Record Collector").[\[93\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marissa_Nadler#cite_note-93) Other reviewers were even more positive, with [All Music Guide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AllMusic "AllMusic") (4 ½ stars) praising the "epic, aching songs, which refuse to keep tragedy at arm's length"[\[94\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marissa_Nadler#cite_note-94) and Sputnikmusic (superb: 4.5) noting that it "sounds so lush, sweeping, and powerful that all the subtle, intricate melodies are merely the cherry on top."[\[95\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marissa_Nadler#cite_note-95) In its five-star "essential" review, Contact Music called the album "exquisitely crafted and utterly beguiling" and recommended that, " _The Path of the Clouds_ … should be listened to, uninterrupted, in its entirety."[\[96\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marissa_Nadler#cite_note-96)

### 2025: _New Radiations_

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Marissa_Nadler&action=edit&section=10 "Edit section: 2025: New Radiations")\]

In June 2025, Marissa Nadler released the title track of her upcoming album _New Radiations_, accompanied by a [self-made video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=au30lCanU8w) featuring animations of her fine art photography, drawing and painting, followed and videos for [Hatchet Man](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=dGByJuZgdM4) and [Light Years](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=kYmdfoDmQwI). The album was released in full on 15 August.[\[97\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marissa_Nadler#cite_note-MM-97) Compared to its predecessors, it has an intimate, lush feeling, full of atmosphere and stacked harmonies. It was produced by Nadler and mixed by [Randell Dunn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Randall_Dunn "Randall Dunn") and features Milky Burgess on instrumental arrangements.

The reviews of the album were overwhelmingly positive. About the style, many noted the album’s cohesiveness and consistency. For example, in a 5-star rating HiFi Choice wrote that “Across all 11 songs, Nadler manages a rare feat: balancing expansive, wide-eyed beauty with fragility and vulnerability.” [Paste Magazine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paste_(magazine) "Paste (magazine)") similarly noted that “Each track immerses you in a haze of contemplation through minimalist production and poetic lyrics”[\[98\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marissa_Nadler#cite_note-98) while [Pitchfork](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pitchfork_(website) "Pitchfork (website)") called the songs "lullabies" that "seem designed to induce the sort of sleep that hovers on the precipice between dream and nightmare.”[\[99\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marissa_Nadler#cite_note-auto3-99) Stephen Thompson of [NPR Music](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NPR_Music "NPR Music") also remarked on this dual quality: “It's got kind of a wispy softness to it but also real heaviness."[\[100\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marissa_Nadler#cite_note-auto4-100) Finally, [KEXP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KEXP-FM "KEXP-FM") called it, “an emotive set of dark [Americana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Americana_music "Americana music") that still radiates warmth, thanks to the rich, vivid textures achieved by her breathy voice, captivating guitar picking, and atmospheric keys.”[\[101\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marissa_Nadler#cite_note-101)

With respect to its subject matter, Pitchfork writes that the album “tells stories of surreal and introspective adventure in tones at once heavenly and subterranean”[\[99\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marissa_Nadler#cite_note-auto3-99) and Amelia Mason of [NPR Music](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NPR_Music "NPR Music") noted "this feeling of being like you're in a short story or a novel or a film."[\[100\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marissa_Nadler#cite_note-auto4-100) According to [KLOF magazine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KLOF_Magazine "KLOF Magazine"), "She is perhaps the most distinctive and gifted songwriter working in the nebulous realm of dark folk, and _New Radiations_ \[…\] could easily become a career-defining album”[\[102\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marissa_Nadler#cite_note-102)

The release of the album was followed by a European tour in the autumn of 2025 and a North American tour in 2026.[\[103\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marissa_Nadler#cite_note-103)
