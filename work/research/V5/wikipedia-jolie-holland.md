Musical artist

**Jolie Holland** (born September 11, 1975)[\[5\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jolie_Holland#cite_note-AMG-5) is an American singer and performer who combines elements of [folk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Folk_music "Folk music"), [traditional](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Traditional_music "Traditional music"), [experimental](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Experimental_music "Experimental music"), and rock.[\[6\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jolie_Holland#cite_note-QUIET-6)

## Career

[![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/6/67/Jolie_Holland_performing_at_the_Kings_Arms_in_Auckland.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail_unscaled)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Jolie_Holland_performing_at_the_Kings_Arms_in_Auckland.jpg) Holland at the keyboards

Growing up in her birthplace of [Houston](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Houston "Houston"), Texas,[\[5\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jolie_Holland#cite_note-AMG-5) Holland left in 1994, moving to [Austin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austin,_Texas "Austin, Texas"), [New Orleans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Orleans "New Orleans"), and [San Francisco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Francisco "San Francisco") before ending up in [Vancouver](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vancouver "Vancouver"), Canada, where she was one of the founding members of [The Be Good Tanyas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Be_Good_Tanyas "The Be Good Tanyas"). Holland left the band before the release of their debut album, _[Blue Horse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blue_Horse_(album) "Blue Horse (album)")_, on which she appears prominently.[\[7\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jolie_Holland#cite_note-7) She returned to San Francisco, where she recorded her first album, 2002's _[Catalpa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catalpa_(album) "Catalpa (album)")_. Originally self-released, the album was later picked up and re-released by [ANTI-](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ANTI- "ANTI-"). In 2004 she released her first album for ANTI-, _[Escondida](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Escondida_(album) "Escondida (album)")_. ANTI- labelmates [Tom Waits](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tom_Waits "Tom Waits") and [Sage Francis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sage_Francis "Sage Francis") are both outspoken fans of Holland's: Waits nominated her for the [Shortlist music prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shortlist_Prize "Shortlist Prize"),[\[8\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jolie_Holland#cite_note-8) while Francis has said that _Escondida_ was his most listened to album of 2005.[\[9\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jolie_Holland#cite_note-9)

As well as releasing another four solo albums for ANTI- and touring globally, Holland has performed a number of collaborations with other artists, including with fan Sage Francis (on his album _[Human the Death Dance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_the_Death_Dance "Human the Death Dance"))_ and [Booker T.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Booker_T._Jones "Booker T. Jones") (with whom she recorded " [What A Wonderful World](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/What_A_Wonderful_World "What A Wonderful World")" in 2007). In 2016, she reunited with Be Good Tanyas bandmate Samantha Parton and they began touring as a duo,[\[10\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jolie_Holland#cite_note-10) releasing the jointly-credited album _Wildflower Blues_, on Cinquefoil Records, in 2017 and embarking on a joint tour through 2018.[\[11\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jolie_Holland#cite_note-11)

### Contributions

- Multiple vocal, instrumental, and writing contributions on the [Be Good Tanyas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Be_Good_Tanyas "Be Good Tanyas")' debut album, [_Blue Horse_](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blue_Horse_(album) "Blue Horse (album)"), released in 2000 (after Holland's departure from the band).
- Multiple collaborations with [David Dondero](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Dondero "David Dondero"), including backing vocals on the song "Analysis of a 1970's Divorce" on Dondero's 2001 album _Shooting at the Sun With a Water Gun_[\[12\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jolie_Holland#cite_note-12)
- Lead vocals on _The Grey Funnel Line_ on [Hal Willner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hal_Willner "Hal Willner")'s _[Rogue's Gallery: Pirate Ballads, Sea Songs, and Chanteys](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rogue's_Gallery:_Pirate_Ballads,_Sea_Songs,_and_Chanteys "Rogue's Gallery: Pirate Ballads, Sea Songs, and Chanteys")_ (2006, [ANTI-](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ANTI- "ANTI-"))[\[13\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jolie_Holland#cite_note-13)
- Backing vocals for [Bad Religion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bad_Religion "Bad Religion") frontman Greg Graffin's studio solo album _[Cold as the Clay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cold_as_the_Clay "Cold as the Clay")_[\[14\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jolie_Holland#cite_note-14)
- Violin on The Speakers' album _Yeats Is Greats_[\[15\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jolie_Holland#cite_note-15)
- Backing vocals on [Chuck Ragan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chuck_Ragan "Chuck Ragan")'s album _[Feast or Famine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Feast_or_Famine_(Chuck_Ragan_album) "Feast or Famine (Chuck Ragan album)")_[\[16\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jolie_Holland#cite_note-16)
- Lyrics and performance of _Flood of Dreams_ in the film _[King of California](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/King_of_California "King of California")_[\[17\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jolie_Holland#cite_note-17)
- Backing vocals on "Cinders of the Sun" and "Heart of Misery"[\[18\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jolie_Holland#cite_note-18) for [Steve Abel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steve_Abel "Steve Abel") and the Chrysalids' album _Flax Happyon_
- Backing vocals on [David Gray's](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Gray_(British_musician) "David Gray (British musician)") "Kathleen"[\[19\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jolie_Holland#cite_note-19)
- Contributed to two tracks on Sage Francis' _[Human the Death Dance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_the_Death_Dance "Human the Death Dance")_ and is featured in the video for "Got Up This Morning"[\[20\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jolie_Holland#cite_note-20)[\[21\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jolie_Holland#cite_note-Epitaph-GotUpThisMorning-MakingOf-2007-21)
- Backing vocals on "Songs for Love Drunk Sinners" by Jan Bell and the Cheap Dates[\[22\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jolie_Holland#cite_note-22)
- Collaboration with [Booker T. Jones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Booker_T._Jones "Booker T. Jones") on the album "What A Wonderful World"[\[23\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jolie_Holland#cite_note-23)
- Backing vocals on "Old/New" by Frally[\[24\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jolie_Holland#cite_note-24)
- Backing vocals on "Alabama chicken" and "Rattlesnake charm (Dream machine)" by [Sean Hayes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sean_Hayes_(musician) "Sean Hayes (musician)")[\[25\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jolie_Holland#cite_note-25)
- Mixing and engineering by [Joel Hamilton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joel_Hamilton "Joel Hamilton") on the album "The Living and The Dead" [\[26\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jolie_Holland#cite_note-26)
- Duet with [Guy Garvey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guy_Garvey "Guy Garvey") on "Electricity" from the 2015 album _[Courting the Squall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Courting_the_Squall "Courting the Squall")_
- Backing vocals and violin on Eric Terino's "Innovations of Grave Perversity" LP[\[27\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jolie_Holland#cite_note-27) and "Body Gets Stoned" EP[\[28\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jolie_Holland#cite_note-28)
