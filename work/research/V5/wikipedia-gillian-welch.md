Musical artist

**Gillian Howard Welch** (born October 2, 1967) is an American singer-songwriter who performs with guitarist [David Rawlings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Rawlings "David Rawlings"). Their spare, often dark sound, which _[The New Yorker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_New_Yorker "The New Yorker")_ calls "at once innovative and obliquely reminiscent of past rural forms",[\[1\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gillian_Welch#cite_note-new_yorker-1) blends [Appalachian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Appalachian_music "Appalachian music"), [bluegrass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bluegrass_music "Bluegrass music"), [country](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Country_music "Country music") and [Americana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Americana_(music) "Americana (music)").

Welch and Rawlings have collaborated on nine critically acclaimed albums, five under her name, three under Rawlings', and two jointly. Her debut _[Revival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Revival_(Gillian_Welch_album) "Revival (Gillian Welch album)")_ (1996) was nominated for [Grammy Award for Best Contemporary Folk Album](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grammy_Award_for_Best_Contemporary_Folk_Album "Grammy Award for Best Contemporary Folk Album"), as was _[Time (The Revelator)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Time_(The_Revelator) "Time (The Revelator)")_, from 2001. _[Soul Journey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soul_Journey "Soul Journey")_ (2003) introduced a fuller, more upbeat sound, with drums and electric guitar. After an eight-year gap, _[The Harrow & the Harvest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Harrow_&_the_Harvest "The Harrow & the Harvest")_ (2011) was also nominated for a Best Contemporary Folk Album Grammy.

In 2020, Welch and Rawlings released _[All the Good Times (Are Past & Gone)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/All_the_Good_Times_(Are_Past_&_Gone) "All the Good Times (Are Past & Gone)")_, which won the 2021 [Grammy Award for Best Folk Album](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grammy_Award_for_Best_Folk_Album "Grammy Award for Best Folk Album").[\[2\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gillian_Welch#cite_note-:0-2) In 2024, Welch and Rawlings released [_Woodland_](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Woodland_(album) "Woodland (album)"), which would win the 2025 Grammy Award for Best Folk Album, currently making Welch and Rawlings the only duo to win the award more than once.[\[3\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gillian_Welch#cite_note-auto-3)

Welch was an associate producer and performed on two songs of the [soundtrack](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/O_Brother,_Where_Art_Thou%3F_(soundtrack) "O Brother, Where Art Thou? (soundtrack)") of the [Coen brothers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coen_brothers "Coen brothers") 2000 film _[O Brother, Where Art Thou?](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/O_Brother,_Where_Art_Thou%3F "O Brother, Where Art Thou?")_, a [platinum album](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Platinum_album "Platinum album") that won the [Grammy Award for Album of the Year](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grammy_Award_for_Album_of_the_Year "Grammy Award for Album of the Year") in 2002. She also appeared in the film attempting to buy a Soggy Bottom Boys record. Welch, while not one of the principal actors, did sing and provide additional lyrics to the Sirens song "Didn't Leave Nobody but the Baby." In 2018 she and Rawlings wrote the song "When a Cowboy Trades His Spurs for Wings" for the Coens' _[The Ballad of Buster Scruggs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Ballad_of_Buster_Scruggs "The Ballad of Buster Scruggs")_, for which they received a nomination for the [Academy Award for Best Original Song](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academy_Award_for_Best_Original_Song "Academy Award for Best Original Song").

## Early life

Welch was born on October 2, 1967, in New York City, and was adopted by Mitzie Welch (née Marilyn Cottle)[\[4\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gillian_Welch#cite_note-4) and Ken Welch, comedy and music entertainers.[\[1\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gillian_Welch#cite_note-new_yorker-1) Her biological mother was a [freshman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Freshman "Freshman") in college, and her father was a musician visiting New York City.[\[1\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gillian_Welch#cite_note-new_yorker-1)[\[5\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gillian_Welch#cite_note-guardian_2003-5)[\[6\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gillian_Welch#cite_note-paste_2003-6) Welch has speculated that her biological father could have been one of her favorite musicians, and she later discovered from her adoptive parents that he was a drummer.[\[1\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gillian_Welch#cite_note-new_yorker-1)[\[5\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gillian_Welch#cite_note-guardian_2003-5)[\[6\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gillian_Welch#cite_note-paste_2003-6) [Alec Wilkinson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alec_Wilkinson "Alec Wilkinson") of _The New Yorker_ stated that "from an address they had been given, it appeared that her mother ... may have grown up in the [mountains of North Carolina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_North_Carolina "Western North Carolina")".[\[1\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gillian_Welch#cite_note-new_yorker-1) When Welch was three, her adoptive parents moved to Los Angeles to write music for _[The Carol Burnett Show](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Carol_Burnett_Show "The Carol Burnett Show")_. They also appeared on _[The Tonight Show](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Tonight_Show "The Tonight Show")_.[\[1\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gillian_Welch#cite_note-new_yorker-1)

As a child, Welch was introduced to the music of American [folk singers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Folk_singers "Folk singers") [Bob Dylan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bob_Dylan "Bob Dylan"), [Woody Guthrie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Woody_Guthrie "Woody Guthrie"), and the [Carter Family](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carter_Family "Carter Family"). She performed folk songs with her peers at the [Westland Elementary School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Westland_School_(Los_Angeles) "The Westland School (Los Angeles)") in Los Angeles.[\[1\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gillian_Welch#cite_note-new_yorker-1)[\[7\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gillian_Welch#cite_note-la_times_2004-7) Welch later attended [Crossroads School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crossroads_School_(Santa_Monica,_California) "Crossroads School (Santa Monica, California)"), a high school in [Santa Monica, California](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santa_Monica,_California "Santa Monica, California"). While in high school, a local television program featured her as a student who "excelled at everything she did."[\[1\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gillian_Welch#cite_note-new_yorker-1)

While a student at the [University of California, Santa Cruz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_Santa_Cruz "University of California, Santa Cruz"), Welch played bass in a [goth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Goth_music "Goth music") band, and drums in a [psychedelic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Psychedelic_rock "Psychedelic rock") [surf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Surf_rock "Surf rock") band.[\[1\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gillian_Welch#cite_note-new_yorker-1) In college, a roommate played an album by the bluegrass band [the Stanley Brothers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Stanley_Brothers "The Stanley Brothers"), and she had an [epiphany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Epiphany_(feeling) "Epiphany (feeling)"):

> The first song came on and I just stood up and I kind of walked into the other room as if I was in a tractor beam and stood there in front of the stereo. It was just as powerful as the electric stuff, and it was songs I'd grown up singing. All of a sudden I'd found my music.[\[8\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gillian_Welch#cite_note-sf_chronicle_2005-8)

After graduating from UC Santa Cruz with a degree in photography, Welch attended the [Berklee College of Music](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berklee_College_of_Music "Berklee College of Music") in [Boston](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boston "Boston"), where she majored in songwriting.[\[9\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gillian_Welch#cite_note-nodep_book-9) During her two years studying at Berklee, Welch gained confidence as a performer.[\[1\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gillian_Welch#cite_note-new_yorker-1)[\[9\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gillian_Welch#cite_note-nodep_book-9) Welch met her music partner [David Rawlings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Rawlings "David Rawlings") at a successful audition for Berklee's only country band.[\[10\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gillian_Welch#cite_note-boston_globe_2003-10)[\[11\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gillian_Welch#cite_note-theage_bevhillybilly-11)

## Career

[![A man with his head down playing a guitar with a smiling Welch also playing guitar on stage. Both play in front of microphones.](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/0/09/Gillian_Smile.jpg/500px-Gillian_Smile.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Gillian_Smile.jpg) Rawlings and Welch performing in Seattle in 2009

Upon finishing college in 1992, Welch moved to [Nashville, Tennessee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nashville,_Tennessee "Nashville, Tennessee").[\[12\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gillian_Welch#cite_note-times_london_2003-12) She recalled, "I looked at my record collection and saw that all the music I loved had been made in Nashville— [Bill Monroe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bill_Monroe "Bill Monroe"), Dylan, [the Stanley Brothers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Stanley_Brothers "The Stanley Brothers"), [Neil Young](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neil_Young "Neil Young")—so I moved there. Not ever thinking I was thirty years too late."[\[1\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gillian_Welch#cite_note-new_yorker-1) Rawlings soon followed. In Nashville, after singing " [Long Black Veil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Long_Black_Veil_(song) "Long Black Veil (song)")," the two first realized that their voices harmonized well and they started to perform as a duo.[\[1\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gillian_Welch#cite_note-new_yorker-1) They never considered using a working name, so the duo were simply billed as "Gillian Welch."[\[1\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gillian_Welch#cite_note-new_yorker-1) A year after moving to Nashville, Welch found a [manager](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Talent_manager "Talent manager"), [Denise Stiff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Denise_Stiff "Denise Stiff"), who already managed [Alison Krauss](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alison_Krauss "Alison Krauss"). Both Welch and Stiff ignored frequent advice that Welch should stop playing with Rawlings and join a band.[\[1\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gillian_Welch#cite_note-new_yorker-1)[\[7\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gillian_Welch#cite_note-la_times_2004-7) They eventually signed a recording contract with [Almo Sounds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Almo_Sounds "Almo Sounds").[\[7\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gillian_Welch#cite_note-la_times_2004-7) Following a performance opening for [Peter Rowan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_Rowan "Peter Rowan") at the [Station Inn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Station_Inn "Station Inn"), producer [T Bone Burnett](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/T_Bone_Burnett "T Bone Burnett") expressed interest in recording an album. Burnett did not plan to disturb Welch's and Rawlings' preference for minimal instrumentation, and Welch agreed to take him on as a producer.[\[13\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gillian_Welch#cite_note-no_dep_1996-13)

Welch gave a homemade copy of her demos to [Tim O'Brien](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tim_O'Brien_(musician) "Tim O'Brien (musician)") who then recorded two of Welch's songs with his sister [Mollie O'Brien](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mollie_O'Brien "Mollie O'Brien"). "Orphan Girl" and "Wichita" are featured on Tim and Mollie's album _Away out on the Mountain_ (1994), standing as the first published songs of Welch's.

### _Revival_

For the recording sessions of Welch's debut, _[Revival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Revival_(Gillian_Welch_album) "Revival (Gillian Welch album)")_, Burnett wanted to recapture the bare sound of Welch's live performance.[\[13\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gillian_Welch#cite_note-no_dep_1996-13) Welch recalled, "That first week was really intense. It was just T Bone, the engineer, and Dave and myself. We got so inside our little world. There was very little distance between our singing and playing. The sound was very immediate. It was so light and small."[\[13\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gillian_Welch#cite_note-no_dep_1996-13) Later, they recorded several more songs and played with an expanded group of musicians: guitarist and [Rock and Roll Hall of Fame](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rock_and_Roll_Hall_of_Fame "Rock and Roll Hall of Fame") inductee [James Burton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Burton "James Burton"), bassist [Roy Huskey, Jr.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roy_Huskey,_Jr. "Roy Huskey, Jr."), and veteran session drummers [Jim Keltner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jim_Keltner "Jim Keltner") and [Buddy Harman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buddy_Harman "Buddy Harman").[\[13\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gillian_Welch#cite_note-no_dep_1996-13)

The album was released in April 1996 to mostly positive reviews. Mark Deming of [AllMusic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AllMusic "AllMusic") called it a "superb debut" and wrote, "Welch's debts to artists of the past are obvious and clearly acknowledged, but there's a maturity, intelligence, and keen eye for detail in her songs you wouldn't expect from someone simply trying to ape the Carter Family."[\[14\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gillian_Welch#cite_note-amg_revival-14) Bill Friskics-Warren of _[No Depression](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/No_Depression_(magazine) "No Depression (magazine)")_ praised the album as "breathtakingly austere evocations of rural culture."[\[13\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gillian_Welch#cite_note-no_dep_1996-13) The Arlington Heights, Illinois _[Daily Herald](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daily_Herald_(Arlington_Heights) "Daily Herald (Arlington Heights)")_'s Mark Guarino observed that _Revival_ was "cheered and scrutinized as a staunch revivalist of Depression-era music only because her originals sounded so much like that era." He attributed this to the biblical imagery of the lyrics, Burnett's threadbare production, and the plainly-sung bleakness in Welch's vocals.[\[15\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gillian_Welch#cite_note-cdh_2003-15) [Ann Powers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ann_Powers "Ann Powers") of _[Rolling Stone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rolling_Stone "Rolling Stone")_ gave _Revival_ a lukewarm review and criticized Welch for not singing of her own experiences, and "manufacturing emotion."[\[16\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gillian_Welch#cite_note-rollingstone_revival-16) [Robert Christgau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Christgau "Robert Christgau") echoed Powers: Welch "just doesn't have the voice, eye, or way with words to bring her simulation off."[\[17\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gillian_Welch#cite_note-robchrist_revival-17)

The song, "Orphan Girl," from _Revival_ has been covered by [Emmylou Harris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emmylou_Harris "Emmylou Harris"), [Ann Wilson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ann_Wilson "Ann Wilson"), [Karin Bergquist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karin_Bergquist "Karin Bergquist") of [Over the Rhine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Over_the_Rhine_(band) "Over the Rhine (band)"), [Mindy Smith](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mindy_Smith "Mindy Smith"), [Patty Griffin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Patty_Griffin "Patty Griffin"), [Linda Ronstadt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linda_Ronstadt "Linda Ronstadt"), [Tim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tim_O'Brien_(musician) "Tim O'Brien (musician)") & [Mollie O'Brien](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mollie_O'Brien "Mollie O'Brien"), [Holly Williams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holly_Williams_(musician) "Holly Williams (musician)") and [Crooked Still](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crooked_Still "Crooked Still").

_Revival_ was nominated for the [1997](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grammy_Awards_of_1997 "Grammy Awards of 1997") [Grammy Award for Best Contemporary Folk Album](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grammy_Award_for_Best_Contemporary_Folk_Album "Grammy Award for Best Contemporary Folk Album"), but lost to [Bruce Springsteen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bruce_Springsteen "Bruce Springsteen")'s _[The Ghost of Tom Joad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Ghost_of_Tom_Joad "The Ghost of Tom Joad")._[\[18\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gillian_Welch#cite_note-nodep_1998-18)

### _Hell Among the Yearlings_

The duo's 1998 _[Hell Among the Yearlings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hell_Among_the_Yearlings "Hell Among the Yearlings")_ continued the rustic and dark themes; the songs' subject matter varies from a female character killing a rapist, a mining accident, a [murder ballad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Murder_ballad "Murder ballad"), and an ode to [morphine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Morphine "Morphine") before death.[\[19\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gillian_Welch#cite_note-rs_hell-19) Like _Revival_, _Hell Among the Yearlings_ featured a sparse style that focused on Rawlings and Welch's voices and guitars.[\[19\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gillian_Welch#cite_note-rs_hell-19)[\[20\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gillian_Welch#cite_note-wsj_1998-20)

[![Welch singing and playing guitar on stage, wearing a black dress.](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/5/5c/Gillian_Welch_2007.jpg/500px-Gillian_Welch_2007.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Gillian_Welch_2007.jpg) Welch performing at the 2007 New Orleans [Jazz Fest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jazz_Fest "Jazz Fest")

The album also received favorable reviews. [Robert Wilonsky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Wilonsky "Robert Wilonsky") of the _[Dallas Observer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dallas_Observer "Dallas Observer")_ observed that Welch "inhabits a role so completely, the fiction separating character and audience disappears".[\[21\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gillian_Welch#cite_note-dallas_hell-21) Thom Owens of AllMusic stated that the album "lacks some of the focus" of _Revival_, but is "a thoroughly satisfying second album" and proof that her debut was not a fluke.[\[22\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gillian_Welch#cite_note-amg_hell-22) _No Depression'_ s Farnum Brown commended the live and "immediate feel" of the album, Welch's [clawhammer banjo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clawhammer_banjo "Clawhammer banjo"),[\[23\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gillian_Welch#cite_note-livedaily_2004-23) and Rawlings' harmonies.[\[18\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gillian_Welch#cite_note-nodep_1998-18) Similar to _Revival_, Welch was praised for reflecting influences such as the Stanley Brothers, but still managing to create an original sound,[\[20\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gillian_Welch#cite_note-wsj_1998-20) while Chris Herrington from [Minneapolis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minneapolis "Minneapolis")'s _[City Pages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City_Pages "City Pages")_ criticized the songs' lack of authenticity. He wrote "Welch doesn't write folk songs; she writes folk songs about writing folk songs."[\[24\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gillian_Welch#cite_note-minn_city_pages-24)

### _O Brother, Where Art Thou?_

[![A man and two women crowded around a microphone and singing. The man is on the left and wearing a dark blue suit and a white cowboy hat. Welch, in the middle, is wearing a black dress, and the woman on the right is wearing a green dress.](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/a/a5/ACL_2008_-_Gillian_Welch_%26_Alison_Krauss.jpg/500px-ACL_2008_-_Gillian_Welch_%26_Alison_Krauss.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:ACL_2008_-_Gillian_Welch_&_Alison_Krauss.jpg) David Rawlings, Welch and Alison Krauss performing at the 2008 [Austin City Limits Music Festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austin_City_Limits_Music_Festival "Austin City Limits Music Festival")

Welch sang two songs and served as the associate producer for the Burnett-produced [soundtrack](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/O_Brother,_Where_Art_Thou%3F_(soundtrack) "O Brother, Where Art Thou? (soundtrack)") to the 2000 film of the [same name](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/O_Brother,_Where_Art_Thou%3F "O Brother, Where Art Thou?").[\[25\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gillian_Welch#cite_note-nodep_2001-25) She shared vocals with Alison Krauss on a rendition of the gospel song " [I'll Fly Away](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/I'll_Fly_Away "I'll Fly Away")." Dave McKenna of _[The Washington Post](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Washington_Post "The Washington Post")_ praised their version: the singers "soar together."[\[26\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gillian_Welch#cite_note-washpost_dec_24_2000-26) Burnett and Welch wrote additional lyrics for the song "Didn't Leave Nobody but the Baby," sung by Welch, Emmylou Harris, and Krauss. The song is an elaboration of an [old Mississippi tune](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Music_of_Mississippi "Music of Mississippi") discovered by [Alan Lomax](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alan_Lomax "Alan Lomax"), and was nominated for the [2002](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2002_Grammy_Awards "2002 Grammy Awards") [Grammy Award for Best Country Collaboration with Vocals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grammy_Award_for_Best_Country_Collaboration_with_Vocals "Grammy Award for Best Country Collaboration with Vocals").[\[27\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gillian_Welch#cite_note-cmt_1_2002-27) The [platinum album](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Platinum_album "Platinum album") won the 2002 [Grammy Award for Album of the Year](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grammy_Award_for_Album_of_the_Year "Grammy Award for Album of the Year"). The surprise success of the soundtrack gave Welch a career boost.[\[28\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gillian_Welch#cite_note-latimes_2003-28)[\[29\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gillian_Welch#cite_note-msnbc_2003-29) Welch also made a [cameo appearance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cameo_appearance "Cameo appearance") in the film.[\[30\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gillian_Welch#cite_note-la_times_cameo-30)

### _Time (The Revelator)_

When [Universal Music Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Universal_Music_Group "Universal Music Group") purchased Almo Sounds, Welch began her own independent label, Acony Records (named for the Appalachian wildflower, [Acony Bell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shortia_galacifolia "Shortia galacifolia"), subject of the song of that name on _Revival_).[\[7\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gillian_Welch#cite_note-la_times_2004-7)[\[15\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gillian_Welch#cite_note-cdh_2003-15) Rawlings produced the first release on Welch's new label, the 2001 album _[Time (The Revelator)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Time_(The_Revelator) "Time (The Revelator)")._[\[15\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gillian_Welch#cite_note-cdh_2003-15)[\[31\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gillian_Welch#cite_note-ottawa_time-31) All but one song on the album was recorded in the historic [RCA Studio B](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RCA_Studio_B "RCA Studio B") in Nashville.[\[32\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gillian_Welch#cite_note-billboard_time-32) "I Want To Sing That Rock and Roll" was recorded live at the [Ryman Auditorium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ryman_Auditorium "Ryman Auditorium") in the recording sessions for the concert film _[Down from the Mountain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Down_from_the_Mountain "Down from the Mountain")_.

For the 2003 release, _[Soul Journey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soul_Journey "Soul Journey")_, Welch and Rawlings explored new territory. Welch said: "I wanted to make it a happier record. Out of our four records, I thought this might be the one where you're driving down the road listening to it on a sunny summer day."[\[44\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gillian_Welch#cite_note-greensboro_nr_2003-44) Rawlings again produced the record. The album also reflected a change in the typically sparse instrumentation: Welch and Rawlings introduced a [dobro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dobro "Dobro"), violin, electric bass and drums, and Welch later said, "Everything's not supposed to sound the same, you want it to reflect change and growth."[\[10\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gillian_Welch#cite_note-boston_globe_2003-10)

In three songs of _Soul Journey,_ for the first time Welch and Rawlings recorded their own versions of traditional folk songs.[\[45\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gillian_Welch#cite_note-nodep_2003-45)

_Soul Journey_ also garnered significant acclaim. [John Harris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Harris_(critic) "John Harris (critic)") of _[Mojo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mojo_(magazine) "Mojo (magazine)")_ magazine described the album as "pretty much perfect", and _[Uncut](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uncut_(magazine) "Uncut (magazine)")_'s [Barney Hoskyns](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barney_Hoskyns "Barney Hoskyns") favorably compared it to [Bob Dylan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bob_Dylan "Bob Dylan") and [the Band](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Band "The Band")'s _[The Basement Tapes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Basement_Tapes "The Basement Tapes")_.[\[46\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gillian_Welch#cite_note-uncut_soulj-46)[\[47\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gillian_Welch#cite_note-mojo_soulj-47) [Will Hermes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Will_Hermes "Will Hermes") of _[Entertainment Weekly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Entertainment_Weekly "Entertainment Weekly")_ wrote that Welch has "never sounded deeper, realer, or sexier."[\[48\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gillian_Welch#cite_note-ew_2003-48) _Soul Journey_ peaked at No.107 on the Billboard charts, and reached No.3 for Independent Albums.[\[49\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gillian_Welch#cite_note-billboard_souljourney-49)

### David Rawlings Projects

In addition to their work released under her own name, Welch and Rawlings have continued to build upon their partnership with several releases under Rawlings' name. The Rawlings releases generally feature a larger string band and more lush arrangements than their Welch material, and have usually been released under the band name [Dave Rawlings Machine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dave_Rawlings_Machine "Dave Rawlings Machine"). Andy Gill of _[The Independent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Independent "The Independent")_ described the band's 2009 debut album _A Friend of a Friend_ as "akin to one of Welch's albums, but with the balance of their harmonies swapped to favour Rawlings' voice".[\[50\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gillian_Welch#cite_note-guardian_rawlings-50) Welch co-wrote five of the songs with Rawlings, and provided guitar and harmony vocals.[\[51\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gillian_Welch#cite_note-tenn_blog-51)[\[52\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gillian_Welch#cite_note-popmatters_rawlings-52) Although ostensibly Rawlings' first solo album, Alex Ramon of _[PopMatters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PopMatters "PopMatters")_ noted the similarities to Welch albums.[\[52\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gillian_Welch#cite_note-popmatters_rawlings-52) _Paste Magazine_'s [Stephen Deusner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stephen_Deusner "Stephen Deusner") praised _A Friend of a Friend_ for incorporating "a wide swath of traditional American music," comments echoed by _Rolling Stone_'s Will Hermes and in the _PopMatters_ piece.[\[52\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gillian_Welch#cite_note-popmatters_rawlings-52)[\[53\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gillian_Welch#cite_note-paste_rawlings_review-53)[\[54\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gillian_Welch#cite_note-rolling_stone_rawlings-54)

On September 18, 2015, the duo released their second album under the band title Dave Rawlings Machine, _Nashville Obsolete_. The band includes [Willie Watson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Willie_Watson_(musician) "Willie Watson (musician)"), [Paul Kowert](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_Kowert "Paul Kowert"), [Brittany Haas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brittany_Haas "Brittany Haas"), and occasionally includes [Jordan Tice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jordan_Tice "Jordan Tice").

Released on August 11, 2017, _Poor David's Almanack_ was the first Welch/Rawlings collaboration to be released under the name David Rawlings, dropping the previous Dave Rawlings Machine moniker. The song “Cumberland Gap,” which features on the album, was nominated for the 2018 [Grammy Award for Best American Roots Song](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grammy_Award_for_Best_American_Roots_Song "Grammy Award for Best American Roots Song"). It was also utilized in the opening sequence of the 2019 [Guy Ritchie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guy_Ritchie "Guy Ritchie") film, _[The Gentlemen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Gentlemen_(2019_film) "The Gentlemen (2019 film)")._[\[55\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gillian_Welch#cite_note-55)

### _The Harrow & the Harvest_

In a 2007 feature in _[The Guardian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Guardian "The Guardian")_, critic John Harris expressed frustration that there had not been a Gillian Welch release in four years.[\[56\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gillian_Welch#cite_note-guardian_2007-56) [Creation Records](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Creation_Records "Creation Records") founder [Alan McGee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alan_McGee "Alan McGee") showed optimism about Welch and Rawlings testing out some new songs while opening some concerts for [Rilo Kiley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rilo_Kiley "Rilo Kiley"), and wrote in a 2009 blog entry "the long gestation period signals nothing less than a perfect album".[\[57\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gillian_Welch#cite_note-guardian_sept_2009-57) In 2009, Rawlings said that recording for the next Gillian Welch album has started, but did not give a release date.[\[58\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gillian_Welch#cite_note-paste_rawlings-58)

_[The Harrow & the Harvest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Harrow_&_the_Harvest "The Harrow & the Harvest")_ was released on June 28, 2011.[\[59\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gillian_Welch#cite_note-asw-59) Welch attributed the long time period between releases to dissatisfaction with the songs they were writing.[\[60\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gillian_Welch#cite_note-harvesttime-60) She explained: "Our songcraft slipped and I really don't know why. It's not uncommon. It's something that happens to writers. It's the deepest frustration we have come through, hence the album title."[\[60\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gillian_Welch#cite_note-harvesttime-60) The writing process involved "this endless back and forth between the two of us," Welch said, stating that "It's our most intertwined, co-authored, jointly-composed album."[\[61\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gillian_Welch#cite_note-the_mountain-61)

The album received praise from publications such as the _[Los Angeles Times](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Los_Angeles_Times "Los Angeles Times")_, _[Uncut](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uncut_(magazine) "Uncut (magazine)")_, and _[Rolling Stone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rolling_Stone "Rolling Stone")_.[\[62\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gillian_Welch#cite_note-lat_rev-62)[\[63\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gillian_Welch#cite_note-uncut_rev-63)[\[64\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gillian_Welch#cite_note-rs_rev-64) Thom Jurek of AllMusic wrote that the album "is stunning for its intimacy, its lack of studio artifice, its warmth and its timeless, if hard won, songcraft".[\[65\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gillian_Welch#cite_note-amg_rev-65)

The album peaked at No.20 on the US Billboard 200 and No.25 on the [UK Albums Chart](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UK_Albums_Chart "UK Albums Chart").[\[66\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gillian_Welch#cite_note-66)[\[67\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gillian_Welch#cite_note-ukcharts-67) It was nominated for a Grammy for Best Contemporary Folk Album, as well as Best Engineered Album.[\[68\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gillian_Welch#cite_note-68)

### _Boots No 1: The Official Revival Bootleg_

_Boots No 1: The Official Revival Bootleg_, was released on November 25, 2016. The album celebrates the 20th anniversary of Welch's debut album, _[Revival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Revival_(Gillian_Welch_album) "Revival (Gillian Welch album)")_, and includes outtakes, alternate versions, and demos of the songs featured on the original, as well as eight new unreleased tracks.

### _All the Good Times (Are Past & Gone)_

In July 2020, Welch and Rawlings announced _[All the Good Times (Are Past & Gone)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/All_the_Good_Times_(Are_Past_&_Gone) "All the Good Times (Are Past & Gone)")_, an album of covers and traditional songs recorded at their home during the COVID-19 lockdowns of 2020.[\[69\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gillian_Welch#cite_note-69) _All the Good Times_ is notably the first album in their decades-long history of collaboration to be released jointly in both of their names.[\[70\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gillian_Welch#cite_note-70) The album won the [2021 Grammy Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/63rd_Annual_Grammy_Awards "63rd Annual Grammy Awards") for [Best Folk Album](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grammy_Award_for_Best_Folk_Album "Grammy Award for Best Folk Album").[\[2\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gillian_Welch#cite_note-:0-2)

### _Woodland_

On July 19, 2024, Welch and Rawlings announced _[Woodland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Woodland_(Gillian_Welch_and_David_Rawlings_album) "Woodland (Gillian Welch and David Rawlings album)")_, to be released August 23, 2024 through Acony Records, and shared its first single, "Empty Trainload of Sky." The album is the first collection of original material from Welch since 2011's _The Harrow & the Harvest_, and the first from Rawlings since 2017's _Poor David's Almanack_.[\[71\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gillian_Welch#cite_note-71)[\[72\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gillian_Welch#cite_note-72) The album won the [2025 Grammy Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/67th_Annual_Grammy_Awards "67th Annual Grammy Awards") for [Best Folk Album](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grammy_Award_for_Best_Folk_Album "Grammy Award for Best Folk Album"), marking Rawling's second win of the award and making him and Welch the only two people to have won the award more than once.[\[3\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gillian_Welch#cite_note-auto-3)

## Musical style

Welch and Rawlings incorporate elements of early twentieth century music such as [old time](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old-time_music "Old-time music"), [classic country](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Classic_country "Classic country"), [gospel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gospel_music "Gospel music") and [traditional bluegrass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Traditional_bluegrass "Traditional bluegrass") with modern elements of [rhythm and blues](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rhythm_and_blues "Rhythm and blues"), [rock 'n' roll](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rock_'n'_roll "Rock 'n' roll"), [jazz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jazz "Jazz"), and [punk rock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Punk_rock "Punk rock").[\[1\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gillian_Welch#cite_note-new_yorker-1) _[The New Yorker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_New_Yorker "The New Yorker")_'s [Alec Wilkinson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alec_Wilkinson "Alec Wilkinson") maintained their musical style is "not easily classified—it is at once innovative and obliquely reminiscent of past rural forms".[\[1\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gillian_Welch#cite_note-new_yorker-1)

The instrumentation on their songs is usually a simple arrangement, with Welch and Rawlings accompanying their own vocals with acoustic guitars, banjos, or a mandolin.[\[1\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gillian_Welch#cite_note-new_yorker-1) Welch plays rhythm guitar with a 1956 [Gibson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gibson_Guitar_Corporation "Gibson Guitar Corporation") J-50 (or banjo), while Rawlings plays lead on a 1935 [Epiphone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Epiphone "Epiphone") Olympic Guitar.[\[73\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gillian_Welch#cite_note-acoustic_guitar_what_they_play-73) _The New Yorker_'s Wilkinson described Rawlings as a "strikingly inventive guitarist" who plays solos that are "daring melodic leaps".[\[1\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gillian_Welch#cite_note-new_yorker-1) A review in _No Depression_ by Andy Moore observed that Rawlings "squeezes, strokes, chokes and does just about everything but blow into" his guitar.[\[74\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gillian_Welch#cite_note-nodep_2009_rawlings-74)

### Themes

Many songs performed by Welch and Rawlings contain dark themes about social outcasts struggling against such elements as poverty, drug addiction, death, a disconnection from their family, and an unresponsive God.[\[1\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gillian_Welch#cite_note-new_yorker-1) Despite Welch being the lead singer, several of these characters are male.[\[1\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gillian_Welch#cite_note-new_yorker-1) Welch has commented, "To be commercial, everybody wants happy love songs. People would flat-out ask me, 'Don't you have any happy love songs?' Well, as a matter of fact, I don't. I've got songs about orphans and morphine addicts."[\[12\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gillian_Welch#cite_note-times_london_2003-12) To reflect these themes, Welch and Rawlings often employ a slow pace to their songs. Their tempo is compared to a "slow heartbeat", and Cowperthwait of _Rolling Stone_ observed that their songs "can lull you into near-hypnosis and then make your jaw drop with one final revelation".[\[1\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gillian_Welch#cite_note-new_yorker-1)[\[75\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gillian_Welch#cite_note-rs_1998-75)

### Reception

[![Welch in a green tanktop sings into a microphone while playing guitar.](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/b/b8/Gillian_welch.jpg/500px-Gillian_welch.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Gillian_welch.jpg) Welch performing at the 2009 [Newport Folk Festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Newport_Folk_Festival "Newport Folk Festival")

[Geoffrey Himes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geoffrey_Himes "Geoffrey Himes") of _The Washington Post_ described Welch as "one of the most interesting singer-songwriters of her generation".[\[76\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gillian_Welch#cite_note-wash_post_2001-76) In 2003, Tom Kielty of _[The Boston Globe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Boston_Globe "The Boston Globe")_ observed that she was "quietly establishing one of the most impressive catalogs in contemporary roots music", and a 2007 piece in _The Guardian_ by John Harris called Welch "one of the decade's greatest talents".[\[56\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gillian_Welch#cite_note-guardian_2007-56)[\[77\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gillian_Welch#cite_note-boston_2003-77) Critic [Robert Hilburn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Hilburn "Robert Hilburn") of the _[Los Angeles Times](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Los_Angeles_Times "Los Angeles Times")_ wrote, "At every turn, she demonstrates a spark and commitment that should endear her to anyone from country and folk to pop and rock fans who appreciate imagination and heart."[\[78\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gillian_Welch#cite_note-latimes_1996-78)

When Welch's first two albums came out, critics questioned the authenticity of her music, as she was raised in [Southern California](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_California "Southern California"), but performed [Appalachian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Appalachia "Appalachia") themed songs.[\[11\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gillian_Welch#cite_note-theage_bevhillybilly-11)[\[20\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gillian_Welch#cite_note-wsj_1998-20)[\[79\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gillian_Welch#cite_note-boston_globe_1998-79) For _Revival_, Welch was criticized for "manufacturing emotion", and a review of _Hell Among the Yearlings_ by Chris Herrington of _City Pages_ stated, "Welch is someone who discovered old-time music in college and decided that her own sheltered life could never be worth writing about", and that she is "completely devoid of individuality".[\[16\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gillian_Welch#cite_note-rollingstone_revival-16)[\[24\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gillian_Welch#cite_note-minn_city_pages-24) Other critics rejected the notion that her background affects the authenticity of her music. Music critic [Mark Kemp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mark_Kemp "Mark Kemp") defended Welch in a _[New York Times](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_Times "New York Times")_ piece:

> The first-person protagonist of Ms. Welch's song ("Caleb Meyer") may be a young girl from a time and place that Ms. Welch will never fully understand, but the feelings the singer expresses about rape, and the respect she displays for her chosen musical genre, are nothing if not poignantly authentic. Likewise, it matters not whether Ms. Welch has ever walked the streets of "the black dust towns of [East Tennessee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Tennessee "East Tennessee")" about which she sings in "Miner's Refrain" because the sense of foreboding that she expresses for the men who once labored in coal mines with futile hopes of a better life comes through loud and clear.[\[80\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gillian_Welch#cite_note-nyt_1998-80)

_[The Wall Street Journal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Wall_Street_Journal "The Wall Street Journal")'_ s Taylor Holliday echoed this: "Stingy critics give Ms. Welch a hard time because she's a California city girl, not an Appalachian coal miner's daughter. But as [Lucinda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lucinda_Williams "Lucinda Williams") or Emmylou might attest, love of the music is not a birthright, but an earned right. Listen to Ms. Welch yodel, in a tune about that no-good "gal" Morphine, and you know she's as mountain as they come."[\[20\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gillian_Welch#cite_note-wsj_1998-20)

On September 16, 2015, the duo was awarded the Lifetime Achievement Award for Songwriting by the Americana Music Association.

### Influences and collaborations

[![A bearded man who looks to be in his 60s wearing a dark red shirt and Welch, smiling, with her arm around him. Huge white tents are in the background.](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/b/bd/Bob_Weir_%26_Gillian_Welch.jpg/500px-Bob_Weir_%26_Gillian_Welch.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Bob_Weir_&_Gillian_Welch.jpg) Welch with [Bob Weir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bob_Weir "Bob Weir") of the [Grateful Dead](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grateful_Dead "Grateful Dead") at [Hardly Strictly Bluegrass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hardly_Strictly_Bluegrass "Hardly Strictly Bluegrass"), 2006

> ... it wasn't until I became friends with Dave and Gil, about ten years ago, that I had people who understood songwriting and could express it to me in a way that left out the guesswork.[\[81\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gillian_Welch#cite_note-Berkowitz-81)

—Ketch Secor, [Old Crow Medicine Show](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_Crow_Medicine_Show "Old Crow Medicine Show")

Welch emphasizes music from a previous era as her major influence. She said that "by and large I listen to people who are dead. I'm really of the tried-and-true school. I let 50 years go by and see what's really relevant."[\[79\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gillian_Welch#cite_note-boston_globe_1998-79) Welch has acknowledged inspiration from several traditional country artists, including the Stanley Brothers, the [Carter Family](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carter_Family "Carter Family"), the [Louvin Brothers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louvin_Brothers "Louvin Brothers"), and the [Blue Sky Boys](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blue_Sky_Boys "Blue Sky Boys").[\[1\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gillian_Welch#cite_note-new_yorker-1)[\[82\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gillian_Welch#cite_note-nyt_feb_2004-82) She explained her relationship with traditional music by saying, "I've never tried to be traditional. It's been a springboard for me and I love it and revere it and would not be doing what I do without the music of the [Monroe Brothers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bill_Monroe "Bill Monroe"), the Stanley Brothers and the Carter Family. However, it was clear I was never going to be able to do exactly that; I'm a songwriter."[\[83\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gillian_Welch#cite_note-newzealand_2004-83)

In addition to the strong country influence, Welch also draws on a repertoire of rock 'n' roll such artists as [Bob Dylan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bob_Dylan "Bob Dylan"), [Chuck Berry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chuck_Berry "Chuck Berry"), [Neil Young](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neil_Young "Neil Young"), the [Grateful Dead](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grateful_Dead "Grateful Dead") and the [Velvet Underground](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Velvet_Underground "Velvet Underground").[\[1\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gillian_Welch#cite_note-new_yorker-1)[\[11\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gillian_Welch#cite_note-theage_bevhillybilly-11)[\[84\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gillian_Welch#cite_note-metro_sv-84) She said the [alternative rock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alternative_rock "Alternative rock") bands [Throwing Muses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Throwing_Muses "Throwing Muses"), [Pixies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pixies_(band) "Pixies (band)") and [Camper Van Beethoven](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Camper_Van_Beethoven "Camper Van Beethoven") "don't directly inform my music, but they're in there."[\[79\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gillian_Welch#cite_note-boston_globe_1998-79)[\[84\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gillian_Welch#cite_note-metro_sv-84)

Welch has recorded songs with a variety of notable artists. Welch and Rawlings' contributions on Hitchcock's album _[Spooked](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spooked_(album) "Spooked (album)")_ was described by Christopher Bahn of _[The A.V. Club](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_A.V._Club "The A.V. Club")_ as "subtle but vital".[\[85\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gillian_Welch#cite_note-avclub_hitchcock-85) She later created the cover art for Hitchcock's 2014 album _[The Man Upstairs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Man_Upstairs_(Robyn_Hitchcock_album) "The Man Upstairs (Robyn Hitchcock album)")_.[\[86\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gillian_Welch#cite_note-exclaim_robyn-86) Mark Deming of AllMusic wrote that their work on [Ryan Adams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ryan_Adams "Ryan Adams")' album _[Heartbreaker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heartbreaker_(Ryan_Adams_album) "Heartbreaker (Ryan Adams album)")_ "brought out the best in Adams".[\[87\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gillian_Welch#cite_note-amg_heartbreaker-87)[\[88\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gillian_Welch#cite_note-decemberists25Jan2011-88)

### Performances

[![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/0/0d/Gillian_Welch_at_MerleFest-2006-04-29.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail_unscaled)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Gillian_Welch_at_MerleFest-2006-04-29.jpg) Welch performing in 2006

Welch and Rawlings have played many music festivals, including the [Newport Folk Festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Newport_Folk_Festival "Newport Folk Festival"), [Coachella Festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coachella_Festival "Coachella Festival"), the [Telluride Bluegrass Festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Telluride_Bluegrass_Festival "Telluride Bluegrass Festival"), the [Cambridge Folk Festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cambridge_Folk_Festival "Cambridge Folk Festival"), [Bonnaroo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bonnaroo "Bonnaroo"), [MerleFest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MerleFest "MerleFest"), the [Austin City Limits Festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austin_City_Limits_Festival "Austin City Limits Festival"), and [Farm Aid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Farm_Aid "Farm Aid").[\[11\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gillian_Welch#cite_note-theage_bevhillybilly-11)[\[83\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gillian_Welch#cite_note-newzealand_2004-83)[\[89\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gillian_Welch#cite_note-npr_newport-89)[\[90\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gillian_Welch#cite_note-nyt_coachella-90)[\[91\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gillian_Welch#cite_note-usa_today_telluride-91)[\[92\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gillian_Welch#cite_note-paste_ACL-92)[\[93\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gillian_Welch#cite_note-cmt_merlefest-93)[\[94\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gillian_Welch#cite_note-bbc_cambridge-94)[\[95\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gillian_Welch#cite_note-farmaid-95) They have toured North America extensively, and have played in Europe, Australia, and New Zealand.[\[11\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gillian_Welch#cite_note-theage_bevhillybilly-11)[\[23\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gillian_Welch#cite_note-livedaily_2004-23)[\[83\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gillian_Welch#cite_note-newzealand_2004-83)[\[96\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gillian_Welch#cite_note-guardian_concertreview_2004-96) Concert reviews have praised the chemistry between Welch and Rawlings on stage.[\[10\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gillian_Welch#cite_note-boston_globe_2003-10)[\[96\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gillian_Welch#cite_note-guardian_concertreview_2004-96)[\[97\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gillian_Welch#cite_note-seattle_pi_concert-97) Tizzy Asher of the _[Seattle Post-Intelligencer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seattle_Post-Intelligencer "Seattle Post-Intelligencer")_ wrote "there was a startling unspoken intimacy between them. They anticipated each other's movements and shifted when necessary to fit each other."[\[97\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gillian_Welch#cite_note-seattle_pi_concert-97) On August 6, 2022, they performed on the [Grand Ole Opry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grand_Ole_Opry "Grand Ole Opry").[\[98\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gillian_Welch#cite_note-grand_ole_opry_2022-98) In April 2026, they began the "Acoustic Reckoning" tour, designed as a tribute to the music of the [Grateful Dead](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grateful_Dead "Grateful Dead").[\[99\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gillian_Welch#cite_note-99)

The Dave Rawlings Machine have toured North America, with the band originally composed of Rawlings, Welch and three members of Old Crow Medicine Show.[\[100\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gillian_Welch#cite_note-nodep_drm_tour-100) The band is currently composed of Rawlings, Welch, Wilie Watson, Paul Kowert, and Brittany Haas. Welch and Rawlings also participate in group tours with notable musicians. In 2004, they were part of the [Sweet Harmony Traveling Revue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sweet_Harmony_Traveling_Revue?action=edit&redlink=1 "Sweet Harmony Traveling Revue"), a three-week US tour with [Patty Griffin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Patty_Griffin "Patty Griffin"), [Buddy Miller](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buddy_Miller "Buddy Miller") and Emmylou Harris.[\[101\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gillian_Welch#cite_note-nodep_sweet-101) In 2009, the Dave Rawlings Machine joined Old Crow Medicine Show, [the Felice Brothers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Felice_Brothers "The Felice Brothers") and [Justin Townes Earle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Justin_Townes_Earle "Justin Townes Earle") for the Big Surprise Tour, a US tour described as a "roots-music extravaganza".[\[102\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gillian_Welch#cite_note-nyt_big_surprise-102) In 2011, Welch was a support act for [Buffalo Springfield](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buffalo_Springfield "Buffalo Springfield"), who performed and toured that year.[\[103\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gillian_Welch#cite_note-rs30Mar2011-103)
