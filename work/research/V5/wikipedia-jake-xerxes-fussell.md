Musical artist

**Jake Xerxes Fussell** (born 1981, in [Columbus, Georgia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Columbus,_Georgia "Columbus, Georgia")) is an American singer and guitarist who plays [folk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Folk_music "Folk music") and [blues](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blues "Blues") music, with a focus on traditional Southern folk songs.[\[1\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jake_Xerxes_Fussell#cite_note-1)

## Background

[![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/e/e5/Jake_Xerxes_Fussell_opening_for_The_Decemberists.jpg/500px-Jake_Xerxes_Fussell_opening_for_The_Decemberists.jpg)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Jake_Xerxes_Fussell_opening_for_The_Decemberists.jpg) Fussell opening for [The Decemberists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Decemberists "The Decemberists") at [Surly Brewing Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Surly_Brewing_Company "Surly Brewing Company") in Minneapolis.

Fussell was raised in Columbus, Georgia, the son of Fred C. Fussell, a folklorist, curator, and photographer.[\[2\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jake_Xerxes_Fussell#cite_note-2) As a teenager Jake began playing and studying with elder musicians in the Chattahoochee Valley, apprenticing with Piedmont blues legend [Precious Bryant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Precious_Bryant "Precious Bryant"), with whom he toured and recorded. He joined a [Phenix City, Alabama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phenix_City,_Alabama "Phenix City, Alabama"), country band who were students of Jimmie Tarlton of [Darby and Tarlton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Darby_and_Tarlton "Darby and Tarlton"), and accompanied [Etta Baker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Etta_Baker "Etta Baker") in concert in North Carolina.[\[3\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jake_Xerxes_Fussell#cite_note-:0-3)

Before releasing his debut album, Fussell recorded vernacular Southern Music in the field with music historian [George Mitchell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Mitchell_(music_historian) "George Mitchell (music historian)") and Grammy Award-winning folklorist [Art Rosenbaum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Art_Rosenbaum "Art Rosenbaum").[\[3\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jake_Xerxes_Fussell#cite_note-:0-3) He often leverages his knowledge of traditional American folk music in his own work, for example, by adapting the cry of a 19th Century fishmonger for his song "The River St. John’s".[\[4\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jake_Xerxes_Fussell#cite_note-4)

Fussell has appeared on _[A Prairie Home Companion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A_Prairie_Home_Companion "A Prairie Home Companion")_.[\[5\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jake_Xerxes_Fussell#cite_note-5) He toured in the U.S. as an opening act for [Wilco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wilco "Wilco"), [Bill Callahan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bill_Callahan_(musician) "Bill Callahan (musician)"), and [The Decemberists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Decemberists "The Decemberists").[\[6\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jake_Xerxes_Fussell#cite_note-6)[\[7\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jake_Xerxes_Fussell#cite_note-7)[\[8\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jake_Xerxes_Fussell#cite_note-8)

## Music

In 2015, Fussell released his debut self-titled album composed entirely of adapted folk and blues songs. [William Tyler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Tyler_(musician) "William Tyler (musician)") produced the album and contributed features on the guitar and organ. [Chris Scruggs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chris_Scruggs "Chris Scruggs") recorded features on steel guitar, bass, and mandolin, and [Hoot Hester](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hoot_Hester "Hoot Hester") on the fiddle.[\[9\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jake_Xerxes_Fussell#cite_note-9)

In 2017, Fussell released his second album, _What in the Natural World._ Writing for _[The New Yorker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_New_Yorker "The New Yorker")_, [Amanda Petrusich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amanda_Petrusich "Amanda Petrusich") named _What in the Natural World_ to her "My Ten Best Albums of 2017".[\[10\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jake_Xerxes_Fussell#cite_note-10)

Fussell released his third album in January 2019. In a review of his 2019 album _Out of Sight_, _[Pitchfork](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pitchfork_(website) "Pitchfork (website)")_ magazine noted,

> Musicians like Jake Xerxes Fussell are nearly as rare nowadays as the material he performs. “All songs are traditional & in the public domain,” reads the sole composition credit on _Out of Sight_, Fussell’s often-transcendent third album. Put another way: Each of these nine songs survived the great folk-pop copyright round-up of the 1950s and ’60s (and beyond), when publishers hunted down and claimed untold numbers of “traditional” melodies as their own.[\[11\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jake_Xerxes_Fussell#cite_note-11)

In 2022, Fussell released his fourth album, _Good and Green Again._ Produced by James Elkington, the album is a combination of traditional song adaptations and original compositions.

Fussell's fifth album, _When I'm Called_, was announced on April 17, 2024, along with the release of the album's first single, "Going to Georgia". The album, produced by Elkington, was released on July 12, 2024, on Fat Possum Records. The album features nine songs, "some of them traditional \[and\] some not so traditional."[\[12\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jake_Xerxes_Fussell#cite_note-12)
