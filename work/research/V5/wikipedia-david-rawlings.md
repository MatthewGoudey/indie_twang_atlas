Musical artist

**David Todd Rawlings** (born December 30, 1969) is an American guitarist, singer, and record producer. He is known for his musical partnership with singer and songwriter [Gillian Welch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gillian_Welch "Gillian Welch").[\[1\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Rawlings#cite_note-NPR.org-1) He and Welch were nominated for the [Academy Award for Best Original Song](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academy_Award_for_Best_Original_Song "Academy Award for Best Original Song") at the [91st Academy Awards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/91st_Academy_Awards "91st Academy Awards") for " [When a Cowboy Trades His Spurs for Wings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/When_a_Cowboy_Trades_His_Spurs_for_Wings "When a Cowboy Trades His Spurs for Wings")" from _[The Ballad of Buster Scruggs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Ballad_of_Buster_Scruggs "The Ballad of Buster Scruggs")_. In 2020, Welch and Rawlings released _[All the Good Times (Are Past & Gone)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/All_the_Good_Times_(Are_Past_&_Gone) "All the Good Times (Are Past & Gone)")_, which won the 2021 [Grammy Award for Best Folk Album](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grammy_Award_for_Best_Folk_Album "Grammy Award for Best Folk Album").[\[2\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Rawlings#cite_note-:0-2) In 2024, Welch and Rawlings released [_Woodland_](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Woodland_(album) "Woodland (album)"), which won the 2025 Grammy Award for Best Folk Album, currently making Welch and Rawlings the only duo to win the award more than once.[\[3\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Rawlings#cite_note-3)

## Life and career

Rawlings attended the [Berklee College of Music](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berklee_College_of_Music "Berklee College of Music") and studied with Lauren Passarelli.[\[4\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Rawlings#cite_note-4) He produced albums by Gillian Welch, Willie Watson, [Dawes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dawes_(band) "Dawes (band)"), and [Old Crow Medicine Show](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_Crow_Medicine_Show "Old Crow Medicine Show"). In the mid-2010s he led a band called the Dave Rawlings Machine, composed of Rawlings, Gillian Welch, [Willie Watson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Willie_Watson_(musician) "Willie Watson (musician)"), [Paul Kowert](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_Kowert "Paul Kowert"), and [Brittany Haas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brittany_Haas "Brittany Haas"). [John Paul Jones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Paul_Jones_(musician) "John Paul Jones (musician)") of [Led Zeppelin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Led_Zeppelin "Led Zeppelin") has been known to play mandolin with the band occasionally.[\[5\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Rawlings#cite_note-auto-5) Rawlings contributed to the albums _[Cassadaga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cassadaga_(album) "Cassadaga (album)")_ by [Bright Eyes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bright_Eyes_(band) "Bright Eyes (band)"), _[Spooked](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spooked_(album) "Spooked (album)")_ by [Robyn Hitchcock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robyn_Hitchcock "Robyn Hitchcock"), and _[Heartbreaker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heartbreaker_(Ryan_Adams_album) "Heartbreaker (Ryan Adams album)")_ by [Ryan Adams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ryan_Adams "Ryan Adams"), with whom he wrote two songs, "To Be Young (Is to Be Sad, Is to Be High)" and " [Touch, Feel and Lose](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Touch,_Feel_and_Lose "Touch, Feel and Lose")".[\[6\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Rawlings#cite_note-6) His vocal style has often been compared to that of Bob Dylan.[\[7\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Rawlings#cite_note-pastemagazine.com-7)[\[8\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Rawlings#cite_note-8)

## Recordings

### _A Friend of a Friend_ (2009)

The Dave Rawlings Machine album _A Friend of a Friend_ was released on November 17, 2009. Rawlings recorded the album in [Nashville](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nashville "Nashville"), and produced it himself. Gillian Welch is credited as a co-writer on five of the album's songs as well playing in the band with members of [Tom Petty and the Heartbreakers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tom_Petty_and_the_Heartbreakers "Tom Petty and the Heartbreakers"), Old Crow Medicine Show, and Bright Eyes.[\[9\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Rawlings#cite_note-9)[\[10\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Rawlings#cite_note-billboard_9_2009-10) The album features a medley of a [Neil Young](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neil_Young "Neil Young") and [Bright Eyes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bright_Eyes_(band) "Bright Eyes (band)") song, as well as songs Rawlings co-wrote with Ryan Adams and Old Crow Medicine Show.[\[7\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Rawlings#cite_note-pastemagazine.com-7) [Morgan Nagler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Morgan_Nagler "Morgan Nagler") (of the band Whispertown) is credited with co-writing the song "Sweet Tooth".[\[11\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Rawlings#cite_note-11)

### _Nashville Obsolete_ (2015)

In 2015 Dave Rawlings Machine released a second album, _Nashville Obsolete_.[\[12\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Rawlings#cite_note-12) The album was named to Rolling Stone's list of the top 40 country albums of 2016.[\[5\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Rawlings#cite_note-auto-5) In late 2016 Acony issued _Boots No 1: The Official Revival Bootleg_, a deluxe version of the 1996 Welch album considered a "modern Americana classic" and the first collaboration for the duo.[\[1\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Rawlings#cite_note-NPR.org-1)

### _Poor David's Almanack_ (2017)

_Poor David's Almanack_, released on August 11, 2017, via [Acony Records](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Acony_Records "Acony Records"), is the eighth collaborative LP for Rawlings and Welch and the first under the name David Rawlings.[\[13\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Rawlings#cite_note-13)[\[14\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Rawlings#cite_note-14) In addition to Welch and Watson, the backup band includes Brittany Haas, Paul Kowert, Ketch Secor of Old Crow Medicine Show, and [Taylor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taylor_Goldsmith "Taylor Goldsmith") and Griffin Goldsmith of [Dawes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dawes_(band) "Dawes (band)"). The album was recorded with Ken Scott and Matt Andrews at the Rawlings/Welch duo's [Woodland Sound Studios](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Woodland_Sound_Studios "Woodland Sound Studios") in Nashville.[\[15\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Rawlings#cite_note-McKenna-15) The album features an original woodcut piece by Gillian Welch on the cover and was notably the first vinyl release on Welch and Rawlings' Acony record label.[\[16\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Rawlings#cite_note-16)[\[17\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Rawlings#cite_note-17)

> Start to finish, the album is a testament both to his immeasurable talent and to his essential place in the roots and Americana music scene. It's also a chance for a guy otherwise happy to play sideman or stand behind the boards to step out just a touch further into a well-deserved spotlight.[\[15\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Rawlings#cite_note-McKenna-15)

—Brittney McKenna, Rolling Stone

"Cumberland Gap" was used as the opening song to [Guy Ritchie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guy_Ritchie "Guy Ritchie")'s 2019 film, [_The Gentlemen_](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Gentlemen_(2019_film) "The Gentlemen (2019 film)"). It was nominated for [Best American Roots Song](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Best_American_Roots_Song "Best American Roots Song") at the [2018 Grammy Awards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018_Grammy_Awards "2018 Grammy Awards").

### _All the Good Times (Are Past and Gone)_ (2020)

In July 2020, Rawlings and Welch announced _[All the Good Times (Are Past & Gone)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/All_the_Good_Times_(Are_Past_&_Gone) "All the Good Times (Are Past & Gone)")_, an album of covers and traditional songs recorded at their home during the COVID-19 lockdowns of 2020.[\[18\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Rawlings#cite_note-18) _All the Good Times_ is notably the first album in their decades-long history of collaboration to be released jointly in both of their names.[\[19\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Rawlings#cite_note-19) The album won the [2021 Grammy Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/63rd_Annual_Grammy_Awards "63rd Annual Grammy Awards") for [Best Folk Album](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Best_Folk_Album "Best Folk Album").[\[2\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Rawlings#cite_note-:0-2)

## Instrument

Rawlings achieves his signature guitar sound [flatpicking](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flatpicking "Flatpicking") a small archtop guitar. The 1935 [Epiphone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Epiphone "Epiphone") Olympic that has been his primary instrument was a mid-priced guitar for its time, with a carved arched solid sprucewood top, carved arched solid mahogany back and mahogany sides.[\[20\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Rawlings#cite_note-1934_Epiphone_sales_catalog-20) It sold for about $35 in 1935[\[21\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Rawlings#cite_note-21) (equivalent to $820in 2025[\[22\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Rawlings#cite_note-inflation-US-22)). The guitar's lower bout measures 13 5/8inches wide, and it has three piece f-holes.[\[20\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Rawlings#cite_note-1934_Epiphone_sales_catalog-20)

Rawlings "scavenged" the guitar from a friend's garage and is now hardly seen playing anything else. As he states, "I just picked it up. It was filthy, and it didn't have strings. You could just see the shape of it under the sawdust." Rawlings had a new one piece bridge made for it and brought it to a recording session for Welch's first record. "As soon as I heard it through the microphone and through the speakers I was like, 'I love this guitar'." he says.[\[23\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Rawlings#cite_note-23)
