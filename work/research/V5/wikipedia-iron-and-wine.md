Musical artist

[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/f/fc/Sam_beam_0001.jpg/500px-Sam_beam_0001.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Sam_beam_0001.jpg) Iron & Wine at a 2006 concert at [Brooklyn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brooklyn "Brooklyn")'s [McCarren Park Pool](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/McCarren_Park_Pool "McCarren Park Pool")

**Samuel Ervin Beam**[\[2\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iron_&_Wine#cite_note-2) (born July 26, 1974), better known by his stage name **Iron & Wine**, is an American singer-songwriter. He has released eight studio albums, several [EPs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Extended_play "Extended play") and [singles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Single_(music) "Single (music)"), as well as a few [download-only](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Music_download "Music download") releases, which include a [live album](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iron_&_Wine_Live_Bonnaroo "Iron & Wine Live Bonnaroo") (a recording of his 2005 [Bonnaroo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bonnaroo_Music_Festival "Bonnaroo Music Festival") performance). He occasionally tours with a full band.[\[3\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iron_&_Wine#cite_note-3)

The name _Iron & Wine_ is taken from a dietary supplement named "Beef, Iron & Wine" that he found in a general store while shooting a film.[\[4\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iron_&_Wine#cite_note-4)

## Early life

Beam was raised in [Chapin, South Carolina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chapin,_South_Carolina "Chapin, South Carolina"), where his father worked in land management and his mother was a schoolteacher. When he was a child, his family took regular trips to the country, where his grandfather ran a farm. He attended Seven Oaks Elementary School and [Chapin High School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chapin_High_School "Chapin High School"). While home from college, he was a waiter at California Dreaming restaurant in Columbia. Beam earned a bachelor's degree in art from [Virginia Commonwealth University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Virginia_Commonwealth_University "Virginia Commonwealth University") in [Richmond, Virginia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richmond,_Virginia "Richmond, Virginia"). He specialized in painting before graduating from the [Florida State University Film School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Florida_State_University_Film_School "Florida State University Film School") with an [MFA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Master_of_Fine_Arts "Master of Fine Arts") degree. Before the release of the first Iron & Wine album, Beam's main source of income was as a professor of film and cinematography at the [University of Miami](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Miami "University of Miami") and [Miami International University of Art & Design](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miami_International_University_of_Art_&_Design "Miami International University of Art & Design").[\[5\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iron_&_Wine#cite_note-5)

He had been writing songs for over seven years before a friend lent him a four-track recorder. One of Beam's home recordings came to the attention of [Ben Bridwell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ben_Bridwell "Ben Bridwell"), of [Band of Horses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Band_of_Horses "Band of Horses"), and eventually to Mike McGonigal, editor of [_Yeti_ magazine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yeti_(magazine) "Yeti (magazine)"). McGonigal chose the song "Dead Man's Will", later released on _[In the Reins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/In_the_Reins "In the Reins")_, for inclusion on one of his magazine's compilation CDs. [Jonathan Poneman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jonathan_Poneman "Jonathan Poneman") of [Sub Pop](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sub_Pop "Sub Pop") records contacted Beam to propose a deal.[\[6\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iron_&_Wine#cite_note-6)[\[7\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iron_&_Wine#cite_note-AmericanStatesman-7)[\[8\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iron_&_Wine#cite_note-8)[\[9\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iron_&_Wine#cite_note-9)

## Musical career

Beam released his first Iron & Wine album, _[The Creek Drank the Cradle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Creek_Drank_the_Cradle "The Creek Drank the Cradle")_, on the Sub Pop label in 2002. Beam wrote, performed, recorded and produced the album in his home studio. Featuring acoustic guitars, [banjo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Banjo "Banjo"), and [slide guitar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slide_guitar "Slide guitar"), the album's music has been compared to that of [Nick Drake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nick_Drake "Nick Drake"),[\[10\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iron_&_Wine#cite_note-10) [Simon and Garfunkel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simon_and_Garfunkel "Simon and Garfunkel"),[\[11\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iron_&_Wine#cite_note-11) [Elliott Smith](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elliott_Smith "Elliott Smith"), [Neil Young](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neil_Young "Neil Young") and [John Fahey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Fahey_(musician) "John Fahey (musician)").

Also in 2002, Beam recorded a [cover](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cover_version "Cover version") of [The Postal Service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Postal_Service "The Postal Service")'s then-unreleased song " [Such Great Heights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Such_Great_Heights "Such Great Heights")". Rather than being included on an Iron & Wine release, the track was initially included as a [b-side](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A-side_and_B-side "A-side and B-side") of the original version by The Postal Service. It was later included on the B-sides and rarities album, _[Around the Well](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Around_the_Well "Around the Well")_. He then followed up on his debut album in 2003 with _[The Sea & The Rhythm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Sea_&_The_Rhythm "The Sea & The Rhythm")_, an EP containing other home-recorded tracks with a similar style to the songs on the debut.

Beam's second full-length album, _[Our Endless Numbered Days](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Our_Endless_Numbered_Days "Our Endless Numbered Days")_ (2004), was recorded in a professional studio with a significant increase in fidelity. Produced in Chicago by [Brian Deck](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brian_Deck "Brian Deck"), the focus was still on acoustic material, but the inclusion of other band members gave rise to a slightly different sound. That same year, he recorded the song "The Trapeze Swinger" for the film _[In Good Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/In_Good_Company_(2004_film) "In Good Company (2004 film)")_, and had his version of "Such Great Heights" featured in an advertisement for [M&M's](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M&M's "M&M's") and in the film and soundtrack for _[Garden State](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garden_State_(film) "Garden State (film)")_. This version was later used in a 2006 [Ask.com](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ask.com "Ask.com") advertisement, and eventually released as a single in 2006 backed with recordings of "The Trapeze Swinger" and "Naked as We Came" made for Radio Vienna.

[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/en/thumb/9/95/Sarahbeam.JPG/500px-Sarahbeam.JPG?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Sarahbeam.JPG) Sarah Beam, Samuel's sister, at the Beachland Ballroom in Cleveland

In February 2005, he released an EP entitled _[Woman King](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Woman_King "Woman King")_, which expanded on the sounds of his previous LP with the addition of electric guitars. Each track features a spiritual female figure, and had [Biblical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bible "Bible") overtones.

The EP _[In the Reins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/In_the_Reins "In the Reins")_, a collaboration with the Arizona-based rock band [Calexico](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Calexico_(band) "Calexico (band)"), was released in September 2005. Beam wrote all of the EP's songs years earlier, but Calexico added their trademark fusion of [southwestern](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southwestern_United_States "Southwestern United States") rock, traditional Mexican music and [jazz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jazz "Jazz") to the songs' arrangements. Several tracks, most notably, "Burn That Broken Bed", feature brass instruments, a first for Beam's music.[\[12\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iron_&_Wine#cite_note-12)

The third full-length Iron & Wine album, entitled _[The Shepherd's Dog](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Shepherd's_Dog "The Shepherd's Dog")_, was released September 25, 2007.[\[13\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iron_&_Wine#cite_note-13) This album was voted one of the ten best of 2007 by _[Paste](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paste_(magazine) "Paste (magazine)")_ magazine.[\[14\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iron_&_Wine#cite_note-14) Contributors included Joey Burns and Paul Niehaus of [Calexico](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Calexico_(band) "Calexico (band)"), as well as jazz musicians Matt Lux and [Bob Burger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bob_Burger?action=edit&redlink=1 "Bob Burger").[\[15\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iron_&_Wine#cite_note-exclaimmag-15) When asked to describe the album to _The Independent_, Beam remarked that "it's not a political propaganda record, but it's definitely inspired by political confusion, because I was really taken aback when [Bush](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_W._Bush "George W. Bush") got reelected."[\[16\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iron_&_Wine#cite_note-16)

Beam has released most of his music on [iTunes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ITunes "ITunes"), including several exclusive EPs. The _Iron & Wine iTunes Exclusive EP_ features unreleased studio recordings, including a [Stereolab](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stereolab "Stereolab") cover and two tracks which had previously only appeared on [vinyl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gramophone_record "Gramophone record"). The _Live Session (iTunes Exclusive)_ features Beam and his sister, Sarah Beam, performing a number of tracks from his albums, as well as a cover of [New Order](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Order_(band) "New Order (band)")'s " [Love Vigilantes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Love_Vigilantes "Love Vigilantes")". Sarah Beam has contributed backing vocals on many of Beam's studio recordings.

Beam's music has appeared in television series such as _[Grey's Anatomy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grey's_Anatomy "Grey's Anatomy")_, _[The L Word](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_L_Word "The L Word")_ and _[House M.D.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/House_(TV_series) "House (TV series)")_ "Flightless Bird, American Mouth" was used in the film _[Twilight](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twilight_(2008_film) "Twilight (2008 film)")_. The song was specifically chosen for the film's prom scene by [Kristen Stewart](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kristen_Stewart "Kristen Stewart"), the female lead, and appears on the [film's soundtrack](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twilight_(soundtrack) "Twilight (soundtrack)").

The B-sides and rarities album _[Around the Well](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Around_the_Well "Around the Well")_ was released in 2009. Iron & Wine also contributed the song "Stolen Houses (Die)" to the AIDS benefit album _[Dark Was the Night](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dark_Was_the_Night_(album) "Dark Was the Night (album)")_ produced by the [Red Hot Organization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Red_Hot_Organization "Red Hot Organization").

On November 26, 2010, Iron & Wine released a special edition _Record Store Day Black Friday_ 12" vinyl and CD single called, _Walking Far From Home_ for independent record stores.

_[Kiss Each Other Clean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kiss_Each_Other_Clean "Kiss Each Other Clean")_, Iron & Wine's fourth full-length album, was released on January 25, 2011, on [Warner Bros. Records](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warner_Bros._Records "Warner Bros. Records") in North America and 4AD for the rest of the world.[\[17\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iron_&_Wine#cite_note-17) With this album, Beam blended his earlier styles with a stronger pop influence.[\[18\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iron_&_Wine#cite_note-18)

[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/2/28/Iron_%26_Wine_in_concert_in_Atlanta%2C_August_5%2C_2024.jpg/500px-Iron_%26_Wine_in_concert_in_Atlanta%2C_August_5%2C_2024.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Iron_&_Wine_in_concert_in_Atlanta,_August_5,_2024.jpg) Beam in concert in [Atlanta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atlanta "Atlanta") on August 5, 2024

_[Ghost on Ghost](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ghost_on_Ghost "Ghost on Ghost")_, Iron & Wine's fifth studio album, was released in April 2013 on [Nonesuch Records](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nonesuch_Records "Nonesuch Records") in North America and 4AD for the rest of the world.[\[19\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iron_&_Wine#cite_note-19) _Ghost on Ghost_ marked a further exploration into the pop sounds of _Kiss Each Other Clean_ while also exhibiting jazz and R&B influences, with jazz drummer [Brian Blade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brian_Blade "Brian Blade") contributing to the album.[\[20\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iron_&_Wine#cite_note-20) In January 2014, recording during the polar vortex in Chicago, Beam and his regular collaborator Brian Deck co-produced eight of the ten songs on [Chadwick Stokes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chadwick_Stokes_Urmston "Chadwick Stokes Urmston")' 2015 album _The Horse Comanche_.[\[21\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iron_&_Wine#cite_note-21) Beam contributed the Iron & Wine band to the sessions and sang backing vocals.

Iron & Wine released two albums in 2015. _Archive Series: Volume 1_, released in February, featured unreleased songs recorded during the same period as _The Creek Drank the Cradle_. A covers album, _[Sing Into My Mouth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sing_Into_My_Mouth "Sing Into My Mouth")_, recorded with [Band of Horses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Band_of_Horses "Band of Horses") singer [Ben Bridwell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ben_Bridwell "Ben Bridwell"), was released in July.[\[22\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iron_&_Wine#cite_note-siym-22) _[Love Letter for Fire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Love_Letter_for_Fire "Love Letter for Fire")_, an album of duets with American singer-songwriter [Jesca Hoop](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jesca_Hoop "Jesca Hoop"), was released in 2016 on Sub Pop. Produced, recorded, and mixed by [Tucker Martine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tucker_Martine "Tucker Martine"), the album also featured contributions from [Wilco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wilco "Wilco")'s [Glenn Kotche](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glenn_Kotche "Glenn Kotche"), [Rob Burger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rob_Burger "Rob Burger"), [Eyvind Kang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eyvind_Kang "Eyvind Kang"), [Sebastian Steinberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sebastian_Steinberg "Sebastian Steinberg"), and Edward Rankin-Parker.[\[23\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iron_&_Wine#cite_note-23)

In August 2017, Iron & Wine's sixth studio album [_Beast Epic_](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beast_Epic_(album) "Beast Epic (album)") was released through [Sub Pop](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sub_Pop "Sub Pop") Records. The record saw Beam strip back the production and array of instruments from previous records to return to more simple and melodic song structures.[\[24\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iron_&_Wine#cite_note-24)

On March 21, 2019, [Calexico](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Calexico_(band) "Calexico (band)") and Iron & Wine announced _[Years to Burn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Years_to_Burn "Years to Burn")_, their first collaboration album. It was released on June 14, 2019, via Sub Pop.[\[25\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iron_&_Wine#cite_note-calexico-25)

_[Live at Third Man Records](https://ironandwine.com/album/live-at-third-man-records/)_ was released in 2019, after being recorded on August 31, 2017.[\[26\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iron_&_Wine#cite_note-26)

In 2024, Iron & Wine released their seventh studio album, _[Light Verse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Light_Verse_(album) "Light Verse (album)")_ and a cover of [American Football's](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Football_(band) "American Football (band)") _Never Meant_.[\[27\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iron_&_Wine#cite_note-:0-27)

## Personal life

Beam, his wife Kim, and their five daughters live in [Durham, North Carolina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Durham,_North_Carolina "Durham, North Carolina").[\[28\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iron_&_Wine#cite_note-28) He was raised in the [Bible belt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bible_belt "Bible belt") as a Christian, but is now an agnostic: "That was a confusing time for me, but I don't miss being misled. I'm not an atheist. There's an undeniable unseen world that some people call God and think they know more about than other people. I try not to get hung up on the names."[\[29\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iron_&_Wine#cite_note-29)[\[30\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iron_&_Wine#cite_note-30)

07. [↑](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iron_&_Wine#cite_ref-62)_Years to Burn_ did not enter the UK Albums Chart, but peaked at number 42 on the UK Album Sales Chart.[\[39\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iron_&_Wine#cite_note-uk_chart-39)

09. [↑](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iron_&_Wine#cite_ref-69)_Weed Garden_ did not enter the _Billboard_ 200, but peaked at number 53 on the US Top Album Sales chart.[\[55\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iron_&_Wine#cite_note-Album-Sales-60)
10. [↑](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iron_&_Wine#cite_ref-75)"Flightless Bird, American Mouth" did not enter the Hot Rock & Alternative Songs chart, but peaked at number 23 on the [Rock Digital Song Sales](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Billboard_charts#Rock "Billboard charts") chart.[\[65\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iron_&_Wine#cite_note-Rock-Digital-74)
