**Waxahatchee** is an American [indie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Independent_music "Independent music") music project, formed in 2010 by American singer-songwriter **Kathryn Crutchfield**[\[2\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Waxahatchee#cite_note-2) (born January 4, 1989), known professionally as **Katie Crutchfield**, following the breakup of her previous band [P.S. Eliot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/P.S._Eliot "P.S. Eliot"). The band is named after [Waxahatchee Creek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Waxahatchee_Creek "Waxahatchee Creek") in [Alabama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alabama "Alabama"), where Crutchfield grew up.[\[3\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Waxahatchee#cite_note-pitchrising-3) Originally an acoustic solo project, her recordings now tend to involve a full backing band.

As Waxahatchee, she has released six solo studio albums to date: _[American Weekend](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Weekend "American Weekend")_ (2012), _[Cerulean Salt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cerulean_Salt "Cerulean Salt")_ (2013), _[Ivy Tripp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ivy_Tripp "Ivy Tripp")_ (2015), _[Out in the Storm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Out_in_the_Storm "Out in the Storm")_ (2017), _[Saint Cloud](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Cloud_(album) "Saint Cloud (album)")_ (2020) and _[Tigers Blood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tigers_Blood "Tigers Blood")_ (2024), which was nominated for the 2025 [Grammy Award for Best Americana Album](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grammy_Award_for_Best_Americana_Album "Grammy Award for Best Americana Album"). Outside of Waxahatchee, Crutchfield is a member of [alternative country](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alternative_country "Alternative country") duo [Plains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plains_(band) "Plains (band)"), alongside [Jess Williamson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jess_Williamson "Jess Williamson"), and the [indie rock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indie_rock "Indie rock") band [Snocaps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Snocaps "Snocaps"), which features her twin sister [Allison Crutchfield](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swearin' "Swearin'"), [MJ Lenderman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MJ_Lenderman "MJ Lenderman") and Brad Cook.

## History

### 2010–2017: _American Weekend_, _Cerulean Salt_, and _Ivy Tripp_

While a member of [P.S. Eliot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/P.S._Eliot "P.S. Eliot"), a band formed with her twin sister [Allison](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Allison_Crutchfield "Allison Crutchfield"), Crutchfield released her first music as Waxahatchee as a cassette. Her bedroom-recorded debut album, _[American Weekend](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Weekend "American Weekend")_, was recorded in 2011 and released on [Don Giovanni Records](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Don_Giovanni_Records "Don Giovanni Records") in 2012.[\[4\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Waxahatchee#cite_note-4)[\[5\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Waxahatchee#cite_note-5)[\[6\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Waxahatchee#cite_note-6) Crutchfield wrote and recorded the album in one week at her family home in [Birmingham, Alabama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Birmingham,_Alabama "Birmingham, Alabama"). Her lyrics focused on personal relationships, devastation and longing.[\[7\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Waxahatchee#cite_note-7)

The album garnered positive reviews and was named a top album of 2012 by Dusted magazine.[\[8\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Waxahatchee#cite_note-8) "Be Good" was a song of the day on National Public Radio,[\[9\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Waxahatchee#cite_note-9) and listed as one of the best 50 songs of 2012.[\[10\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Waxahatchee#cite_note-10) "Catfish" was featured on an episode of the podcast _[Welcome to Night Vale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Welcome_to_Night_Vale "Welcome to Night Vale")_.

Waxahatchee's second album, _[Cerulean Salt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cerulean_Salt "Cerulean Salt")_, was released in March 2013 through [Don Giovanni Records](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Don_Giovanni_Records "Don Giovanni Records") in the United States, and four months later on [Wichita Recordings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wichita_Recordings "Wichita Recordings") in the U.K.[\[11\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Waxahatchee#cite_note-11)[\[12\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Waxahatchee#cite_note-:0-12) The critically acclaimed album reached number one on the [Official Record Store Chart](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Official_Record_Store_Chart "Official Record Store Chart") in July 2013 and scored 8.4 on [_Pitchfork_](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pitchfork_(website) "Pitchfork (website)").[\[13\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Waxahatchee#cite_note-13)[\[14\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Waxahatchee#cite_note-14) Waxahatchee supported [Tegan And Sara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tegan_and_Sara "Tegan and Sara") on their U.K. tour, before playing a headline U.K. tour in October that same year.[\[12\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Waxahatchee#cite_note-:0-12)[\[15\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Waxahatchee#cite_note-15)

In 2015 Crutchfield signed to Merge Records, which released her third album, _[Ivy Tripp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ivy_Tripp "Ivy Tripp")_, in April of that year.[\[16\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Waxahatchee#cite_note-Merge_Records-16) Waxahatchee toured non-stop for the rest of 2015, including tours with [Kurt Vile and the Violators](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kurt_Vile "Kurt Vile") and [Sleater Kinney](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sleater_Kinney "Sleater Kinney").

### 2017–present: _Out in the Storm_, _Saint Cloud_, Plains, _Tigers Blood_ and Snocaps

In 2017, Waxahatchee toured with [The New Pornographers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_New_Pornographers "The New Pornographers"), and also embarked on a headlining tour around the United States.[\[17\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Waxahatchee#cite_note-TNPW-17) In the autumn months the band toured parts of Europe, including several festival dates. Waxahatchee's fourth album, _[Out in the Storm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Out_in_the_Storm "Out in the Storm")_, was released on July 14, 2017, on Merge Records. It moves away from the lo-fi sound of previous albums, partly due to the guidance of co-producer [John Agnello](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Agnello "John Agnello"). It was recorded in the Miner Street Recordings studio with her former touring band.[\[18\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Waxahatchee#cite_note-18) Sam Sodomsky of _Pitchfork_ wrote of "Katie Crutchfield’s sharp, gorgeous songwriting", "immersive" band sound and "songs that play like fiery exorcisms" in a review of the album.[\[19\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Waxahatchee#cite_note-19) Waxahatchee opened [Jawbreaker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jawbreaker_(band) "Jawbreaker (band)")'s first Los Angeles shows in 22 years at the [Hollywood Palladium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hollywood_Palladium "Hollywood Palladium") on March 10, 2018, and in [New York City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_City "New York City") at [Brooklyn Steel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brooklyn_Steel "Brooklyn Steel") on February 27, 2018.[\[20\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Waxahatchee#cite_note-20)

In January 2020, Waxahatchee announced her fifth album, _[Saint Cloud](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Cloud_(album) "Saint Cloud (album)"),_ and released a single called "Fire". The album was recorded in 2019 at [Sonic Ranch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sonic_Ranch "Sonic Ranch") in Texas and at Long Pond in [Stuyvesant, New York](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stuyvesant,_New_York "Stuyvesant, New York") with producer Brad Cook. The album features Detroit-based band [Bonny Doon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bonny_Doon_(band) "Bonny Doon (band)"). On February 18, Waxahatchee released the single "Lilacs" and on March 16, she released the single "Can't Do Much."[\[21\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Waxahatchee#cite_note-21)[\[22\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Waxahatchee#cite_note-22) In a comprehensive interview with Will Gottsegen at Billboard, she spoke about her musical influences and recent sobriety.[\[23\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Waxahatchee#cite_note-Billboard_Saint_Cloud-23) The record made it at No. 7 on Billboard's [Emerging Artists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emerging_Artists "Emerging Artists") chart of April 2020, No. 1 on Heatseekers Albums, No. 2 on Americana/Folk Albums and No. 6 on Alternative Albums with 7,000 units. At the same time, the single "Lilacs" ranked at No. 36 on the Adult Alternative Songs airplay chart.[\[24\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Waxahatchee#cite_note-24)

[![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/b/b1/Waxahatchee_at_Huichica_Walla_Walla_in_2019.jpg/500px-Waxahatchee_at_Huichica_Walla_Walla_in_2019.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Waxahatchee_at_Huichica_Walla_Walla_in_2019.jpg) Waxahatchee performing at The Huichica festival near Walla Walla, Washington in 2019.

In the first months of the [COVID-19 pandemic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/COVID-19_pandemic "COVID-19 pandemic") in 2020, Crutchfield performed a series of [live streamed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Live_streaming "Live streaming") concerts comprising all the songs from one of her studio albums. She announced the series as a deep dive into her backlist in an attempt to reach out to her fans and also to generate some income after the [pandemic caused suspension of all touring](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Impact_of_the_COVID-19_pandemic_on_the_music_industry#Music_events "Impact of the COVID-19 pandemic on the music industry").[\[25\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Waxahatchee#cite_note-25)

In 2021, Waxahatchee performed at the [Newport Folk Festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Newport_Folk_Festival "Newport Folk Festival") as well the Mempho Music Festival in the Radians Amphitheater of Memphis, Tennessee.[\[26\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Waxahatchee#cite_note-26) Her album _Saint Cloud_ won the [Libera Awards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Libera_Awards "Libera Awards") 2021 as _Best Country Record_.[\[27\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Waxahatchee#cite_note-LibAwa21-27)

In July 2022, Crutchfield announced Plains, a collaboration project with singer-songwriter [Jess Williamson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jess_Williamson "Jess Williamson"). Their debut album, _[I Walked with You a Ways](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/I_Walked_with_You_a_Ways "I Walked with You a Ways")_, was released in October 2022.[\[28\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Waxahatchee#cite_note-28)

In December 2023, Waxahatchee made a guest appearance on the song "Pretty Paper" on the Philly Specials album _[A Philly Special Christmas Special](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A_Philly_Special_Christmas_Special "A Philly Special Christmas Special")_. The following month, she announced that the sixth Waxahatchee album _[Tigers Blood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tigers_Blood "Tigers Blood")_ would be released in March 2024. To coincide with the announcement, the album's lead single " [Right Back to It](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Right_Back_to_It "Right Back to It")" was released. It features harmonies from [MJ Lenderman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MJ_Lenderman "MJ Lenderman").[\[29\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Waxahatchee#cite_note-tigers-29) Waxahatchee toured in support of the album in 2024, traveling on a tour bus and playing 2,500-capacity theaters, having declined opportunities to play larger venues.[\[30\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Waxahatchee#cite_note-30) Waxahatchee has played the 2025 Barcelona and Porto editions of [Primavera Sound](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Primavera_Sound "Primavera Sound").[\[31\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Waxahatchee#cite_note-31)

In October 2025, Crutchfield posted a series of Polaroid photos of herself, Allison Crutchfield, MJ Lenderman and Brad Cook together. Allison, Lenderman and Cook all shared these photos on their own Instagram accounts as well.[\[32\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Waxahatchee#cite_note-32) On October 31, 2025, this tease was revealed to be a surprise new studio album for the Crutchfield sisters and Lenderman. The album was released on Anti- under the project name of Snocaps, and was self-titled. Cook produced the album, with the sisters sharing lead vocals and Lenderman playing multiple instruments.[\[33\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Waxahatchee#cite_note-33)

On January 20, 2026, Waxahatchee featured on the [Courtney Barnett](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Courtney_Barnett "Courtney Barnett") single "Site Unseen", contributing vocals. In the press release, Barnett said that she is "a big Waxahatchee fan" and added "I really love Katie's songwriting and her voice".[\[34\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Waxahatchee#cite_note-34)

## Personal life

Crutchfield’s twin sister Allison is also a musician, performing solo and with the band [Swearin'](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swearin' "Swearin'"). Crutchfield is sober; her 2020 album _Saint Cloud_ was largely written about her decision to stop drinking alcohol.[\[23\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Waxahatchee#cite_note-Billboard_Saint_Cloud-23)

Crutchfield has been in a relationship with songwriter [Kevin Morby](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kevin_Morby "Kevin Morby") since 2017, and they live together in [Overland Park, Kansas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Overland_Park,_Kansas "Overland Park, Kansas").[\[35\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Waxahatchee#cite_note-vulture-35) In 2017 the couple released a cover of " [After Hours](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/After_Hours_(The_Velvet_Underground_song) "After Hours (The Velvet Underground song)")" by [the Velvet Underground](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Velvet_Underground "The Velvet Underground").[\[36\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Waxahatchee#cite_note-36) In January 2018, indie label [Dead Oceans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dead_Oceans "Dead Oceans") released the single "Farewell Transmission" with "The Dark Don't Hide It" by Morby & Waxahatchee as the b-side, in [homage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homage_(arts) "Homage (arts)") to songwriter [Jason Molina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jason_Molina "Jason Molina").[\[37\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Waxahatchee#cite_note-37)[\[38\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Waxahatchee#cite_note-38) Merge Records published the digital single video _Chapel of Pines,_ on [YouTube](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/YouTube "YouTube") on July 17, 2018 _,_ which led Waxahatchee back to solo work.[\[39\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Waxahatchee#cite_note-39) Waxahatchee's cover of Kevin Morby's "Downtown's Lights" was featured over the closing credits of episode six of _[American Rust](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Rust_(TV_series) "American Rust (TV series)")_.[\[40\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Waxahatchee#cite_note-40)

On April 13, 2026, during her concert with [MJ Lenderman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MJ_Lenderman "MJ Lenderman") at the [Atlanta Symphony Hall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atlanta_Symphony_Hall "Atlanta Symphony Hall"), Crutchfield announced that she and Morby were expecting their first child.[\[41\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Waxahatchee#cite_note-41) The baby, a son, was born in July 2026.[\[42\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Waxahatchee#cite_note-42)
