**The Weather Station** is a Canadian [folk music](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Folk_music "Folk music") band fronted by [Tamara Lindeman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tamara_Hope "Tamara Hope"), formed in 2006. Band membership has changed over the years, but as of 2022[\[update\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=The_Weather_Station&action=edit) the lineup includes Lindeman (lead vocals, piano), Ben Whiteley (bass), Johnny Spence (keyboards), [Will Kidman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Will_Kidman "Will Kidman") (guitar), Christine Bougie (guitar), [Karen Ng](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karen_Ng_(musician) "Karen Ng (musician)") (saxophone, clarinet), Evan Cartwright (drums), and Kieran Adams (drums).

## History

Tamara Lindeman was born in Ontario in 1984. She started in acting before forming her band.

The band's debut album _The Line_ was released in 2009. Their second album, _All of It Was Mine_, made in collaboration with [Daniel Romano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daniel_Romano "Daniel Romano"), was released in 2011.[\[1\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Weather_Station#cite_note-1)[\[2\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Weather_Station#cite_note-2)

Lindeman was a nominee for the 2013 [SOCAN Songwriting Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SOCAN_Songwriting_Prize "SOCAN Songwriting Prize") for The Weather Station's song "Mule in the Flowers", co-written with [Steve Lambke](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steve_Lambke "Steve Lambke").[\[3\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Weather_Station#cite_note-3)

[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/8/88/Tamara_Lindeman_at_Hillside_Festival_2015.jpg/500px-Tamara_Lindeman_at_Hillside_Festival_2015.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Tamara_Lindeman_at_Hillside_Festival_2015.jpg) Frontwoman Tamara Lindeman performing at the 2015 [Hillside Festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hillside_Festival "Hillside Festival")

The band's third album, _Loyalty_, was recorded at La Frette studios near Paris, France, with [Afie Jurvanen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Afie_Jurvanen "Afie Jurvanen") and Robbie Lackritz, who have worked on albums with [Bahamas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bahamas_(musician) "Bahamas (musician)"), [Feist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Feist_(singer) "Feist (singer)"), [Zeus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zeus_(band) "Zeus (band)"), and [Jason Collett](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jason_Collett "Jason Collett"). _Loyalty_ was released on May 5, 2015, on Paradise of Bachelors (U.S. and worldwide) and [Outside Music](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Outside_Music "Outside Music") (Canada).[\[4\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Weather_Station#cite_note-4)[\[5\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Weather_Station#cite_note-5)

The self-titled fourth studio album was released in October 2017, on the Paradise of Bachelors (U.S. and worldwide) and Outside Music (Canada). The 11 tracks are purest indie folk.[\[6\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Weather_Station#cite_note-6) On October 18, 2018, _Live At Union_ was released independently as a limited edition available on Bandcamp.

The band's fifth album _[Ignorance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ignorance_(The_Weather_Station_album) "Ignorance (The Weather Station album)")_ was released worldwide on [Fat Possum Records](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fat_Possum_Records "Fat Possum Records") on February 5, 2021, and a small part (digital or vinyl only) on Next Door Records in Canada. The ten tracks were recorded with two percussionists, a saxophonist and a flutist, plus bass, keys, and guitar. Songwriter Tamara Lindeman wrote and produced all the songs. The album's theme is based on Lindeman's contemplation of the global climate crisis. Lindeman explained the album's title in an NPR interview as being "about this process of moving through denial into understanding".[\[7\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Weather_Station#cite_note-7) It was shortlisted for the [2021 Polaris Music Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_Polaris_Music_Prize "2021 Polaris Music Prize") and nominated for a [2022 Juno Award](https://junoawards.ca/awards/winners-nominees/).[\[8\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Weather_Station#cite_note-8)

On March 4, 2022, the band released their sixth album, _[How Is It That I Should Look at the Stars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/How_Is_It_That_I_Should_Look_at_the_Stars "How Is It That I Should Look at the Stars")_, also through Fat Possum. The album consists of ten songs written simultaneously with those on _Ignorance_ but deemed too dissimilar to be included on that album; Lindeman called the album "the moon to _Ignorance_'s sun". The album was recorded in March 2020 in Toronto. It was nominated for a 2023 Juno Award.[\[9\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Weather_Station#cite_note-9)

The Weather Station's seventh album, _[Humanhood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Humanhood "Humanhood")_, was released everywhere on January 17, 2025. The album was longlisted for the [2025 Polaris Music Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_Polaris_Music_Prize "2025 Polaris Music Prize"),[\[10\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Weather_Station#cite_note-10) and was a [Juno Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Juno_Award "Juno Award") nominee for [Adult Alternative Album of the Year](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Juno_Award_for_Adult_Alternative_Album_of_the_Year "Juno Award for Adult Alternative Album of the Year") at the [Juno Awards of 2026](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Juno_Awards_of_2026 "Juno Awards of 2026").[\[11\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Weather_Station#cite_note-11)
