**Bon Iver** ([/ˌboʊniːˈvɛər/](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help:IPA/English "Help:IPA/English") [_BONE ee-VAIR_](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help:Pronunciation_respelling_key "Help:Pronunciation respelling key")) is an American [indie folk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indie_folk "Indie folk") band founded in 2006 by singer-songwriter [Justin Vernon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Justin_Vernon "Justin Vernon")[\[2\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bon_Iver#cite_note-Bon_Iver-2) in [Eau Claire, Wisconsin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eau_Claire,_Wisconsin "Eau Claire, Wisconsin"). Vernon had originally formed Bon Iver as a solo project, but it eventually became a band consisting of Vernon (vocals, guitar), [Sean Carey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/S._Carey "S. Carey") (drums, keyboards, vocals), [Michael Lewis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_Lewis_(musician) "Michael Lewis (musician)") (vocals, baritone guitar, guitar, violin, saxophone), Matthew McCaughan (drums, bass, vocals), Andrew Fitzpatrick (guitar, keyboards, vocals), and [Jenn Wasner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jenn_Wasner "Jenn Wasner") (guitar, keyboards, vocals).

Vernon released Bon Iver's debut album, _[For Emma, Forever Ago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/For_Emma,_Forever_Ago "For Emma, Forever Ago"),_ independently in July 2007.[\[7\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bon_Iver#cite_note-auto-7) The majority of the album was recorded while Vernon spent three months isolated in a cabin in western [Wisconsin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wisconsin "Wisconsin").[\[7\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bon_Iver#cite_note-auto-7) In 2012, the band won the [Grammy Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grammy_Award "Grammy Award") for [Best Alternative Music Album](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grammy_Award_for_Best_Alternative_Music_Album "Grammy Award for Best Alternative Music Album") for their eponymous album _[Bon Iver](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bon_Iver,_Bon_Iver "Bon Iver, Bon Iver")_.[\[8\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bon_Iver#cite_note-Grammys-8)[\[9\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bon_Iver#cite_note-Wappler-9)[\[10\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bon_Iver#cite_note-Judki-10) They released their third album _[22, A Million](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/22,_A_Million "22, A Million")_ to critical acclaim in 2016.[\[11\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bon_Iver#cite_note-pitchfork.com-11) Their fourth album, _[I, I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/I,_I "I, I")_, was released in 2019. The album was nominated at the [2020 Grammy Awards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/62nd_Annual_Grammy_Awards "62nd Annual Grammy Awards") for [Album of the Year](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grammy_Award_for_Album_of_the_Year "Grammy Award for Album of the Year").[\[12\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bon_Iver#cite_note-12) Their fifth and most recent album, _[Sable, Fable](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sable,_Fable "Sable, Fable")_, was released in 2025.

The name "Bon Iver" derives from the French phrase _bon hiver_ (pronounced[\[bɔn‿ivɛʁ\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help:IPA/French "Help:IPA/French"); 'good winter'),[\[13\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bon_Iver#cite_note-Wickman@Slate-13) taken from a greeting on 1990s TV series _[Northern Exposure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_Exposure "Northern Exposure")_.[\[14\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bon_Iver#cite_note-Pitchfork_Mason-Campbell-14)

## History

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Bon_Iver&action=edit&section=1 "Edit section: History")\]

After the breakup of his band [DeYarmond Edison](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DeYarmond_Edison "DeYarmond Edison"), the ending of a relationship, and a bout of [mononucleosis hepatitis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Infectious_mononucleosis "Infectious mononucleosis"),[\[15\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bon_Iver#cite_note-Frere-15) Vernon left [Raleigh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Raleigh,_North_Carolina "Raleigh, North Carolina"), North Carolina, and moved back to Wisconsin. He spent the oncoming winter months at his father's cabin in [Dunn County, Wisconsin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dunn_County,_Wisconsin "Dunn County, Wisconsin"). According to Vernon, it was during this time that the "Bon Iver" moniker first entered his mind; while bedridden with mononucleosis, he began watching the 1990s TV series _[Northern Exposure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_Exposure "Northern Exposure")_ on DVD. One episode depicts a group of citizens in Alaska, where the show is set, emerging from their homes into the first snowfall of the winter and wishing one another _bon hiver_ ([lit.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Literal_translation "Literal translation") “good winter”). This was initially transcribed by Vernon as "boniverre"; however, when he learned of its proper [French](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_language "French language") spelling, he elected not to use it, deciding _hiver_ reminded him too much of " [liver](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liver "Liver")", the site of his illness at the time.[\[15\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bon_Iver#cite_note-Frere-15)

### 2006–2010: _For Emma, Forever Ago_

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Bon_Iver&action=edit&section=2 "Edit section: 2006–2010: For Emma, Forever Ago")\]

Main article: [For Emma, Forever Ago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/For_Emma,_Forever_Ago "For Emma, Forever Ago")

Vernon did not intend to write or record any music during the time but rather to recuperate from the events of the previous year. Eventually, however, a record began to evolve during this cathartic time of isolation.[\[16\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bon_Iver#cite_note-16) He had recently finished helping the band [The Rosebuds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Rosebuds "The Rosebuds") do some recording and had with him some basic recording gear when he made his move to the cabin in late 2006. Vernon played all the instruments during recording and each song was heavily edited with a large number of overdubs. Vernon wrote the lyrics for the album by recording a word-less melody and listening to the recording over and over while writing words according to the sound of the syllables of the melody.[\[17\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bon_Iver#cite_note-treble-17) In an interview, Vernon said, "Words like 'decision' and 'intention' aren't words that float in my head because I just went." Vernon explains, "I left North Carolina and went up there because I didn't know where else to go and I knew that I wanted to be alone and I knew that I wanted to be where it was cold."[\[18\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bon_Iver#cite_note-Whibbs-18) In another interview, Vernon describes what he used to record the album: "I had a very light set-up, a basic small recording set-up: a [Shure SM57](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shure_SM57 "Shure SM57") and an old [Silvertone guitar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silvertone_(brand) "Silvertone (brand)"). I had my brother drop off his old drums ... some other small things—things I would make or find lying around."[\[19\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bon_Iver#cite_note-19)

[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/2/25/Bon_Iver2.jpg/250px-Bon_Iver2.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Bon_Iver2.jpg) Vernon performing with Bon Iver in [Shepherd's Bush](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shepherd's_Bush_Empire "Shepherd's Bush Empire"), London, UK in 2008

The record was almost not released and was originally intended as a group of demos to be sent out to labels and potentially rerecorded. But after getting very encouraging reactions from a number of his friends, Vernon decided to release the songs himself in their present state.[\[17\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bon_Iver#cite_note-treble-17) According to his manager Kyle Frenette, an initial run of 500 CDs were pressed.[\[20\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bon_Iver#cite_note-hitquarters-20) The original CD release show took place at the House of Rock in [Eau Claire, Wisconsin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eau_Claire,_Wisconsin "Eau Claire, Wisconsin"). Justin played an earlier "all-ages" show and an evening 21+ show to release the original pressing of the album in its [card stock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Card_stock "Card stock") CD case. Of the 500 copies issued, 17 were sent to press, mainly blogs.[\[20\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bon_Iver#cite_note-hitquarters-20) The first significant attention the album received was from My Old Kentucky Blog in June 2007, after which point "it snowballed," according to manager Frenette.[\[20\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bon_Iver#cite_note-hitquarters-20) Another further breakthrough came when, in October 2007, the album received a very positive review from influential indie internet publication [_Pitchfork_](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pitchfork_(website) "Pitchfork (website)"). That exposure in turn led to a performance the same month at the industry showcase festival [CMJ Music Marathon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CMJ_Music_Marathon "CMJ Music Marathon"). The appearance attracted much label interest and Frenette later told [HitQuarters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HitQuarters "HitQuarters") that they subsequently spoke to various record companies, both independent and major.[\[20\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bon_Iver#cite_note-hitquarters-20) Out of all of them they decided to sign with the indie [Jagjaguwar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jagjaguwar "Jagjaguwar") because their ideals most resembled their own.[\[20\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bon_Iver#cite_note-hitquarters-20) The signing was confirmed on October 29, 2007.[\[21\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bon_Iver#cite_note-muzzleofbees-21)

_For Emma, Forever Ago_ was given an official release by Jagjaguwar.[\[22\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bon_Iver#cite_note-Maher-22) Vernon has said that he will continue to make albums without engineers and producers because he is capable of doing it all himself.[\[17\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bon_Iver#cite_note-treble-17) Since being released by Jagjaguwar on February 19, 2008, _For Emma, Forever Ago_ has garnered critical acclaim.[\[22\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bon_Iver#cite_note-Maher-22) The album was released in the UK and Europe by iconic indie label 4AD on May 12, 2008.[\[23\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bon_Iver#cite_note-23) When released in the UK, _For Emma, Forever Ago_ received 5/5 and "Album of the Month" reviews in both _[Mojo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mojo_(magazine) "Mojo (magazine)")_ and _[Uncut](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uncut_(magazine) "Uncut (magazine)")_ magazines. It was the seventh highest rated album of 2008 on [review aggregator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Review_aggregator "Review aggregator") site [Metacritic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metacritic "Metacritic"), with accolades from publications including _[The Village Voice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Village_Voice "The Village Voice")_, _[The Hartford Courant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Hartford_Courant "The Hartford Courant")_ and _[The A.V. ### 2011–2012: _Bon Iver, Bon Iver_

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Bon_Iver&action=edit&section=3 "Edit section: 2011–2012: Bon Iver, Bon Iver")\]

Main article: [Bon Iver (album)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bon_Iver_(album) "Bon Iver (album)")

_Bon Iver, Bon Iver_ was released on June 17, 2011. On March 23, 2011, [Justin Vernon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Justin_Vernon "Justin Vernon") told _[Rolling Stone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rolling_Stone "Rolling Stone")_ that a follow-up of _For Emma, Forever Ago_ would be released in December 2011. The album is composed of 10 songs and takes a new musical direction: "I brought in a lot of people to change my voice—not my singing voice, but my role as the author of this band, this project," says Vernon, who hired well-known players like [bass saxophonist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bass_saxophone "Bass saxophone") [Colin Stetson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colin_Stetson "Colin Stetson") and pedal-steel guitarist [Greg Leisz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greg_Leisz "Greg Leisz"). "I built the record myself, but I allowed those people to come in and change the scene." The second album is described as an "ambitious musical departure" from the first.[\[41\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bon_Iver#cite_note-41) Prior to the album's release, Vernon said that each song on the new album represents a place. The song "Perth" was described as a "Civil War-sounding heavy metal song," the song "Minnesota, WI" was described as featuring "finger-picked guitars, double bass drums and distorted bass saxophone," and the closing song " [Beth/Rest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beth/Rest "Beth/Rest")" would be "horn heavy."[\[42\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bon_Iver#cite_note-Serpick-42)

The album was recorded in a remodeled veterinarian clinic in Fall Creek, Wisconsin, which was bought by Vernon and his brother in 2008. It was converted into April Base Studios, built mainly over the defunct swimming pool attached to the clinic. Vernon's reason for recording in the location was that "\[it's\] been a wonderful freedom, working in a place we built. It's also only three miles from the house I grew up in, and just ten minutes from the bar where my parents met."[\[43\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bon_Iver#cite_note-43)

[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/4/46/Bon_Iver_2011.jpg/250px-Bon_Iver_2011.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Bon_Iver_2011.jpg) Bon Iver performing in 2011

On April 20, Bon Iver announced details of the release through the official Jagjaguwar and 4AD websites.[\[44\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bon_Iver#cite_note-44) The artwork for the album was also released, painted by the American artist [Gregory Euclide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gregory_Euclide "Gregory Euclide").[\[45\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bon_Iver#cite_note-45) Gregory also released two YouTube videos entitled the _Making of Bon Iver Album Art_.[\[46\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bon_Iver#cite_note-46)[\[47\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bon_Iver#cite_note-47) Over one month before the album release date, the album was accidentally made available for purchase on the iTunes Music Store on May 23. Apple removed the item from the store, but not before a handful of users were able to purchase and download it. There was no comment from Apple, the artist, or his label.[\[48\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bon_Iver#cite_note-48) However, the lyrics to the album were promptly uploaded to their record site.[\[49\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bon_Iver#cite_note-49)

Josh Jackson reviewed _Bon Iver_ for _[Paste](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paste_(magazine) "Paste (magazine)")_, saying, "It retains the beautiful melancholy of _For Emma_, but in nearly every way, it's just more. More layered, more diverse, more interesting. He brings in collaborators to do what they do best, but never at the expense of his sound and vision. It treads into new sonic directions without getting lost." He gave the album a score of nine out of ten.[\[50\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bon_Iver#cite_note-50) In an unfavorable review, Tim Sendra of [AllMusic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AllMusic "AllMusic") wrote, "\[Vernon's\] style comes off more like sub- [Enya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Enya "Enya") with a beard than a true studio wizard... _For Emma, Pt. 2_ would have been far more satisfying than this overblown debacle."[\[51\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bon_Iver#cite_note-51)

In late August 2011, English [post-dubstep](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Post-dubstep "Post-dubstep") musician and singer-songwriter [James Blake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Blake_(musician) "James Blake (musician)") uploaded a track called " [Fall Creek Boys Choir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fall_Creek_Boys_Choir "Fall Creek Boys Choir")", a collaboration with Bon Iver, to his YouTube channel. It was released as the first single from Blake's EP _[Enough Thunder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Enough_Thunder "Enough Thunder")_ and was also included on the deluxe edition of his self-titled [debut album](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Blake_(album) "James Blake (album)").[\[52\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bon_Iver#cite_note-52)

On November 30, 2011, the band earned four nominations for the [54th Grammy Awards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/54th_Grammy_Awards "54th Grammy Awards"): [Best New Artist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grammy_Award_for_Best_New_Artist "Grammy Award for Best New Artist"), [Best Alternative Music Album](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grammy_Award_for_Best_Alternative_Music_Album "Grammy Award for Best Alternative Music Album") for their album _Bon Iver_, and both [Song of the Year](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grammy_Award_for_Song_of_the_Year "Grammy Award for Song of the Year") and [Record of the Year](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grammy_Award_for_Record_of_the_Year "Grammy Award for Record of the Year") for ["Holocene"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holocene_(song) "Holocene (song)").[\[53\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bon_Iver#cite_note-53) On December 15, 2011, _Pitchfork_ awarded _Bon Iver_ the number one album of 2011.[\[54\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bon_Iver#cite_note-54) On February 12, 2012, Bon Iver won the Grammy for Best New Artist.[\[55\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bon_Iver#cite_note-Grammy-55) Bon Iver also won the Grammy for Best Alternative Album for _Bon Iver_, beating out Radiohead, Death Cab for Cutie, Foster the People and My Morning Jacket.[\[9\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bon_Iver#cite_note-Wappler-9) On February 15, a five-song studio session was released on YouTube, featuring Justin Vernon and Bon Iver drummer Sean Carey performing piano renditions of the tracks. The recording took place at [AIR Studio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Associated_Independent_Recording "Associated Independent Recording")-owned Lyndurst Hall in London, England.

### 2012–2018: Hiatus, _Wish I Was Here_, and _22, A Million_

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Bon_Iver&action=edit&section=4 "Edit section: 2012–2018: Hiatus, Wish I Was Here, and 22, A Million")\]

Main article: [22, A Million](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/22,_A_Million "22, A Million")

During the last performance of their 2012 tour in [Dublin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dublin "Dublin")'s [The O2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/3Arena_(Dublin) "3Arena (Dublin)"), Vernon announced that it was their last performance as a band, "... at least for a while..." At the time, these two events provoked speculation about whether this was a definite end to the band or simply a hiatus. A representative for Bon Iver's label, [Jagjaguwar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jagjaguwar "Jagjaguwar"), issued the statement, "They are just going off cycle after two very busy years on this record."[\[56\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bon_Iver#cite_note-56)

On November 12, 2012, the band announced its intent to take a break from performing after the last event of its 2012 tour in Dublin.[\[57\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bon_Iver#cite_note-57) Their label asserted that the band had not broken up.

On June 30, 2014, the band released a new song titled "Heavenly Father", which was featured on the soundtrack of [Zach Braff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zach_Braff "Zach Braff")'s film _[Wish I Was Here](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wish_I_Was_Here "Wish I Was Here")_.[\[58\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bon_Iver#cite_note-58) Bon Iver's song "Woods" was featured in the [Bluecoats](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bluecoats "Bluecoats") 2015 program _Kinetic Noise_, which was awarded a bronze medal at the [Drum Corps International](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drum_Corps_International "Drum Corps International") World Championships.[\[59\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bon_Iver#cite_note-59) On July 18, 2015, the band played their first show in almost three years at the Eaux Claires Music Festival in [Eau Claire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eau_Claire,_Wisconsin "Eau Claire, Wisconsin") where they debuted two new songs, "666 ʇ" and "89". Originally, both were slated to be on their next album, but only "666 ʇ" made it to the finished record. Unreleased track "89" has been played occasionally since, soon becoming a fan favorite.[\[60\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bon_Iver#cite_note-60) On November 18, 2015, the band announced a tour of Asia starting early 2016.\[ _[citation needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed "Wikipedia:Citation needed")_\]

On July 22, 2016, a 22-second live stream was broadcast on the band's official Facebook page, accompanied by the caption '#22days'. A short clip of previously unreleased music could be heard in the video, accompanied by an updated profile and cover photo. On August 17, 2016, the band announced that their next album's title would be _[22, A Million](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/22,_A_Million "22, A Million")_. They also provided a track list of 10 songs and released lyric videos for two songs: "22 (OVER S∞∞N)" and "10 dEATh bREasT". On August 29, 2016, the band released their lyric video for "33 "GOD"", the third single from the album.

_22, A Million_ was released on September 30, 2016. It was noted by many as a large stylistic shift for the band. One aspect of this shift included a drastic change in instrumentation; on _22, A Million_ and subsequent releases, Bon Iver often collaborated with a group of saxophonists known as [Sad Sax of Shit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sad_Sax_of_Shit?action=edit&redlink=1 "Sad Sax of Shit (page does not exist)").[\[61\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bon_Iver#cite_note-61) In [_Pitchfork_](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pitchfork_(website) "Pitchfork (website)")'s review of the album, the publication likened it to [Radiohead](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radiohead "Radiohead")'s _[Kid A](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kid_A "Kid A")_, saying, "Bon Iver's first album in five years takes an unexpected turn toward the strange and experimental. But behind the arranged glitches and processed voices are deeply felt songs about uncertainty."[\[11\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bon_Iver#cite_note-pitchfork.com-11)

### 2018–2023: _Come Through_ shows and _I, I_

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Bon_Iver&action=edit&section=5 "Edit section: 2018–2023: Come Through shows and I, I")\]

Main article: [I, I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/I,_I "I, I")

In mid 2018, Bon Iver partnered with TU Dance for _Come Through_, a series of shows featuring a group of contemporary dancers and during which several new songs were premiered. In late 2018, during an Instagram Live session, frontman Justin Vernon revealed a folder named "BI LP4", containing 13 new songs. He also revealed a snippet of the song "We Maddie Parry" (later shortened to "We").

[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/3/31/Bon_Iver_-_First_Avenue_Star.jpg/250px-Bon_Iver_-_First_Avenue_Star.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Bon_Iver_-_First_Avenue_Star.jpg) Bon Iver's star on the outside mural of [Minneapolis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minneapolis "Minneapolis") nightclub [First Avenue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Avenue_(nightclub) "First Avenue (nightclub)")

The band has been honored with a star on the outside mural of the [Minneapolis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minneapolis "Minneapolis") nightclub [First Avenue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Avenue_(nightclub) "First Avenue (nightclub)"),[\[62\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bon_Iver#cite_note-FirstAveStars-62) recognizing performers that have played sold-out shows or have otherwise demonstrated a major contribution to the culture at the iconic venue.[\[63\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bon_Iver#cite_note-StarTribune2019-63) Receiving a star "might be the most prestigious public honor an artist can receive in Minneapolis," according to journalist Steve Marsh.[\[64\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bon_Iver#cite_note-MSPMag2019-64)

Bon Iver recorded their fourth album in [Texas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Texas "Texas") during February and March 2019.[\[65\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bon_Iver#cite_note-65) On June 2, 2019, Bon Iver debuted two new songs from the upcoming album at [All Points East](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/All_Points_East "All Points East"), a more polished version of the previously played " [Hey, Ma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hey,_Ma_(Bon_Iver_song) "Hey, Ma (Bon Iver song)")" and the new song "U (Man Like)" (which was on the setlist Vernon showed in December and which features guest vocals from American singer-songwriter [Moses Sumney](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moses_Sumney "Moses Sumney"), [Bruce Hornsby](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bruce_Hornsby "Bruce Hornsby"), Elsa Jensen, [Jenn Wasner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jenn_Wasner "Jenn Wasner"), and the Brooklyn Youth Chorus).[\[66\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bon_Iver#cite_note-66) At the end of the same concert, Bon Iver advertised the website iCOMMAi, featuring similar cryptic messages as their recent lyric videos.[\[67\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bon_Iver#cite_note-67) The next day, both songs were released as singles alongside a list of fall tour dates. On July 2, the band released a teaser called "Sincerity is Forever in Season", voiced by Michael Perry and relating every album by the band to a season, with the then-upcoming fourth album being autumn.

A week later, on July 11, the band announced that their forthcoming album, _[I, I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/I,_I "I, I")_ (stylized as _i,i_), would be released on August 30, 2019. They also released the songs "Jelmore" and "Faith" as the third and fourth singles for the album, respectively. On July 31, the band released a documentary short called _Bon Iver: Autumn_, featuring Vernon and bandmates discussing the _I, I_ album and the upcoming _Autumn_ tour.[\[68\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bon_Iver#cite_note-68) On August 7, Bon Iver held free worldwide listening parties to preview the new album at over 60 locations around the world.[\[69\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bon_Iver#cite_note-69) On August 8, starting at 6:00am EST, the band began to release the previously unreleased album tracks one at a time, every hour on the hour, until 11:00am EST. The album as a whole was then released on several streaming services, 22 days before it was slated to be officially released. In September 2019, dates for Asian and European tours in 2020 were released.[\[70\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bon_Iver#cite_note-70)[\[71\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bon_Iver#cite_note-71)

During promotion of i,i Vernon revealed that he saw Bon Iver’s four primary albums as representative of the four seasons.[\[72\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bon_Iver#cite_note-72)

On January 21, 2020, the band announced a reissue of their first EP "Blood Bank", which contains new artwork by the usual collaborator Eric Timothy Carlson as well as both original songs and live versions of each one of them. It was released on March 27. On April 4, 2020, Justin Vernon premiered a new Bon Iver song, "Things Behind Things Behind Things" in "Where We Go From Here", a special event from Bernie Sanders.[\[73\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bon_Iver#cite_note-73) On April 17, Bon Iver released a brand new song called "PDLIF" (Please Don't Live In Fear), which 100% of the proceeds will go to [Direct Relief](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Direct_Relief "Direct Relief"), a company providing resources during the [COVID-19 pandemic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/COVID-19_pandemic "COVID-19 pandemic").[\[74\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bon_Iver#cite_note-74) The song was later sampled by [Baby Keem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baby_Keem "Baby Keem") and [Kendrick Lamar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kendrick_Lamar "Kendrick Lamar") for their song " [The Hillbillies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Hillbillies "The Hillbillies")".[\[75\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bon_Iver#cite_note-75)

On July 23, American singer-songwriter [Taylor Swift](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taylor_Swift "Taylor Swift") announced that Bon Iver would feature on " [Exile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exile_(Taylor_Swift_song) "Exile (Taylor Swift song)")" (stylized in [all lowercase](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Letter_case#All_lowercase "Letter case")), the second single from her eighth studio album, _[Folklore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Folklore_(Taylor_Swift_album) "Folklore (Taylor Swift album)")_.[\[76\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bon_Iver#cite_note-76) The song became Bon Iver's first top-ten hit on the _Billboard_ Hot 100. On August 5, Bon Iver released "AUATC" (Ate Up All Their Cake) featuring Elsa Jensen, Jenny Lewis, [Bruce Springsteen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bruce_Springsteen "Bruce Springsteen"), and Jenn Wasner. The song was accompanied by a press release critiquing capitalism.[\[77\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bon_Iver#cite_note-77) Swift then announced _[Evermore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Evermore_(Taylor_Swift_album) "Evermore (Taylor Swift album)")_, a surprise follow-up album to _Folklore_ on December 10, 2020, which also features Bon Iver on the title track.[\[78\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bon_Iver#cite_note-78) The band recorded the song "Second Nature" for the 2021 film _[Don't Look Up](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Don't_Look_Up_(2021_film) "Don't Look Up (2021 film)")_.[\[79\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bon_Iver#cite_note-79)

Bon Iver collaborated with American country star [Zach Bryan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zach_Bryan "Zach Bryan") on the title track "Boys of Faith" off Bryan's _[Boys of Faith](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boys_of_Faith "Boys of Faith")_ EP. The single charted on the [_Billboard_ Hot 100](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Billboard_Hot_100 "Billboard Hot 100") at a peak of number 26.[\[80\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bon_Iver#cite_note-80)

On July 28, 2023, Bon Iver member [Justin Vernon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Justin_Vernon "Justin Vernon") collaborated with American rap star [Travis Scott](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Travis_Scott "Travis Scott") on the tracks " [My Eyes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/My_Eyes_(Travis_Scott_song) "My Eyes (Travis Scott song)")" and " [Delresto (Echoes)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Delresto_(Echoes) "Delresto (Echoes)")". ### 2024–present: _SABLE, fABLE_

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Bon_Iver&action=edit&section=6 "Edit section: 2024–present: SABLE, fABLE")\]

Main article: [Sable, Fable](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sable,_Fable "Sable, Fable")

In September 2024, after years of touring in support of their previous album, Bon Iver returned with the single "Speyside", and announced a three-song EP, called _[Sable](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sable_(EP) "Sable (EP)")_, which was released on October 18.[\[82\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bon_Iver#cite_note-82)[\[83\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bon_Iver#cite_note-83) The EP marked a return to the project's roots, relying heavily on Vernon's voice and guitar, akin to their debut album, and consists of three songs recorded between 2020 and 2023.[\[84\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bon_Iver#cite_note-84) The EP was produced by Vernon and [Jim-E Stack](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jim-E_Stack "Jim-E Stack"), with the three songs grouped together as a deliberate " [triptych](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Triptych "Triptych")" which "runs the gamut from accepting anxiety to accepting guilt to accepting hope." All three songs are accompanied by black and white music videos featuring Vernon, and directed by [Erinn Springer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Erinn_Springer?action=edit&redlink=1 "Erinn Springer (page does not exist)").[\[85\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bon_Iver#cite_note-NewYorkerSable-85)

Regarding the EP's return to a stripped-back [indie folk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indie_folk "Indie folk") sound, Vernon noted: "From _[For Emma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/For_Emma,_Forever_Ago "For Emma, Forever Ago")_ until _[i,i](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/I,_I "I, I")_ it felt like it was an arc, or an expansion — from One to All. _I,I_ was very much me trying to talk about the We — the Us, outside of I. And when I got to these songs, the obvious thing was, well, people might think this is a return to something. But it really feels like a kind of raw second skin. I think about time in cylindrical, forward-moving circles. This feels like a new person, new skin. A new everything, more than a return. But I did feel like it was important to strip it down to just the bare essentials and get out of the way, to not hide with swaths of choirs. Just get it as close to the human ear as possible."[\[85\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bon_Iver#cite_note-NewYorkerSable-85)

On February 11, 2025, Vernon revealed that the _Sable_ EP, would be incorporated into a full-length studio album titled, _[Sable, Fable](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sable,_Fable "Sable, Fable")_, which was released on April 11, 2025. The album was announced with the following statement: " _SABLE,_ was the prologue, a controlled burn clearing the way for new possibilities. _fABLE_ is the book. Stories of introduction and celebration. The fresh growth that blankets the charred ground. Where _SABLE,_ was a work of solitude, _fABLE_ is an outstretched hand."[\[86\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bon_Iver#cite_note-SecretlyStoreSF-86)

On February 23, 2026, Bon Iver announced _Volumes_, a series of archival releases starting with an anthology of live recordings. The first release, _Volumes: One (Selections From Music Concerts 2019–2023 Bon Iver 6 Piece Band)_, will be released on April 3, 2026, on Jagjaguwar. In advance, a live version of "Heavenly Father" recorded at [Mediolanum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mediolanum "Mediolanum") in [Milan, Italy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Milan,_Italy "Milan, Italy") in 2022 was released. The six-piece band includes Vernon, Andrew Fitzpatrick, [Jenn Wasner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jenn_Wasner "Jenn Wasner"), Matthew McCaughan, [Michael Lewis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_Lewis_(musician) "Michael Lewis (musician)"), and [Sean Carey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/S._Carey "S. Carey").[\[87\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bon_Iver#cite_note-87)

## Live performances

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Bon_Iver&action=edit&section=7 "Edit section: Live performances")\]

In live performances, [Justin Vernon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Justin_Vernon "Justin Vernon") is joined by [Sean Carey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/S._Carey "S. Carey") (drums, vocals, piano), Michael Noyce (vocals, baritone guitar, guitar, violin) and Matthew McCaughan (bass, drums, vocals). Noyce was Vernon's guitar student during high school.[\[14\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bon_Iver#cite_note-Pitchfork_Mason-Campbell-14) Vernon uses various keyboard instruments during live performances including synthesisers such as a Dave Smith Instruments Prophet 6 and a [Teenage Engineering OP-1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Teenage_Engineering_OP-1 "Teenage Engineering OP-1"), while during studio recordings his instruments have included a Steinway & Sons Model D Concert Grand Piano.[\[88\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bon_Iver#cite_note-88)

Carey approached Vernon during one of the first Bon Iver shows, telling him he could play and sing all the songs. Thereafter, the two played a number of songs together.[\[89\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bon_Iver#cite_note-pitchfork-89) McCaughan and Vernon met while on tour with [indie rock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indie_rock "Indie rock") band [The Rosebuds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Rosebuds "The Rosebuds") in May 2007.

[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/7/70/Flickr_-_moses_namkung_-_Bon_Iver_5.jpg/250px-Flickr_-_moses_namkung_-_Bon_Iver_5.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Flickr_-_moses_namkung_-_Bon_Iver_5.jpg) From left to right: Noyce, Carey, and Vernon performing at [The Fillmore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Fillmore "The Fillmore") in 2009

Because of the extensive use of vocal harmony on the songs of _For Emma, Forever Ago_, Vernon was concerned that there would not be enough voices to duplicate the sound of the songs on the album in a live setting. To compensate for this problem in early performances, Vernon passed out lyrics for some songs to the audience to sing along to. In an interview with [_Pitchfork_](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pitchfork_(website) "Pitchfork (website)"), Vernon described this dilemma. "I don't want to be the guy with an acoustic guitar singing songs, because that's boring for the most part. The song actually needs 80–500 people singing or whatever the vibe is of that room, it needs that fight."[\[14\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bon_Iver#cite_note-Pitchfork_Mason-Campbell-14)

Bon Iver performed at [Lollapalooza](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lollapalooza "Lollapalooza") 2009\. Bon Iver has also performed at [Austin City Limits Music Festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austin_City_Limits_Music_Festival "Austin City Limits Music Festival"), [Glastonbury](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glastonbury_Festival_2009 "Glastonbury Festival 2009"), [Coachella](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coachella_Valley_Music_and_Arts_Festival "Coachella Valley Music and Arts Festival"), [Øyafestivalen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C3%98yafestivalen "Øyafestivalen"), [Way Out West](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Way_Out_West_Festival "Way Out West Festival"), [Bonnaroo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bonnaroo_Music_and_Arts_Festival "Bonnaroo Music and Arts Festival"), [Sasquatch!](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sasquatch!_Music_Festival "Sasquatch! Music Festival") and [All Points East](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/All_Points_East "All Points East") among others.

After the release of Bon Iver's second album, the live performances underwent a great deal of change. With older performances consisting of Vernon playing guitar, while Noyce, Carey, and McCaughan performed on varied instruments, the concerts following _[Bon Iver](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bon_Iver,_Bon_Iver "Bon Iver, Bon Iver")_ involved a much larger, full band. The new lineup consisted of the original four musicians, and included [Rob Moose](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rob_Moose "Rob Moose") on violin and guitar ( [Antony and the Johnsons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antony_and_the_Johnsons "Antony and the Johnsons"), [The National](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_National_(band) "The National (band)")), Mike Lewis on bass ( [Andrew Bird](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andrew_Bird "Andrew Bird"), [Happy Apple](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Happy_Apple "Happy Apple")), and a horn section including Reginald Pace, [Colin Stetson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colin_Stetson "Colin Stetson") ( [Tom Waits](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tom_Waits "Tom Waits"), [Arcade Fire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arcade_Fire "Arcade Fire")) and C.J. Camerieri ( [Rufus Wainwright](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rufus_Wainwright "Rufus Wainwright"), [Sufjan Stevens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sufjan_Stevens "Sufjan Stevens")) as well.\[ _[citation needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed "Wikipedia:Citation needed")_\] On February 2, 2012 [Sasquatch!](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sasquatch!_Music_Festival "Sasquatch! Music Festival") announced Bon Iver as a headliner for their 2012 festival.

Bon Iver appeared as the musical guest on _[Saturday Night Live](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saturday_Night_Live "Saturday Night Live")_ on February 4, 2012, where they performed their songs " [Holocene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holocene_(song) "Holocene (song)")"[\[90\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bon_Iver#cite_note-90) and " [Beth/Rest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beth/Rest "Beth/Rest")."[\[91\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bon_Iver#cite_note-91)

During Bon Iver's Asia tour in late February to early March 2016—the first tour since 2012—the live band still consisted of Vernon, Carey, McCaughan and Lewis, in addition to frequent Vernon collaborator Andrew Fitzpatrick (All Tiny Creatures, [Volcano Choir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volcano_Choir "Volcano Choir")) on guitar, synths and vocals; and sisters Emily, Jessica and Camilla Staveley-Taylor, also known as the folk vocal trio [The Staves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Staves "The Staves"), whose album _If I Was_ was produced by Vernon.[\[92\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bon_Iver#cite_note-92)[\[93\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bon_Iver#cite_note-93)

Bon Iver endorsed [Kamala Harris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kamala_Harris "Kamala Harris") in the 2024 US election, and performed at a rally in [Eau Claire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eau_Claire,_Wisconsin "Eau Claire, Wisconsin") for [Democratic candidates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Kamala_Harris_2024_presidential_campaign_endorsements "List of Kamala Harris 2024 presidential campaign endorsements") Kamala Harris and [Tim Walz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tim_Walz "Tim Walz") on August 7, 2024.[\[94\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bon_Iver#cite_note-94)[\[95\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bon_Iver#cite_note-95)

## Mini-documentary

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Bon_Iver&action=edit&section=8 "Edit section: Mini-documentary")\]

_**Bon Iver: Autumn**_ is a 12-minute film directed by [Andrew Swant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andrew_Swant "Andrew Swant"), documenting the preparation for Bon Iver's 2019 North American tour to support their fourth album, _[I, I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/I,_I "I, I")_. The film focuses on Justin Vernon discussing the production of the upcoming live shows, the desire to maintain an intimate feeling despite the fact that they will be playing in larger, arena-sized venues. It previews the motion-tracking technology used in the light show, and the immersive three-dimensional sound design planning.
