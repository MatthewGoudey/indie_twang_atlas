Musical artist

**Laura Pauline Veirs**[\[1\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laura_Veirs#cite_note-BMI-RepertoireProfile-1) (born October 24, 1973)[\[2\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laura_Veirs#cite_note-2) is an American singer-songwriter based in [Portland, Oregon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portland,_Oregon "Portland, Oregon").[\[3\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laura_Veirs#cite_note-NYTimes-YearOfMeteors-2005-3) She is known for her [folk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Folk_music "Folk music") and [alternative country](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alternative_country "Alternative country") records and live performances as well as her collaboration with [Neko Case](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neko_Case "Neko Case") and [k.d. lang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K.d._lang "K.d. lang") on the [case/lang/veirs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Case/lang/veirs "Case/lang/veirs") project. Veirs has written a children's book and hosts a podcast about parenting and performing.

## Early life and education

Veirs graduated from [General William J. Palmer High School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palmer_High_School_(Colorado) "Palmer High School (Colorado)") in [Colorado Springs, Colorado](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colorado_Springs,_Colorado "Colorado Springs, Colorado").[\[4\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laura_Veirs#cite_note-Gazette-JulyFlame-2010-4)

In 1997, Veirs graduated from [Carleton College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carleton_College "Carleton College"), where she was a geology major and studied [Mandarin Chinese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mandarin_Chinese "Mandarin Chinese").[\[4\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laura_Veirs#cite_note-Gazette-JulyFlame-2010-4)[\[5\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laura_Veirs#cite_note-Carleton-GeologyDept-1997-5) During this time, she worked as a translator for a geological expedition in China.[\[6\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laura_Veirs#cite_note-Carleton-Sasquatch-2006-6)

## Career

[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/6/63/Laura_Veirs_and_Jos%C3%A9_Medeles_play_at_Mississippi_Studios.jpg/500px-Laura_Veirs_and_Jos%C3%A9_Medeles_play_at_Mississippi_Studios.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Laura_Veirs_and_Jos%C3%A9_Medeles_play_at_Mississippi_Studios.jpg) Veirs playing [Mississippi Studios](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mississippi_Studios "Mississippi Studios") with José Medeles, 2022

Growing up, Veirs heard folk-country, [classical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Classical_music "Classical music"), and pop music at home. However, according to herself, she did not "listen seriously," until she was in her twenties.[\[7\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laura_Veirs#cite_note-Biography-7)

At Carleton, she joined all-girl [punk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Punk_rock "Punk rock") band Rair Kx![\[8\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laura_Veirs#cite_note-8) After graduation, her taste moved to older country and folk, and during her time in China she began writing lyrics.[\[7\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laura_Veirs#cite_note-Biography-7)

In 1999, Veirs released her [eponymous debut studio album](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laura_Veirs_(album) "Laura Veirs (album)"), which was recorded live and featuring just herself and guitar. 2003 saw the release of _[Troubled by the Fire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Troubled_by_the_Fire "Troubled by the Fire")_, accompanied by veteran musicians such as [Bill Frisell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bill_Frisell "Bill Frisell"). She then signed to [Nonesuch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nonesuch_Records "Nonesuch Records") and released _[Carbon Glacier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carbon_Glacier_(album) "Carbon Glacier (album)")_ (2004). She also signed to [Bella Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bella_Union "Bella Union") for European distribution. _[Year of Meteors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Year_of_Meteors "Year of Meteors")_ followed in August 2005, and was selected by _[The New York Times](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_New_York_Times "The New York Times")_ as a "critic's choice".[\[3\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laura_Veirs#cite_note-NYTimes-YearOfMeteors-2005-3)

Veirs released her final studio album for Nonesuch, _[Saltbreakers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saltbreakers "Saltbreakers")_, in 2007.

In January 2010, Veirs released _[July Flame](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/July_Flame "July Flame")_ on her own Raven Marching Band Records.[\[9\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laura_Veirs#cite_note-NPR-FirstListen-JulyFlame-2009-9) The album received praise from [The Washington Post](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Washington_Post "The Washington Post")'s Express tabloid, among other publications.[\[10\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laura_Veirs#cite_note-WashPost-JulyFlame-Review-2011-10)

In November 2011, Veirs released the album _Tumble Bee: Laura Veirs Sings Folk Songs for Children_. The album presented mostly traditional songs, and featured an array of guest musicians, including [Colin Meloy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colin_Meloy "Colin Meloy"), [Jim James](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jim_James "Jim James") and [Béla Fleck](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B%C3%A9la_Fleck "Béla Fleck").[\[11\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laura_Veirs#cite_note-11) _Tumble Bee_ won a [Parents' Choice Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parents'_Choice_Award "Parents' Choice Award") in 2012.\[ _[citation needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed "Wikipedia:Citation needed")_\]

In September 2012, her first feature film soundtrack was released: _Hello I Must Be Going_, for the [movie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hello_I_Must_Be_Going_(2012_film) "Hello I Must Be Going (2012 film)") of the same name. The exclusive soundtrack featured eight instrumental versions of previously released songs, five catalog favorites and one new vocal track, "Spring Song".

In August 2013, she released _[Warp & Weft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warp_&_Weft "Warp & Weft")_. In 2015, she worked on the album _[Carrie & Lowell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carrie_&_Lowell "Carrie & Lowell")_ by [Sufjan Stevens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sufjan_Stevens "Sufjan Stevens"). Veirs' long term collaborator and then husband, [Tucker Martine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tucker_Martine "Tucker Martine"), engineered some of the album.

Veirs was lead songwriter\[ _[citation needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed "Wikipedia:Citation needed")_\] for the 2016 album _[case/lang/veirs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Case/lang/veirs#Album "Case/lang/veirs")_, a collaborative project between Veirs, [k.d. lang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K.d._lang "K.d. lang") and Neko Case. The album received positive reviews and a [Metacritic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metacritic "Metacritic") rating of 82.[\[12\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laura_Veirs#cite_note-Metacritic-12)[\[13\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laura_Veirs#cite_note-RollStone2016-13)

In April 2018, she released her tenth studio album _[The Lookout](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Lookout_(album) "The Lookout (album)")_ on her own label Raven Marching Band in North American and [Bella Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bella_Union "Bella Union") in the rest of the world. It was a concept album about the fragility of precious things. It featured contributions from [Sufjan Stevens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sufjan_Stevens "Sufjan Stevens"), [Jim James](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jim_James "Jim James") and was produced by Tucker Martine. Earlier, in January 2018, she had launched a podcast, _Midnight Lightning_, about the lives of musician parents. The first season featured weekly interviews with 14 mother musicians such as [Corin Tucker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corin_Tucker "Corin Tucker") ( [Sleater-Kinney](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sleater-Kinney "Sleater-Kinney")), [Carol Kaye](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carol_Kaye "Carol Kaye"), [Rosanne Cash](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rosanne_Cash "Rosanne Cash"), [Rhiannon Giddens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rhiannon_Giddens "Rhiannon Giddens"), [Meshell Ndegeocello](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meshell_Ndegeocello "Meshell Ndegeocello"), [Amanda Bergman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amanda_Bergman "Amanda Bergman") among others. In early 2018, [Chronicle Books](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chronicle_Books "Chronicle Books") published her first picture book, _Libba: The Magnificent Musical Life of Elizabeth Cotten_.[\[14\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laura_Veirs#cite_note-LauraVeirs-Libba-2018-14) A story about the influential American folk and [blues](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blues "Blues") musician [Elizabeth Cotten](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elizabeth_Cotten "Elizabeth Cotten") (aka "Libba") it was illustrated by [Tatyana Fazlalizadeh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tatyana_Fazlalizadeh "Tatyana Fazlalizadeh"). _Libba_ was a [Junior Library Guild](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Junior_Library_Guild "Junior Library Guild") selection and received the [Parents' Choice Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parents'_Choice_Award "Parents' Choice Award").

On [Valentine's Day](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Valentine's_Day "Valentine's Day") 2020, Veirs released the song "I Was a Fool", exploring her divorce.[\[15\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laura_Veirs#cite_note-15) In July, her eleventh studio album _[My Echo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/My_Echo "My Echo")_ was made available for [pre-order](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pre-order "Pre-order") with a scheduled release in October.[\[16\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laura_Veirs#cite_note-16) Veirs continued to write and record, finishing most of an album's worth of material by the time, _[My Echo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/My_Echo "My Echo")_, was released.[\[17\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laura_Veirs#cite_note-guard-17) In 2022, Veirs co-produced her first studio album _[Found Light](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Found_Light "Found Light")_ with Shahzad Ismaily.

_[Phone Orphans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phone_Orphans "Phone Orphans")_, an album of [demos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Demo_(music) "Demo (music)") that Veirs recorded on her [iPhone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IPhone "IPhone"), was released in 2023.\[ _[citation needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed "Wikipedia:Citation needed")_\] In the same year she recorded a live album at a show at [Brooklyn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brooklyn "Brooklyn")'s The Owl Parlor. It was released 2024, called _Laura Veirs and Her Band – Live in Brooklyn._ It featured her band mates Shahzad Ismaily, Sam Amidon, Karl Blau and Charlotte Greve.[\[18\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laura_Veirs#cite_note-:0-18) Veirs was inducted into the Oregon Music Hall of Fame in October, 2024.\[ _[citation needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed "Wikipedia:Citation needed")_\]

Veirs' album _Temple Songs_ was released in August 2026.[\[19\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laura_Veirs#cite_note-19)

## Raven Marching Band Records

Veirs runs a record label, Raven Marching Band Records, that has released her own and other artists' music. In 2016, the label released _[Introducing Karl Blau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karl_Blau "Karl Blau")_, which was crowdfunded.[\[22\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laura_Veirs#cite_note-22)[\[23\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laura_Veirs#cite_note-23)

## Style

In 2007, Veirs said about her lyrics, "I like to be personal in my writing, but not overly confessional."[\[24\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laura_Veirs#cite_note-24)

Many of her lyrics are inspired by literary works. The song "Saltbreakers" on her sixth studio album _[Saltbreakers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saltbreakers "Saltbreakers")_ (2007) was partially inspired by [A. S. Byatt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A._S._Byatt "A. S. Byatt")'s _[Possession](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Possession_(Byatt_novel) "Possession (Byatt novel)")_ (1990). Another song on the same album, "Don't Lose Yourself", was partially inspired by [José Saramago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jos%C3%A9_Saramago "José Saramago")'s novel, _[Blindness](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blindness_(novel) "Blindness (novel)")_ (1995). [T. S. Eliot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/T._S._Eliot "T. S. Eliot") was an inspiration for the song "Margaret Sands" on _The Lookout_.

## Personal life

Veirs lives in [Portland, Oregon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portland,_Oregon "Portland, Oregon"). Around 2000, Veirs married Tucker Martine, who has collaborated with her musically. Veirs and Martine had two children,[\[18\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laura_Veirs#cite_note-:0-18) born 2010 and 2013. In November 2019, Veirs announced she and Martine were divorcing.[\[25\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laura_Veirs#cite_note-MotherMaker-Lookout-2018-25)[\[26\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laura_Veirs#cite_note-Oregonian-JulyFlame-2010-26)[\[20\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laura_Veirs#cite_note-thelineofbestfit.com-20) In the summer of 2024, Veirs announced her engagement to Reed College music professor and ethnomusicologist Morgan Luker. Veirs and Luker married on June 28, 2025 in Portland, Oregon at Mississippi Studios.
