# Cowork Prompt: "The V" — an album atlas of indie twang, slacker rock, and everything that fed them

## 0. The job

Build two deliverables in this folder:

1. **`V_Album_Atlas.xlsx`**: a master album database, as large as quality allows (floors in §6), with supporting sheets.
2. **`V_Map.docx`**: a genre-genealogy document that explains the shape of the V, with a lineage diagram, an essay per lane, and guided listening paths.

Also keep a `work/` folder of staging files, checkpoints, and logs so the project can be resumed if the session stops.

How to run:
- Work through the phases in §8 on your own. Don't stop to ask me questions. When you hit a judgment call, decide it using §2–§4, log it in the Borderline Log, and keep going.
- Accuracy comes before size. A smaller atlas with no invented albums beats a bigger one with errors.

- Read your xlsx and docx skills before building those files.

## 1. Who this is for

I love country, and I love indie rock descended from the college-rock and slacker-rock scenes. My anchors are MJ Lenderman, Wednesday (and Karly Hartzman solo), Villagerrr, Big Thief, and Greg Freeman, plus the broader wave people call indie twang, yallternative, or slacker twang. A lot of it traces back to Neil Young & Crazy Horse. For the modern end, my best guide is Josh Terry's newsletter *No Expectations*.

The attached field guide is my starting sketch: about 150 artists across ten regions, with Artists, Roots, and Labels sheets. It works at the artist level with one or two "start with" records each, and it's far from complete. This atlas goes down to the album level and should reuse the field guide's Region and Style vocabulary so the two files match.

The purpose is discovery. I want a map I can dig through for years, with enough description on every album to decide what to play next.

## 2. The shape: a V

Picture a V. The bottom point is the modern indie-twang and slacker scene (2014 to now). Going up the V goes back in time through the genres that fed it, and the V widens with each era because there are more ancestor genres. At the outer edges, where a popular ancestor genre spawned descendants that went somewhere else (country → Morgan Wallen), those branches appear too, but only as context.

Every album is placed with two fields.

**Layer** (vertical position), set purely by the album's original release year:

| Layer | Years |
|---|---|
| L1 Now | 2014–2026 |
| L2 Bridge | 1998–2013 |
| L3 Nineties | 1987–1997 |
| L4 Underground | 1978–1986 |
| L5 Classic | 1965–1977 |
| L6 Roots | before 1965 |

**Zone** (horizontal position), set by the album's primary lane (§3):

| Zone | What it means | Depth |
|---|---|---|
| Core | The bottom of the V: the scenes I'm living in now | Completionist. Every album that fits and has credible critical, blog, or Bandcamp attention |
| V | Direct ancestors and close siblings of the Core | Deep. The full shelf a devoted fan of that lane would own, plus a strong second tier of lesser-known artists |
| Context | Popular branches whose main line leads outside the V | Landmarks and hits only, 8–20 albums per lane, each explaining where the branch diverges |

The top of the V: the rock side stops around 1965 (Dylan goes electric, the Byrds, the Velvet Underground). The country side reaches back to Hank Williams and 1950s Bakersfield. Anything older sits in a thin roots lane (R1) of 25–40 records, which counts as V zone.

### Rulings already made (apply these)

- Countrygaze is Core. Classic shoegaze and dream pop are Context. The 2020s non-twang shoegaze revival is out entirely.
- Twangy emo crossovers are Core. Emo proper is Context.
- The modern Dead-indebted guitar bands in my field guide are Core (C6). The older solo-guitar and American Primitive line is V (V8).
- Sonic Youth and Dinosaur Jr. are V. Other post-punk and noise rock is Context.
- Heartland rock is Context, but flag records like Springsteen's Nebraska as influences in their Lineage field.
- Pop and indie artists' country turns are Context by default. Promote an individual album to V only when it clearly overlaps the Core sound, and log it.
- Field-guide artists who belong to a scene but not the sound (for example Indigo De Souza, Robber Robber) go in the closest Context lane with their Scene filled in.
- Out entirely: anything with no traceable path to the Core.

## 4. Inclusion rules

**The V test.** An album belongs in the V zone if it sits in a V lane and it's either (a) a documented influence on Core artists, or (b) an audible ancestor of the Core sound along one of these eight trunk lines:

1. Neil Young & Crazy Horse guitar: loose, loud, ragged, long solos
2. Country played by punk and indie kids: cowpunk → No Depression → indie twang
3. Deadpan, funny-sad songwriting: Prine and Tom T. Hall → Drag City → today
4. Southern storytelling rock: Skynyrd → Drive-By Truckers → Wednesday
5. Slow, sad, and noisy textures: Velvet Underground → slowcore and noise → countrygaze
6. The DIY network: SST and college radio → lo-fi → 2010s Bandcamp → today
7. Cosmic American Music: Gram Parsons and the Dead → the 2010s revival
8. Folk songwriting: Carter Family and Guthrie → Townes → Gillian Welch → Big Thief

**Context test.** A lane is Context when it descends from a V ancestor but its main line leads elsewhere. Each Context album's Lineage field must say where the branch split off.

**Anchor artists get full coverage.** For MJ Lenderman, Wednesday, Karly Hartzman, Villagerrr, Big Thief (plus Adrianne Lenker and Buck Meek solo), Greg Freeman, and Neil Young, include every studio album, notable EP, live album, and side project. For Neil, be completionist through 1995, then include the key later records.

**Field guide coverage.** Every artist in my field guide must appear with at least one album.

**Release types.** Include LPs, notable EPs, landmark live albums, and collaborations. Exclude bootlegs, deluxe or reissue duplicates, and singles. Include compilations only when they're canonical, such as Wanted! The Outlaws, Harry Smith's Anthology, or the standard Hank Williams, Carter Family, and Jimmie Rodgers collections in R1, where the singles era left no albums to use.

**Priority.** Tag each album Essential (a lane's defining records), Recommended (strong and representative), or Deep cut (for the committed). Mark one or two "Start here" albums per lane.

**Lineage honesty.** Mark lineage Documented only when an interview, cover, credit, shared member, or reputable critic establishes it. Otherwise mark it Inferred and phrase it as resemblance ("sounds indebted to...").

## 8. Phases

Parallelize the research inside each phase with sub-agents, split by lane or by source. Sub-agents write rows to `work/staging/` in the Albums schema and never touch the master file. You merge, deduplicate, and validate.

**Phase 0: Setup.** Read this prompt fully, all three sheets of my field guide, and your xlsx and docx skills. Create `work/staging/`, `work/checkpoints/`, and `work/logs/`. Write `work/plan.md` (lanes, sources per phase, sub-agent split) and `work/state.json` (status of each phase). If `state.json` already exists, you're resuming: continue from the last unfinished phase instead of starting over.
Exit check: every field-guide artist has a draft lane assignment in `work/fieldguide_mapping.csv`.

**Phase 1: Skeleton.** Research each lane enough to write its definition, parent and child lanes, scenes, and labels, and to expand its seed list (aim for 15+ artists per Core and V lane, and 8+ per Context lane where the lane supports it). Draft the Lanes, Scenes, Labels, and Tags sheets.
Exit check: every Core lane traces upward through V lanes to an L5 or L6 ancestor, and no lane is an orphan.

**Phase 2: Core harvest.** Work through §5's Core sources in order, starting with the full No Expectations archive. Then do an artist-completion pass (each Core artist's full discography within the lane) and a network pass (members, side projects, session players, labels, "for fans of" chains). Continue until new searches mostly return albums you already have.

**Phases 3–6: Climbing the V.** Phase 3 is L2 Bridge, Phase 4 is L3 Nineties, Phase 5 is L4 Underground, and Phase 6 is L5 Classic plus R1 Roots. For each layer, build the canon first (each lane's essential records, confirmed across several canon sources). Then go deep: second-tier artists, label catalogs, and influences cited by Core artists.

**Phase 7: Context lanes.** Landmarks and hits for each Context lane across all layers, each with a Lineage note on where the branch diverges.

**Error-correction loop.** Run this at the end of every phase from 2 through 7:

1. **Facts.** Every row has at least one verification URL confirming the artist, exact title, original year, and original label. Two agreeing sources means High confidence; one reliable source means Medium. A row you can't verify goes to Needs Verification, never to Albums.
2. **Independent re-check.** A sub-agent that didn't create the rows re-verifies a random 15% sample plus every Medium row. If more than 5% of the sample has any error (wrong year, label, or title, or an album that doesn't exist), re-verify the phase's entire batch, then sample again.
3. **Dedup and normalize.** One row per album, canonical artist names, no reissue duplicates.
4. **Consistency.** Layer matches Year. Zone matches the primary lane's zone (log any exception). Tags come only from the Tags sheet. Required fields are filled.
5. **Coverage.** Compare against the phase's canon sources (Terry's lists, RYM charts by year, canon lists). Any fitting album that appears in two or more sources must be in Albums or logged as a deliberate exclusion in the Borderline Log.
6. **Descriptions.** Review 10% of descriptions: written in your own words, specific to the album, accurate, no quote over 10 words, and no invented quotes, chart claims, or biographical details.

Log each run in the QA Log. After each phase, save staging files to `work/checkpoints/`, update `state.json`, and write a short summary to `work/logs/`.

**Phase 8: Global QA.** Run the full loop across the whole database. Also check that every anchor artist's discography is complete, every field-guide artist appears, no L1 year has fewer than about 20 Core albums without an explanation, every lane meets its depth expectation, and artist names, lanes, and scenes are consistent across all sheets.

**Phase 9: Build the spreadsheet** per §7.1.

**Phase 10: Write the V Map** per §7.2. Check that every Album ID it cites exists and matches.

**Phase 11: Final audit.** Open both files and confirm they render properly, the formulas calculate, and the dropdowns work.

## 9. Writing rules
